## Catalyst and Catalysis

Substances which alter the rate of a chemical reaction and themselves remain chemically and quantitatively unchanged after the reaction are known as catalysts, and the phenomenon is known as catalysis.

* The term catalyst was suggested by Berzelius in 1835, who first studied the effect of foreign substances on reaction rates systematically.

!!! example "Example"

    Potassium chlorate decomposes slowly to give oxygen in the temperature range 653–873 K:

    $$2KClO_3 &rarr; 2KCl + 3O_2$$

    When a little MnO<sub>2</sub> is added, the decomposition takes place at a much lower temperature (473–633 K) and at a much faster rate. The MnO<sub>2</sub> remains unchanged in mass and composition.

## Promoters and Poisons

* **Promoters** are substances that enhance the activity of a catalyst.
* **Poisons** are substances that decrease the activity of a catalyst.
* Example: In Haber's process for the manufacture of ammonia, molybdenum acts as a promoter for iron, which is the catalyst.

!!! tip ""

    $$N_{2(g)} + 3H_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{Fe(s),\ Mo(s)} 2NH_{3(g)}$$

## Types of Catalysis

### 1. Homogeneous catalysis

When the reactants and the catalyst are in the same phase (liquid or gas), the process is called homogeneous catalysis.

!!! example "Examples"

    * Oxidation of SO<sub>2</sub> to SO<sub>3</sub> with oxides of nitrogen as catalyst in the lead chamber process (all in gas phase):

    $$2SO_{2(g)} + O_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{NO(g)} 2SO_{3(g)}$$

    * Hydrolysis of methyl acetate catalysed by H<sup>+</sup> ions from hydrochloric acid (all in liquid phase):

    $$CH_3COOCH_{3(l)} + H_2O_{(l)} \xrightarrow{HCl(l)} CH_3COOH_{(aq)} + CH_3OH_{(aq)}$$

    * Hydrolysis of sugar catalysed by H<sup>+</sup> ions from sulphuric acid (all in solution):

    $$C_{12}H_{22}O_{11(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} \xrightarrow{H_2SO_4(l)} C_6H_{12}O_{6(aq)}\ (glucose) + C_6H_{12}O_{6(aq)}\ (fructose)$$

### 2. Heterogeneous catalysis

When the reactants and the catalyst are in different phases, the process is called heterogeneous catalysis.

!!! example "Examples"

    * Oxidation of SO<sub>2</sub> to SO<sub>3</sub> in the presence of Pt (gas reactants, solid catalyst):

    $$2SO_{2(g)} + O_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{Pt(s)} 2SO_{3(g)}$$

    * Formation of ammonia using finely divided iron in Haber's process (gas reactants, solid catalyst):

    $$N_{2(g)} + 3H_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{Fe(s)} 2NH_{3(g)}$$

    * Oxidation of ammonia to nitric oxide using platinum gauze in Ostwald's process (gas reactants, solid catalyst):

    $$4NH_{3(g)} + 5O_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{Pt(s)} 4NO_{(g)} + 6H_2O_{(g)}$$

    * Hydrogenation of vegetable oils using finely divided nickel (one reactant liquid, other gas, catalyst solid):

    $$Vegetable\ oil_{(l)} + H_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{Ni(s)} Vegetable\ ghee_{(s)}$$

## Adsorption Theory of Heterogeneous Catalysis

* **Old adsorption theory:** Reactants (gases or solutions) are adsorbed on the surface of the solid catalyst. The increase in concentration of reactants at the surface increases the rate. Also, the heat released during adsorption (exothermic) helps increase the rate.
* **Modern adsorption theory** combines the old adsorption theory with the intermediate compound formation theory. The catalytic activity is localised on the surface of the catalyst.
* The surface of the catalyst, unlike its bulk, has **free valencies**. These provide the seat for chemical forces of attraction. Gas molecules coming in contact with the surface are held there by loose chemical combination.
* When different molecules are adsorbed side by side, they react to form new molecules, which then leave the surface for fresh reactant molecules.

### Steps of the mechanism

* **Step 1:** Diffusion of reactants to the surface of the catalyst.
* **Step 2:** Adsorption of reactant molecules on the surface of the catalyst.
* **Step 3:** Chemical reaction on the catalyst surface through formation of an intermediate.
* **Step 4:** Desorption of products from the catalyst surface, making the surface available again.
* **Step 5:** Diffusion of products away from the catalyst surface.

!!! tip ""

    $$Catalyst\ surface + A + B &rarr; A\ and\ B\ adsorbed &rarr; Intermediate &rarr; Catalyst + A-B$$

* This theory explains why the catalyst remains unchanged in mass and chemical composition at the end of the reaction and why it is effective even in small quantities.
* However, it does not explain the action of catalytic promoters and catalytic poisons.

## Important Features of Solid Catalysts

### 1. Activity

* The activity of a catalyst depends largely on the strength of chemisorption.
* Reactants must be adsorbed reasonably strongly for the catalyst to be active, but not so strongly that they become immobilised and leave no space for other reactants.
* For hydrogenation reactions, catalytic activity increases from Group 5 to Group 11 metals, with maximum activity shown by Group 7–9 elements.

!!! tip ""

    $$2H_{2(g)} + O_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{Pt} 2H_2O_{(l)}$$

### 2. Selectivity

The selectivity of a catalyst is its ability to direct a reaction to yield a particular product. Starting with the same reactants, H<sub>2</sub> and CO, different catalysts give different products:

!!! tip ""

    $$CO_{(g)} + 3H_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{Ni} CH_{4(g)} + H_2O_{(g)}$$

    $$CO_{(g)} + 2H_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{Cu/ZnO-Cr_2O_3} CH_3OH_{(g)}$$

    $$CO_{(g)} + H_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{Cu} HCHO_{(g)}$$

* So a catalyst is highly selective: a substance which catalyses one reaction may fail to catalyse another.

## Shape-Selective Catalysis by Zeolites

The catalytic reaction that depends upon the pore structure of the catalyst and the size of the reactant and product molecules is called shape-selective catalysis.

* Zeolites are good shape-selective catalysts because of their honeycomb-like structures.
* They are microporous aluminosilicates with a three-dimensional network of silicates in which some silicon atoms are replaced by aluminium atoms, giving an Al–O–Si framework.
* Reactions in zeolites depend on the size and shape of reactant and product molecules, and on the pores and cavities of the zeolite.
* Zeolites occur in nature and are also synthesised for catalytic selectivity.
* They are widely used in petrochemical industries for cracking of hydrocarbons and isomerisation.
* **ZSM-5** is an important zeolite catalyst in the petroleum industry. It converts alcohols directly into gasoline (petrol) by dehydrating them to give a mixture of hydrocarbons.

## Questions

!!! question "What role does adsorption play in heterogeneous catalysis?"

    In heterogeneous catalysis, the reactant molecules are first adsorbed on the surface of the solid catalyst through free valencies. This increases the concentration of reactants at the surface, weakens their bonds, and brings the molecules close together, so they react through an intermediate. The heat of adsorption also helps increase the rate. The products are then desorbed, leaving the surface free for fresh reactants.

!!! question "Why is it necessary to remove CO when ammonia is obtained by Haber's process?"

    CO acts as a poison for the iron catalyst used in Haber's process. It gets strongly adsorbed on the catalyst surface and blocks the active sites, so the activity of the catalyst decreases. Hence, CO must be removed.

!!! question "Why is the ester hydrolysis slow in the beginning and becomes faster after sometime?"

    $$CH_3COOC_2H_5 + H_2O &rarr; CH_3COOH + C_2H_5OH$$

    The acid (CH<sub>3</sub>COOH) formed in the reaction furnishes H<sup>+</sup> ions, which catalyse the hydrolysis. In the beginning, very little acid is present, so the reaction is slow. As more acid is formed, the reaction becomes faster. Such a reaction, where a product acts as a catalyst, is called autocatalysis.

!!! question "What is the role of desorption in the process of catalysis?"

    After the reaction on the catalyst surface, the products must leave the surface (desorption). This makes the surface free again for fresh reactant molecules to be adsorbed, so the catalyst can continue its action.

!!! question "What do you mean by activity and selectivity of catalysts?"

    * **Activity:** It is the ability of a catalyst to increase the rate of a reaction. It depends on the strength of chemisorption; reactants must be adsorbed reasonably strongly but not so strongly that they get immobilised. Example: Pt catalyses 2H<sub>2</sub> + O<sub>2</sub> &rarr; 2H<sub>2</sub>O.

    * **Selectivity:** It is the ability of a catalyst to direct a reaction to yield a particular product. Example: CO and H<sub>2</sub> give CH<sub>4</sub> with Ni, CH<sub>3</sub>OH with Cu/ZnO-Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> and HCHO with Cu.

!!! question "What is shape selective catalysis? Describe some features of catalysis by zeolites."

    A catalytic reaction that depends on the pore structure of the catalyst and the size of the reactant and product molecules is called shape-selective catalysis.

    Features of zeolites:

    * They are microporous aluminosilicates with an Al–O–Si framework and a honeycomb-like structure.
    * Reactions depend on the size and shape of reactant and product molecules and on the pores and cavities of the zeolite.
    * They are used for cracking of hydrocarbons and isomerisation in petrochemical industries.
    * ZSM-5 converts alcohols directly into gasoline by dehydration.
