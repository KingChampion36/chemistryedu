# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Surface Chemistry | Preparation and Purification of Colloids

In this article, we will discuss the important methods for preparing colloidal solutions and the methods used to purify them.

## Preparation of Colloids

### 1. Chemical methods

* Colloidal solutions can be prepared by chemical reactions such as double decomposition, oxidation, reduction or hydrolysis.
* These reactions form molecules which then aggregate to form sols.

!!! tip ""

    $$Double\ decomposition:\ As_2O_3 + 3H_2S &rarr; As_2S_3\ (sol) + 3H_2O$$

    $$Oxidation:\ SO_2 + 2H_2S &rarr; 3S\ (sol) + 2H_2O$$

    $$Reduction:\ 2AuCl_3 + 3HCHO + 3H_2O &rarr; 2Au\ (sol) + 3HCOOH + 6HCl$$

    $$Hydrolysis:\ FeCl_3 + 3H_2O &rarr; Fe(OH)_3\ (sol) + 3HCl$$

### 2. Electrical disintegration or Bredig's arc method

* This method involves both dispersion and condensation.
* It is used to prepare colloidal sols of metals such as gold, silver, platinum, etc.
* An electric arc is struck between two electrodes of the metal immersed in the dispersion medium, which is kept cold in an ice-bath.
* The intense heat of the arc vaporises the metal, and the vapours condense in the cold medium to form particles of colloidal size.

### 3. Peptization

Peptization may be defined as the process of converting a precipitate into colloidal sol by shaking it with dispersion medium in the presence of a small amount of electrolyte. The electrolyte used for this purpose is called a **peptizing agent**.

* It is generally used to convert a freshly prepared precipitate into a colloidal sol.
* During peptization, the precipitate adsorbs one of the ions of the electrolyte on its surface. This develops a positive or negative charge on the precipitate, and the like charges make it break up into smaller particles of colloidal size.

!!! example "Example"

    A freshly prepared precipitate of ferric hydroxide is converted into a sol by shaking it with a small amount of FeCl<sub>3</sub> solution. The precipitate adsorbs Fe<sup>3+</sup> ions and breaks up into positively charged colloidal particles.

## Purification of Colloidal Solutions

The process used for reducing the amount of impurities to a requisite minimum is known as purification of colloidal solution.

* Freshly prepared colloidal solutions usually contain excess electrolytes and other soluble impurities.
* Traces of electrolyte are essential for the stability of the colloid, but larger quantities coagulate it. So the impurities must be reduced to a minimum.

### 1. Dialysis

It is a process of removing a dissolved substance from a colloidal solution by means of diffusion through a suitable membrane.

* Ions and small molecules of a true solution can pass through animal membrane (bladder), parchment paper or cellophane sheet, but colloidal particles cannot.
* The apparatus used is called a **dialyser**.
* A bag of the membrane containing the colloidal solution is suspended in a vessel through which fresh water flows continuously.
* The molecules and ions (crystalloids) diffuse through the membrane into the outer water, and the pure colloidal solution is left behind in the bag.

### 2. Electro-dialysis

* Ordinary dialysis is quite slow. It can be made faster by applying an electric field, if the dissolved impurity is only an electrolyte. This is called electrodialysis.
* The colloidal solution is placed in a bag of membrane and pure water is taken outside. Electrodes are fitted in the outer compartment.
* The ions in the colloidal solution migrate out towards the oppositely charged electrodes, while the colloidal particles stay in the bag.

### 3. Ultrafiltration

Ultrafiltration is the process of separating the colloidal particles from the solvent and soluble solutes present in the colloidal solution by specially prepared filters, which are permeable to all substances except the colloidal particles.

* Colloidal particles pass through ordinary filter paper because its pores are too large.
* The pores can be made smaller by impregnating the filter paper with **colloidion** solution. The usual colloidion is a 4% solution of nitrocellulose in a mixture of alcohol and ether.
* An ultra-filter paper is made by soaking filter paper in colloidion solution, hardening it with formaldehyde and then drying it.
* Ultrafiltration is slow. To speed it up, pressure or suction is applied.
* The colloidal particles left on the ultra-filter paper are stirred with fresh dispersion medium to get a pure colloidal solution.

Method          | Principle                                         | Speed     |
----------------|---------------------------------------------------|-----------|
Dialysis        | Diffusion of ions and small molecules through a membrane | Slow |
Electro-dialysis | Migration of ions through a membrane under an electric field | Faster (only for electrolyte impurities) |
Ultrafiltration | Filtration through filter paper with very fine pores | Slow; speeded up by pressure or suction |

## Questions

!!! question "Explain the term dialysis."

    Dialysis is the process of removing a dissolved substance (ions or small molecules) from a colloidal solution by diffusion through a suitable membrane such as parchment paper, animal bladder or cellophane. The colloidal solution is kept in a bag of the membrane, which is placed in flowing water. The crystalloids diffuse out into the water while colloidal particles, being too big, remain inside. Thus, a pure colloidal solution is obtained.

!!! question "What is peptization? Explain with an example."

    Peptization is the conversion of a freshly prepared precipitate into a colloidal sol by shaking it with the dispersion medium in presence of a small amount of electrolyte (peptizing agent). The precipitate adsorbs ions of the electrolyte, gets charged, and breaks into colloidal particles. For example, freshly precipitated Fe(OH)<sub>3</sub> forms a positively charged sol on shaking with a little FeCl<sub>3</sub> solution, because Fe<sup>3+</sup> ions are adsorbed.

!!! question "Give the chemical reactions for preparing (i) arsenious sulphide sol (ii) sulphur sol (iii) gold sol (iv) ferric hydroxide sol."

    $$(i)\ As_2O_3 + 3H_2S &rarr; As_2S_3\ (sol) + 3H_2O$$

    $$(ii)\ SO_2 + 2H_2S &rarr; 3S\ (sol) + 2H_2O$$

    $$(iii)\ 2AuCl_3 + 3HCHO + 3H_2O &rarr; 2Au\ (sol) + 3HCOOH + 6HCl$$

    $$(iv)\ FeCl_3 + 3H_2O &rarr; Fe(OH)_3\ (sol) + 3HCl$$

!!! question "Why is it essential to wash the precipitate with water before estimating it quantitatively?"

    Some amount of the electrolytes mixed during the formation of the precipitate gets adsorbed on its surface. These adsorbed impurities would add to the mass of the precipitate and give a wrong result. Also, they may cause peptization of the precipitate. So the precipitate is washed with water to remove the adsorbed electrolytes before estimation.
