## Questions

!!! question "From the rate expression for the following reactions, determine their order of reaction and the dimensions of the rate constants. <br>(i) 3NO<sub>(g)</sub> &rarr; N<sub>2</sub>O<sub>(g)</sub>; Rate = k[NO]<sup>2</sup> <br>(ii) H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2(aq)</sub> + 3I<sup>-</sup><sub>(aq)</sub> + 2H<sup>+</sup> &rarr; 2H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>(l)</sub> + I<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>; Rate = k[H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub>][I<sup>-</sup>] <br>(iii) CH<sub>3</sub>CHO<sub>(g)</sub> &rarr; CH<sub>4(g)</sub> + CO<sub>(g)</sub>; Rate = k[CH<sub>3</sub>CHO]<sup>3/2</sup> <br>(iv) C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>Cl<sub>(g)</sub> &rarr; C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4(g)</sub> + HCl<sub>(g)</sub>; Rate = k[C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>Cl]"

    * Units of k for order n:

    $$Units\ of\ k = (mol\ L^{-1})^{1-n}\ s^{-1}$$

    Reaction | Order | Units of k
    ---------|-------|-----------
    (i)   | 2   | mol<sup>-1</sup>L s<sup>-1</sup>
    (ii)  | 1 + 1 = 2 | mol<sup>-1</sup>L s<sup>-1</sup>
    (iii) | 3/2 | mol<sup>-1/2</sup>L<sup>1/2</sup>s<sup>-1</sup>
    (iv)  | 1   | s<sup>-1</sup>

!!! question "For the reaction: 2A + B &rarr; A<sub>2</sub>B, the rate = k[A][B]<sup>2</sup> with k = 2.0 &times; 10<sup>-6</sup> mol<sup>-2</sup>L<sup>2</sup>s<sup>-1</sup>. Calculate the initial rate of the reaction when [A] = 0.1 mol L<sup>-1</sup>, [B] = 0.2 mol L<sup>-1</sup>. Calculate the rate of reaction after [A] is reduced to 0.06 mol L<sup>-1</sup>."

    * Initial rate:

    $$Rate = k[A][B]^2 = 2.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 0.1 \times (0.2)^2$$

    $$Rate = 8.0 \times 10^{-9}\ mol\ L^{-1}s^{-1}$$

    * When [A] falls to 0.06 mol L<sup>-1</sup>, A consumed = 0.1 - 0.06 = 0.04 mol L<sup>-1</sup>.
    * From the equation, 2 mol A reacts with 1 mol B. So B consumed = 0.04/2 = 0.02 mol L<sup>-1</sup>.

    $$[B] = 0.2 - 0.02 = 0.18\ mol\ L^{-1}$$

    $$Rate = 2.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 0.06 \times (0.18)^2$$

    $$Rate = 3.89 \times 10^{-9}\ mol\ L^{-1}s^{-1}$$

!!! question "The decomposition of NH<sub>3</sub> on platinum surface is zero order reaction. What are the rates of production of N<sub>2</sub> and H<sub>2</sub> if k = 2.5 &times; 10<sup>-4</sup> mol L<sup>-1</sup>s<sup>-1</sup>?"

    $$2NH_3 &rarr; N_2 + 3H_2$$

    $$Rate = -{1 \over 2}{d[NH_3] \over dt} = {d[N_2] \over dt} = {1 \over 3}{d[H_2] \over dt}$$

    * For a zero order reaction, rate = k = 2.5 &times; 10<sup>-4</sup> mol L<sup>-1</sup>s<sup>-1</sup>.

    $$Rate\ of\ production\ of\ N_2 = {d[N_2] \over dt} = 2.5 \times 10^{-4}\ mol\ L^{-1}s^{-1}$$

    $$Rate\ of\ production\ of\ H_2 = {d[H_2] \over dt} = 3 \times 2.5 \times 10^{-4} = 7.5 \times 10^{-4}\ mol\ L^{-1}s^{-1}$$

!!! question "The decomposition of dimethyl ether leads to the formation of CH<sub>4</sub>, H<sub>2</sub> and CO and the reaction rate is given by Rate = k[CH<sub>3</sub>OCH<sub>3</sub>]<sup>3/2</sup>. The rate of reaction is followed by increase in pressure in a closed vessel, so the rate can also be expressed in terms of the partial pressure of dimethyl ether, i.e., Rate = k(p<sub>CH<sub>3</sub>OCH<sub>3</sub></sub>)<sup>3/2</sup>. If the pressure is measured in bar and time in minutes, then what are the units of rate and rate constants?"

    * Rate is the change in pressure per unit time:

    $$Units\ of\ rate = bar\ min^{-1}$$

    $$k = {Rate \over (p_{CH_3OCH_3})^{3/2}} = {bar\ min^{-1} \over bar^{3/2}}$$

    $$Units\ of\ k = bar^{-1/2}\ min^{-1}$$

!!! question "Mention the factors that affect the rate of a chemical reaction."

    * **Concentration of reactants:** Rate generally increases with increase in concentration (pressure for gases).
    * **Temperature:** Rate increases with temperature. A 10&deg; rise nearly doubles the rate constant.
    * **Catalyst:** A catalyst lowers the activation energy and increases the rate.
    * **Nature of reactants and surface area:** Ionic reactions are faster than covalent ones, and finely divided solids react faster.
    * **Radiation:** Some reactions, such as photochemical reactions, speed up in the presence of light.

!!! question "A reaction is second order with respect to a reactant. How is the rate of reaction affected if the concentration of the reactant is (i) doubled (ii) reduced to half?"

    $$Rate = k[A]^2$$

    * (i) When [A] is doubled:

    $$Rate' = k(2[A])^2 = 4k[A]^2 = 4 \times Rate$$

    The rate becomes 4 times.

    * (ii) When [A] is halved:

    $$Rate' = k\left({[A] \over 2}\right)^2 = {1 \over 4}k[A]^2$$

    The rate becomes one-fourth.

!!! question "What is the effect of temperature on the rate constant of a reaction? How can this temperature effect on rate constant be represented quantitatively?"

    * The rate constant increases with temperature. For a 10&deg; rise in temperature, the rate constant nearly doubles.
    * This effect is represented quantitatively by the Arrhenius equation:

    $$k = A\ e^{-E_a/RT}$$

    Where A is the Arrhenius (frequency) factor, E<sub>a</sub> is the activation energy, R is the gas constant and T is the absolute temperature.

!!! question "In a pseudo first order hydrolysis of ester in water, the following results were obtained: <br>t/s: 0, 30, 60, 90 <br>[Ester]/mol L<sup>-1</sup>: 0.55, 0.31, 0.17, 0.085 <br>(i) Calculate the average rate of reaction between the time interval 30 to 60 seconds. <br>(ii) Calculate the pseudo first order rate constant for the hydrolysis of ester."

    * (i) Average rate between 30 s and 60 s:

    $$r_{av} = -{&Delta;[Ester] \over &Delta;t} = -{(0.17 - 0.31) \over (60 - 30)}$$

    $$r_{av} = 4.67 \times 10^{-3}\ mol\ L^{-1}s^{-1}$$

    * (ii) Using the first order equation:

    $$k = {2.303 \over t} log{[R]_0 \over [R]}$$

    $$At\ t = 30\ s:\ k = {2.303 \over 30} log{0.55 \over 0.31} = 1.911 \times 10^{-2}\ s^{-1}$$

    $$At\ t = 60\ s:\ k = {2.303 \over 60} log{0.55 \over 0.17} = 1.957 \times 10^{-2}\ s^{-1}$$

    $$At\ t = 90\ s:\ k = {2.303 \over 90} log{0.55 \over 0.085} = 2.075 \times 10^{-2}\ s^{-1}$$

    $$Average\ k = {(1.911 + 1.957 + 2.075) \times 10^{-2} \over 3} = 1.98 \times 10^{-2}\ s^{-1}$$

!!! question "A reaction is first order in A and second order in B. <br>(i) Write the differential rate equation. <br>(ii) How is the rate affected on increasing the concentration of B three times? <br>(iii) How is the rate affected when the concentrations of both A and B are doubled?"

    * (i) Differential rate equation:

    $$-{d[R] \over dt} = k[A][B]^2$$

    * (ii) When [B] is made three times:

    $$Rate' = k[A](3[B])^2 = 9k[A][B]^2$$

    The rate increases 9 times.

    * (iii) When both [A] and [B] are doubled:

    $$Rate' = k(2[A])(2[B])^2 = 8k[A][B]^2$$

    The rate increases 8 times.

!!! question "In a reaction between A and B, the initial rate of reaction (r<sub>0</sub>) was measured for different initial concentrations of A and B as given below: <br>[A]/mol L<sup>-1</sup>: 0.20, 0.20, 0.40 <br>[B]/mol L<sup>-1</sup>: 0.30, 0.10, 0.05 <br>r<sub>0</sub>/mol L<sup>-1</sup>s<sup>-1</sup>: 5.07 &times; 10<sup>-5</sup>, 5.07 &times; 10<sup>-5</sup>, 1.43 &times; 10<sup>-4</sup> <br>What is the order of the reaction with respect to A and B?"

    * Let rate = k[A]<sup>x</sup>[B]<sup>y</sup>.

    $$(1):\ 5.07 \times 10^{-5} = k(0.20)^x(0.30)^y$$

    $$(2):\ 5.07 \times 10^{-5} = k(0.20)^x(0.10)^y$$

    $$(3):\ 1.43 \times 10^{-4} = k(0.40)^x(0.05)^y$$

    * Dividing (1) by (2):

    $$1 = \left({0.30 \over 0.10}\right)^y = 3^y$$

    $$y = 0$$

    * Dividing (3) by (2), with y = 0:

    $${1.43 \times 10^{-4} \over 5.07 \times 10^{-5}} = \left({0.40 \over 0.20}\right)^x$$

    $$2.82 = 2^x$$

    $$x = {log\ 2.82 \over log\ 2} = {0.4503 \over 0.3010} = 1.5$$

    * Hence, the order with respect to A is 1.5 and with respect to B is zero.

!!! question "The following results have been obtained during the kinetic studies of the reaction: 2A + B &rarr; C + D <br>Experiment I: [A] = 0.1, [B] = 0.1, initial rate of formation of D = 6.0 &times; 10<sup>-3</sup> mol L<sup>-1</sup>min<sup>-1</sup> <br>Experiment II: [A] = 0.3, [B] = 0.2, rate = 7.2 &times; 10<sup>-2</sup> <br>Experiment III: [A] = 0.3, [B] = 0.4, rate = 2.88 &times; 10<sup>-1</sup> <br>Experiment IV: [A] = 0.4, [B] = 0.1, rate = 2.40 &times; 10<sup>-2</sup> <br>Determine the rate law and the rate constant for the reaction."

    * Let rate = k[A]<sup>x</sup>[B]<sup>y</sup>.
    * Experiments I and IV ([B] constant):

    $${2.40 \times 10^{-2} \over 6.0 \times 10^{-3}} = \left({0.4 \over 0.1}\right)^x$$

    $$4 = 4^x,\ \ x = 1$$

    * Experiments II and III ([A] constant):

    $${2.88 \times 10^{-1} \over 7.2 \times 10^{-2}} = \left({0.4 \over 0.2}\right)^y$$

    $$4 = 2^y,\ \ y = 2$$

    $$Rate = k[A][B]^2$$

    * Rate constant from Experiment I:

    $$k = {Rate \over [A][B]^2} = {6.0 \times 10^{-3} \over 0.1 \times (0.1)^2}$$

    $$k = 6.0\ mol^{-2}L^2min^{-1}$$

!!! question "The reaction between A and B is first order with respect to A and zero order with respect to B. Fill in the blanks in the following table: <br>Experiment I: [A] = 0.1, [B] = 0.1, initial rate = 2.0 &times; 10<sup>-2</sup> mol L<sup>-1</sup>min<sup>-1</sup> <br>Experiment II: [A] = ?, [B] = 0.2, rate = 4.0 &times; 10<sup>-2</sup> <br>Experiment III: [A] = 0.4, [B] = 0.4, rate = ? <br>Experiment IV: [A] = ?, [B] = 0.2, rate = 2.0 &times; 10<sup>-2</sup>"

    $$Rate = k[A]^1[B]^0 = k[A]$$

    * From Experiment I:

    $$k = {2.0 \times 10^{-2} \over 0.1} = 0.2\ min^{-1}$$

    $$Experiment\ II:\ [A] = {4.0 \times 10^{-2} \over 0.2} = 0.2\ mol\ L^{-1}$$

    $$Experiment\ III:\ Rate = 0.2 \times 0.4 = 8.0 \times 10^{-2}\ mol\ L^{-1}min^{-1}$$

    $$Experiment\ IV:\ [A] = {2.0 \times 10^{-2} \over 0.2} = 0.1\ mol\ L^{-1}$$
