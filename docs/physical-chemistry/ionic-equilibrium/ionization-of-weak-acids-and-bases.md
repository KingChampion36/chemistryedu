# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Ionic Equilibrium | Ionization of Weak Acids and Bases

The equilibrium constant for the ionization of a weak acid is called its ionization constant, K<sub>a</sub>, and for a weak base is called the base ionization constant, K<sub>b</sub>. These constants tell us how strong a weak acid or base is.

## Ionization Constant of Weak Acids (K<sub>a</sub>)

* Consider a weak acid HX that is partially ionized in water. Let c be its initial concentration and &alpha; the degree of ionization (the fraction of HX ionized).

!!! tip ""

    $$HX_{(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; H_3O^+_{(aq)} + X^-_{(aq)}$$

    $$Initial\ concentration\ (M):\ \ c\ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ \ \ 0$$

    $$Change\ (M):\ \ -c&alpha;\ \ \ \ \ \ +c&alpha;\ \ \ \ \ \ +c&alpha;$$

    $$Equilibrium\ concentration\ (M):\ \ c - c&alpha;\ \ \ \ \ \ c&alpha;\ \ \ \ \ \ c&alpha;$$

* The equilibrium constant for this acid dissociation is:

!!! tip ""

    $$K_a = {[H^+][X^-] \over [HX]}$$

    $$K_a = {c^2&alpha;^2 \over c(1-&alpha;)} = {c&alpha;^2 \over 1-&alpha;}$$

* K<sub>a</sub> is called the dissociation or ionization constant of the acid HX.
* At a given temperature, K<sub>a</sub> is a measure of the strength of the acid. The larger the value of K<sub>a</sub>, the stronger the acid.
* K<sub>a</sub> is a dimensionless quantity, since the standard state concentration of all species is taken as 1 M.
* The pH idea is extended to K<sub>a</sub> as well:

!!! tip ""

    $$pK_a = -log\ K_a$$

* A larger K<sub>a</sub> means a smaller pK<sub>a</sub>. So, the smaller the pK<sub>a</sub>, the stronger the acid.

### Ostwald's dilution law (approximation)

* For weak acids, &alpha; is usually very small, so 1 - &alpha; &asymp; 1. Then:

!!! tip ""

    $$K_a = c&alpha;^2\ \ \ \ or\ \ \ \ &alpha; = \sqrt{K_a \over c}$$

    $$[H^+] = c&alpha; = \sqrt{K_a\ c}$$

    $$pH = {1 \over 2}(pK_a - log\ c)$$

* So the degree of ionization of a weak electrolyte increases on dilution (as c decreases).
* This approximation works well when &alpha; is less than about 5%. If &alpha; comes out larger, solve the full quadratic equation.

### Step-wise approach to find the pH of a weak electrolyte

Knowing K<sub>a</sub> and the initial concentration c, we can find the concentration of all species, the degree of ionization and the pH:

* **Step 1:** Identify the species present before dissociation as Brönsted-Lowry acids/bases.
* **Step 2:** Write balanced equations for all possible reactions, including species acting both as acid and base (such as water).
* **Step 3:** The reaction with the higher K<sub>a</sub> is the primary reaction; the other is a subsidiary reaction.
* **Step 4:** For the primary reaction, tabulate (a) initial concentration, (b) change on reaching equilibrium in terms of &alpha;, and (c) equilibrium concentration.
* **Step 5:** Substitute the equilibrium concentrations in the K<sub>a</sub> expression and solve for &alpha;.
* **Step 6:** Calculate the concentration of each species.
* **Step 7:** Calculate pH = -log[H<sub>3</sub>O<sup>+</sup>].

### Percent dissociation

* Another useful measure of the strength of a weak acid is percent dissociation:

!!! tip ""

    $$Percent\ dissociation = {[HA]_{dissociated} \over [HA]_{initial}} \times 100\%$$

## Ionization Constant of Weak Bases (K<sub>b</sub>)

* A base MOH ionizes as:

!!! tip ""

    $$MOH_{(aq)} &#8652; M^+_{(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)}$$

    $$K_b = {[M^+][OH^-] \over [MOH]}$$

* A weak base ionizes only partially, just like a weak acid. K<sub>b</sub> is called the base ionization constant.
* If c is the initial concentration and &alpha; the degree of ionization:

!!! tip ""

    $$K_b = {(c&alpha;)^2 \over c(1-&alpha;)} = {c&alpha;^2 \over 1-&alpha;}$$

    $$If\ &alpha; \ll 1:\ \ &alpha; = \sqrt{K_b \over c},\ \ [OH^-] = \sqrt{K_b\ c}$$

    $$pK_b = -log\ K_b$$

* Many organic compounds like amines (derivatives of ammonia in which H atoms are replaced by other groups) are weak bases. Methylamine, codeine, quinine and nicotine are very weak bases due to their very small K<sub>b</sub>.

!!! tip ""

    $$NH_{3(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; NH^+_{4(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)}$$

## Relation between K<sub>a</sub> and K<sub>b</sub>

* For a conjugate acid-base pair, K<sub>a</sub> and K<sub>b</sub> are related, so if one is known the other can be found. Consider NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> and NH<sub>3</sub>:

!!! tip ""

    $$NH^+_{4(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; H_3O^+_{(aq)} + NH_{3(aq)},\ \ K_a = {[H_3O^+][NH_3] \over [NH_4^+]} = 5.6 \times 10^{-10}$$

    $$NH_{3(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; NH^+_{4(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)},\ \ K_b = {[NH_4^+][OH^-] \over [NH_3]} = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$$

    $$Net:\ 2H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; H_3O^+_{(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)},\ \ K_w = [H_3O^+][OH^-] = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}$$

* The net reaction is the sum of the two reactions, so its equilibrium constant is the product of the two constants:

!!! tip ""

    $$K_a \times K_b = {[H_3O^+][NH_3] \over [NH_4^+]} \times {[NH_4^+][OH^-] \over [NH_3]} = [H_3O^+][OH^-] = K_w$$

    $$(5.6 \times 10^{-10}) \times (1.8 \times 10^{-5}) = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}$$

!!! tip "Conclusion"

    * The equilibrium constant for a net reaction obtained by adding two (or more) reactions equals the product of the equilibrium constants of the individual reactions: K<sub>NET</sub> = K<sub>1</sub> &times; K<sub>2</sub> &times; ...

    * For a conjugate acid-base pair:

    $$K_a \times K_b = K_w$$

    $$pK_a + pK_b = pK_w = 14\ (at\ 298\ K)$$

    * A strong acid has a weak conjugate base and vice-versa.

### Alternative derivation

* Consider a general base B:

!!! tip ""

    $$B_{(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; BH^+_{(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)},\ \ K_b = {[BH^+][OH^-] \over [B]}$$

    Multiplying and dividing by [H<sup>+</sup>]:

    $$K_b = {[BH^+][OH^-][H^+] \over [B][H^+]} = [OH^-][H^+] \times {[BH^+] \over [B][H^+]} = {K_w \over K_a}$$

    $$&Therefore;\ K_a \times K_b = K_w$$

## Questions

!!! question "The ionization constant of HF is 3.2 &times; 10<sup>-4</sup>. Calculate the degree of dissociation of HF in its 0.02 M solution. Calculate the concentration of all species present (H<sub>3</sub>O<sup>+</sup>, F<sup>-</sup> and HF) in the solution and its pH."

    Possible proton transfer reactions:

    $$(1)\ HF + H_2O &#8652; H_3O^+ + F^-,\ \ K_a = 3.2 \times 10^{-4}$$

    $$(2)\ H_2O + H_2O &#8652; H_3O^+ + OH^-,\ \ K_w = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}$$

    As K<sub>a</sub> >> K<sub>w</sub>, reaction (1) is the principal reaction.

    $$Initial\ (M):\ HF = 0.02,\ H_3O^+ = 0,\ F^- = 0$$

    $$Equilibrium\ (M):\ HF = 0.02 - 0.02&alpha;,\ H_3O^+ = 0.02&alpha;,\ F^- = 0.02&alpha;$$

    $$K_a = {(0.02&alpha;)^2 \over 0.02(1-&alpha;)} = {0.02&alpha;^2 \over 1-&alpha;} = 3.2 \times 10^{-4}$$

    $$&alpha;^2 + 1.6 \times 10^{-2}&alpha; - 1.6 \times 10^{-2} = 0$$

    Solving, &alpha; = +0.12 or -0.12. The negative root is not acceptable, so &alpha; = 0.12.

    $$[H_3O^+] = [F^-] = c&alpha; = 0.02 \times 0.12 = 2.4 \times 10^{-3}\ M$$

    $$[HF] = c(1-&alpha;) = 0.02(1 - 0.12) = 17.6 \times 10^{-3}\ M$$

    $$pH = -log(2.4 \times 10^{-3}) = 2.62$$

!!! question "The pH of 0.1 M monobasic acid is 4.50. Calculate the concentration of species H<sup>+</sup>, A<sup>-</sup> and HA at equilibrium. Also, determine the value of K<sub>a</sub> and pK<sub>a</sub> of the monobasic acid."

    $$[H^+] = 10^{-pH} = 10^{-4.50} = 3.16 \times 10^{-5}\ M$$

    $$[H^+] = [A^-] = 3.16 \times 10^{-5}\ M$$

    $$[HA]_{eqlbm} = 0.1 - (3.16 \times 10^{-5}) &asymp; 0.1\ M$$

    $$K_a = {[H^+][A^-] \over [HA]} = {(3.16 \times 10^{-5})^2 \over 0.1} = 1.0 \times 10^{-8}$$

    $$pK_a = -log(10^{-8}) = 8$$

!!! question "Calculate the pH of 0.08 M solution of hypochlorous acid, HOCl. The ionization constant of the acid is 2.5 &times; 10<sup>-5</sup>. Determine the percent dissociation of HOCl."

    $$HOCl_{(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; H_3O^+_{(aq)} + ClO^-_{(aq)}$$

    $$Equilibrium\ (M):\ HOCl = 0.08 - x,\ H_3O^+ = x,\ ClO^- = x$$

    $$K_a = {x^2 \over 0.08 - x} = 2.5 \times 10^{-5}$$

    As x << 0.08, 0.08 - x &asymp; 0.08:

    $$x^2 = 0.08 \times 2.5 \times 10^{-5} = 2.0 \times 10^{-6}$$

    $$x = [H^+] = 1.41 \times 10^{-3}\ M$$

    $$Percent\ dissociation = {1.41 \times 10^{-3} \over 0.08} \times 100 = 1.76\%$$

    $$pH = -log(1.41 \times 10^{-3}) = 2.85$$

!!! question "The pH of 0.004 M hydrazine solution is 9.7. Calculate its ionization constant K<sub>b</sub> and pK<sub>b</sub>."

    $$NH_2NH_2 + H_2O &#8652; NH_2NH_3^+ + OH^-$$

    From pH, find [H<sup>+</sup>], then [OH<sup>-</sup>] using K<sub>w</sub>:

    $$[H^+] = 10^{-9.7} = 2.0 \times 10^{-10}\ M$$

    $$[OH^-] = {K_w \over [H^+]} = {1 \times 10^{-14} \over 2.0 \times 10^{-10}} = 5.0 \times 10^{-5}\ M$$

    [NH<sub>2</sub>NH<sub>3</sub><sup>+</sup>] = [OH<sup>-</sup>]. Both are very small, so the concentration of undissociated base &asymp; 0.004 M.

    $$K_b = {[NH_2NH_3^+][OH^-] \over [NH_2NH_2]} = {(5.0 \times 10^{-5})^2 \over 0.004} = 6.3 \times 10^{-7}$$

    $$pK_b = -log(6.3 \times 10^{-7}) = 6.20$$

!!! question "Determine the degree of ionization and pH of a 0.05 M ammonia solution (K<sub>b</sub> = 1.77 &times; 10<sup>-5</sup>). Also, calculate the ionization constant of the conjugate acid of ammonia."

    $$NH_3 + H_2O &#8652; NH_4^+ + OH^-$$

    $$K_b = {0.05&alpha;^2 \over 1-&alpha;}$$

    &alpha; is small, so 1 - &alpha; &asymp; 1:

    $$&alpha; = \sqrt{1.77 \times 10^{-5} \over 0.05} = 0.0188$$

    $$[OH^-] = c&alpha; = 0.05 \times 0.0188 = 9.4 \times 10^{-4}\ M$$

    $$[H^+] = {10^{-14} \over 9.4 \times 10^{-4}} = 1.06 \times 10^{-11}\ M$$

    $$pH = -log(1.06 \times 10^{-11}) = 10.97$$

    For the conjugate acid NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>:

    $$K_a = {K_w \over K_b} = {10^{-14} \over 1.77 \times 10^{-5}} = 5.64 \times 10^{-10}$$

!!! question "The ionization constant of HF, HCOOH and HCN at 298 K are 6.8 &times; 10<sup>-4</sup>, 1.8 &times; 10<sup>-4</sup> and 4.8 &times; 10<sup>-9</sup> respectively. Calculate the ionization constants of the corresponding conjugate base."

    Using K<sub>b</sub> = K<sub>w</sub>/K<sub>a</sub>:

    $$F^-:\ K_b = {10^{-14} \over 6.8 \times 10^{-4}} = 1.47 \times 10^{-11}$$

    $$HCOO^-:\ K_b = {10^{-14} \over 1.8 \times 10^{-4}} = 5.56 \times 10^{-11}$$

    $$CN^-:\ K_b = {10^{-14} \over 4.8 \times 10^{-9}} = 2.08 \times 10^{-6}$$

!!! question "The ionization constant of acetic acid is 1.74 &times; 10<sup>-5</sup>. Calculate the degree of dissociation of acetic acid in its 0.05 M solution. Calculate the concentration of acetate ion in the solution and its pH."

    $$&alpha; = \sqrt{K_a \over c} = \sqrt{1.74 \times 10^{-5} \over 0.05} = 1.87 \times 10^{-2}$$

    $$[CH_3COO^-] = [H^+] = c&alpha; = 0.05 \times 1.87 \times 10^{-2} = 9.33 \times 10^{-4}\ M$$

    $$pH = -log(9.33 \times 10^{-4}) = 3.03$$

!!! question "It has been found that the pH of a 0.01 M solution of an organic acid is 4.15. Calculate the concentration of the anion, the ionization constant of the acid and its pK<sub>a</sub>."

    $$[H^+] = [A^-] = 10^{-4.15} = 7.08 \times 10^{-5}\ M$$

    $$K_a = {[H^+][A^-] \over [HA]} = {(7.08 \times 10^{-5})^2 \over 0.01 - 7.08 \times 10^{-5}} = 5.05 \times 10^{-7}$$

    $$pK_a = -log(5.05 \times 10^{-7}) = 6.30$$

!!! question "The degree of ionization of a 0.1 M bromoacetic acid solution is 0.132. Calculate the pH of the solution and the pK<sub>a</sub> of bromoacetic acid."

    $$[H^+] = c&alpha; = 0.1 \times 0.132 = 0.0132\ M$$

    $$pH = -log(0.0132) = 1.88$$

    Here &alpha; is not small, so use the full expression:

    $$K_a = {c&alpha;^2 \over 1-&alpha;} = {0.1 \times (0.132)^2 \over 1 - 0.132} = 2.0 \times 10^{-3}$$

    $$pK_a = -log(2.0 \times 10^{-3}) = 2.70$$

!!! question "The pH of 0.005 M codeine (C<sub>18</sub>H<sub>21</sub>NO<sub>3</sub>) solution is 9.95. Calculate its ionization constant and pK<sub>b</sub>."

    $$pOH = 14 - 9.95 = 4.05$$

    $$[OH^-] = 10^{-4.05} = 8.91 \times 10^{-5}\ M$$

    $$K_b = {[OH^-]^2 \over c} = {(8.91 \times 10^{-5})^2 \over 0.005} = 1.59 \times 10^{-6}$$

    $$pK_b = -log(1.59 \times 10^{-6}) = 5.80$$

!!! question "What is the pH of 0.001 M aniline solution? The ionization constant of aniline is 4.27 &times; 10<sup>-10</sup>. Calculate the degree of ionization of aniline in the solution. Also calculate the ionization constant of the conjugate acid of aniline."

    $$[OH^-] = \sqrt{K_b\ c} = \sqrt{4.27 \times 10^{-10} \times 0.001} = 6.53 \times 10^{-7}\ M$$

    $$&alpha; = {[OH^-] \over c} = {6.53 \times 10^{-7} \over 0.001} = 6.53 \times 10^{-4}$$

    $$pOH = 6.19,\ \ pH = 14 - 6.19 = 7.81$$

    For the conjugate acid (anilinium ion, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>3</sub><sup>+</sup>):

    $$K_a = {K_w \over K_b} = {10^{-14} \over 4.27 \times 10^{-10}} = 2.34 \times 10^{-5}$$

!!! question "The pH of 0.1 M solution of cyanic acid (HCNO) is 2.34. Calculate the ionization constant of the acid and its degree of ionization in the solution."

    $$[H^+] = 10^{-2.34} = 4.57 \times 10^{-3}\ M$$

    $$K_a = {[H^+]^2 \over c} = {(4.57 \times 10^{-3})^2 \over 0.1} = 2.09 \times 10^{-4}$$

    $$&alpha; = {[H^+] \over c} = {4.57 \times 10^{-3} \over 0.1} = 0.0457$$
