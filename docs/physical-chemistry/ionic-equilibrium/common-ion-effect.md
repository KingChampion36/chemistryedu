## Common Ion Effect

Common ion effect can be defined as the shift in equilibrium on adding a substance that provides more of an ionic species already present in the dissociation equilibrium. It is an application of Le Chatelier's principle.

* Consider the dissociation of acetic acid:

!!! tip ""

    $$CH_3COOH_{(aq)} &#8652; H^+_{(aq)} + CH_3COO^-_{(aq)}$$

    $$or\ \ HAc_{(aq)} &#8652; H^+_{(aq)} + Ac^-_{(aq)}$$

    $$K_a = {[H^+][Ac^-] \over [HAc]}$$

* If acetate ions are added (for example, as sodium acetate, CH<sub>3</sub>COONa), the concentration of Ac<sup>-</sup> increases. To keep K<sub>a</sub> constant, the equilibrium shifts backward, and [H<sup>+</sup>] decreases.
* Similarly, if H<sup>+</sup> ions are added from an external source (such as HCl), the equilibrium moves towards undissociated acetic acid, reducing the ionization of the acid.

!!! tip "Conclusion"

    * Addition of a common ion suppresses the ionization of a weak electrolyte.

    * The degree of ionization of a weak acid decreases in presence of a strong acid or its own salt. The degree of ionization of a weak base decreases in presence of a strong base or its own salt.

### pH of 0.05 M acetic acid containing 0.05 M acetate ion

!!! example "Example"

    $$HAc_{(aq)} &#8652; H^+_{(aq)} + Ac^-_{(aq)}$$

    $$Initial\ (M):\ \ 0.05\ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ 0.05$$

    Let x be the extent of ionization of acetic acid.

    $$Change\ (M):\ \ -x\ \ \ \ +x\ \ \ \ +x$$

    $$Equilibrium\ (M):\ \ 0.05 - x\ \ \ \ x\ \ \ \ 0.05 + x$$

    $$K_a = {[H^+][Ac^-] \over [HAc]} = {(0.05 + x)(x) \over 0.05 - x}$$

    As K<sub>a</sub> is small for a very weak acid, x << 0.05. Hence, (0.05 + x) &asymp; (0.05 - x) &asymp; 0.05.

    $$1.8 \times 10^{-5} = {x(0.05) \over 0.05}$$

    $$x = [H^+] = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}\ M$$

    $$pH = -log(1.8 \times 10^{-5}) = 4.74$$

* Without the added acetate, the same 0.05 M acetic acid has [H<sup>+</sup>] = &radic;(K<sub>a</sub>c) &asymp; 9.5 &times; 10<sup>-4</sup> M and pH &asymp; 3.0. The common ion has reduced [H<sup>+</sup>] about 50 times.

### General result

* For a weak acid HA (concentration c) in presence of a strong acid giving [H<sup>+</sup>] = C', or its salt giving [A<sup>-</sup>] = C':

!!! tip ""

    $$Weak\ acid + strong\ acid:\ \ &alpha; = {K_a \over C'}$$

    $$Weak\ acid + its\ salt:\ \ [H^+] = {K_a \times [HA] \over [A^-]}$$

* Similarly, for a weak base in presence of a strong base giving [OH<sup>-</sup>] = C':

!!! tip ""

    $$&alpha; = {K_b \over C'}$$

* A mixture of a weak acid and its salt (or a weak base and its salt) resists change in pH. Such mixtures are buffer solutions, discussed in a later page.

## Questions

!!! question "Calculate the pH of a 0.10 M ammonia solution. Calculate the pH after 50.0 mL of this solution is treated with 25.0 mL of 0.10 M HCl. The dissociation constant of ammonia, K<sub>b</sub> = 1.77 &times; 10<sup>-5</sup>."

    **Before neutralization**

    $$NH_3 + H_2O &#8652; NH_4^+ + OH^-$$

    $$K_b = {[NH_4^+][OH^-] \over [NH_3]} = 1.77 \times 10^{-5}$$

    Let [NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>] = [OH<sup>-</sup>] = x, then [NH<sub>3</sub>] = 0.10 - x &asymp; 0.10.

    $${x^2 \over 0.10} = 1.77 \times 10^{-5}$$

    $$x = 1.33 \times 10^{-3}\ M = [OH^-]$$

    $$[H^+] = {K_w \over [OH^-]} = {10^{-14} \over 1.33 \times 10^{-3}} = 7.51 \times 10^{-12}\ M$$

    $$pH = -log(7.51 \times 10^{-12}) = 11.12$$

    **After adding HCl**

    25 mL of 0.1 M HCl = 2.5 mmol HCl. 50 mL of 0.1 M NH<sub>3</sub> = 5 mmol NH<sub>3</sub>. So 2.5 mmol NH<sub>3</sub> is neutralized.

    $$NH_3 + HCl &rarr; NH_4^+ + Cl^-$$

    $$Before:\ \ 5\ \ \ \ 2.5\ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ 0\ \ (mmol)$$

    $$After:\ \ 2.5\ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ 2.5\ \ \ \ 2.5\ \ (mmol)$$

    The resulting 75 mL contains 2.5 mmol NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> (0.033 M) and 2.5 mmol unneutralized NH<sub>3</sub> (0.033 M). This NH<sub>3</sub> ionizes in presence of the common ion NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>:

    $$NH_4OH &#8652; NH_4^+ + OH^-$$

    $$Equilibrium:\ \ 0.033 - y\ \ \ \ 0.033 + y\ \ \ \ y$$

    As y is small, [NH<sub>4</sub>OH] &asymp; 0.033 M and [NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>] &asymp; 0.033 M.

    $$K_b = {[NH_4^+][OH^-] \over [NH_4OH]} = {y(0.033) \over 0.033} = 1.77 \times 10^{-5}$$

    $$y = [OH^-] = 1.77 \times 10^{-5}\ M$$

    $$[H^+] = {10^{-14} \over 1.77 \times 10^{-5}} = 0.56 \times 10^{-9}\ M$$

    $$pH = 9.24$$

!!! question "The ionization constant of phenol is 1.0 &times; 10<sup>-10</sup>. What is the concentration of phenolate ion in 0.05 M solution of phenol? What will be its degree of ionization if the solution is also 0.01 M in sodium phenolate?"

    $$C_6H_5OH &#8652; H^+ + C_6H_5O^-$$

    **In 0.05 M phenol alone**

    $$[C_6H_5O^-] = \sqrt{K_a\ c} = \sqrt{1.0 \times 10^{-10} \times 0.05} = 2.24 \times 10^{-6}\ M$$

    $$&alpha; = {2.24 \times 10^{-6} \over 0.05} = 4.47 \times 10^{-5}$$

    **In presence of 0.01 M sodium phenolate**

    Sodium phenolate is fully ionized, so [C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>O<sup>-</sup>] &asymp; 0.01 M (the phenol's own contribution is negligible).

    $$[H^+] = {K_a[C_6H_5OH] \over [C_6H_5O^-]} = {1.0 \times 10^{-10} \times 0.05 \over 0.01} = 5.0 \times 10^{-10}\ M$$

    $$&alpha; = {[H^+] \over c} = {5.0 \times 10^{-10} \over 0.05} = 1.0 \times 10^{-8}$$

    The degree of ionization drops from 4.47 &times; 10<sup>-5</sup> to 1.0 &times; 10<sup>-8</sup> because of the common ion.

!!! question "Calculate the degree of ionization of 0.05 M acetic acid if its pK<sub>a</sub> value is 4.74. How is the degree of dissociation affected when its solution also contains (a) 0.01 M (b) 0.1 M in HCl?"

    $$K_a = 10^{-4.74} = 1.82 \times 10^{-5}$$

    **Without HCl**

    $$&alpha; = \sqrt{K_a \over c} = \sqrt{1.82 \times 10^{-5} \over 0.05} = 0.019$$

    **With HCl**, [H<sup>+</sup>] &asymp; [HCl], and &alpha; = K<sub>a</sub>/[H<sup>+</sup>]:

    $$(a)\ &alpha; = {1.82 \times 10^{-5} \over 0.01} = 1.82 \times 10^{-3}$$

    $$(b)\ &alpha; = {1.82 \times 10^{-5} \over 0.1} = 1.82 \times 10^{-4}$$

    The degree of dissociation decreases as more HCl (common ion H<sup>+</sup>) is added.

!!! question "The ionization constant of dimethylamine is 5.4 &times; 10<sup>-4</sup>. Calculate its degree of ionization in its 0.02 M solution. What percentage of dimethylamine is ionized if the solution is also 0.1 M in NaOH?"

    $$(CH_3)_2NH + H_2O &#8652; (CH_3)_2NH_2^+ + OH^-$$

    **In 0.02 M solution**

    $$&alpha; = \sqrt{K_b \over c} = \sqrt{5.4 \times 10^{-4} \over 0.02} = 0.164$$

    Since &alpha; is more than 5%, solving the full quadratic, 0.02&alpha;<sup>2</sup> + 5.4 &times; 10<sup>-4</sup>&alpha; - 5.4 &times; 10<sup>-4</sup> = 0, gives the more exact value &alpha; = 0.151.

    **In presence of 0.1 M NaOH**

    [OH<sup>-</sup>] &asymp; 0.1 M from NaOH, so:

    $$&alpha; = {K_b \over [OH^-]} = {5.4 \times 10^{-4} \over 0.1} = 5.4 \times 10^{-3}$$

    So only 0.54% of dimethylamine is ionized.

!!! question "The ionization constant of propanoic acid is 1.32 &times; 10<sup>-5</sup>. Calculate the degree of ionization of the acid in its 0.05 M solution and also its pH. What will be its degree of ionization if the solution is 0.01 M in HCl also?"

    $$&alpha; = \sqrt{K_a \over c} = \sqrt{1.32 \times 10^{-5} \over 0.05} = 1.63 \times 10^{-2}$$

    $$[H^+] = c&alpha; = 0.05 \times 1.63 \times 10^{-2} = 8.12 \times 10^{-4}\ M$$

    $$pH = -log(8.12 \times 10^{-4}) = 3.09$$

    In presence of 0.01 M HCl:

    $$&alpha; = {K_a \over [H^+]} = {1.32 \times 10^{-5} \over 0.01} = 1.32 \times 10^{-3}$$
