## Salt Hydrolysis

Salt hydrolysis is the process of interaction between water and the cations, anions or both of a salt, which changes the pH of the solution.

* Salts are formed by the reaction of acids and bases in definite proportions. In water, salts ionize completely.
* The ions formed either simply get hydrated, or react with water to re-form the corresponding acid or base. The second process is hydrolysis.
* Cations of strong bases (Na<sup>+</sup>, K<sup>+</sup>, Ca<sup>2+</sup>, Ba<sup>2+</sup> etc.) and anions of strong acids (Cl<sup>-</sup>, Br<sup>-</sup>, NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>, ClO<sub>4</sub><sup>-</sup> etc.) only get hydrated and do not hydrolyse.
* So, solutions of salts of strong acids and strong bases (like NaCl, KNO<sub>3</sub>) are neutral, with pH = 7.
* Ions coming from weak acids or weak bases do hydrolyse. We consider three types of such salts:

Type of salt                     | Example                          | Ion that hydrolyses                          | Nature of solution
---------------------------------|----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|-------------------
Weak acid + strong base          | CH<sub>3</sub>COONa              | Anion (CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup>)        | Basic (pH > 7)
Strong acid + weak base          | NH<sub>4</sub>Cl                 | Cation (NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>)          | Acidic (pH < 7)
Weak acid + weak base            | CH<sub>3</sub>COONH<sub>4</sub>  | Both ions                                    | Depends on pK<sub>a</sub> and pK<sub>b</sub>
Strong acid + strong base        | NaCl                             | None                                         | Neutral (pH = 7)

## 1. Salt of Weak Acid and Strong Base

* CH<sub>3</sub>COONa, a salt of the weak acid CH<sub>3</sub>COOH and the strong base NaOH, is completely ionized in water.

!!! tip ""

    $$CH_3COONa_{(aq)} &rarr; CH_3COO^-_{(aq)} + Na^+_{(aq)}$$

* The acetate ion undergoes hydrolysis to give acetic acid and OH<sup>-</sup> ions:

!!! tip ""

    $$CH_3COO^-_{(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; CH_3COOH_{(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)}$$

* Acetic acid is a weak acid (K<sub>a</sub> = 1.8 &times; 10<sup>-5</sup>), so it remains mainly unionized. OH<sup>-</sup> ions build up and the solution becomes alkaline (pH > 7).

### Hydrolysis constant, degree of hydrolysis and pH

* Let c be the concentration of the salt and h the degree of hydrolysis (fraction of the salt hydrolysed).

!!! tip ""

    $$CH_3COO^- + H_2O &#8652; CH_3COOH + OH^-$$

    $$Equilibrium:\ \ c(1-h)\ \ \ \ \ \ ch\ \ \ \ \ \ ch$$

    $$K_h = {[CH_3COOH][OH^-] \over [CH_3COO^-]}$$

* Multiplying numerator and denominator by [H<sup>+</sup>]:

!!! tip ""

    $$K_h = {[CH_3COOH] \over [CH_3COO^-][H^+]} \times [H^+][OH^-] = {K_w \over K_a}$$

    $$K_h = {ch^2 \over 1-h} &asymp; ch^2\ \ \ (h \ll 1)$$

    $$h = \sqrt{K_h \over c} = \sqrt{K_w \over K_a\ c}$$

    $$[OH^-] = ch = \sqrt{K_w\ c \over K_a}$$

    $$pH = 7 + {1 \over 2}pK_a + {1 \over 2}log\ c\ \ \ (at\ 298\ K)$$

## 2. Salt of Strong Acid and Weak Base

* NH<sub>4</sub>Cl, formed from the weak base NH<sub>4</sub>OH and the strong acid HCl, dissociates completely in water.

!!! tip ""

    $$NH_4Cl_{(aq)} &rarr; NH^+_{4(aq)} + Cl^-_{(aq)}$$

* Ammonium ions undergo hydrolysis to form NH<sub>4</sub>OH and H<sup>+</sup> ions:

!!! tip ""

    $$NH^+_{4(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; NH_4OH_{(aq)} + H^+_{(aq)}$$

* NH<sub>4</sub>OH is a weak base (K<sub>b</sub> = 1.77 &times; 10<sup>-5</sup>) and remains almost unionized. H<sup>+</sup> ion concentration increases, so the solution is acidic and its pH is less than 7.

### Hydrolysis constant, degree of hydrolysis and pH

!!! tip ""

    $$K_h = {[NH_4OH][H^+] \over [NH_4^+]} = {K_w \over K_b}$$

    $$h = \sqrt{K_w \over K_b\ c}$$

    $$[H^+] = ch = \sqrt{K_w\ c \over K_b}$$

    $$pH = 7 - {1 \over 2}pK_b - {1 \over 2}log\ c\ \ \ (at\ 298\ K)$$

## 3. Salt of Weak Acid and Weak Base

* In CH<sub>3</sub>COONH<sub>4</sub>, both ions undergo hydrolysis:

!!! tip ""

    $$CH_3COO^- + NH_4^+ + H_2O &#8652; CH_3COOH + NH_4OH$$

* CH<sub>3</sub>COOH and NH<sub>4</sub>OH also remain in partially dissociated form:

!!! tip ""

    $$CH_3COOH &#8652; CH_3COO^- + H^+$$

    $$NH_4OH &#8652; NH_4^+ + OH^-$$

    $$H_2O &#8652; H^+ + OH^-$$

### Hydrolysis constant, degree of hydrolysis and pH

!!! tip ""

    $$Equilibrium:\ \ c(1-h)\ \ \ \ c(1-h)\ \ \ \ \ \ ch\ \ \ \ ch$$

    $$K_h = {[CH_3COOH][NH_4OH] \over [CH_3COO^-][NH_4^+]} = {K_w \over K_a K_b}$$

    $$K_h = {h^2 \over (1-h)^2},\ \ \ {h \over 1-h} = \sqrt{K_h}$$

    $$[H^+] = K_a{[CH_3COOH] \over [CH_3COO^-]} = K_a{h \over 1-h} = \sqrt{K_w K_a \over K_b}$$

    $$pH = 7 + {1 \over 2}(pK_a - pK_b)$$

!!! tip "Conclusion"

    * For a salt of a weak acid and a weak base, the degree of hydrolysis and the pH are independent of the concentration of the solution. The pH depends only on pK<sub>a</sub> and pK<sub>b</sub>.

    * pH > 7 if pK<sub>a</sub> > pK<sub>b</sub> (the base is relatively stronger), pH < 7 if pK<sub>a</sub> < pK<sub>b</sub>, and pH &asymp; 7 if pK<sub>a</sub> = pK<sub>b</sub>.

## Questions

!!! question "The pK<sub>a</sub> of acetic acid and pK<sub>b</sub> of ammonium hydroxide are 4.76 and 4.75 respectively. Calculate the pH of ammonium acetate solution."

    $$pH = 7 + {1 \over 2}[pK_a - pK_b]$$

    $$pH = 7 + {1 \over 2}[4.76 - 4.75]$$

    $$pH = 7 + {1 \over 2}[0.01] = 7 + 0.005 = 7.005$$

!!! question "Calculate the pH of a 0.1 M sodium acetate solution and its degree of hydrolysis. (K<sub>a</sub> of acetic acid = 1.8 &times; 10<sup>-5</sup>)"

    $$K_h = {K_w \over K_a} = {10^{-14} \over 1.8 \times 10^{-5}} = 5.56 \times 10^{-10}$$

    $$h = \sqrt{K_h \over c} = \sqrt{5.56 \times 10^{-10} \over 0.1} = 7.45 \times 10^{-5}$$

    $$[OH^-] = ch = 0.1 \times 7.45 \times 10^{-5} = 7.45 \times 10^{-6}\ M$$

    $$pOH = 5.13,\ \ pH = 14 - 5.13 = 8.87$$

    Check with the formula: pH = 7 + &frac12;(4.74) + &frac12;log(0.1) = 7 + 2.37 - 0.5 = 8.87.

!!! question "Calculate the pH of a 0.1 M ammonium chloride solution. (K<sub>b</sub> of NH<sub>4</sub>OH = 1.77 &times; 10<sup>-5</sup>)"

    $$pK_b = -log(1.77 \times 10^{-5}) = 4.75$$

    $$pH = 7 - {1 \over 2}pK_b - {1 \over 2}log\ c$$

    $$pH = 7 - {1 \over 2}(4.75) - {1 \over 2}log(0.1) = 7 - 2.375 + 0.5 = 5.12$$

!!! question "The ionization constant of nitrous acid is 4.5 &times; 10<sup>-4</sup>. Calculate the pH of 0.04 M sodium nitrite solution and also its degree of hydrolysis."

    NaNO<sub>2</sub> is a salt of a weak acid and strong base, so NO<sub>2</sub><sup>-</sup> hydrolyses:

    $$NO_2^- + H_2O &#8652; HNO_2 + OH^-$$

    $$K_h = {K_w \over K_a} = {10^{-14} \over 4.5 \times 10^{-4}} = 2.22 \times 10^{-11}$$

    $$h = \sqrt{K_h \over c} = \sqrt{2.22 \times 10^{-11} \over 0.04} = 2.36 \times 10^{-5}$$

    $$[OH^-] = ch = 0.04 \times 2.36 \times 10^{-5} = 9.43 \times 10^{-7}\ M$$

    $$pOH = 6.03,\ \ pH = 14 - 6.03 = 7.97$$

!!! question "A 0.02 M solution of pyridinium hydrochloride has pH = 3.44. Calculate the ionization constant of pyridine."

    Pyridinium hydrochloride (C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sup>+</sup>Cl<sup>-</sup>) is a salt of a weak base and strong acid. The cation hydrolyses:

    $$C_5H_5NH^+ + H_2O &#8652; C_5H_5N + H_3O^+$$

    $$[H^+] = 10^{-3.44} = 3.63 \times 10^{-4}\ M$$

    $$K_h = {[H^+]^2 \over c} = {(3.63 \times 10^{-4})^2 \over 0.02} = 6.6 \times 10^{-6}$$

    $$K_b = {K_w \over K_h} = {10^{-14} \over 6.6 \times 10^{-6}} = 1.5 \times 10^{-9}$$

!!! question "Predict if the solutions of the following salts are neutral, acidic or basic: NaCl, KBr, NaCN, NH<sub>4</sub>NO<sub>3</sub>, NaNO<sub>2</sub> and KF."

    Salt                             | Formed from                                       | Nature
    ---------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|--------
    NaCl                             | Strong acid (HCl) + strong base (NaOH)            | Neutral
    KBr                              | Strong acid (HBr) + strong base (KOH)             | Neutral
    NaCN                             | Weak acid (HCN) + strong base (NaOH)              | Basic
    NH<sub>4</sub>NO<sub>3</sub>     | Strong acid (HNO<sub>3</sub>) + weak base (NH<sub>4</sub>OH) | Acidic
    NaNO<sub>2</sub>                 | Weak acid (HNO<sub>2</sub>) + strong base (NaOH)  | Basic
    KF                               | Weak acid (HF) + strong base (KOH)                | Basic

!!! question "The ionization constant of chloroacetic acid is 1.35 &times; 10<sup>-3</sup>. What will be the pH of 0.1 M acid and its 0.1 M sodium salt solution?"

    **0.1 M chloroacetic acid**

    $$[H^+] = \sqrt{K_a\ c} = \sqrt{1.35 \times 10^{-3} \times 0.1} = 1.16 \times 10^{-2}\ M$$

    $$pH = -log(1.16 \times 10^{-2}) = 1.94$$

    (Here &alpha; &asymp; 0.12; solving the full quadratic gives [H<sup>+</sup>] = 1.10 &times; 10<sup>-2</sup> M and pH = 1.96.)

    **0.1 M sodium chloroacetate**

    The anion hydrolyses, giving a basic solution:

    $$K_h = {K_w \over K_a} = {10^{-14} \over 1.35 \times 10^{-3}} = 7.41 \times 10^{-12}$$

    $$[OH^-] = \sqrt{K_h\ c} = \sqrt{7.41 \times 10^{-12} \times 0.1} = 8.61 \times 10^{-7}\ M$$

    $$pOH = 6.07,\ \ pH = 14 - 6.07 = 7.93$$
