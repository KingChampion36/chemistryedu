## Questions on Photons and Photoelectric Effect

!!! question "Yellow light emitted from a sodium lamp has a wavelength (λ) of 580 nm. Calculate the frequency (ν) and wavenumber of the yellow light."

    $$&nu; = {c \over &lambda;} = {3 \times 10^8 \over 580 \times 10^{-9}} = 5.17 \times 10^{14}\ s^{-1}$$

    $$\bar{\nu} = {1 \over &lambda;} = {1 \over 580 \times 10^{-9}} = 1.72 \times 10^6\ m^{-1}$$

!!! question "Find energy of each of the photons which <br>(i) correspond to light of frequency 3 × 10<sup>15</sup> Hz <br>(ii) have wavelength of 0.50 Å."

    $$(i)\ E = h&nu; = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^{15} = 1.988 \times 10^{-18}\ J$$

    $$(ii)\ E = {hc \over &lambda;} = {6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^8 \over 0.50 \times 10^{-10}} = 3.98 \times 10^{-15}\ J$$

!!! question "What is the number of photons of light with a wavelength of 4000 pm that provide 1 J of energy?"

    $$E = {hc \over &lambda;} = {6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^8 \over 4000 \times 10^{-12}} = 4.97 \times 10^{-17}\ J$$

    $$Number\ of\ photons = {1 \over 4.97 \times 10^{-17}} = 2.01 \times 10^{16}$$

!!! question "Electromagnetic radiation of wavelength 242 nm is just sufficient to ionise the sodium atom. Calculate the ionisation energy of sodium in kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>."

    $$E = {hc \over &lambda;} = {6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^8 \over 242 \times 10^{-9}} = 8.21 \times 10^{-19}\ J$$

    $$Ionisation\ energy = 8.21 \times 10^{-19} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = 4.94 \times 10^5\ J\ mol^{-1}$$

    $$Ionisation\ energy = 494\ kJ\ mol^{-1}$$

!!! question "Electrons are emitted with zero velocity from a metal surface when it is exposed to radiation of wavelength 6800 Å. Calculate threshold frequency (ν<sub>0</sub>) and work function (W<sub>0</sub>) of the metal."

    * Since electrons are emitted with zero velocity, the photon energy just equals the work function.

    $$&nu;_0 = {c \over &lambda;_0} = {3 \times 10^8 \over 6800 \times 10^{-10}} = 4.41 \times 10^{14}\ s^{-1}$$

    $$W_0 = h&nu;_0 = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 4.41 \times 10^{14} = 2.92 \times 10^{-19}\ J$$

## Questions on Bohr's Model and Hydrogen Spectrum

!!! question "What is the wavelength of light emitted when the electron in a hydrogen atom undergoes transition from an energy level with n = 4 to an energy level with n = 2?"

    $$&Delta;E = 2.18 \times 10^{-18}\left({1 \over 2^2} - {1 \over 4^2}\right) = 2.18 \times 10^{-18} \times {3 \over 16}$$

    $$&Delta;E = 4.09 \times 10^{-19}\ J$$

    $$&lambda; = {hc \over &Delta;E} = {6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^8 \over 4.09 \times 10^{-19}} = 4.86 \times 10^{-7}\ m = 486\ nm$$

!!! question "How much energy is required to ionise a H atom if the electron occupies n = 5 orbit? Compare your answer with the ionization enthalpy of H atom (energy required to remove the electron from n = 1 orbit)."

    $$E_5 = -{2.18 \times 10^{-18} \over 5^2} = -8.72 \times 10^{-20}\ J$$

    $$Energy\ required\ (n = 5 &rarr; &infin;) = 0 - (-8.72 \times 10^{-20}) = 8.72 \times 10^{-20}\ J$$

    $$Energy\ required\ (n = 1 &rarr; &infin;) = 2.18 \times 10^{-18}\ J$$

    $${2.18 \times 10^{-18} \over 8.72 \times 10^{-20}} = 25$$

    So, 25 times more energy is needed to remove the electron from n = 1 than from n = 5.

!!! question "What is the maximum number of emission lines when the excited electron of a H atom in n = 6 drops to the ground state?"

    $$Number\ of\ lines = {n(n-1) \over 2} = {6 \times 5 \over 2} = 15$$

!!! question "(i) The energy associated with the first orbit in the hydrogen atom is –2.18 × 10<sup>-18</sup> J atom<sup>-1</sup>. What is the energy associated with the fifth orbit? <br>(ii) Calculate the radius of Bohr's fifth orbit for hydrogen atom."

    $$(i)\ E_5 = -{2.18 \times 10^{-18} \over 5^2} = -8.72 \times 10^{-20}\ J$$

    $$(ii)\ r_5 = n^2 a_0 = 25 \times 52.9\ pm = 1322.5\ pm = 1.3225\ nm$$

!!! question "Calculate the wavenumber for the longest wavelength transition in the Balmer series of atomic hydrogen."

    * Longest wavelength means smallest energy gap. In the Balmer series, this is n = 3 to n = 2.

    $$\bar{\nu} = 109677\left({1 \over 2^2} - {1 \over 3^2}\right) = 109677 \times {5 \over 36}$$

    $$\bar{\nu} = 15233\ cm^{-1} = 1.523 \times 10^6\ m^{-1}$$

!!! question "The electron energy in hydrogen atom is given by E<sub>n</sub> = (–2.18 × 10<sup>-18</sup>)/n<sup>2</sup> J. Calculate the energy required to remove an electron completely from the n = 2 orbit. What is the longest wavelength of light in cm that can be used to cause this transition?"

    $$&Delta;E = E_&infin; - E_2 = 0 - \left(-{2.18 \times 10^{-18} \over 4}\right) = 5.45 \times 10^{-19}\ J$$

    $$&lambda; = {hc \over &Delta;E} = {6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^8 \over 5.45 \times 10^{-19}} = 3.65 \times 10^{-7}\ m$$

    $$&lambda; = 3.65 \times 10^{-5}\ cm$$

!!! question "What transition in the hydrogen spectrum would have the same wavelength as the Balmer transition n = 4 to n = 2 of He<sup>+</sup> spectrum?"

    $$For\ He^+:\ \bar{\nu} = R Z^2\left({1 \over 2^2} - {1 \over 4^2}\right) = R \times 4 \times {3 \over 16} = {3R \over 4}$$

    $$For\ H:\ R\left({1 \over n_1^2} - {1 \over n_2^2}\right) = {3R \over 4}$$

    $$\left({1 \over n_1^2} - {1 \over n_2^2}\right) = {3 \over 4} = {1 \over 1^2} - {1 \over 2^2}$$

    So the transition is n = 2 to n = 1 (first line of Lyman series).

!!! question "Calculate the energy required for the process: He<sup>+</sup><sub>(g)</sub> &rarr; He<sup>2+</sup><sub>(g)</sub> + e<sup>–</sup>. <br>The ionization energy for the H atom in the ground state is 2.18 × 10<sup>-18</sup> J atom<sup>-1</sup>."

    $$E_n = -2.18 \times 10^{-18} \times {Z^2 \over n^2}$$

    $$For\ He^+,\ Z = 2,\ n = 1:\ E_1 = -2.18 \times 10^{-18} \times 4 = -8.72 \times 10^{-18}\ J$$

    $$Energy\ required = 8.72 \times 10^{-18}\ J$$

!!! question "Emission transitions in the Paschen series end at orbit n = 3 and start from orbit n, and can be represented as ν = 3.29 × 10<sup>15</sup> (Hz) [1/3<sup>2</sup> – 1/n<sup>2</sup>]. Calculate the value of n if the transition is observed at 1285 nm. Find the region of the spectrum."

    $$&nu; = {c \over &lambda;} = {3 \times 10^8 \over 1285 \times 10^{-9}} = 2.335 \times 10^{14}\ Hz$$

    $${1 \over 9} - {1 \over n^2} = {2.335 \times 10^{14} \over 3.29 \times 10^{15}} = 0.0710$$

    $${1 \over n^2} = 0.1111 - 0.0710 = 0.0401$$

    $$n^2 = 24.9 &asymp; 25,\ so\ n = 5$$

    The radiation of 1285 nm lies in the infrared region.

## Questions on Quantum Numbers

!!! question "What is the lowest value of n that allows g orbitals to exist?"

    * For g orbitals, l = 4. Since l can be at most (n – 1), n – 1 = 4, so n = 5.

!!! question "An electron is in one of the 3d orbitals. Give the possible values of n, l and m<sub>l</sub> for this electron."

    $$n = 3,\ l = 2,\ m_l = -2, -1, 0, +1, +2$$

!!! question "Explain, giving reasons, which of the following sets of quantum numbers are not possible. <br>(a) n = 0, l = 0, m<sub>l</sub> = 0, m<sub>s</sub> = +½ <br>(b) n = 1, l = 0, m<sub>l</sub> = 0, m<sub>s</sub> = –½ <br>(c) n = 1, l = 1, m<sub>l</sub> = 0, m<sub>s</sub> = +½ <br>(d) n = 2, l = 1, m<sub>l</sub> = 0, m<sub>s</sub> = –½ <br>(e) n = 3, l = 3, m<sub>l</sub> = –3, m<sub>s</sub> = +½ <br>(f) n = 3, l = 1, m<sub>l</sub> = 0, m<sub>s</sub> = +½"

    * (a) Not possible, because n cannot be zero.

    * (b) Possible (1s orbital).

    * (c) Not possible, because for n = 1, l can only be 0.

    * (d) Possible (2p orbital).

    * (e) Not possible, because for n = 3, l can be 0, 1 or 2 only.

    * (f) Possible (3p orbital).

!!! question "How many electrons in an atom may have the following quantum numbers? <br>(a) n = 4, m<sub>s</sub> = –½ (b) n = 3, l = 0"

    * (a) For n = 4, total electrons = 2n<sup>2</sup> = 32. Half of them have m<sub>s</sub> = –½. So, 16 electrons.

    * (b) n = 3, l = 0 is the 3s orbital, which can hold 2 electrons.

!!! question "(a) How many sub-shells are associated with n = 4? (b) How many electrons will be present in the sub-shells having m<sub>s</sub> value of –½ for n = 4?"

    * (a) For n = 4, l = 0, 1, 2, 3. So there are 4 sub-shells: 4s, 4p, 4d and 4f.

    * (b) Number of orbitals = n<sup>2</sup> = 16. Each orbital has one electron with m<sub>s</sub> = –½. So, 16 electrons.

!!! question "Which of the following are isoelectronic species, i.e. those having the same number of electrons? <br>Na<sup>+</sup>, K<sup>+</sup>, Mg<sup>2+</sup>, Ca<sup>2+</sup>, S<sup>2–</sup>, Ar"

    Species          | Na<sup>+</sup> | K<sup>+</sup> | Mg<sup>2+</sup> | Ca<sup>2+</sup> | S<sup>2–</sup> | Ar |
    -----------------|----------------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|----|
    Electrons        | 10             | 18            | 10              | 18              | 18             | 18 |

    * Na<sup>+</sup> and Mg<sup>2+</sup> are isoelectronic (10 electrons).

    * K<sup>+</sup>, Ca<sup>2+</sup>, S<sup>2–</sup> and Ar are isoelectronic (18 electrons).
