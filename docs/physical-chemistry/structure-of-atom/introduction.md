# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Structure of Atom | Introduction and Sub-atomic Particles

The idea of atoms is very old. Early Indian and Greek philosophers (around 400 B.C.) believed that if matter is divided again and again, we finally reach a particle that cannot be divided further. The word 'atom' comes from the Greek word 'a-tomio', meaning 'uncut-able' or 'non-divisible'.

* John Dalton (1808) gave the first scientific atomic theory, called **Dalton's atomic theory**, which treated the atom as the ultimate indivisible particle of matter.
* Experiments at the end of the nineteenth century showed that atoms are actually made up of smaller **sub-atomic particles**: electrons, protons and neutrons.
* After these discoveries, scientists had to answer some important questions:
    * Why is the atom stable even though it contains charged particles?
    * Why do different elements show different physical and chemical properties?
    * How do atoms combine to form different molecules?
    * What is the origin of the radiation absorbed or emitted by atoms?

## Sub-atomic Particles

Dalton's theory could explain the law of conservation of mass, the law of constant composition and the law of multiple proportions. However, it could not explain why substances like glass or ebonite produce electricity when rubbed with silk or fur. This pointed to the existence of charged particles inside the atom.

## Discovery of Electron

* Michael Faraday (1830) showed that when electricity is passed through a solution of an electrolyte, matter is liberated and deposited at the electrodes. This suggested that electricity itself is made of particles.
* In the mid 1850s, Faraday and others studied electrical discharge through partially evacuated glass tubes called **cathode ray discharge tubes**.
* A cathode ray tube is a glass tube with two thin metal electrodes sealed in it. Discharge through the gas is seen only at **very low pressure** and **very high voltage**.
* When high voltage is applied, a stream of particles moves from the negative electrode (cathode) to the positive electrode (anode). These are called **cathode rays** or **cathode ray particles**.
* When a hole is made in the anode and the tube behind it is coated with zinc sulphide (a phosphorescent material), a bright spot appears on the coating where the rays strike it.

### Properties of Cathode Rays

1. Cathode rays start from the cathode and move towards the anode.
2. The rays themselves are invisible. Their behaviour is observed with the help of fluorescent or phosphorescent materials that glow when hit by them. Old television picture tubes work on this principle.
3. In the absence of an electric or magnetic field, cathode rays travel in straight lines.
4. In the presence of an electric or magnetic field, they behave like negatively charged particles. These negatively charged particles are called **electrons**.
5. The characteristics of cathode rays do not depend on the material of the electrodes or the gas present in the tube.

!!! tip "Conclusion"

    Since the properties of cathode rays are the same whatever the electrode material or gas used, electrons must be a basic constituent of all atoms.

## Charge to Mass Ratio of Electron

* In 1897, J.J. Thomson measured the ratio of charge (e) to mass (m<sub>e</sub>) of the electron. He applied electric and magnetic fields perpendicular to each other and to the path of the electrons.
* The deviation of the particles from their path depends on:
    * **Magnitude of charge:** greater the charge, greater the interaction with the field and greater the deflection.
    * **Mass of the particle:** lighter the particle, greater the deflection.
    * **Strength of the field:** deflection increases with the voltage across the electrodes or the strength of the magnetic field.
* With only the electric field applied, the electrons hit the screen at one point (A). With only the magnetic field, they hit another point (C). By balancing the two fields, the electrons can be brought back to their original path (point B).
* From these deflections Thomson found:

!!! tip ""

    $${e \over m_e} = 1.758820 \times 10^{11}\ C\ kg^{-1}$$

    Where, m<sub>e</sub> is mass of electron in kg and e is magnitude of charge on electron in coulomb (C).

* Since electrons are negatively charged, the charge on an electron is written as –e.

## Charge on the Electron: Millikan's Oil Drop Method

* R.A. Millikan (1906–14) found the charge on an electron using the **oil drop experiment**.
* Fine oil droplets from an atomiser enter an electrical condenser through a tiny hole in its upper plate. Their fall is watched through a telescope fitted with a micrometer eye piece.
* The air in the chamber is ionised by X-rays. The oil droplets pick up charge by colliding with the gaseous ions.
* By changing the voltage on the plates, the fall of a charged droplet can be slowed down, speeded up or stopped. From this, the charge on the droplet is calculated.
* Millikan found that the charge q on any droplet is always an integral multiple of a fundamental charge e:

!!! tip ""

    $$q = ne,\ where\ n = 1, 2, 3, ...$$

* Millikan's value of charge was –1.6 × 10<sup>-19</sup> C. The present accepted value is –1.6022 × 10<sup>-19</sup> C.
* Combining this with Thomson's e/m<sub>e</sub> value gives the mass of the electron:

!!! tip ""

    $$m_e = {e \over e/m_e} = {1.6022 \times 10^{-19}\ C \over 1.758820 \times 10^{11}\ C\ kg^{-1}}$$

    $$m_e = 9.1094 \times 10^{-31}\ kg$$

## Discovery of Protons and Neutrons

* Electrical discharge in a modified cathode ray tube produced rays of positively charged particles called **canal rays** (or anode rays).
* Properties of these positively charged particles:
    1. Unlike cathode rays, they depend on the nature of the gas in the tube. They are simply positively charged gaseous ions.
    2. Their charge to mass ratio depends on the gas from which they are formed.
    3. Some of them carry a multiple of the fundamental unit of charge.
    4. Their behaviour in electric or magnetic fields is opposite to that of electrons.
* The smallest and lightest positive ion was obtained from hydrogen. It was called the **proton** and was characterised in 1919.
* Chadwick (1932) bombarded a thin sheet of beryllium with α-particles. Electrically neutral particles with mass slightly greater than that of protons were emitted. He named them **neutrons**.

## Properties of Fundamental Particles

Name     | Symbol | Absolute charge/C             | Relative charge | Mass/kg                        | Mass/u  | Approx. mass/u |
---------|--------|-------------------------------|-----------------|--------------------------------|---------|----------------|
Electron | e      | –1.6022 × 10<sup>-19</sup>    | –1              | 9.10939 × 10<sup>-31</sup>     | 0.00054 | 0              |
Proton   | p      | +1.6022 × 10<sup>-19</sup>    | +1              | 1.67262 × 10<sup>-27</sup>     | 1.00727 | 1              |
Neutron  | n      | 0                             | 0               | 1.67493 × 10<sup>-27</sup>     | 1.00867 | 1              |

!!! note "Note"

    * **X-rays:** Röentgen (1895) found that when electrons strike a material in a cathode ray tube, rays are produced that cause fluorescence. He called them X-rays. They are produced most effectively when electrons strike a dense metal anode (called a target). X-rays are not deflected by electric or magnetic fields, have very high penetrating power and very short wavelength (~0.1 nm).

    * **Radioactivity:** Becquerel observed that certain elements emit radiation on their own. This is called radioactivity and such elements are called radioactive elements. Three kinds of rays are emitted: **α-rays** (high energy particles with two units of positive charge and four units of atomic mass, i.e. helium nuclei), **β-rays** (negatively charged particles similar to electrons) and **γ-rays** (high energy neutral radiation like X-rays, not made of particles).

    * **Penetrating power:** α-particles are the least penetrating, β-rays are about 100 times and γ-rays about 1000 times as penetrating as α-particles.

## Questions

!!! question "Calculate the number of electrons which will together weigh one gram. Also calculate the mass and charge of one mole of electrons."

    $$Mass\ of\ one\ electron = 9.10939 \times 10^{-31}\ kg = 9.10939 \times 10^{-28}\ g$$

    $$Number\ of\ electrons\ in\ 1\ g = {1 \over 9.10939 \times 10^{-28}} = 1.098 \times 10^{27}$$

    $$Mass\ of\ 1\ mole\ electrons = 9.10939 \times 10^{-31} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = 5.486 \times 10^{-7}\ kg$$

    $$Charge\ on\ 1\ mole\ electrons = 1.6022 \times 10^{-19} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = 9.648 \times 10^4\ C$$

!!! question "In Millikan's experiment, static electric charge on an oil drop is found to be –1.282 × 10<sup>-18</sup> C. Calculate the number of electrons present on it."

    $$Number\ of\ electrons = {q \over e} = {1.282 \times 10^{-18} \over 1.6022 \times 10^{-19}}$$

    $$Number\ of\ electrons = 8$$
