# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Redox Reactions | Balancing of Redox Reactions

Two methods are used to balance redox equations:

* **Oxidation number method:** based on the change in oxidation number of the reducing agent and the oxidising agent.
* **Half reaction (ion-electron) method:** based on splitting the reaction into an oxidation half and a reduction half.

Both methods give the same answer; the choice is a matter of convenience.

## Oxidation Number Method

* **Step 1:** Write the correct formula of each reactant and product (the skeletal equation).
* **Step 2:** Assign oxidation numbers to all elements and identify the atoms whose oxidation number changes.
* **Step 3:** Calculate the increase or decrease in oxidation number per atom and for the whole molecule/ion. If these are not equal, multiply by suitable coefficients to make the **total increase equal to the total decrease**.
* **Step 4:** If the reaction is in water, add H<sup>+</sup> (acidic medium) or OH<sup>-</sup> (basic medium) on the appropriate side so that the total ionic charges on both sides are equal.
* **Step 5:** Balance hydrogen atoms by adding H<sub>2</sub>O molecules. Finally check oxygen atoms; if they are equal on both sides, the equation is balanced.

!!! note "Note"

    If you find that two substances are reduced and nothing is oxidised (or vice versa), something is wrong: either the formulas or the oxidation numbers have been assigned incorrectly.

!!! example "Example 1: Dichromate and sulphite in acidic medium"

    Write the net ionic equation for the reaction of K<sub>2</sub>Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub> with Na<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>3</sub> in acid solution to give Cr<sup>3+</sup> and sulphate ion.

    * **Step 1:** Skeletal ionic equation:

    $$Cr_2O_7^{2-}{}_{(aq)} + SO_3^{2-}{}_{(aq)} &rarr; Cr^{3+}_{(aq)} + SO_4^{2-}{}_{(aq)}$$

    * **Step 2:** Assign oxidation numbers to Cr and S:

    $$\overset{+6}{Cr_2}O_7^{2-} + \overset{+4}{S}O_3^{2-} &rarr; \overset{+3}{Cr^{3+}} + \overset{+6}{S}O_4^{2-}$$

    Dichromate is the oxidant and sulphite is the reductant.

    * **Step 3:** Each Cr decreases by 3, so two Cr atoms decrease by 6 in total. Each S increases by 2. To make increase = decrease, take 3 sulphite ions:

    $$Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 3SO_3^{2-} &rarr; 2Cr^{3+} + 3SO_4^{2-}$$

    * **Step 4:** Charge on left = -2 - 6 = -8; charge on right = +6 - 6 = 0. In acidic medium, add 8H<sup>+</sup> on the left:

    $$Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 3SO_3^{2-} + 8H^+ &rarr; 2Cr^{3+} + 3SO_4^{2-}$$

    * **Step 5:** Add 4H<sub>2</sub>O on the right to balance hydrogen:

    $$Cr_2O_7^{2-}{}_{(aq)} + 3SO_3^{2-}{}_{(aq)} + 8H^+_{(aq)} &rarr; 2Cr^{3+}_{(aq)} + 3SO_4^{2-}{}_{(aq)} + 4H_2O_{(l)}$$

    Oxygen: 7 + 9 = 16 on the left and 12 + 4 = 16 on the right. The equation is balanced.

!!! example "Example 2: Permanganate and bromide in basic medium"

    Permanganate ion reacts with bromide ion in basic medium to give manganese dioxide and bromate ion. Write the balanced ionic equation.

    * **Step 1:** Skeletal ionic equation:

    $$MnO_4^-{}_{(aq)} + Br^-_{(aq)} &rarr; MnO_{2(s)} + BrO_3^-{}_{(aq)}$$

    * **Step 2:** Assign oxidation numbers to Mn and Br:

    $$\overset{+7}{Mn}O_4^- + \overset{-1}{Br^-} &rarr; \overset{+4}{Mn}O_2 + \overset{+5}{Br}O_3^-$$

    Permanganate is the oxidant and bromide is the reductant.

    * **Step 3:** Mn decreases by 3 and Br increases by 6. So take 2MnO<sub>4</sub><sup>-</sup>:

    $$2MnO_4^- + Br^- &rarr; 2MnO_2 + BrO_3^-$$

    * **Step 4:** Charge on left = -3; charge on right = -1. In basic medium, add 2OH<sup>-</sup> on the right:

    $$2MnO_4^- + Br^- &rarr; 2MnO_2 + BrO_3^- + 2OH^-$$

    * **Step 5:** Add one H<sub>2</sub>O on the left to balance hydrogen:

    $$2MnO_4^-{}_{(aq)} + Br^-_{(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &rarr; 2MnO_{2(s)} + BrO_3^-{}_{(aq)} + 2OH^-_{(aq)}$$

## Half Reaction (Ion-Electron) Method

* In this method, the two half equations are balanced separately and then added together.
* **Step 1:** Write the unbalanced equation in ionic form.
* **Step 2:** Separate it into an oxidation half and a reduction half.
* **Step 3:** Balance atoms other than O and H in each half reaction.
* **Step 4:** In acidic medium, add H<sub>2</sub>O to balance O atoms and H<sup>+</sup> to balance H atoms.
* **Step 5:** Add electrons to one side of each half reaction to balance the charges. Multiply one or both half reactions by suitable coefficients so that the number of electrons is equal.
* **Step 6:** Add the two half reactions and cancel the electrons (and any common species) on both sides.
* **Step 7:** Verify that atoms and charges are balanced on both sides.

!!! tip "Basic medium"

    First balance the atoms exactly as in acidic medium. Then, for each H<sup>+</sup> ion, add an equal number of OH<sup>-</sup> ions to **both sides**. Where H<sup>+</sup> and OH<sup>-</sup> appear on the same side, combine them to give H<sub>2</sub>O.

!!! example "Example 3: Fe<sup>2+</sup> and dichromate in acidic medium"

    Balance the oxidation of Fe<sup>2+</sup> to Fe<sup>3+</sup> by Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub><sup>2-</sup> (which is reduced to Cr<sup>3+</sup>) in acidic medium.

    * **Step 1:** Unbalanced ionic equation:

    $$Fe^{2+}_{(aq)} + Cr_2O_7^{2-}{}_{(aq)} &rarr; Fe^{3+}_{(aq)} + Cr^{3+}_{(aq)}$$

    * **Step 2:** Half reactions:

    $$Oxidation\ half:\ \overset{+2}{Fe^{2+}} &rarr; \overset{+3}{Fe^{3+}}$$

    $$Reduction\ half:\ \overset{+6}{Cr_2}O_7^{2-} &rarr; \overset{+3}{Cr^{3+}}$$

    * **Step 3:** Fe is already balanced. Multiply Cr<sup>3+</sup> by 2:

    $$Cr_2O_7^{2-} &rarr; 2Cr^{3+}$$

    * **Step 4:** Add 7H<sub>2</sub>O on the right to balance O and 14H<sup>+</sup> on the left to balance H:

    $$Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 14H^+ &rarr; 2Cr^{3+} + 7H_2O$$

    * **Step 5:** Balance charges with electrons. The reduction half has +12 on the left and +6 on the right, so add 6e<sup>-</sup> on the left:

    $$Fe^{2+} &rarr; Fe^{3+} + e^-$$

    $$Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 14H^+ + 6e^- &rarr; 2Cr^{3+} + 7H_2O$$

    Multiply the oxidation half by 6 to equalise electrons:

    $$6Fe^{2+} &rarr; 6Fe^{3+} + 6e^-$$

    * **Step 6:** Add the two halves and cancel electrons:

    $$6Fe^{2+}_{(aq)} + Cr_2O_7^{2-}{}_{(aq)} + 14H^+_{(aq)} &rarr; 6Fe^{3+}_{(aq)} + 2Cr^{3+}_{(aq)} + 7H_2O_{(l)}$$

    * **Step 7:** Charge on each side = +24. Atoms are balanced. The equation is fully balanced.

!!! example "Example 4: Permanganate and iodide in basic medium"

    MnO<sub>4</sub><sup>-</sup> in basic solution oxidises I<sup>-</sup> to I<sub>2</sub> and is itself reduced to MnO<sub>2</sub>. Write the balanced ionic equation.

    * **Step 1:** Skeletal ionic equation:

    $$MnO_4^-{}_{(aq)} + I^-_{(aq)} &rarr; MnO_{2(s)} + I_{2(s)}$$

    * **Step 2:** Half reactions:

    $$Oxidation\ half:\ \overset{-1}{I^-} &rarr; \overset{0}{I_2}$$

    $$Reduction\ half:\ \overset{+7}{Mn}O_4^- &rarr; \overset{+4}{Mn}O_2$$

    * **Step 3:** Balance iodine:

    $$2I^- &rarr; I_2$$

    * **Step 4:** In the reduction half, add 2H<sub>2</sub>O on the right for O and 4H<sup>+</sup> on the left for H:

    $$MnO_4^- + 4H^+ &rarr; MnO_2 + 2H_2O$$

    Since the medium is basic, add 4OH<sup>-</sup> to both sides. 4H<sup>+</sup> + 4OH<sup>-</sup> gives 4H<sub>2</sub>O, and after cancelling water:

    $$MnO_4^- + 2H_2O &rarr; MnO_2 + 4OH^-$$

    * **Step 5:** Balance charges with electrons:

    $$2I^- &rarr; I_2 + 2e^-$$

    $$MnO_4^- + 2H_2O + 3e^- &rarr; MnO_2 + 4OH^-$$

    Multiply the oxidation half by 3 and the reduction half by 2:

    $$6I^- &rarr; 3I_2 + 6e^-$$

    $$2MnO_4^- + 4H_2O + 6e^- &rarr; 2MnO_2 + 8OH^-$$

    * **Step 6:** Add and cancel electrons:

    $$6I^-_{(aq)} + 2MnO_4^-{}_{(aq)} + 4H_2O_{(l)} &rarr; 3I_{2(s)} + 2MnO_{2(s)} + 8OH^-_{(aq)}$$

    * **Step 7:** Charge on each side = -8. Atoms are balanced.
