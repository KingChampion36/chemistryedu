# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers | Preparation of Alcohols and Phenols

In this article, we will discuss the important methods for preparing alcohols and phenols.

## Preparation of Alcohols

### 1. From Alkenes

#### (i) Acid catalysed hydration

* Alkenes react with water in the presence of an acid catalyst to form alcohols.
* For unsymmetrical alkenes, the addition follows **Markovnikov's rule**: H goes to the double-bonded carbon that already has more hydrogens, and –OH goes to the other carbon.

!!! tip ""

    $$>C=C< + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+} >C(H)-C(OH)<$$

    $$CH_3CH=CH_2 + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+} CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_3$$

**Mechanism:** The reaction takes place in three steps.

* **Step 1: Protonation of alkene** to form a carbocation by electrophilic attack of H<sub>3</sub>O<sup>+</sup>.

!!! tip ""

    $$H_2O + H^+ &rarr; H_3O^+$$

    $$>C=C< + H_3O^+ &#8652; >CH-\overset{+}{C}< + H_2O$$

* **Step 2: Nucleophilic attack of water** on the carbocation to form a protonated alcohol (oxonium ion).

!!! tip ""

    $$>CH-\overset{+}{C}< + H_2O &#8652; >CH-C(\overset{+}{O}H_2)<$$

* **Step 3: Deprotonation** to form the alcohol.

!!! tip ""

    $$>CH-C(\overset{+}{O}H_2)< + H_2O &rarr; >CH-C(OH)< + H_3O^+$$

* Since the more stable carbocation is formed in step 1, the –OH finally ends up on the more substituted carbon. This is why the reaction obeys Markovnikov's rule.

#### (ii) Hydroboration–oxidation

* Diborane, (BH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>, adds to alkenes to give trialkylboranes. These are oxidised to alcohols by hydrogen peroxide in the presence of aqueous sodium hydroxide.

!!! tip ""

    $$CH_3-CH=CH_2 + (H-BH_2)_2 &rarr; CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-BH_2$$

    $$CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-BH_2 \xrightarrow{CH_3-CH=CH_2} (CH_3-CH_2-CH_2)_2BH \xrightarrow{CH_3-CH=CH_2} (CH_3-CH_2-CH_2)_3B$$

    $$(CH_3-CH_2-CH_2)_3B \xrightarrow{H_2O,\ 3H_2O_2,\ \overset{-}{O}H} 3CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-OH + B(OH)_3$$

    $$(Propan-1-ol)$$

* Boron gets attached to the sp<sup>2</sup> carbon carrying the greater number of hydrogen atoms.
* As a result, the alcohol formed looks as if water has been added to the alkene **opposite to Markovnikov's rule** (anti-Markovnikov product).
* The yield of alcohol is excellent.

!!! note "Note"

    Compare the two methods for propene: acid catalysed hydration gives propan-2-ol, while hydroboration–oxidation gives propan-1-ol. Hydroboration–oxidation was first reported by H.C. Brown in 1959.

### 2. From Carbonyl Compounds

#### (i) Reduction of aldehydes and ketones

* Aldehydes and ketones are reduced to alcohols by adding hydrogen in the presence of a finely divided metal catalyst such as Pt, Pd or Ni (catalytic hydrogenation).
* They can also be reduced by sodium borohydride (NaBH<sub>4</sub>) or lithium aluminium hydride (LiAlH<sub>4</sub>).
* Aldehydes give **primary** alcohols, while ketones give **secondary** alcohols.

!!! tip ""

    $$RCHO + H_2 \xrightarrow{Pd} RCH_2OH$$

    $$RCOR' \xrightarrow{NaBH_4} R-CH(OH)-R'$$

#### (ii) Reduction of carboxylic acids and esters

* Carboxylic acids are reduced to primary alcohols in excellent yields by LiAlH<sub>4</sub>, a strong reducing agent.

!!! tip ""

    $$RCOOH \xrightarrow{(i)\ LiAlH_4,\ (ii)\ H_2O} RCH_2OH$$

    The numbering (i), (ii) means the second reagent is added only after the reaction with the first is complete.

* LiAlH<sub>4</sub> is expensive, so it is used only for special chemicals. Commercially, acids are first converted to esters, which are then reduced by hydrogen in the presence of a catalyst.

!!! tip ""

    $$RCOOH \xrightarrow{R'OH,\ H^+} RCOOR' \xrightarrow{H_2,\ catalyst} RCH_2OH + R'OH$$

### 3. From Grignard Reagents

* Grignard reagents (RMgX) react with aldehydes and ketones to give alcohols.
* **Step 1:** Nucleophilic addition of the Grignard reagent to the carbonyl group forms an adduct. The alkyl group (R<sup>&delta;-</sup>) attacks the carbonyl carbon and the MgX part bonds to oxygen.
* **Step 2:** Hydrolysis of the adduct gives the alcohol.

!!! tip ""

    $$>C=O + R-MgX &rarr; >C(R)-OMgX\ \ (adduct)$$

    $$>C(R)-OMgX \xrightarrow{H_2O} >C(R)-OH + Mg(OH)X$$

The overall reactions with different carbonyl compounds are:

!!! tip ""

    $$HCHO + RMgX &rarr; RCH_2OMgX \xrightarrow{H_2O} RCH_2OH + Mg(OH)X$$

    $$RCHO + R'MgX &rarr; RCH(R')OMgX \xrightarrow{H_2O} RCH(R')OH + Mg(OH)X$$

    $$RCOR + R'MgX &rarr; R_2C(R')OMgX \xrightarrow{H_2O} R_2C(R')OH + Mg(OH)X$$

!!! tip "Conclusion"

    * Methanal (HCHO) gives a **primary** alcohol.

    * Other aldehydes give **secondary** alcohols.

    * Ketones give **tertiary** alcohols.

## Preparation of Phenols

* Phenol, also called **carbolic acid**, was first isolated from coal tar in the early nineteenth century. Today it is made synthetically.
* In the laboratory, phenols are prepared from benzene derivatives by the following methods.

### 1. From Haloarenes

* Chlorobenzene is fused with NaOH at 623 K and 320 atm pressure to form sodium phenoxide. Acidification of sodium phenoxide gives phenol.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_6H_5Cl + NaOH \xrightarrow{623\ K,\ 320\ atm} C_6H_5ONa \xrightarrow{HCl} C_6H_5OH$$

### 2. From Benzenesulphonic Acid

* Benzene is sulphonated with oleum to give benzenesulphonic acid.
* Benzenesulphonic acid is converted to sodium phenoxide by heating with molten NaOH. Acidification then gives phenol.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_6H_6 \xrightarrow{Oleum} C_6H_5SO_3H \xrightarrow{(i)\ NaOH,\ (ii)\ H^+} C_6H_5OH$$

### 3. From Diazonium Salts

* An aromatic primary amine is treated with nitrous acid (NaNO<sub>2</sub> + HCl) at 273–278 K to form a diazonium salt. Aniline, for example, gives benzene diazonium chloride.
* Diazonium salts are hydrolysed to phenols by warming with water or by treating with dilute acids.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_6H_5NH_2 \xrightarrow{NaNO_2 + HCl,\ 273-278\ K} C_6H_5\overset{+}{N_2}\overset{-}{Cl}$$

    $$C_6H_5\overset{+}{N_2}\overset{-}{Cl} + H_2O \xrightarrow{Warm} C_6H_5OH + N_2 + HCl$$

### 4. From Cumene

* Most of the world's phenol is manufactured from cumene (isopropylbenzene).
* Cumene is oxidised by air to **cumene hydroperoxide**, which is converted to phenol and acetone on treatment with dilute acid.
* Acetone, the by-product, is also obtained in large quantities by this method.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_6H_5CH(CH_3)_2 \xrightarrow{O_2} C_6H_5C(CH_3)_2-O-O-H$$

    $$C_6H_5C(CH_3)_2-O-O-H \xrightarrow{H^+,\ H_2O} C_6H_5OH + CH_3COCH_3$$
