In this article, we will solve some questions based on nomenclature, preparation, properties and reactions of alcohols, phenols and ethers.

## Classification and Nomenclature

!!! question "Classify the following as primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols, and identify the allylic alcohols: <br>(i) (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>C–CH<sub>2</sub>OH <br>(ii) H<sub>2</sub>C=CH–CH<sub>2</sub>OH <br>(iii) CH<sub>3</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>–OH <br>(iv) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>–CH(OH)–CH<sub>3</sub> <br>(v) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>–CH(OH)–CH<sub>3</sub> <br>(vi) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>–CH=CH–C(CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>–OH"

    * Primary alcohols: (i), (ii), (iii) — the carbinol carbon is bonded to only one carbon.

    * Secondary alcohols: (iv) and (v) — the carbinol carbon is bonded to two carbons.

    * Tertiary alcohol: (vi) — the carbinol carbon is bonded to three carbons.

    * Allylic alcohols: (ii) and (vi), since in both the –OH is on an sp<sup>3</sup> carbon next to a C=C double bond.

!!! question "Give IUPAC names of the following compounds: <br>(i) CH<sub>3</sub>–CH(Cl)–CH(CH<sub>3</sub>)–CH(CH<sub>3</sub>)–CH<sub>2</sub>OH <br>(ii) CH<sub>3</sub>–CH(CH<sub>3</sub>)–O–CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub> <br>(iii) Phenol with –CH<sub>3</sub> groups at both ortho positions <br>(iv) Cyclohexane with –OC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub> and –NO<sub>2</sub> on adjacent carbons"

    * (i) The chain is numbered from the –CH<sub>2</sub>OH end: **4-Chloro-2,3-dimethylpentan-1-ol**

    * (ii) The larger group (propane) is the parent; ethoxy is the substituent: **2-Ethoxypropane**

    * (iii) **2,6-Dimethylphenol**

    * (iv) Substituents are numbered in alphabetical order (ethoxy before nitro): **1-Ethoxy-2-nitrocyclohexane**

!!! question "Name the following according to the IUPAC system: <br>(i) CH<sub>3</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>–CH(CH<sub>2</sub>Cl)–CH(CH<sub>2</sub>OH)–CH(CH<sub>3</sub>)–CH<sub>3</sub> <br>(ii) (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>CH–CH<sub>2</sub>–CH(OH)–CH(CH<sub>3</sub>)–CH<sub>2</sub>OH <br>(iii) Cyclohexanol with Br at C-3 <br>(iv) H<sub>2</sub>C=CH–CH(OH)–CH<sub>2</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>–CH<sub>3</sub> <br>(v) CH<sub>3</sub>–C(CH<sub>3</sub>)=C(Br)–CH<sub>2</sub>OH"

    * (i) The longest chain containing the –CH<sub>2</sub>OH carbon is numbered from that carbon: **3-Chloromethyl-2-isopropylpentan-1-ol**

    * (ii) Both –OH groups lie on the main chain: **2,5-Dimethylhexane-1,3-diol**

    * (iii) **3-Bromocyclohexanol**

    * (iv) **Hex-1-en-3-ol**

    * (v) **2-Bromo-3-methylbut-2-en-1-ol**

!!! question "Give the structures and IUPAC names of monohydric phenols of molecular formula C<sub>7</sub>H<sub>8</sub>O."

    C<sub>7</sub>H<sub>8</sub>O as a monohydric phenol means C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>(CH<sub>3</sub>)OH. The –CH<sub>3</sub> can be ortho, meta or para to –OH:

    * o-CH<sub>3</sub>C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>OH: **2-Methylphenol** (o-cresol)

    * m-CH<sub>3</sub>C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>OH: **3-Methylphenol** (m-cresol)

    * p-CH<sub>3</sub>C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>OH: **4-Methylphenol** (p-cresol)

## Preparation

!!! question "Give the structures and IUPAC names of the products expected from: <br>(a) Catalytic reduction of butanal. <br>(b) Hydration of propene in the presence of dilute sulphuric acid. <br>(c) Reaction of propanone with methylmagnesium bromide followed by hydrolysis."

    $$(a)\ CH_3CH_2CH_2CHO + H_2 \xrightarrow{Pd} CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-OH\ \ (Butan-1-ol)$$

    $$(b)\ CH_3CH=CH_2 + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+} CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_3\ \ (Propan-2-ol)$$

    Markovnikov addition places –OH on the middle carbon.

    $$(c)\ CH_3COCH_3 + CH_3MgBr &rarr; (CH_3)_3C-OMgBr \xrightarrow{H_2O} (CH_3)_3C-OH$$

    The product is 2-methylpropan-2-ol (a ketone gives a tertiary alcohol).

!!! question "Show how the following alcohols are prepared by the reaction of a suitable Grignard reagent on methanal: <br>(i) (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>CH–CH<sub>2</sub>OH <br>(ii) Cyclohexylmethanol, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>11</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>OH"

    Methanal adds one –CH<sub>2</sub>OH carbon to the R group of RMgX, so R must be the rest of the molecule.

    $$(i)\ (CH_3)_2CH-MgBr + HCHO &rarr; (CH_3)_2CH-CH_2-OMgBr \xrightarrow{H_2O} (CH_3)_2CH-CH_2OH + Mg(OH)Br$$

    $$(ii)\ C_6H_{11}MgBr + HCHO &rarr; C_6H_{11}CH_2OMgBr \xrightarrow{H_2O} C_6H_{11}CH_2OH + Mg(OH)Br$$

!!! question "Write the products of the following reactions: <br>(i) CH<sub>3</sub>–CH=CH<sub>2</sub> with H<sub>2</sub>O/H<sup>+</sup> <br>(ii) Methyl 2-(2-oxocyclohexyl)ethanoate (cyclohexanone carrying a –CH<sub>2</sub>COOCH<sub>3</sub> group on C-2) with NaBH<sub>4</sub> <br>(iii) CH<sub>3</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>–CH(CH<sub>3</sub>)–CHO with NaBH<sub>4</sub>"

    * (i) CH<sub>3</sub>–CH(OH)–CH<sub>3</sub> (Markovnikov addition)

    * (ii) NaBH<sub>4</sub> reduces the ketone C=O to –CH(OH)– but does not reduce the ester group. The product is the cyclohexanol carrying the unchanged –CH<sub>2</sub>COOCH<sub>3</sub> group on the adjacent carbon.

    * (iii) The aldehyde is reduced to a primary alcohol: CH<sub>3</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>–CH(CH<sub>3</sub>)–CH<sub>2</sub>OH

!!! question "How are the following conversions carried out? <br>(i) Propene to propan-2-ol <br>(ii) Benzyl chloride to benzyl alcohol <br>(iii) Ethylmagnesium chloride to propan-1-ol <br>(iv) Methylmagnesium bromide to 2-methylpropan-2-ol"

    $$(i)\ CH_3CH=CH_2 + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+} CH_3CH(OH)CH_3$$

    $$(ii)\ C_6H_5CH_2Cl + aq\ NaOH &rarr; C_6H_5CH_2OH + NaCl$$

    $$(iii)\ C_2H_5MgCl + HCHO &rarr; CH_3CH_2CH_2OMgCl \xrightarrow{H_2O} CH_3CH_2CH_2OH$$

    $$(iv)\ CH_3MgBr + CH_3COCH_3 &rarr; (CH_3)_3COMgBr \xrightarrow{H_2O} (CH_3)_3COH$$

## Physical Properties and Acidity

!!! question "Arrange the following in increasing order of boiling points: <br>(a) Pentan-1-ol, butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, ethanol, propan-1-ol, methanol. <br>(b) Pentan-1-ol, n-butane, pentanal, ethoxyethane."

    * (a) Boiling point increases with molar mass; among isomers, the branched one (butan-2-ol) boils lower:

    $$Methanol < Ethanol < Propan-1-ol < Butan-2-ol < Butan-1-ol < Pentan-1-ol$$

    * (b) n-Butane is non-polar; ethoxyethane is weakly polar; pentanal has a stronger C=O dipole; pentan-1-ol has hydrogen bonding:

    $$n-Butane < Ethoxyethane < Pentanal < Pentan-1-ol$$

!!! question "Why does ethanol have a higher boiling point than methoxymethane, although both have molecular mass 46?"

    Ethanol has an O–H group, so its molecules are associated through intermolecular hydrogen bonding. Methoxymethane has no hydrogen attached to oxygen and cannot form hydrogen bonds between its own molecules. More energy is needed to separate ethanol molecules, so ethanol boils at 351 K while methoxymethane boils at 248 K.

!!! question "Arrange the following in increasing order of acid strength: <br>Propan-1-ol, 2,4,6-trinitrophenol, 3-nitrophenol, 3,5-dinitrophenol, phenol, 4-methylphenol."

    * An alcohol is the weakest acid. Electron releasing –CH<sub>3</sub> makes 4-methylphenol weaker than phenol.

    * Electron withdrawing –NO<sub>2</sub> groups increase acidity; the more nitro groups, and the more of them at ortho/para positions, the stronger the acid.

    $$Propan-1-ol < 4-Methylphenol < Phenol < 3-Nitrophenol < 3,5-Dinitrophenol < 2,4,6-Trinitrophenol$$

!!! question "Explain why ortho-nitrophenol is more acidic than ortho-methoxyphenol."

    * –NO<sub>2</sub> is an electron withdrawing group. It pulls electron density away from oxygen and delocalises the negative charge of the phenoxide ion onto itself, stabilising the ion. So o-nitrophenol ionises more easily.

    * –OCH<sub>3</sub> is an electron releasing group (by resonance). It increases the electron density on the ring and on the phenoxide oxygen, destabilising the phenoxide ion. So o-methoxyphenol is less acidic.

!!! question "While separating a mixture of ortho and para nitrophenols by steam distillation, which isomer will be steam volatile? Give reason."

    o-Nitrophenol is steam volatile. It has intramolecular hydrogen bonding between the –OH and the neighbouring –NO<sub>2</sub> group, so its molecules do not associate with each other. p-Nitrophenol has intermolecular hydrogen bonding, which associates its molecules and lowers its volatility.

## Reactions of Alcohols and Phenols

!!! question "Give the products when (i) butan-1-ol and (ii) 2-methylbutan-2-ol react with (a) HCl–ZnCl<sub>2</sub> (b) HBr and (c) SOCl<sub>2</sub>."

    In each case, –OH is replaced by a halogen.

    Reagent             | Butan-1-ol                                                       | 2-Methylbutan-2-ol
    --------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------
    HCl–ZnCl<sub>2</sub>| CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Cl (slow; no turbidity at room temperature) | CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>C(CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>Cl (immediate turbidity)
    HBr                 | CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Br       | CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>C(CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>Br
    SOCl<sub>2</sub>    | CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Cl       | CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>C(CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>Cl

!!! question "Predict the major product of acid catalysed dehydration of (i) 1-methylcyclohexanol and (ii) butan-1-ol."

    * (i) The tertiary carbocation loses a proton from the ring carbon to give the more substituted alkene: **1-Methylcyclohexene**.

    * (ii) Loss of water and a proton from C-2 gives **but-1-ene**.

    $$CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2OH \xrightarrow{H^+} CH_3CH_2CH=CH_2 + H_2O$$

!!! question "Write the mechanism of acid dehydration of ethanol to yield ethene."

    $$Step\ 1\ (fast):\ CH_3CH_2OH + H^+ &#8652; CH_3CH_2\overset{+}{O}H_2$$

    $$Step\ 2\ (slow):\ CH_3CH_2\overset{+}{O}H_2 &#8652; CH_3\overset{+}{C}H_2 + H_2O$$

    $$Step\ 3:\ CH_3\overset{+}{C}H_2 &#8652; CH_2=CH_2 + H^+$$

    Step 2 is the rate determining step. Ethene is removed as it forms to drive the equilibrium forward.

!!! question "Name the reagents used for: <br>(i) Oxidation of a primary alcohol to carboxylic acid <br>(ii) Oxidation of a primary alcohol to aldehyde <br>(iii) Bromination of phenol to 2,4,6-tribromophenol <br>(iv) Benzyl alcohol to benzoic acid <br>(v) Dehydration of propan-2-ol to propene <br>(vi) Butan-2-one to butan-2-ol"

    * (i) Acidified potassium permanganate (KMnO<sub>4</sub>/H<sup>+</sup>)

    * (ii) Pyridinium chlorochromate (PCC), or CrO<sub>3</sub> in anhydrous medium

    * (iii) Bromine water

    * (iv) Acidified KMnO<sub>4</sub>

    * (v) 85% H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub> at 440 K (or conc. H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>)

    * (vi) NaBH<sub>4</sub> or LiAlH<sub>4</sub> (or H<sub>2</sub> with Pt/Pd/Ni)

!!! question "Write the structures of the major products expected from: <br>(a) Mononitration of 3-methylphenol <br>(b) Dinitration of 3-methylphenol"

    Both –OH and –CH<sub>3</sub> are ortho, para directing; –OH is the stronger director. The nitro group enters positions that are ortho/para to –OH and not too crowded.

    * (a) A mixture of **3-methyl-4-nitrophenol** (–NO<sub>2</sub> para to –OH) and **5-methyl-2-nitrophenol** (–NO<sub>2</sub> ortho to –OH, on the side away from –CH<sub>3</sub>).

    * (b) **5-Methyl-2,4-dinitrophenol** (nitro groups at one ortho and the para position of –OH).

!!! question "Ortho and para nitrophenols are more acidic than phenol. Explain using the resonance structures of the corresponding phenoxide ions."

    * In o- and p-nitrophenoxide ions, the negative charge on oxygen is delocalised into the ring and onto the ortho and para carbons, just as in phenoxide ion.

    * Since the –NO<sub>2</sub> group sits on one of these carbons, the charge can move further onto the nitro group, giving an extra resonance structure in which the negative charge is on the oxygen of –NO<sub>2</sub> (–N<sup>+</sup>(O<sup>-</sup>)O<sup>-</sup>).

    * This extra delocalisation stabilises the o- and p-nitrophenoxide ions more than phenoxide ion, so o- and p-nitrophenols ionise more readily. In m-nitrophenoxide, the charge never reaches the carbon bearing –NO<sub>2</sub>, so only the weaker inductive effect operates, which is why m-nitrophenol (pK<sub>a</sub> 8.3) is less acidic than the o- and p-isomers.

!!! question "When 3-methylbutan-2-ol is treated with HBr, 2-bromo-2-methylbutane is formed. Give a mechanism for this reaction."

    $$Step\ 1:\ (CH_3)_2CH-CH(OH)-CH_3 + H^+ &rarr; (CH_3)_2CH-CH(\overset{+}{O}H_2)-CH_3$$

    $$Step\ 2:\ (CH_3)_2CH-CH(\overset{+}{O}H_2)-CH_3 &rarr; (CH_3)_2CH-\overset{+}{C}H-CH_3 + H_2O$$

    A secondary carbocation is formed. A hydride ion shifts from the neighbouring (3rd) carbon to give a more stable tertiary carbocation:

    $$Step\ 3:\ (CH_3)_2CH-\overset{+}{C}H-CH_3 &rarr; (CH_3)_2\overset{+}{C}-CH_2-CH_3$$

    $$Step\ 4:\ (CH_3)_2\overset{+}{C}-CH_2-CH_3 + Br^- &rarr; (CH_3)_2C(Br)-CH_2-CH_3$$

## Ethers

!!! question "The reaction C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>ONa + (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>C–Cl is not appropriate for preparing t-butyl ethyl ether. <br>(i) What would be the major product? <br>(ii) Write a suitable reaction for the preparation of t-butyl ethyl ether."

    * (i) The major product is **2-methylprop-1-ene**, (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>C=CH<sub>2</sub>. Sodium ethoxide is a strong base as well as a nucleophile, and with a tertiary halide elimination predominates over substitution.

    * (ii) Take the tertiary group from the alkoxide and use a primary halide:

    $$(CH_3)_3C-O^-Na^+ + CH_3CH_2Cl &rarr; (CH_3)_3C-OC_2H_5 + NaCl$$

!!! question "Write the reactions of Williamson synthesis of 2-ethoxy-3-methylpentane starting from ethanol and 3-methylpentan-2-ol."

    The secondary group comes from the alkoxide; ethanol is converted into the primary halide.

    $$CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)CH(OH)CH_3 + Na &rarr; CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)CH(ONa)CH_3 + {1 \over 2}H_2$$

    $$C_2H_5OH \xrightarrow{HBr} C_2H_5Br$$

    $$CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)CH(ONa)CH_3 + C_2H_5Br &rarr; CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)CH(OC_2H_5)CH_3 + NaBr$$

!!! question "Which of the following is an appropriate set of reactants for the preparation of 1-methoxy-4-nitrobenzene, and why? <br>(i) p-BrC<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>NO<sub>2</sub> + CH<sub>3</sub>ONa <br>(ii) p-NaOC<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>NO<sub>2</sub> + CH<sub>3</sub>Br"

    Set (ii) is appropriate. Williamson synthesis needs S<sub>N</sub>2 attack on an alkyl halide. In (ii), the phenoxide ion attacks CH<sub>3</sub>Br easily. In (i), the halogen is on an aryl (sp<sup>2</sup>) carbon, and aryl halides do not undergo S<sub>N</sub>2 substitution under these conditions because the C–Br bond has partial double bond character.

    $$p-O_2NC_6H_4ONa + CH_3Br &rarr; p-O_2NC_6H_4OCH_3 + NaBr$$

!!! question "Why is acid dehydration of secondary or tertiary alcohols not a suitable method for preparing ethers?"

    With secondary and tertiary alcohols, the reaction goes through a carbocation (S<sub>N</sub>1). Such carbocations readily lose a proton, so elimination competes strongly with substitution and alkenes are formed instead of ethers.

!!! question "Give the major products formed by heating each of the following ethers with HI: <br>(i) CH<sub>3</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>–CH(CH<sub>3</sub>)–CH<sub>2</sub>–O–CH<sub>2</sub>–CH<sub>3</sub> <br>(ii) CH<sub>3</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>–O–C(CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub> <br>(iii) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>–O–C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>"

    * (i) Both groups are primary; I<sup>-</sup> attacks the less hindered ethyl group (S<sub>N</sub>2):

    $$CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)CH_2OH + CH_3CH_2I$$

    * (ii) One group is tertiary, so the reaction is S<sub>N</sub>1 and the tertiary iodide forms:

    $$CH_3CH_2CH_2OH + CH_3CH_2C(CH_3)_2I$$

    * (iii) The aryl–O bond is not broken; the benzyl–O bond breaks:

    $$C_6H_5CH_2I + C_6H_5OH$$

!!! question "Predict the products of the following reactions: <br>(i) CH<sub>3</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>–CH<sub>2</sub>–O–CH<sub>3</sub> + HBr <br>(ii) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub> + HBr <br>(iii) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub> with conc. H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> / conc. HNO<sub>3</sub> <br>(iv) (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>C–OC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub> + HI"

    $$(i)\ CH_3CH_2CH_2OH + CH_3Br$$

    $$(ii)\ C_6H_5OH + C_2H_5Br$$

    $$(iii)\ o-O_2NC_6H_4OC_2H_5 + p-O_2NC_6H_4OC_2H_5$$

    $$(iv)\ (CH_3)_3C-I + C_2H_5OH$$

    In (i), Br<sup>-</sup> attacks the smaller methyl group (S<sub>N</sub>2); in (iv), the tertiary carbocation forms (S<sub>N</sub>1).
