## Nomenclature and Preparation

!!! question "Give the IUPAC names of:<br>(i) PhCH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>COOH<br>(ii) (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>C=CHCOOH<br>(iii) CH<sub>3</sub>CH(CH<sub>3</sub>)CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CHO<br>(iv) CH<sub>3</sub>COCH<sub>2</sub>COCH<sub>3</sub><br>(v) (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>CCH<sub>2</sub>COOH"

    * (i) The –COOH carbon is C-1 and the phenyl group is on C-3: **3-Phenylpropanoic acid**.

    * (ii) Numbering from –COOH, the double bond is at C-2 and a methyl group is on C-3: **3-Methylbut-2-enoic acid**.

    * (iii) Numbering from –CHO, the methyl group is on C-4: **4-Methylpentanal**.

    * (iv) A five carbon chain with C=O groups at C-2 and C-4: **Pentane-2,4-dione**.

    * (v) Numbering from –COOH, three methyl groups are on C-3: **3,3-Dimethylbutanoic acid**.

!!! question "Name the reagents used to bring about the following transformations:<br>(i) Hexan-1-ol to hexanal<br>(ii) Cyclohexanol to cyclohexanone<br>(iii) p-Fluorotoluene to p-fluorobenzaldehyde<br>(iv) Ethanenitrile to ethanal<br>(v) Allyl alcohol to propenal<br>(vi) But-2-ene to ethanal"

    * (i) Pyridinium chlorochromate, C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sup>+</sup>CrO<sub>3</sub>Cl<sup>-</sup> (PCC), which stops the oxidation at the aldehyde stage.

    * (ii) K<sub>2</sub>Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub> in acidic medium.

    * (iii) CrO<sub>3</sub> in acetic anhydride followed by hydrolysis, or 1. CrO<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub> 2. H<sub>2</sub>O (Etard reaction).

    * (iv) Diisobutylaluminium hydride (DIBAL-H) followed by hydrolysis.

    * (v) PCC (it does not attack the C=C bond).

    * (vi) O<sub>3</sub> followed by H<sub>2</sub>O/Zn dust (ozonolysis).

    $$CH_3CH=CHCH_3 \xrightarrow{1.\ O_3\ \ 2.\ Zn/H_2O} 2CH_3CHO$$

!!! question "Write the products of the following reactions:<br>(i) Benzene + C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>COCl in presence of anhyd. AlCl<sub>3</sub>/CS<sub>2</sub><br>(ii) (C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>2</sub>Cd + 2CH<sub>3</sub>COCl<br>(iii) CH<sub>3</sub>C&equiv;CH with Hg<sup>2+</sup>, H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub><br>(iv) p-Nitrotoluene with 1. CrO<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub> 2. H<sub>3</sub>O<sup>+</sup>"

    * (i) Friedel-Crafts acylation gives propiophenone:

    $$C_6H_6 + C_2H_5COCl \xrightarrow{anhyd.\ AlCl_3} C_6H_5COC_2H_5 + HCl$$

    * (ii) The dialkylcadmium gives a ketone, 1-phenylpropan-2-one:

    $$(C_6H_5CH_2)_2Cd + 2CH_3COCl &rarr; 2C_6H_5CH_2COCH_3 + CdCl_2$$

    * (iii) Hydration of propyne gives acetone:

    $$CH_3C \equiv CH + H_2O \xrightarrow{Hg^{2+},\ H_2SO_4} CH_3COCH_3$$

    * (iv) The Etard reaction gives 4-nitrobenzaldehyde, p-NO<sub>2</sub>C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>CHO.

!!! question "Write chemical reactions to bring about the following transformations:<br>(i) Butan-1-ol to butanoic acid<br>(ii) Benzyl alcohol to phenylethanoic acid<br>(iii) 3-Nitrobromobenzene to 3-nitrobenzoic acid<br>(iv) 4-Methylacetophenone to benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid<br>(v) Cyclohexene to hexane-1,6-dioic acid<br>(vi) Butanal to butanoic acid"

    $$(i)\ CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2OH \xrightarrow{CrO_3-H_2SO_4\ (Jones\ reagent)} CH_3CH_2CH_2COOH$$

    $$(ii)\ C_6H_5CH_2OH \xrightarrow{HBr} C_6H_5CH_2Br \xrightarrow{KCN} C_6H_5CH_2CN \xrightarrow[&Delta;]{H_3O^+} C_6H_5CH_2COOH$$

    $$(iii)\ m-O_2NC_6H_4Br \xrightarrow{Mg,\ ether} m-O_2NC_6H_4MgBr \xrightarrow{CO_2\ (dry\ ice)} m-O_2NC_6H_4COOMgBr \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} m-O_2NC_6H_4COOH$$

    $$(iv)\ p-CH_3C_6H_4COCH_3 \xrightarrow{KMnO_4/KOH} p-KOOCC_6H_4COOK \xrightarrow{dil.\ H_2SO_4} p-HOOCC_6H_4COOH$$

    The product in (iv) is terephthalic acid. Both side chains are oxidised completely to –COOH.

    $$(v)\ Cyclohexene \xrightarrow[Heat]{KMnO_4-H_2SO_4} HOOC(CH_2)_4COOH\ (Adipic\ acid)$$

    $$(vi)\ CH_3CH_2CH_2CHO \xrightarrow{Tollens'\ reagent} CH_3CH_2CH_2COOH$$

!!! question "Show how each of the following compounds can be converted to benzoic acid:<br>(i) Ethylbenzene (ii) Acetophenone (iii) Bromobenzene (iv) Phenylethene (Styrene)"

    $$(i)\ C_6H_5CH_2CH_3 \xrightarrow{KMnO_4-KOH} C_6H_5COOK \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} C_6H_5COOH$$

    $$(ii)\ C_6H_5COCH_3 \xrightarrow{KMnO_4-KOH} C_6H_5COOK \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} C_6H_5COOH$$

    $$(iii)\ C_6H_5Br \xrightarrow{Mg,\ ether} C_6H_5MgBr \xrightarrow{CO_2} C_6H_5COOMgBr \xrightarrow{H_2O} C_6H_5COOH$$

    $$(iv)\ C_6H_5CH=CH_2 \xrightarrow{KMnO_4-KOH} C_6H_5COOK + HCOOK \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} C_6H_5COOH$$

## Physical Properties and Reactivity

!!! question "Arrange the following in increasing order of boiling points:<br>CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CHO, CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>OH, H<sub>5</sub>C<sub>2</sub>–O–C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>, CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub>"

    * All four compounds have molecular masses in the range 72 to 74.

    * n-Pentane has only weak van der Waals forces, so it boils lowest.

    * Butanal is more polar than ethoxyethane, so dipole-dipole attraction is stronger in butanal.

    * Only butan-1-ol has extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding, so it boils highest.

    $$CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2CH_3 < H_5C_2-O-C_2H_5 < CH_3CH_2CH_2CHO < CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2OH$$

!!! question "Arrange in increasing order of boiling points: CH<sub>3</sub>CHO, CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>OH, CH<sub>3</sub>OCH<sub>3</sub>, CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub>"

    Propane is non-polar, dimethyl ether is weakly polar, ethanal has a strongly polar C=O group, and ethanol forms hydrogen bonds.

    $$CH_3CH_2CH_3 < CH_3OCH_3 < CH_3CHO < CH_3CH_2OH$$

!!! question "Arrange in increasing order of reactivity in nucleophilic addition reactions:<br>(i) Ethanal, Propanal, Propanone, Butanone<br>(ii) Benzaldehyde, p-Tolualdehyde, p-Nitrobenzaldehyde, Acetophenone"

    * (i) Ketones are less reactive than aldehydes. Among the ketones, the larger ethyl group in butanone gives more steric hindrance and more electron release. Among the aldehydes, ethanal has the smaller alkyl group.

    $$Butanone < Propanone < Propanal < Ethanal$$

    * (ii) Acetophenone is a ketone, so it is least reactive. The –CH<sub>3</sub> group in p-tolualdehyde is electron releasing and reduces the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon, while the –NO<sub>2</sub> group in p-nitrobenzaldehyde is electron withdrawing and increases it.

    $$Acetophenone < p-Tolualdehyde < Benzaldehyde < p-Nitrobenzaldehyde$$

!!! question "Arrange in increasing order of reactivity towards HCN: Acetaldehyde, Acetone, Di-tert-butyl ketone, Methyl tert-butyl ketone"

    Reactivity falls as the groups on the carbonyl carbon become bulkier and more electron releasing.

    $$Di-tert-butyl\ ketone < Methyl\ tert-butyl\ ketone < Acetone < Acetaldehyde$$

!!! question "Predict the products:<br>(i) Cyclopentanone + HO–NH<sub>2</sub> (H<sup>+</sup>)<br>(ii) Cyclohexanone + 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine<br>(iii) R–CH=CH–CHO + NH<sub>2</sub>CONHNH<sub>2</sub> (H<sup>+</sup>)<br>(iv) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>COCH<sub>3</sub> + CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>NH<sub>2</sub> (H<sup>+</sup>)"

    In each case the –NH<sub>2</sub> group adds to C=O and water is eliminated to form >C=N–.

    * (i) Cyclopentanone oxime: (CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>4</sub>C=N–OH

    * (ii) Cyclohexanone 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone: (CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>5</sub>C=N–NH–C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>3</sub>(NO<sub>2</sub>)<sub>2</sub>

    * (iii) Semicarbazone: R–CH=CH–CH=N–NH–CO–NH<sub>2</sub>

    * (iv) Schiff's base: C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>C(CH<sub>3</sub>)=N–CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub>

!!! question "There are two –NH<sub>2</sub> groups in semicarbazide. However, only one is involved in the formation of semicarbazones. Why?"

    In semicarbazide, H<sub>2</sub>N–CO–NH–NH<sub>2</sub>, the lone pair of the –NH<sub>2</sub> group attached to C=O is delocalised into the carbonyl group by resonance:

    $$H_2N-C(=O)-NH-NH_2 \longleftrightarrow H_2\overset{+}{N}=C(-O^-)-NH-NH_2$$

    So this –NH<sub>2</sub> group is not nucleophilic. Only the terminal –NH<sub>2</sub> group (attached to NH) has a free lone pair and attacks the carbonyl carbon.

!!! question "Cyclohexanone forms cyanohydrin in good yield but 2,2,6-trimethylcyclohexanone does not. Why?"

    In 2,2,6-trimethylcyclohexanone, the three methyl groups on the carbons next to C=O cause strong steric hindrance. The CN<sup>-</sup> ion cannot easily approach the carbonyl carbon, so the cyanohydrin is formed in poor yield. Cyclohexanone has no such hindrance.

!!! question "During the preparation of esters from a carboxylic acid and an alcohol in the presence of an acid catalyst, the water or the ester should be removed as soon as it is formed. Why?"

    Esterification is reversible:

    $$RCOOH + R'OH \overset{H^+}{&#8652;} RCOOR' + H_2O$$

    By Le Chatelier's principle, removing a product (water or ester) shifts the equilibrium forward, increasing the yield of the ester and preventing its hydrolysis back to acid and alcohol.

## Aldol and Cannizzaro Reactions

!!! question "Which of the following would undergo aldol condensation, which the Cannizzaro reaction and which neither?<br>(i) Methanal (ii) 2-Methylpentanal (iii) Benzaldehyde (iv) Benzophenone (v) Cyclohexanone (vi) 1-Phenylpropanone (vii) Phenylacetaldehyde (viii) Butan-1-ol (ix) 2,2-Dimethylbutanal"

    * **Aldol condensation** (have α-hydrogen): 2-methylpentanal, cyclohexanone, 1-phenylpropanone, phenylacetaldehyde.

    * **Cannizzaro reaction** (aldehydes with no α-hydrogen): methanal, benzaldehyde, 2,2-dimethylbutanal.

    * **Neither**: benzophenone (a ketone with no α-hydrogen) and butan-1-ol (not a carbonyl compound).

    $$2HCHO \xrightarrow{conc.\ NaOH} CH_3OH + HCOONa$$

    $$2CH_3CH_2C(CH_3)_2CHO \xrightarrow{conc.\ NaOH} CH_3CH_2C(CH_3)_2CH_2OH + CH_3CH_2C(CH_3)_2COONa$$

    $$2C_6H_5CH_2CHO \xrightarrow{dil.\ NaOH} C_6H_5CH_2CH(OH)CH(C_6H_5)CHO \xrightarrow[-H_2O]{&Delta;} C_6H_5CH_2CH=C(C_6H_5)CHO$$

!!! question "How will you convert ethanal into (i) Butane-1,3-diol (ii) But-2-enal (iii) But-2-enoic acid?"

    All three start with the aldol reaction of ethanal:

    $$2CH_3CHO \xrightarrow{dil.\ NaOH} CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CHO$$

    $$(i)\ CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CHO \xrightarrow{NaBH_4} CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CH_2OH$$

    $$(ii)\ CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CHO \xrightarrow[-H_2O]{&Delta;} CH_3CH=CHCHO$$

    $$(iii)\ CH_3CH=CHCHO \xrightarrow{Tollens'\ reagent} CH_3CH=CHCOOH$$

    Tollens' reagent is used in (iii) because it oxidises the –CHO group without attacking the C=C bond.

## Acidity of Carboxylic Acids

!!! question "Which acid of each pair would you expect to be stronger?<br>(i) CH<sub>3</sub>CO<sub>2</sub>H or CH<sub>2</sub>FCO<sub>2</sub>H<br>(ii) CH<sub>2</sub>FCO<sub>2</sub>H or CH<sub>2</sub>ClCO<sub>2</sub>H<br>(iii) CH<sub>2</sub>FCH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>2</sub>H or CH<sub>3</sub>CHFCH<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>2</sub>H<br>(iv) F<sub>3</sub>C–C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>–COOH or H<sub>3</sub>C–C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>–COOH (para)"

    * (i) **CH<sub>2</sub>FCO<sub>2</sub>H**: the electron withdrawing F stabilises the carboxylate ion.

    * (ii) **CH<sub>2</sub>FCO<sub>2</sub>H**: F is more electronegative than Cl, so its –I effect is stronger.

    * (iii) **CH<sub>3</sub>CHFCH<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>2</sub>H**: F is closer to the –COOH group, and the –I effect decreases with distance.

    * (iv) **F<sub>3</sub>C–C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>–COOH**: –CF<sub>3</sub> is strongly electron withdrawing, while –CH<sub>3</sub> is electron donating.

!!! question "Arrange in increasing order of acid strength:<br>(i) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH(Br)COOH, CH<sub>3</sub>CH(Br)CH<sub>2</sub>COOH, (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>CHCOOH, CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>COOH<br>(ii) Benzoic acid, 4-Nitrobenzoic acid, 3,4-Dinitrobenzoic acid, 4-Methoxybenzoic acid"

    * (i) The isopropyl group releases more electrons than the n-propyl group. Br is electron withdrawing, and its effect is stronger when it is closer to –COOH.

    $$(CH_3)_2CHCOOH < CH_3CH_2CH_2COOH < CH_3CH(Br)CH_2COOH < CH_3CH_2CH(Br)COOH$$

    * (ii) –OCH<sub>3</sub> is electron donating and decreases acidity, while –NO<sub>2</sub> groups are electron withdrawing and increase it. Two –NO<sub>2</sub> groups increase it more than one.

    $$4-Methoxybenzoic < Benzoic < 4-Nitrobenzoic < 3,4-Dinitrobenzoic$$

!!! question "Although the phenoxide ion has more resonating structures than the carboxylate ion, carboxylic acid is a stronger acid than phenol. Why?"

    * The two resonance structures of the carboxylate ion are **equivalent**, and in both the negative charge is on an electronegative oxygen atom.

    * In the phenoxide ion, the structures are **non-equivalent**, and in most of them the negative charge is on a less electronegative carbon atom. These contribute little to stability.

    * So the negative charge is delocalised more effectively over two oxygens in the carboxylate ion than over one oxygen and ring carbons in phenoxide. The carboxylate ion is more stable, and hence carboxylic acids are stronger acids than phenols.

## Identification Problems

!!! question "An organic compound (A), C<sub>8</sub>H<sub>8</sub>O, forms an orange-red precipitate with 2,4-DNP reagent and gives a yellow precipitate on heating with iodine and sodium hydroxide. It neither reduces Tollens' or Fehling's reagent, nor decolourises bromine water or Baeyer's reagent. On drastic oxidation with chromic acid, it gives a carboxylic acid (B), C<sub>7</sub>H<sub>6</sub>O<sub>2</sub>. Identify (A) and (B)."

    * (A) forms a 2,4-DNP derivative, so it is an aldehyde or a ketone. It does not reduce Tollens' or Fehling's reagent, so it is a **ketone**.

    * (A) gives the iodoform test, so it is a **methyl ketone**.

    * C<sub>8</sub>H<sub>8</sub>O is highly unsaturated, but (A) does not decolourise bromine water or Baeyer's reagent, so the unsaturation is due to an **aromatic ring**.

    * (B), C<sub>7</sub>H<sub>6</sub>O<sub>2</sub>, is benzoic acid. So (A) is a monosubstituted aromatic methyl ketone: **acetophenone**, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>COCH<sub>3</sub>.

    $$C_6H_5COCH_3 + H_2NNHC_6H_3(NO_2)_2 \xrightarrow{-H_2O} C_6H_5C(CH_3)=NNHC_6H_3(NO_2)_2\ (2,4-DNP\ derivative)$$

    $$C_6H_5COCH_3 \xrightarrow{NaOH/I_2} C_6H_5COONa + CHI_3 &darr;$$

    $$C_6H_5COCH_3 \xrightarrow{H_2CrO_4} C_6H_5COOH\ (B)$$

!!! question "An organic compound C<sub>9</sub>H<sub>10</sub>O forms a 2,4-DNP derivative, reduces Tollens' reagent and undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction. On vigorous oxidation, it gives 1,2-benzenedicarboxylic acid. Identify the compound."

    * It forms a 2,4-DNP derivative and reduces Tollens' reagent, so it is an **aldehyde**.

    * It undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction, so the –CHO group has no α-hydrogen. The –CHO group must be directly attached to the benzene ring.

    * Vigorous oxidation gives phthalic acid, so the ring has two substituents in ortho positions.

    * C<sub>9</sub>H<sub>10</sub>O minus C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub> and –CHO leaves C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>. So the compound is **2-ethylbenzaldehyde**, o-C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>CHO.

    $$o-C_2H_5C_6H_4CHO \xrightarrow{KMnO_4,\ vigorous} o-C_6H_4(COOH)_2$$

!!! question "An organic compound (A), C<sub>8</sub>H<sub>16</sub>O<sub>2</sub>, was hydrolysed with dilute sulphuric acid to give a carboxylic acid (B) and an alcohol (C). Oxidation of (C) with chromic acid produced (B). (C) on dehydration gives but-1-ene. Write the reactions involved."

    * (C) dehydrates to but-1-ene, so (C) is butan-1-ol.

    * Oxidation of (C) gives (B), so (B) is butanoic acid, with the same carbon skeleton.

    * (A) is the ester of these two: butyl butanoate, CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>COOCH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub> (C<sub>8</sub>H<sub>16</sub>O<sub>2</sub>).

    $$CH_3CH_2CH_2COOC_4H_9 + H_2O \xrightarrow{dil.\ H_2SO_4} CH_3CH_2CH_2COOH + CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2OH$$

    $$CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2OH \xrightarrow{H_2CrO_4} CH_3CH_2CH_2COOH$$

    $$CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2OH \xrightarrow[-H_2O]{conc.\ H_2SO_4,\ &Delta;} CH_3CH_2CH=CH_2$$

!!! question "An organic compound contains 69.77% carbon, 11.63% hydrogen and the rest oxygen. Its molecular mass is 86. It does not reduce Tollens' reagent but forms an addition compound with sodium hydrogensulphite and gives a positive iodoform test. On vigorous oxidation it gives ethanoic and propanoic acid. Write its possible structure."

    $$\%\ O = 100 - 69.77 - 11.63 = 18.60$$

    $$C : H : O = {69.77 \over 12} : {11.63 \over 1} : {18.60 \over 16} = 5.81 : 11.63 : 1.16 = 5 : 10 : 1$$

    $$Empirical\ formula = C_5H_{10}O,\ mass = 86$$

    $$&Therefore; Molecular\ formula = C_5H_{10}O$$

    * It does not reduce Tollens' reagent, so it is a ketone. It forms a bisulphite compound and gives the iodoform test, so it is a methyl ketone.

    * The possible methyl ketones are pentan-2-one and 3-methylbutan-2-one. Only pentan-2-one gives ethanoic acid and propanoic acid on oxidation:

    $$CH_3COCH_2CH_2CH_3 \xrightarrow{[O]} CH_3COOH + CH_3CH_2COOH$$

    So the compound is **pentan-2-one**.

## Distinguishing Tests

!!! question "Give simple chemical tests to distinguish between:<br>(i) Propanal and Propanone<br>(ii) Acetophenone and Benzophenone<br>(iii) Phenol and Benzoic acid<br>(iv) Benzoic acid and Ethyl benzoate<br>(v) Pentan-2-one and Pentan-3-one<br>(vi) Benzaldehyde and Acetophenone<br>(vii) Ethanal and Propanal"

    * (i) **Tollens' test**: propanal gives a silver mirror; propanone does not.

    * (ii) **Iodoform test**: acetophenone (C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>COCH<sub>3</sub>) gives a yellow precipitate of CHI<sub>3</sub>; benzophenone does not.

    * (iii) **NaHCO<sub>3</sub> test**: benzoic acid gives brisk effervescence of CO<sub>2</sub>; phenol does not. (Phenol gives a violet colour with neutral FeCl<sub>3</sub>.)

    * (iv) **NaHCO<sub>3</sub> test**: benzoic acid gives effervescence; ethyl benzoate does not.

    * (v) **Iodoform test**: pentan-2-one (a methyl ketone) gives a yellow precipitate; pentan-3-one does not.

    * (vi) **Tollens' test**: benzaldehyde gives a silver mirror; acetophenone does not. Alternatively, acetophenone gives the iodoform test while benzaldehyde does not.

    * (vii) **Iodoform test**: ethanal (CH<sub>3</sub>CHO) gives a yellow precipitate; propanal does not.
