# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids | Preparation of Aldehydes and Ketones

Aldehydes and ketones are prepared by oxidation or dehydrogenation of alcohols, from hydrocarbons, and by several named reactions. Some methods give both classes, while others are specific to aldehydes or to ketones.

## Methods for both Aldehydes and Ketones

### 1. By oxidation of alcohols

* Primary alcohols are oxidised to aldehydes and secondary alcohols are oxidised to ketones.
* To stop a primary alcohol at the aldehyde stage, a mild oxidising agent such as pyridinium chlorochromate (PCC, C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sup>+</sup>CrO<sub>3</sub>Cl<sup>-</sup>) is used. Secondary alcohols can be oxidised with K<sub>2</sub>Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub> in acidic medium.

!!! tip ""

    $$RCH_2OH \xrightarrow{[O]} RCHO$$

    $$R_2CHOH \xrightarrow{[O]} R_2C=O$$

### 2. By dehydrogenation of alcohols

* Vapours of the alcohol are passed over a heavy metal catalyst (Ag or Cu).
* Primary alcohols give aldehydes and secondary alcohols give ketones.
* This method suits volatile alcohols and is used industrially.

!!! tip ""

    $$RCH_2OH \xrightarrow{Cu,\ 573\ K} RCHO + H_2$$

    $$R_2CHOH \xrightarrow{Cu,\ 573\ K} R_2CO + H_2$$

### 3. From hydrocarbons

#### (i) Ozonolysis of alkenes

* Alkenes react with ozone to form ozonides, which on treatment with zinc dust and water give aldehydes, ketones or a mixture of both, depending on how the alkene is substituted.

!!! tip ""

    $$CH_3CH=CHCH_3 \xrightarrow{1.\ O_3\ \ 2.\ Zn/H_2O} 2CH_3CHO$$

#### (ii) Hydration of alkynes

* Water adds to alkynes in the presence of H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> and HgSO<sub>4</sub>.
* Ethyne gives acetaldehyde; all other alkynes give ketones.

!!! tip ""

    $$HC \equiv CH + H_2O \xrightarrow{HgSO_4,\ H_2SO_4} CH_3CHO$$

    $$CH_3C \equiv CH + H_2O \xrightarrow{Hg^{2+},\ H_2SO_4} CH_3COCH_3$$

## Preparation of Aldehydes

### 1. From acyl chlorides: Rosenmund reduction

* An acyl chloride (acid chloride) is hydrogenated over palladium supported on barium sulphate. The BaSO<sub>4</sub> poisons the catalyst so that the aldehyde is not reduced further to an alcohol.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_6H_5COCl + H_2 \xrightarrow{Pd-BaSO_4} C_6H_5CHO + HCl$$

    $$Benzoyl\ chloride \qquad \qquad Benzaldehyde$$

### 2. From nitriles and esters

#### (i) Stephen reaction

* Nitriles are reduced by stannous chloride and hydrochloric acid to an imine, which on hydrolysis gives the aldehyde.

!!! tip ""

    $$RCN + SnCl_2 + HCl &rarr; RCH=NH \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} RCHO$$

#### (ii) Reduction with DIBAL-H

* Diisobutylaluminium hydride, AlH(i-Bu)<sub>2</sub> (DIBAL-H), selectively reduces nitriles to imines, which are hydrolysed to aldehydes.
* It does not affect a C=C double bond present in the molecule.
* Esters are also reduced to aldehydes by DIBAL-H.

!!! tip ""

    $$RCN \xrightarrow{1.\ AlH(i-Bu)_2\ \ 2.\ H_2O} RCHO$$

    $$CH_3-CH=CH-CH_2CH_2-CN \xrightarrow{1.\ AlH(i-Bu)_2\ \ 2.\ H_2O} CH_3-CH=CH-CH_2CH_2-CHO$$

    $$CH_3(CH_2)_9COOC_2H_5 \xrightarrow{1.\ DIBAL-H\ \ 2.\ H_2O} CH_3(CH_2)_9CHO$$

### 3. From hydrocarbons (aromatic aldehydes)

Strong oxidising agents oxidise toluene all the way to benzoic acid. To stop at benzaldehyde, reagents are used that convert the –CH<sub>3</sub> group into an intermediate that resists further oxidation.

#### (i) Etard reaction

* Chromyl chloride (CrO<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub>) in CS<sub>2</sub> oxidises the methyl group of toluene to a chromium complex, which on hydrolysis gives benzaldehyde.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_6H_5CH_3 + CrO_2Cl_2 \xrightarrow{CS_2} C_6H_5CH(OCrOHCl_2)_2 \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} C_6H_5CHO$$

    $$Toluene \qquad \qquad Chromium\ complex \qquad \qquad Benzaldehyde$$

#### (ii) Using chromic oxide

* Toluene (or a substituted toluene) with chromic oxide (CrO<sub>3</sub>) in acetic anhydride at 273–283 K gives benzylidene diacetate, which is hydrolysed by aqueous acid to benzaldehyde.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_6H_5CH_3 + CrO_3 + (CH_3CO)_2O \xrightarrow{273-283\ K} C_6H_5CH(OCOCH_3)_2 \xrightarrow[&Delta;]{H_3O^+} C_6H_5CHO$$

#### (iii) Side chain chlorination followed by hydrolysis

* Side chain chlorination of toluene gives benzal chloride, which on hydrolysis at 373 K gives benzaldehyde. This is a commercial method for making benzaldehyde.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_6H_5CH_3 \xrightarrow{Cl_2/h\nu} C_6H_5CHCl_2 \xrightarrow{H_2O,\ 373\ K} C_6H_5CHO$$

    $$Toluene \qquad \qquad Benzal\ chloride \qquad \qquad Benzaldehyde$$

#### (iv) Gatterman-Koch reaction

* Benzene or its derivative, when treated with carbon monoxide and hydrogen chloride in the presence of anhydrous AlCl<sub>3</sub> or CuCl, gives benzaldehyde or a substituted benzaldehyde.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_6H_6 \xrightarrow{CO,\ HCl,\ anhyd.\ AlCl_3/CuCl} C_6H_5CHO$$

## Preparation of Ketones

### 1. From acyl chlorides

* A dialkylcadmium is first prepared by reacting cadmium chloride with a Grignard reagent.
* Treating an acyl chloride with the dialkylcadmium gives a ketone.

!!! tip ""

    $$2R-Mg-X + CdCl_2 &rarr; R_2Cd + 2Mg(X)Cl$$

    $$2R'-COCl + R_2Cd &rarr; 2R'-CO-R + CdCl_2$$

### 2. From nitriles

* A nitrile reacts with a Grignard reagent to give an imine salt, which on hydrolysis yields a ketone.

!!! tip ""

    $$CH_3CH_2C \equiv N + C_6H_5MgBr \xrightarrow{ether} CH_3CH_2C(=NMgBr)C_6H_5 \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} C_2H_5COC_6H_5$$

    $$Propiophenone\ (1-Phenylpropanone)$$

### 3. From benzene or substituted benzenes: Friedel-Crafts acylation

* Benzene or a substituted benzene reacts with an acid chloride in the presence of anhydrous AlCl<sub>3</sub> to give the corresponding aryl ketone.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_6H_6 + Ar/R-COCl \xrightarrow{anhyd.\ AlCl_3} C_6H_5-CO-Ar/R$$
