## Questions

!!! question "Explain the terms polymer and monomer."

    * A **polymer** is a very large molecule of high molecular mass (10<sup>3</sup>-10<sup>7</sup> u) made of a large number of repeating structural units joined by covalent bonds. Examples: polythene, bakelite, rubber, nylon 6,6.
    * A **monomer** is the simple, reactive molecule from which these repeating units are derived. Example: ethene is the monomer of polythene.

!!! question "What are natural and synthetic polymers? Give two examples of each type."

    * **Natural polymers** are found in plants and animals. Examples: proteins, cellulose, starch, natural rubber.
    * **Synthetic polymers** are man-made. Examples: polythene, nylon 6,6, Buna-S, terylene.

!!! question "Distinguish between the terms homopolymer and copolymer and give an example of each."

    * A **homopolymer** is formed from a single type of monomer. Example: polythene from ethene, -(CH<sub>2</sub>-CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>n</sub>-.
    * A **copolymer** is formed from two or more different monomers, with units of each in the same chain. Example: Buna-S from 1,3-butadiene and styrene.

!!! question "How do you explain the functionality of a monomer?"

    The functionality of a monomer is the number of bonding sites (reactive functional groups or reactive bonds) present in it.

    * Ethene, ethylene glycol (HOCH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>OH) and adipic acid are bi-functional.
    * Bi-functional monomers give linear polymers. Tri-functional monomers (for example phenol, with three reactive ortho/para positions, or melamine) can give cross linked polymers.

!!! question "Is -(NH-CHR-CO)<sub>n</sub>- a homopolymer or copolymer?"

    It is a **homopolymer**, because it is formed from a single monomer, the amino acid H<sub>2</sub>N-CHR-COOH. (If R varies from unit to unit, as in a natural protein, the chain becomes a copolymer.)

!!! question "How can you differentiate between addition and condensation polymerisation?"

    * In **addition polymerisation**, unsaturated monomers (alkenes, dienes) add to each other repeatedly without losing any molecule. The chain grows by adding monomers to a reactive end (free radical or ion). Examples: polythene, PVC, Teflon.
    * In **condensation polymerisation**, bi- or tri-functional monomers condense step by step with loss of small molecules such as H<sub>2</sub>O, CH<sub>3</sub>OH or HCl. Examples: nylon 6,6, terylene, bakelite.

!!! question "Write the free radical mechanism for the polymerisation of ethene."

    **Chain initiation:** Benzoyl peroxide breaks into phenyl radicals, which add to ethene.

    $$(C_6H_5COO)_2 &rarr; 2C_6H_5CO\dot{O} &rarr; 2\dot{C}_6H_5 + 2CO_2$$

    $$\dot{C}_6H_5 + CH_2=CH_2 &rarr; C_6H_5-CH_2-\dot{C}H_2$$

    **Chain propagation:** The radical keeps adding ethene molecules.

    $$C_6H_5-CH_2-\dot{C}H_2 + CH_2=CH_2 &rarr; C_6H_5-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-\dot{C}H_2$$

    $$&darr;$$

    $$C_6H_5-(CH_2-CH_2)_n-CH_2-\dot{C}H_2$$

    **Chain termination:** Two growing radicals combine.

    $$2\ C_6H_5-(CH_2-CH_2)_n-CH_2-\dot{C}H_2 &rarr; C_6H_5-(CH_2-CH_2)_n-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-(CH_2-CH_2)_n-C_6H_5$$

!!! question "Define thermoplastics and thermosetting polymers with two examples of each."

    * **Thermoplastics** are linear or slightly branched polymers that soften on heating and harden on cooling repeatedly. Examples: polythene, polystyrene, PVC.
    * **Thermosetting polymers** are cross linked or heavily branched polymers which, on heating in moulds, undergo extensive cross linking and become permanently hard and infusible. They cannot be reused. Examples: bakelite, urea-formaldehyde resin.

!!! question "Write the monomers used for getting the following polymers: <br>(i) Polyvinyl chloride (ii) Teflon (iii) Bakelite"

    (i) Vinyl chloride, CH<sub>2</sub>=CHCl

    (ii) Tetrafluoroethene, CF<sub>2</sub>=CF<sub>2</sub>

    (iii) Phenol, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH and formaldehyde, HCHO

!!! question "Write the name and structure of one of the common initiators used in free radical addition polymerisation."

    Benzoyl peroxide:

    $$C_6H_5-\overset{O}{\overset{||}{C}}-O-O-\overset{O}{\overset{||}{C}}-C_6H_5$$

!!! question "Write the names of monomers of the following polymers: <br>(i) -[NH-(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>6</sub>-NH-CO-(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>4</sub>-CO]<sub>n</sub>- <br>(ii) -[CO-(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>5</sub>-NH]<sub>n</sub>- <br>(iii) -(CF<sub>2</sub>-CF<sub>2</sub>)<sub>n</sub>-"

    (i) Hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid (nylon 6,6)

    (ii) Caprolactam (nylon 6)

    (iii) Tetrafluoroethene (Teflon)

!!! question "Classify the following as addition and condensation polymers: Terylene, Bakelite, Polyvinyl chloride, Polythene."

    * Addition polymers: polyvinyl chloride, polythene
    * Condensation polymers: terylene, bakelite

!!! question "Explain the difference between Buna-N and Buna-S."

    Both are synthetic rubbers made by addition copolymerisation of 1,3-butadiene.

    * **Buna-N** is a copolymer of 1,3-butadiene and **acrylonitrile**. It resists petrol, lubricating oil and organic solvents.
    * **Buna-S** is a copolymer of 1,3-butadiene and **styrene**. It is tough and used as a substitute for natural rubber, e.g. in autotyres.

!!! question "Arrange the following polymers in increasing order of their intermolecular forces. <br>(i) Nylon 6,6, Buna-S, Polythene <br>(ii) Nylon 6, Neoprene, Polyvinyl chloride"

    Elastomers have the weakest forces, thermoplastics intermediate, and fibres (hydrogen bonded) the strongest.

    (i) Buna-S (elastomer) < Polythene (thermoplastic) < Nylon 6,6 (fibre)

    (ii) Neoprene (elastomer) < Polyvinyl chloride (thermoplastic) < Nylon 6 (fibre)

!!! question "How does the presence of double bonds in rubber molecules influence their structure and reactivity?"

    * Natural rubber is cis-1,4-polyisoprene. The cis double bond in each unit bends the chain, so the chains cannot pack closely. They are held only by weak van der Waals forces and take up a coiled shape, which makes rubber elastic.
    * The double bonds (and the allylic positions next to them) are reactive sites. Sulphur forms cross links at these sites during vulcanisation, and oxidising agents also attack them.

!!! question "Discuss the main purpose of vulcanisation of rubber."

    Raw natural rubber becomes soft above 335 K and brittle below 283 K, absorbs a lot of water, dissolves in non-polar solvents and is easily oxidised. Heating it with sulphur (and an additive) at 373-415 K introduces sulphur cross links between chains. This makes rubber stiffer, stronger, more elastic over a wider temperature range and more resistant to solvents and oxidising agents.

!!! question "What are the monomeric repeating units of Nylon-6 and Nylon-6,6?"

    * Nylon 6: -[NH-(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>5</sub>-CO]- , derived from caprolactam.
    * Nylon 6,6: -[NH-(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>6</sub>-NH-CO-(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>4</sub>-CO]- , derived from hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid.

!!! question "Write the names and structures of the monomers of the following polymers: <br>(i) Buna-S (ii) Buna-N (iii) Dacron (iv) Neoprene"

    (i) Buna-S: 1,3-butadiene, CH<sub>2</sub>=CH-CH=CH<sub>2</sub> and styrene, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CH=CH<sub>2</sub>

    (ii) Buna-N: 1,3-butadiene, CH<sub>2</sub>=CH-CH=CH<sub>2</sub> and acrylonitrile, CH<sub>2</sub>=CH-CN

    (iii) Dacron: ethylene glycol, HOCH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>OH and terephthalic acid, HOOC-C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>-COOH (para)

    (iv) Neoprene: chloroprene (2-chloro-1,3-butadiene), CH<sub>2</sub>=C(Cl)-CH=CH<sub>2</sub>

!!! question "Identify the monomers in the following polymeric structures: <br>(i) -[CO-(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>8</sub>-CO-NH-(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>6</sub>-NH]<sub>n</sub>- <br>(ii) the melamine-formaldehyde polymer -[HN-C<sub>3</sub>N<sub>3</sub>(NH-)-NH-CH<sub>2</sub>]<sub>n</sub>-"

    (i) The -CO-(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>8</sub>-CO- part comes from decanedioic acid (sebacic acid), HOOC-(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>8</sub>-COOH, and the -NH-(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>6</sub>-NH- part from hexamethylenediamine, H<sub>2</sub>N-(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>6</sub>-NH<sub>2</sub>. (This polymer is nylon 6,10.)

    (ii) Melamine (2,4,6-triamino-1,3,5-triazine) and formaldehyde, HCHO.

!!! question "How is dacron obtained from ethylene glycol and terephthalic acid?"

    A mixture of ethylene glycol and terephthalic acid is heated at 420 to 460 K in the presence of a zinc acetate-antimony trioxide catalyst. The -OH groups and -COOH groups condense to form ester links, with loss of water.

    $$n\ HOCH_2CH_2OH + n\ HOOC-C_6H_4-COOH &rarr; -[OCH_2CH_2O-CO-C_6H_4-CO]_n- + (2n-1)H_2O$$

!!! question "What is a biodegradable polymer? Give an example of a biodegradable aliphatic polyester."

    A biodegradable polymer is one that can be broken down by microorganisms (bacteria) in the environment. Such polymers contain functional groups similar to those in biopolymers.

    Example of a biodegradable aliphatic polyester: **PHBV** (poly-&beta;-hydroxybutyrate-co-&beta;-hydroxyvalerate), a copolymer of 3-hydroxybutanoic acid and 3-hydroxypentanoic acid.
