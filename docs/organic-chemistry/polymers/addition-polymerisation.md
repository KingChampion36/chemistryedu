## Types of Polymerisation Reactions

There are two broad types of polymerisation reactions:

1. Addition or chain growth polymerisation
2. Condensation or step growth polymerisation

## Addition or Chain Growth Polymerisation

In addition or chain growth polymerisation, molecules of the same or different unsaturated monomers add together on a large scale to form a polymer, without the loss of any small molecule.

* The monomers are unsaturated compounds such as alkenes, alkadienes and their derivatives.
* The chain length increases (chain growth) through reactive intermediates, either **free radicals** or **ionic species**.
* Free radical addition polymerisation is the most common mode.

## Free Radical Mechanism

* Alkenes, dienes and their derivatives polymerise in the presence of a free radical generating **initiator** (catalyst) such as benzoyl peroxide, acetyl peroxide or tert-butyl peroxide.
* For example, polythene is made by heating (or exposing to light) ethene with a small amount of benzoyl peroxide.
* The mechanism has three steps: chain initiation, chain propagation and chain termination.

### 1. Chain initiation steps

* The weak O-O bond of benzoyl peroxide breaks homolytically to give benzoyloxy radicals, which lose CO<sub>2</sub> to form **phenyl radicals**.
* The phenyl radical adds to the C=C double bond of ethene, generating a new, larger free radical.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_6H_5-\overset{O}{\overset{||}{C}}-O-O-\overset{O}{\overset{||}{C}}-C_6H_5 &rarr; 2C_6H_5-\overset{O}{\overset{||}{C}}-\dot{O} &rarr; 2\dot{C}_6H_5 + 2CO_2$$

    $$Benzoyl\ peroxide\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ Phenyl\ radical$$

    $$\dot{C}_6H_5 + CH_2=CH_2 &rarr; C_6H_5-CH_2-\dot{C}H_2$$

### 2. Chain propagating step

* The new radical attacks another ethene molecule, forming a still bigger radical.
* Repetition of this step with bigger and bigger radicals carries the chain forward.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_6H_5-CH_2-\dot{C}H_2 + CH_2=CH_2 &rarr; C_6H_5-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-\dot{C}H_2$$

    $$&darr;$$

    $$C_6H_5-(CH_2-CH_2)_n-CH_2-\dot{C}H_2$$

### 3. Chain terminating step

* Finally, the growing radical meets another radical and the two combine, ending the chain. Radicals can terminate in different ways; one mode is combination of two chain radicals:

!!! tip ""

    $$2\ C_6H_5-(CH_2-CH_2)_n-CH_2-\dot{C}H_2 &rarr; C_6H_5-(CH_2-CH_2)_n-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-(CH_2-CH_2)_n-C_6H_5$$

    $$Polythene$$

!!! note "Note"

    Only a small amount of initiator is needed, because one initiating radical starts a chain that adds thousands of monomer units before it terminates.

## Preparation of Some Important Addition Polymers

### 1. Polythene

There are two types of polythene, made under very different conditions.

#### (a) Low density polythene (LDP)

* Made by polymerising ethene at a high pressure of **1000 to 2000 atm** and a temperature of **350 K to 570 K**, in the presence of traces of dioxygen or a peroxide initiator.
* It forms by free radical addition along with H-atom abstraction, which gives a **highly branched** structure. The branches prevent close packing, so the density is low.
* Properties: chemically inert, tough but flexible, and a poor conductor of electricity.
* Uses: insulation of electric wires, squeeze bottles, toys and flexible pipes.

#### (b) High density polythene (HDP)

* Made by polymerising ethene in a hydrocarbon solvent with a **Ziegler-Natta catalyst** (triethylaluminium and titanium tetrachloride) at **333 K to 343 K** and a pressure of **6-7 atm**.
* It consists of **linear** molecules which pack closely, giving a high density.
* Properties: chemically inert, tougher and harder than LDP.
* Uses: buckets, dustbins, bottles and pipes.

!!! note "Note"

    G. Natta (Italy) and Karl Ziegler (Germany) were awarded the Nobel Prize for Chemistry in 1963 for developing the Ziegler-Natta catalyst.

Property      | LDP                                     | HDP
--------------|-----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------
Conditions    | 1000-2000 atm, 350-570 K, O<sub>2</sub> or peroxide | 6-7 atm, 333-343 K, Ziegler-Natta catalyst
Structure     | Highly branched                         | Linear
Packing       | Loose (low density)                     | Close (high density)
Nature        | Tough, flexible                         | Tougher, harder
Uses          | Wire insulation, squeeze bottles, toys  | Buckets, dustbins, bottles, pipes

### 2. Polytetrafluoroethene (Teflon)

* Made by heating tetrafluoroethene with a free radical or persulphate catalyst at high pressure.

!!! tip ""

    $$n\ CF_2=CF_2 \xrightarrow[High\ pressure]{Catalyst} -(CF_2-CF_2)_n-$$

    $$Tetrafluoroethene\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ Teflon$$

* It is chemically inert and resists attack by corrosive reagents.
* Uses: oil seals, gaskets and non-stick coatings on utensils.
* Teflon coatings decompose at temperatures above 300 °C.

### 3. Polyacrylonitrile (PAN)

* Formed by addition polymerisation of acrylonitrile in the presence of a peroxide catalyst.

!!! tip ""

    $$n\ CH_2=CHCN \xrightarrow[Peroxide\ catalyst]{Polymerisation} -(CH_2-CH(CN))_n-$$

    $$Acrylonitrile\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ Polyacrylonitrile$$

* Used as a substitute for wool in commercial fibres such as **orlon** or **acrilan**.
* Acrylic fibres resist stains, chemicals, insects and fungi well.
