## Molecular Mass of Polymers

A polymer sample contains chains of varying lengths, so its molecular mass is always expressed as an average value.

* The properties of a polymer are closely related to its molecular mass, size and structure.
* During synthesis, how long a chain grows depends on how much monomer is available to it in the reaction mixture. So different chains in the same sample end up with different lengths.
* The average molecular mass of a polymer can be determined by chemical and physical methods.

## Biodegradable Polymers

* Many polymers strongly resist environmental degradation. Their solid waste accumulates and stays undegraded for a long time, causing serious environmental problems.
* To tackle this, new **biodegradable synthetic polymers** have been developed. They contain functional groups similar to those present in biopolymers, so microorganisms can break them down.
* **Aliphatic polyesters** are an important class of biodegradable polymers.

### 1. PHBV (Poly-&beta;-hydroxybutyrate-co-&beta;-hydroxyvalerate)

* Obtained by copolymerisation of **3-hydroxybutanoic acid** and **3-hydroxypentanoic acid**. The -OH of one unit forms an ester with the -COOH of the next.

!!! tip ""

    $$CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_2-COOH + CH_3-CH_2-CH(OH)-CH_2-COOH &rarr; -[O-CH(CH_3)-CH_2-CO-O-CH(CH_2CH_3)-CH_2-CO]_n-$$

    $$3-Hydroxybutanoic\ acid\ +\ 3-Hydroxypentanoic\ acid\ &rarr;\ PHBV$$

* PHBV undergoes bacterial degradation in the environment.
* Uses: speciality packaging, orthopaedic devices and controlled release of drugs.

### 2. Nylon-2-nylon-6

* An alternating polyamide copolymer of **glycine** (H<sub>2</sub>N-CH<sub>2</sub>-COOH) and **amino caproic acid** (H<sub>2</sub>N-(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>5</sub>-COOH).
* Glycine contributes a 2-carbon unit and amino caproic acid a 6-carbon unit, hence the name. It is biodegradable.

!!! tip ""

    $$n\ H_2N-CH_2-COOH + n\ H_2N-(CH_2)_5-COOH &rarr; -[NH-CH_2-CO-NH-(CH_2)_5-CO]_n- + (2n-1)H_2O$$

## Polymers of Commercial Importance

Besides the polymers already discussed, some other commercially important polymers are listed below.

Name of polymer             | Monomer(s)                                  | Structure (repeating unit)                                                  | Uses
----------------------------|---------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------
Polypropene                 | Propene                                     | -(CH<sub>2</sub>-CH(CH<sub>3</sub>))<sub>n</sub>-                           | Ropes, toys, pipes, fibres
Polystyrene                 | Styrene                                     | -(CH<sub>2</sub>-CH(C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>))<sub>n</sub>-               | Insulator, wrapping material, toys, radio and television cabinets
Polyvinyl chloride (PVC)    | Vinyl chloride                              | -(CH<sub>2</sub>-CH(Cl))<sub>n</sub>-                                       | Raincoats, hand bags, vinyl flooring, water pipes
Urea-formaldehyde resin     | (a) Urea (b) Formaldehyde                   | -(NH-CO-NH-CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>n</sub>-                                     | Unbreakable cups, laminated sheets
Glyptal                     | (a) Ethylene glycol (b) Phthalic acid       | -(OCH<sub>2</sub>-CH<sub>2</sub>OOC-C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>-CO)<sub>n</sub>- | Paints and lacquers
Bakelite                    | (a) Phenol (b) Formaldehyde                 | Phenol rings linked by -CH<sub>2</sub>- bridges (cross linked)              | Combs, electrical switches, handles of utensils, computer discs

In glyptal, phthalic acid is benzene-1,2-dicarboxylic acid, so the two -CO- groups are ortho to each other on the ring.
