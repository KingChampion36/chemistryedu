## Carbohydrates

!!! question "Glucose or sucrose are soluble in water but cyclohexane or benzene (simple six membered ring compounds) are insoluble in water. Explain."

    * Glucose has five -OH groups and sucrose has eight -OH groups.
    * These -OH groups form extensive **hydrogen bonds** with water molecules, so glucose and sucrose dissolve readily in water.
    * Cyclohexane and benzene are non-polar hydrocarbons with no -OH groups. They cannot form hydrogen bonds with water, so they are insoluble.

!!! question "What are the expected products of hydrolysis of lactose?"

    * Lactose is made of &beta;-D-galactose and &beta;-D-glucose joined by a C1-C4 glycosidic linkage. Hydrolysis breaks this linkage:

    $$\underset{Lactose}{C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}} + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+\ or\ lactase} \underset{&beta;-D-Galactose}{C_6H_{12}O_6} + \underset{&beta;-D-Glucose}{C_6H_{12}O_6}$$

!!! question "How do you explain the absence of aldehyde group in the pentaacetate of D-glucose?"

    * In the cyclic structure of glucose, the -OH at C-1 (anomeric carbon) is part of a hemiacetal. In glucose itself, this ring can open to give a free -CHO group in solution.
    * In the pentaacetate, the C-1 -OH is also acetylated. The ring can no longer open to form the open chain with a free -CHO group.
    * So glucose pentaacetate does not react with hydroxylamine, showing the absence of a free aldehyde group.

!!! question "What are monosaccharides? What are reducing sugars?"

    * **Monosaccharides** are carbohydrates that cannot be hydrolysed further to give simpler polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones. Examples: glucose, fructose, ribose.
    * **Reducing sugars** are carbohydrates that reduce Fehling's solution and Tollens' reagent. All monosaccharides, and disaccharides with a free aldehydic or ketonic group (e.g. maltose, lactose), are reducing sugars.

!!! question "Write two main functions of carbohydrates in plants."

    * **Storage**: plants store food as starch.
    * **Structure**: cellulose forms the cell wall of plant cells.

!!! question "Classify the following into monosaccharides and disaccharides:<br>Ribose, 2-deoxyribose, maltose, galactose, fructose and lactose."

    * **Monosaccharides**: ribose, 2-deoxyribose, galactose, fructose.
    * **Disaccharides**: maltose, lactose.

!!! question "What is glycogen? How is it different from starch?"

    * Glycogen is the storage carbohydrate of animals, also called **animal starch**. It is found in liver, muscles and brain, and also in yeast and fungi.
    * Starch is the storage carbohydrate of plants and is a mixture of amylose (unbranched, 15-20%) and amylopectin (branched, 80-85%).
    * Glycogen is similar in structure to amylopectin but is **more highly branched**.

!!! question "What are the hydrolysis products of (i) sucrose and (ii) lactose?"

    $$(i)\ Sucrose + H_2O &rarr; D-(+)-Glucose + D-(-)-Fructose$$

    $$(ii)\ Lactose + H_2O &rarr; &beta;-D-Galactose + &beta;-D-Glucose$$

!!! question "What is the basic structural difference between starch and cellulose?"

    * Starch is a polymer of **&alpha;-D-glucose**. Its amylose part is an unbranched C1-C4 chain and its amylopectin part is branched through C1-C6 linkages.
    * Cellulose is a **straight chain** polymer of **&beta;-D-glucose** units joined by C1-C4 glycosidic linkages.

!!! question "What happens when D-glucose is treated with the following reagents?<br>(i) HI (ii) Bromine water (iii) HNO<sub>3</sub>"

    * (i) On prolonged heating with HI, n-hexane is formed:

    $$CHO-(CHOH)_4-CH_2OH \xrightarrow{HI,\ \Delta} CH_3-(CH_2)_4-CH_3$$

    * (ii) Bromine water oxidises the -CHO group to give gluconic acid:

    $$CHO-(CHOH)_4-CH_2OH \xrightarrow{Br_2\ water} COOH-(CHOH)_4-CH_2OH$$

    * (iii) Nitric acid oxidises both the -CHO and the -CH<sub>2</sub>OH groups to give saccharic acid:

    $$CHO-(CHOH)_4-CH_2OH \xrightarrow{HNO_3} COOH-(CHOH)_4-COOH$$

!!! question "Enumerate the reactions of D-glucose which cannot be explained by its open chain structure."

    * Glucose does not give the 2,4-DNP test or Schiff's test, even though it has an aldehyde group.
    * Glucose does not form a hydrogensulphite addition product with NaHSO<sub>3</sub>.
    * Glucose pentaacetate does not react with hydroxylamine, showing no free -CHO group.
    * Glucose exists in two crystalline forms, &alpha; (m.p. 419 K) and &beta; (m.p. 423 K).

    These are explained by the cyclic hemiacetal structure formed by reaction of the C-5 -OH with the -CHO group.

## Proteins and Enzymes

!!! question "The melting points and solubility in water of amino acids are generally higher than that of the corresponding halo acids. Explain."

    * Amino acids exist as **zwitter ions** (H<sub>3</sub>N<sup>+</sup>-CHR-COO<sup>-</sup>). The strong electrostatic attraction between these dipolar ions gives them a salt-like character, so their melting points are high.
    * Being ionic, they are also strongly solvated by water, so they are highly soluble.
    * Halo acids do not form zwitter ions; they have only weaker dipole-dipole and hydrogen bonding interactions. So their melting points and solubility are lower.

!!! question "Where does the water present in the egg go after boiling the egg?"

    * On boiling, the egg white protein is **denatured** and coagulates.
    * The water present in the egg gets trapped in (absorbed by) the coagulated protein network, mainly through hydrogen bonding. So no free water is seen.

!!! question "What are essential and non-essential amino acids? Give two examples of each type."

    * **Essential amino acids** cannot be synthesised in the body and must be taken through the diet. Examples: valine, leucine.
    * **Non-essential amino acids** can be synthesised in the body. Examples: glycine, alanine.

!!! question "Define the following as related to proteins: (i) Peptide linkage (ii) Primary structure (iii) Denaturation."

    * (i) **Peptide linkage**: the amide bond (-CO-NH-) formed between the -COOH group of one amino acid and the -NH<sub>2</sub> group of another, with loss of a water molecule.
    * (ii) **Primary structure**: the specific sequence in which amino acids are linked in a polypeptide chain.
    * (iii) **Denaturation**: the loss of biological activity of a native protein when the hydrogen bonds are disturbed by a change in temperature or pH. The 2° and 3° structures are destroyed but the 1° structure remains intact.

!!! question "What are the common types of secondary structure of proteins? What type of bonding helps in stabilising the &alpha;-helix structure?"

    * The two common types are the **&alpha;-helix** and the **&beta;-pleated sheet**.
    * The &alpha;-helix is stabilised by **hydrogen bonds** between the -NH group of each amino acid residue and the >C=O group of an adjacent turn of the helix.

!!! question "Differentiate between globular and fibrous proteins."

    Fibrous proteins                                                     | Globular proteins
    ---------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------
    Polypeptide chains run parallel, held by hydrogen and disulphide bonds | Polypeptide chains coil around to give a spherical shape
    Fibre-like structure                                                 | Spherical structure
    Generally insoluble in water                                         | Usually soluble in water
    Examples: keratin, myosin                                            | Examples: insulin, albumins

!!! question "How do you explain the amphoteric behaviour of amino acids?"

    * In aqueous solution, amino acids exist as zwitter ions, which contain both an acidic group (-N<sup>+</sup>H<sub>3</sub>) and a basic group (-COO<sup>-</sup>).
    * With an acid, the -COO<sup>-</sup> group accepts a proton; with a base, the -N<sup>+</sup>H<sub>3</sub> group gives up a proton:

    $$H_3\overset{+}{N}-CHR-COOH \xleftarrow{H^+} H_3\overset{+}{N}-CHR-COO^- \xrightarrow{OH^-} H_2N-CHR-COO^-$$

    * Since they react with both acids and bases, amino acids are amphoteric.

!!! question "What are enzymes? What is the effect of denaturation on the structure of proteins?"

    * **Enzymes** are biocatalysts, almost all of which are globular proteins. They are highly specific and speed up reactions in living systems under mild conditions by lowering the activation energy.
    * On **denaturation**, the globules unfold and helices uncoil: the secondary and tertiary structures are destroyed, while the primary structure (amino acid sequence) remains intact. The protein loses its biological activity.

## Vitamins and Nucleic Acids

!!! question "Why cannot vitamin C be stored in our body?"

    * Vitamin C is **water soluble**. It is readily excreted in urine and therefore cannot be stored in the body. It must be supplied regularly through the diet.

!!! question "How are vitamins classified? Name the vitamin responsible for the coagulation of blood."

    * Vitamins are classified as **fat soluble** (A, D, E and K) and **water soluble** (B group and C).
    * **Vitamin K** is responsible for coagulation of blood. Its deficiency increases blood clotting time.

!!! question "Why are vitamin A and vitamin C essential to us? Give their important sources."

    * Deficiency of **vitamin A** causes xerophthalmia (hardening of the cornea) and night blindness. Sources: fish liver oil, carrots, butter and milk.
    * Deficiency of **vitamin C** causes scurvy (bleeding gums). Sources: citrus fruits, amla and green leafy vegetables.

!!! question "What products would be formed when a nucleotide from DNA containing thymine is hydrolysed?"

    * A DNA nucleotide consists of a base, the sugar and a phosphate group. On hydrolysis it gives:

    $$Thymine + &beta;-D-2-Deoxyribose + Phosphoric\ acid$$

!!! question "When RNA is hydrolysed, there is no relationship among the quantities of different bases obtained. What does this fact suggest about the structure of RNA?"

    * In DNA, A always pairs with T and C with G, so the amounts of these pairs are equal. This is a consequence of its double strand structure.
    * In RNA, no such relationship exists between the bases. This suggests that RNA has a **single strand** structure with no complementary base pairing between two strands.

!!! question "What are nucleic acids? Mention their two important functions."

    * Nucleic acids are long chain polymers of nucleotides (polynucleotides) present in the nucleus. They are of two types, DNA and RNA.
    * Functions:
        * DNA is the chemical basis of heredity and transfers characters from parents to offspring through self duplication.
        * Nucleic acids carry out protein synthesis in the cell (the message is in DNA; RNA actually synthesises the protein).

!!! question "What is the difference between a nucleoside and a nucleotide?"

    $$Nucleoside = Base + Sugar$$

    $$Nucleotide = Base + Sugar + Phosphate$$

    * A nucleoside is formed when a base is attached at the 1' position of the sugar.
    * A nucleotide is formed when a nucleoside is linked to phosphoric acid at the 5' position of the sugar.

!!! question "The two strands in DNA are not identical but are complementary. Explain."

    * The two strands of DNA are held together by hydrogen bonds between specific pairs of bases: adenine pairs only with thymine and cytosine pairs only with guanine.
    * So wherever one strand has A, the other has T; wherever one has C, the other has G. The sequence of one strand decides the sequence of the other, but the two sequences are not the same. Hence the strands are complementary, not identical.

!!! question "Write the important structural and functional differences between DNA and RNA. What are the different types of RNA found in the cell?"

    * **Structural differences**:
        * The sugar in DNA is &beta;-D-2-deoxyribose; in RNA it is &beta;-D-ribose.
        * DNA contains thymine (T); RNA contains uracil (U) instead. Both contain A, G and C.
        * DNA has a double strand helix structure; RNA is single stranded.
    * **Functional differences**:
        * DNA is the chemical basis of heredity and carries the coded message for protein synthesis.
        * RNA actually carries out protein synthesis in the cell.
    * **Types of RNA**: messenger RNA (m-RNA), ribosomal RNA (r-RNA) and transfer RNA (t-RNA).
