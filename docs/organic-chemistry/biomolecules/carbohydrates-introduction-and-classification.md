## Biomolecules

* A living system grows, sustains and reproduces itself, yet it is built entirely from non-living atoms and molecules.
* The study of the chemistry that goes on inside living systems is called **biochemistry**.
* Living systems are made up of complex **biomolecules** such as carbohydrates, proteins, nucleic acids and lipids. Simpler molecules like vitamins and mineral salts also play important roles.
* Proteins and carbohydrates are essential constituents of our food.

## Carbohydrates

* Carbohydrates are produced mainly by plants and form a very large group of naturally occurring organic compounds. Common examples are cane sugar, glucose and starch.
* Most of them have the general formula C<sub>x</sub>(H<sub>2</sub>O)<sub>y</sub>, so they were once thought to be "hydrates of carbon". This is where the name **carbohydrate** comes from.

!!! example "Example"

    Glucose, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub>, can be written as C<sub>6</sub>(H<sub>2</sub>O)<sub>6</sub>.

* However, this general formula is not a reliable definition:
    * Acetic acid, CH<sub>3</sub>COOH, fits the formula as C<sub>2</sub>(H<sub>2</sub>O)<sub>2</sub>, but it is **not** a carbohydrate.
    * Rhamnose, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>5</sub>, **is** a carbohydrate but does not fit the formula.
* Their reactions show that carbohydrates contain specific functional groups. So chemically, carbohydrates are defined as:

!!! tip "Definition"

    Carbohydrates are optically active polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or the compounds which produce such units on hydrolysis.

* Carbohydrates which are sweet in taste are called **sugars**. The sugar used at home is **sucrose**, and the sugar present in milk is **lactose**.
* Carbohydrates are also called **saccharides** (Greek *sakcharon* = sugar).

## Classification of Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are classified on the basis of their behaviour on hydrolysis into three groups.

### 1. Monosaccharides

* A carbohydrate that cannot be hydrolysed further to give a simpler polyhydroxy aldehyde or ketone is called a monosaccharide.
* About 20 monosaccharides occur in nature. Examples: glucose, fructose, ribose.

### 2. Oligosaccharides

* Carbohydrates that give **two to ten** monosaccharide units on hydrolysis are called oligosaccharides.
* They are further named disaccharides, trisaccharides, tetrasaccharides, etc., depending on the number of monosaccharide units they give. Disaccharides are the most common.
* The two units from a disaccharide may be the same or different:
    * Sucrose gives one molecule each of glucose and fructose.
    * Maltose gives two molecules of glucose only.

### 3. Polysaccharides

* Carbohydrates which give a **large number** of monosaccharide units on hydrolysis are called polysaccharides. Examples: starch, cellulose, glycogen, gums.
* Polysaccharides are not sweet in taste, so they are also called **non-sugars**.

## Reducing and Non-reducing Sugars

* Carbohydrates which reduce **Fehling's solution** and **Tollens' reagent** are called **reducing sugars**.
* All monosaccharides, whether aldoses or ketoses, are reducing sugars.
* In a disaccharide:
    * If the reducing groups (aldehydic or ketonic groups) of both monosaccharide units are used up in bonding, the sugar is **non-reducing**. Example: sucrose.
    * If one of these groups is still free, the sugar is **reducing**. Examples: maltose and lactose.

## Classification of Monosaccharides

* Monosaccharides are further classified by the **functional group** and the **number of carbon atoms** they contain.
* A monosaccharide with an aldehyde group is called an **aldose**; one with a keto group is called a **ketose**.
* The number of carbon atoms is also included in the name.

Carbon atoms | General term | Aldehyde     | Ketone
-------------|--------------|--------------|------------
3            | Triose       | Aldotriose   | Ketotriose
4            | Tetrose      | Aldotetrose  | Ketotetrose
5            | Pentose      | Aldopentose  | Ketopentose
6            | Hexose       | Aldohexose   | Ketohexose
7            | Heptose      | Aldoheptose  | Ketoheptose

!!! example "Examples"

    Glucose is an aldohexose (6 carbons, aldehyde group). Fructose is a ketohexose (6 carbons, keto group). Ribose is an aldopentose.

## Glucose

* Glucose occurs freely in nature as well as in combined form. It is present in sweet fruits and honey; ripe grapes contain glucose in large amounts.
* Glucose is an **aldohexose** and is also known as **dextrose**.
* It is the monomer of many larger carbohydrates such as starch and cellulose, and is probably the most abundant organic compound on earth.

### Preparation of Glucose

#### 1. From sucrose (cane sugar)

* When sucrose is boiled with dilute HCl or H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> in alcoholic solution, glucose and fructose are obtained in equal amounts.

!!! tip ""

    $$\underset{Sucrose}{C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}} + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+} \underset{Glucose}{C_6H_{12}O_6} + \underset{Fructose}{C_6H_{12}O_6}$$

#### 2. From starch

* Commercially, glucose is obtained by hydrolysis of starch by boiling it with dilute H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> at 393 K under pressure (2-3 atm).

!!! tip ""

    $$\underset{Starch\ or\ cellulose}{(C_6H_{10}O_5)_n} + nH_2O \xrightarrow[393\ K;\ 2-3\ atm]{H^+} \underset{Glucose}{nC_6H_{12}O_6}$$
