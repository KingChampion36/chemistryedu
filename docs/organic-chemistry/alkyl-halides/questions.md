In this article, we will solve some questions based on preparation, properties and reactions of haloalkanes and haloarenes.

## Preparation and Physical Properties

!!! question "Identify all the possible monochloro structural isomers expected to be formed on free radical monochlorination of (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>CHCH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub>."

    * The molecule has four different types of hydrogen atoms: the two equivalent CH<sub>3</sub> groups on C-2, the single H on C-2, the CH<sub>2</sub> hydrogens, and the terminal CH<sub>3</sub> hydrogens.
    * Replacing each type gives a different product:

    $$(CH_3)_2CHCH_2CH_2Cl$$

    $$(CH_3)_2CHCH(Cl)CH_3$$

    $$(CH_3)_2C(Cl)CH_2CH_3$$

    $$CH_3CH(CH_2Cl)CH_2CH_3$$

!!! question "Why is sulphuric acid not used during the reaction of alcohols with KI?"

    * H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> converts KI into HI, but being an oxidising agent, it then oxidises HI to I<sub>2</sub>. So no alkyl iodide is formed.

    $$2KI + H_2SO_4 &rarr; 2HI + K_2SO_4$$

    $$2HI + H_2SO_4 &rarr; I_2 + SO_2 + 2H_2O$$

    * A non-oxidising acid such as H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub> is used instead.

!!! question "Among the isomeric alkanes of molecular formula C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>12</sub>, identify the one that on photochemical chlorination yields: <br>(i) A single monochloride <br>(ii) Three isomeric monochlorides <br>(iii) Four isomeric monochlorides"

    * (i) **Neopentane**, C(CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>4</sub>: all 12 hydrogens are equivalent, so only one monochloride is formed.
    * (ii) **n-Pentane**, CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub>: it has three types of equivalent hydrogens (C-1/C-5, C-2/C-4 and C-3), so three monochlorides are formed.
    * (iii) **Isopentane**, (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>CHCH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub>: it has four types of hydrogens, so four monochlorides are formed.

!!! question "Write the major monohalo product in each of the following: <br>(i) Cyclohexanol + SOCl<sub>2</sub> <br>(ii) p-O<sub>2</sub>NC<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub> + Br<sub>2</sub>, heat or UV light <br>(iii) p-HOC<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>OH + HCl, heat <br>(iv) 1-Methylcyclohexene + HI <br>(v) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Br + NaI <br>(vi) Cyclohexene + Br<sub>2</sub>, heat or UV light"

    * (i) Chlorocyclohexane, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>11</sub>Cl.
    * (ii) p-O<sub>2</sub>NC<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>CH(Br)CH<sub>3</sub>. Free radical bromination occurs at the benzylic carbon, as the benzylic radical is most stable.
    * (iii) p-HOC<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Cl. Only the alcoholic -OH is replaced; the phenolic -OH is not, because its C-O bond has partial double bond character.
    * (iv) 1-Iodo-1-methylcyclohexane (Markovnikov addition: I goes to the more substituted carbon).
    * (v) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>I (Finkelstein reaction).
    * (vi) 3-Bromocyclohexene. Under heat or light, substitution occurs at the allylic position.

!!! question "Arrange each set of compounds in order of increasing boiling points: <br>(i) Bromomethane, Bromoform, Chloromethane, Dibromomethane <br>(ii) 1-Chloropropane, Isopropyl chloride, 1-Chlorobutane"

    * (i) Chloromethane < Bromomethane < Dibromomethane < Bromoform. Boiling point increases with increase in molecular mass (stronger van der Waals forces).
    * (ii) Isopropyl chloride < 1-Chloropropane < 1-Chlorobutane. Isopropyl chloride is branched, so it has a lower boiling point than 1-chloropropane. 1-Chlorobutane has the largest molecular mass.

!!! question "Which one of the following has the highest dipole moment? <br>(i) CH<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub> (ii) CHCl<sub>3</sub> (iii) CCl<sub>4</sub>"

    * CCl<sub>4</sub> is perfectly symmetrical (tetrahedral), so the four C-Cl bond dipoles cancel out and its dipole moment is zero.
    * In CHCl<sub>3</sub>, the C-Cl dipoles partly cancel each other.
    * In CH<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub>, the two C-Cl dipoles reinforce each other the most.
    * So, **CH<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub>** has the highest dipole moment.

!!! question "A hydrocarbon C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>10</sub> does not react with chlorine in dark but gives a single monochloro compound C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>9</sub>Cl in bright sunlight. Identify the hydrocarbon."

    * C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>10</sub> fits C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n</sub>, so it is either an alkene or a cycloalkane.
    * It does not react with Cl<sub>2</sub> in the dark, so there is no double bond. It must be a cycloalkane.
    * It gives only one monochloro product in sunlight, so all hydrogens are equivalent.
    * The hydrocarbon is **cyclopentane**, C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>10</sub>.

    $$C_5H_{10} + Cl_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu} C_5H_9Cl + HCl$$

!!! question "Write the equations for the preparation of 1-iodobutane from: <br>(i) 1-Butanol (ii) 1-Chlorobutane (iii) But-1-ene"

    $$(i)\ CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2OH + KI \xrightarrow{95\%\ H_3PO_4,\ &Delta;} CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2I$$

    $$(ii)\ CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2Cl + NaI \xrightarrow{dry\ acetone} CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2I + NaCl$$

    $$(iii)\ CH_3CH_2CH=CH_2 + HBr \xrightarrow{peroxide} CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2Br \xrightarrow{NaI,\ dry\ acetone} CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2I$$

    In (iii), anti-Markovnikov addition of HBr in presence of peroxide puts Br on the terminal carbon, which is then exchanged for I by the Finkelstein reaction.

## Substitution and Elimination

!!! question "Which compound in each of the following pairs will react faster in S<sub>N</sub>2 reaction with OH<sup>-</sup>? <br>(i) CH<sub>3</sub>Br or CH<sub>3</sub>I <br>(ii) (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>CCl or CH<sub>3</sub>Cl"

    * (i) **CH<sub>3</sub>I**, because I<sup>-</sup> is a better leaving group than Br<sup>-</sup> (C-I bond is weaker).
    * (ii) **CH<sub>3</sub>Cl**, because there is very little steric hindrance around the carbon, while in (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>CCl three bulky methyl groups block the backside attack.

!!! question "Predict the order of reactivity of the four isomeric bromobutanes in S<sub>N</sub>1 and S<sub>N</sub>2 reactions."

    $$S_N1:\ CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2Br < (CH_3)_2CHCH_2Br < CH_3CH_2CH(Br)CH_3 < (CH_3)_3CBr$$

    $$S_N2:\ CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2Br > (CH_3)_2CHCH_2Br > CH_3CH_2CH(Br)CH_3 > (CH_3)_3CBr$$

    * Of the two primary bromides, the carbocation from (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>CHCH<sub>2</sub>Br is more stable due to the greater electron donating inductive effect of the (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>CH- group. So it is more reactive in S<sub>N</sub>1.
    * CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH(Br)CH<sub>3</sub> is secondary and (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>CBr is tertiary, so they follow the order of carbocation stability in S<sub>N</sub>1.
    * S<sub>N</sub>2 follows the reverse order, because steric hindrance around the carbon increases in the same sequence.

!!! question "Predict the order of reactivity of C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Br, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CH(C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>)Br, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CH(CH<sub>3</sub>)Br and C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>C(CH<sub>3</sub>)(C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>)Br in S<sub>N</sub>1 and S<sub>N</sub>2 reactions."

    $$S_N1:\ C_6H_5C(CH_3)(C_6H_5)Br > C_6H_5CH(C_6H_5)Br > C_6H_5CH(CH_3)Br > C_6H_5CH_2Br$$

    $$S_N2:\ C_6H_5C(CH_3)(C_6H_5)Br < C_6H_5CH(C_6H_5)Br < C_6H_5CH(CH_3)Br < C_6H_5CH_2Br$$

    * Of the two secondary bromides, the carbocation from C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CH(C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>)Br is stabilised by resonance with two phenyl groups, so it is more reactive in S<sub>N</sub>1.
    * A phenyl group is bulkier than a methyl group, so C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CH(C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>)Br is less reactive than C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CH(CH<sub>3</sub>)Br in S<sub>N</sub>2.

!!! question "Which alkyl halide from the following pairs would react more rapidly by S<sub>N</sub>2 mechanism? <br>(i) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Br or CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH(Br)CH<sub>3</sub> <br>(ii) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH(Br)CH<sub>3</sub> or (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>CBr <br>(iii) (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>CHCH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Br or CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH(CH<sub>3</sub>)CH<sub>2</sub>Br"

    * (i) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Br. Being a primary halide, it has the least steric hindrance.
    * (ii) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH(Br)CH<sub>3</sub>. A secondary halide reacts faster than a tertiary halide.
    * (iii) (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>CHCH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Br. In the other compound, the methyl branch is closer to the carbon carrying Br, which increases steric hindrance and lowers the rate.

!!! question "Which compound in each pair undergoes faster S<sub>N</sub>1 reaction? <br>(i) 2-Chloro-2-methylpropane or 3-Chloropentane <br>(ii) 2-Chloroheptane or 1-Chlorohexane"

    * (i) 2-Chloro-2-methylpropane, (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>CCl. A tertiary halide reacts faster than a secondary one, because the 3<sup>0</sup> carbocation is more stable.
    * (ii) 2-Chloroheptane. It forms a secondary carbocation, which is more stable than the primary carbocation from 1-chlorohexane.

!!! question "Arrange the compounds of each set in order of reactivity towards S<sub>N</sub>2 displacement: <br>(i) 2-Bromo-2-methylbutane, 1-Bromopentane, 2-Bromopentane <br>(ii) 1-Bromo-3-methylbutane, 2-Bromo-2-methylbutane, 2-Bromo-3-methylbutane <br>(iii) 1-Bromobutane, 1-Bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane, 1-Bromo-2-methylbutane, 1-Bromo-3-methylbutane"

    S<sub>N</sub>2 reactivity decreases as steric hindrance near the carbon carrying Br increases.

    * (i) 1-Bromopentane (1<sup>0</sup>) > 2-Bromopentane (2<sup>0</sup>) > 2-Bromo-2-methylbutane (3<sup>0</sup>)
    * (ii) 1-Bromo-3-methylbutane (1<sup>0</sup>) > 2-Bromo-3-methylbutane (2<sup>0</sup>) > 2-Bromo-2-methylbutane (3<sup>0</sup>)
    * (iii) 1-Bromobutane > 1-Bromo-3-methylbutane > 1-Bromo-2-methylbutane > 1-Bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane. All are primary; the closer and bulkier the branching to the C-Br carbon, the slower the reaction.

!!! question "Out of C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Cl and C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CHClC<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>, which is more easily hydrolysed by aqueous KOH?"

    * Hydrolysis of these benzylic halides by aqueous KOH proceeds mainly through the S<sub>N</sub>1 mechanism.
    * C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CHClC<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub> forms the carbocation (C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>)<sub>2</sub>CH<sup>+</sup>, which is stabilised by resonance with **two** phenyl rings. C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Cl forms C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CH<sub>2</sub><sup>+</sup>, stabilised by only one ring.
    * So **C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CHClC<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>** is hydrolysed more easily.

!!! question "Predict all the alkenes that would be formed by dehydrohalogenation of the following halides with sodium ethoxide in ethanol and identify the major alkene: <br>(i) 1-Bromo-1-methylcyclohexane <br>(ii) 2-Chloro-2-methylbutane <br>(iii) 2,2,3-Trimethyl-3-bromopentane"

    * (i) Two alkenes can form: **1-methylcyclohexene** (from a ring &beta;-hydrogen) and methylenecyclohexane (from the CH<sub>3</sub> &beta;-hydrogen). 1-Methylcyclohexene is more substituted, so it is the major product (Saytzeff rule).
    * (ii) (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>C(Cl)CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub> gives:

    $$\underset{2-Methylbut-2-ene\ (major)}{(CH_3)_2C=CHCH_3} + \underset{2-Methylbut-1-ene\ (minor)}{CH_2=C(CH_3)CH_2CH_3}$$

    * (iii) The halide is (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>C-C(Br)(CH<sub>3</sub>)-CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub>. The C-2 carbon has no hydrogen, so &beta;-hydrogens are available only from the C-3 methyl group and from C-4:

    $$\underset{3,4,4-Trimethylpent-2-ene\ (major)}{(CH_3)_3C-C(CH_3)=CHCH_3} + \underset{2-Ethyl-3,3-dimethylbut-1-ene\ (minor)}{(CH_3)_3C-C(=CH_2)CH_2CH_3}$$

    The major product has three alkyl groups on the double bond, the minor one only two.

!!! question "The treatment of alkyl chlorides with aqueous KOH leads to the formation of alcohols but in the presence of alcoholic KOH, alkenes are major products. Explain."

    * In aqueous solution, KOH gives OH<sup>-</sup> ions which are highly hydrated. Hydrated OH<sup>-</sup> acts mainly as a nucleophile, so substitution occurs and alcohols are formed.
    * In alcoholic solution, KOH forms alkoxide ions (RO<sup>-</sup>), which are stronger bases than OH<sup>-</sup>. They preferentially abstract a &beta;-hydrogen, so elimination (dehydrohalogenation) occurs and alkenes are formed.

    $$R-CH_2CH_2Cl + KOH_{(aq)} &rarr; R-CH_2CH_2OH + KCl$$

    $$R-CH_2CH_2Cl + KOH_{(alc)} &rarr; R-CH=CH_2 + KCl + H_2O$$

## Reactions with Metals and Haloarenes

!!! question "Identify A, B, C, D, E, R and R<sup>1</sup> in the following: <br>Bromocyclohexane + Mg (dry ether) &rarr; A; A + H<sub>2</sub>O &rarr; B <br>R-Br + Mg (dry ether) &rarr; C; C + D<sub>2</sub>O &rarr; CH<sub>3</sub>CH(D)CH<sub>3</sub> <br>R<sup>1</sup>-X + Na/ether &rarr; (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>C-C(CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>; R<sup>1</sup>-X + Mg &rarr; D; D + H<sub>2</sub>O &rarr; E"

    * A = C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>11</sub>MgBr (cyclohexylmagnesium bromide); B = C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub> (cyclohexane).
    * D<sub>2</sub>O places D on the carbon that carried MgBr. Since the product is CH<sub>3</sub>CH(D)CH<sub>3</sub>, R = (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>CH- (isopropyl) and C = (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>CHMgBr.
    * Wurtz reaction joins two R<sup>1</sup> groups. Since the product is (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>C-C(CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>, R<sup>1</sup> = (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>C- (tert-butyl).
    * D = (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>CMgX and E = (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>CH (2-methylpropane).

!!! question "Primary alkyl halide C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>9</sub>Br (a) reacted with alcoholic KOH to give compound (b). Compound (b) is reacted with HBr to give (c) which is an isomer of (a). When (a) is reacted with sodium metal it gives compound (d), C<sub>8</sub>H<sub>18</sub>, which is different from the compound formed when n-butyl bromide is reacted with sodium. Give the structural formula of (a) and write the equations for all the reactions."

    * (a) is a primary C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>9</sub>Br, but it is not n-butyl bromide (its Wurtz product is different). So (a) is **1-bromo-2-methylpropane (isobutyl bromide)**, (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>CHCH<sub>2</sub>Br.

    $$(CH_3)_2CHCH_2Br\ (a) + KOH_{(alc)} &rarr; (CH_3)_2C=CH_2\ (b) + KBr + H_2O$$

    $$(CH_3)_2C=CH_2\ (b) + HBr &rarr; (CH_3)_3CBr\ (c)$$

    $$2(CH_3)_2CHCH_2Br\ (a) + 2Na \xrightarrow{dry\ ether} (CH_3)_2CHCH_2CH_2CH(CH_3)_2\ (d) + 2NaBr$$

    * (b) is 2-methylpropene, (c) is 2-bromo-2-methylpropane (Markovnikov addition), and (d) is 2,5-dimethylhexane. n-Butyl bromide would instead give n-octane.

!!! question "Explain why: <br>(i) the dipole moment of chlorobenzene is lower than that of cyclohexyl chloride <br>(ii) alkyl halides, though polar, are immiscible with water <br>(iii) Grignard reagents should be prepared under anhydrous conditions"

    * (i) In chlorobenzene, the C-Cl carbon is sp<sup>2</sup> hybridised and more electronegative than the sp<sup>3</sup> carbon of cyclohexyl chloride, so the C-Cl bond is less polar. Also, resonance pushes the lone pair of Cl towards the ring, which opposes the C-Cl bond dipole. Hence the dipole moment of chlorobenzene is lower.
    * (ii) Alkyl halides cannot form hydrogen bonds with water. The energy released by the new haloalkane-water attractions is less than the energy needed to break the hydrogen bonds between water molecules. So they do not dissolve.
    * (iii) Grignard reagents react with even traces of water to form hydrocarbons, so they would be destroyed:

    $$RMgX + H_2O &rarr; RH + Mg(OH)X$$

!!! question "p-Dichlorobenzene has a higher melting point than the o- and m-isomers. Discuss."

    * p-Dichlorobenzene is a symmetrical molecule. It fits more closely and neatly into the crystal lattice than the ortho- and meta-isomers.
    * So stronger intermolecular forces hold the crystal together, and more energy is needed to melt it. Hence its melting point is higher.

!!! question "What happens when: <br>(i) n-butyl chloride is treated with alcoholic KOH <br>(ii) bromobenzene is treated with Mg in the presence of dry ether <br>(iii) chlorobenzene is subjected to hydrolysis <br>(iv) ethyl chloride is treated with aqueous KOH <br>(v) methyl bromide is treated with sodium in the presence of dry ether <br>(vi) methyl chloride is treated with KCN"

    $$(i)\ CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2Cl + KOH_{(alc)} &rarr; CH_3CH_2CH=CH_2 + KCl + H_2O$$

    $$(ii)\ C_6H_5Br + Mg \xrightarrow{dry\ ether} C_6H_5MgBr$$

    $$(iii)\ C_6H_5Cl \xrightarrow[(ii)\ H^+]{(i)\ NaOH,\ 623\ K,\ 300\ atm} C_6H_5OH$$

    $$(iv)\ C_2H_5Cl + KOH_{(aq)} &rarr; C_2H_5OH + KCl$$

    $$(v)\ 2CH_3Br + 2Na \xrightarrow{dry\ ether} CH_3CH_3 + 2NaBr$$

    $$(vi)\ CH_3Cl + KCN &rarr; CH_3CN + KCl$$

    The products are but-1-ene, phenylmagnesium bromide, phenol, ethanol, ethane and ethanenitrile (methyl cyanide) respectively.

!!! question "Write the structure of the major organic product in each of the following reactions: <br>(i) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Cl + NaI (acetone, heat) <br>(ii) (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>CBr + KOH (ethanol, heat) <br>(iii) CH<sub>3</sub>CH(Br)CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub> + NaOH (water) <br>(iv) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Br + KCN (aq. ethanol) <br>(v) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>ONa + C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>Cl <br>(vi) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>OH + SOCl<sub>2</sub> <br>(vii) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH=CH<sub>2</sub> + HBr (peroxide) <br>(viii) CH<sub>3</sub>CH=C(CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub> + HBr"

    * (i) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>I (Finkelstein reaction)
    * (ii) (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>C=CH<sub>2</sub> (elimination by alcoholic KOH)
    * (iii) CH<sub>3</sub>CH(OH)CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub> (substitution by aqueous NaOH)
    * (iv) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CN (propanenitrile)
    * (v) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub> (ethoxybenzene)
    * (vi) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Cl
    * (vii) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Br (anti-Markovnikov addition)
    * (viii) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>C(Br)(CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub> (Markovnikov addition)

## Conversions

!!! question "How will you bring about the following conversions? <br>(i) Ethanol to ethyl fluoride <br>(ii) Propene to propan-1-ol <br>(iii) 1-Bromopropane to 2-bromopropane <br>(iv) Ethyl chloride to propanoic acid <br>(v) 2-Chlorobutane to 3,4-dimethylhexane <br>(vi) Aniline to chlorobenzene <br>(vii) Chlorobenzene to p-nitrophenol <br>(viii) Benzene to biphenyl"

    $$(i)\ C_2H_5OH \xrightarrow{SOCl_2} C_2H_5Cl \xrightarrow{AgF} C_2H_5F$$

    $$(ii)\ CH_3CH=CH_2 \xrightarrow{HBr,\ peroxide} CH_3CH_2CH_2Br \xrightarrow{aq.\ KOH} CH_3CH_2CH_2OH$$

    $$(iii)\ CH_3CH_2CH_2Br \xrightarrow{alc.\ KOH} CH_3CH=CH_2 \xrightarrow{HBr} CH_3CHBrCH_3$$

    $$(iv)\ C_2H_5Cl \xrightarrow{KCN} C_2H_5CN \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} C_2H_5COOH$$

    $$(v)\ 2CH_3CH_2CH(Cl)CH_3 + 2Na \xrightarrow{dry\ ether} CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)-CH(CH_3)CH_2CH_3 + 2NaCl$$

    $$(vi)\ C_6H_5NH_2 \xrightarrow[273-278\ K]{NaNO_2\ +\ HCl} C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^- \xrightarrow{Cu_2Cl_2} C_6H_5Cl + N_2$$

    $$(vii)\ C_6H_5Cl \xrightarrow[conc.\ H_2SO_4]{conc.\ HNO_3} p-ClC_6H_4NO_2 \xrightarrow[(ii)\ H^+]{(i)\ NaOH,\ 443\ K} p-HOC_6H_4NO_2$$

    $$(viii)\ C_6H_6 \xrightarrow[FeCl_3]{Br_2} C_6H_5Br \xrightarrow{Na,\ dry\ ether} C_6H_5-C_6H_5$$

    * (v) is a Wurtz reaction, (vi) is Sandmeyer's reaction and the last step of (viii) is the Fittig reaction. In (vii), the p-nitro group (the major nitration product) activates the C-Cl bond towards hydrolysis.
