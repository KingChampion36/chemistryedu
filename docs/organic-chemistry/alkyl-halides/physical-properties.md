# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Alkyl Halides | Physical Properties

In this article, we will discuss the physical properties of haloalkanes and haloarenes like colour, boiling point, melting point, density and solubility.

## 1. Physical State, Colour and Smell

* Alkyl halides are colourless when pure. However, bromides and iodides develop colour on exposure to light (they slowly decompose to give free halogen).
* Many volatile halogen compounds have a sweet smell.
* Methyl chloride, methyl bromide, ethyl chloride and some chlorofluoromethanes are gases at room temperature. Higher members are liquids or solids.

## 2. Boiling Points

### (a) Comparison with hydrocarbons

* Organic halogen compounds are polar. They also have a higher molecular mass than the parent hydrocarbon.
* Because of this, the intermolecular forces (dipole-dipole and van der Waals forces) are stronger in haloalkanes.
* Hence, chlorides, bromides and iodides boil at considerably higher temperatures than hydrocarbons of comparable molecular mass.

### (b) Effect of the halogen

* For the same alkyl group, the boiling point increases as the halogen gets bigger. A larger halogen has more electrons and greater mass, so the van der Waals forces are stronger.

!!! tip ""

    $$RI > RBr > RCl > RF$$

* The boiling point also rises as the size of the alkyl group increases. For example, CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>X > CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>X > CH<sub>3</sub>X for the same halogen.

### (c) Effect of branching

* Boiling points of isomeric haloalkanes decrease with increase in branching. A branched molecule is more compact (nearly spherical), so it has a smaller surface area for intermolecular contact.

Compound | Structure | Boiling point (K)
---------|-----------|------------------
1-Bromobutane | CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Br | 375
2-Bromobutane | CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH(Br)CH<sub>3</sub> | 364
2-Bromo-2-methylpropane | (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>CBr | 346

* 2-Bromo-2-methylpropane, the most branched isomer, has the lowest boiling point.

## 3. Melting Points of Dihalobenzenes

* The boiling points of isomeric dihalobenzenes (ortho, meta and para) are very nearly the same.
* However, the **para-isomer has a much higher melting point** than the ortho- and meta-isomers.
* This is because the para-isomer is symmetrical and fits better into the crystal lattice. More energy is then needed to break the lattice.

## 4. Density

* Bromo, iodo and polychloro derivatives of hydrocarbons are heavier than water.
* Density increases with increase in the number of carbon atoms, the number of halogen atoms and the atomic mass of the halogen.

Compound | Density (g/mL) | Compound | Density (g/mL)
---------|----------------|----------|---------------
n-C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>7</sub>Cl | 0.89 | CH<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub> | 1.336
n-C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>7</sub>Br | 1.335 | CHCl<sub>3</sub> | 1.489
n-C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>7</sub>I | 1.747 | CCl<sub>4</sub> | 1.595

## 5. Solubility

* Haloalkanes are only very slightly soluble in water, even though they are polar.
* To dissolve a haloalkane in water, energy is needed to overcome the attractions between haloalkane molecules and to break the hydrogen bonds between water molecules.
* The new attractions formed between haloalkane and water molecules are weaker than the original hydrogen bonds in water. So less energy is released than is absorbed, and solubility remains low.
* Haloalkanes dissolve readily in organic solvents, because the new attractions between haloalkane and solvent molecules are of about the same strength as the ones being broken.

!!! tip "Conclusion"

    Haloalkanes cannot form hydrogen bonds with water, so they are immiscible with water but completely soluble in organic solvents.
