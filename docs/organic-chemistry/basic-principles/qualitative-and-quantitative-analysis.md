# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Basic Principles of Organic Chemistry | Qualitative and Quantitative Analysis

The elements present in organic compounds are carbon and hydrogen, and they may also contain oxygen, nitrogen, sulphur, halogens and phosphorus. Qualitative analysis detects these elements, while quantitative analysis finds their percentage composition.

## Detection of Carbon and Hydrogen

* The compound is heated with **copper(II) oxide**. Carbon is oxidised to CO<sub>2</sub> and hydrogen to H<sub>2</sub>O.
* CO<sub>2</sub> turns **lime water milky** (turbid), and H<sub>2</sub>O turns **white anhydrous copper sulphate blue**.

!!! tip ""

    $$C + 2CuO \xrightarrow{&Delta;} 2Cu + CO_2$$

    $$2H + CuO \xrightarrow{&Delta;} Cu + H_2O$$

    $$CO_2 + Ca(OH)_2 &rarr; CaCO_3 &darr; + H_2O$$

    $$\underset{White}{CuSO_4} + 5H_2O &rarr; \underset{Blue}{CuSO_4.5H_2O}$$

## Lassaigne's Test (Detection of N, S and Halogens)

* Nitrogen, sulphur and halogens are detected by **Lassaigne's test**. The organic compound is **fused with sodium metal**, which converts these elements from covalent form into ionic form (NaCN, Na<sub>2</sub>S, NaX).

!!! tip ""

    $$Na + C + N \xrightarrow{&Delta;} NaCN$$

    $$2Na + S \xrightarrow{&Delta;} Na_2S$$

    $$Na + X \xrightarrow{&Delta;} NaX\ \ (X = Cl,\ Br\ or\ I)$$

* C, N, S and X come from the organic compound.
* The fused mass is boiled with distilled water to extract the sodium cyanide, sulphide and halide. This is called the **sodium fusion extract**.

### (A) Test for nitrogen

* The sodium fusion extract is boiled with **iron(II) sulphate** and then acidified with **concentrated sulphuric acid**. A **Prussian blue** colour confirms nitrogen.
* Sodium cyanide first reacts with iron(II) sulphate to form sodium hexacyanoferrate(II). On heating with conc. H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>, some Fe<sup>2+</sup> ions are oxidised to Fe<sup>3+</sup>, which react with hexacyanoferrate(II) to give iron(III) hexacyanoferrate(II) (ferriferrocyanide), which is Prussian blue.

!!! tip ""

    $$6CN^- + Fe^{2+} &rarr; [Fe(CN)_6]^{4-}$$

    $$3[Fe(CN)_6]^{4-} + 4Fe^{3+} \xrightarrow{xH_2O} \underset{Prussian\ blue}{Fe_4[Fe(CN)_6]_3.xH_2O}$$

### (B) Test for sulphur

* (a) The extract is acidified with **acetic acid** and **lead acetate** is added. A **black precipitate** of lead sulphide shows sulphur.

!!! tip ""

    $$S^{2-} + Pb^{2+} &rarr; \underset{Black}{PbS}$$

* (b) On treating the extract with **sodium nitroprusside**, a **violet** colour shows sulphur.

!!! tip ""

    $$S^{2-} + [Fe(CN)_5NO]^{2-} &rarr; \underset{Violet}{[Fe(CN)_5NOS]^{4-}}$$

### When both N and S are present

* Sodium thiocyanate is formed. It gives a **blood red** colour with Fe<sup>3+</sup> and no Prussian blue, since there are no free cyanide ions.

!!! tip ""

    $$Na + C + N + S &rarr; NaSCN$$

    $$Fe^{3+} + SCN^- &rarr; \underset{Blood\ red}{[Fe(SCN)]^{2+}}$$

* If fusion is done with excess sodium, the thiocyanate decomposes into cyanide and sulphide, which then give their usual tests.

!!! tip ""

    $$NaSCN + 2Na &rarr; NaCN + Na_2S$$

### (C) Test for halogens

* The extract is acidified with **nitric acid** and then treated with **silver nitrate**.

!!! tip ""

    $$X^- + Ag^+ &rarr; AgX$$

Precipitate | Solubility in ammonium hydroxide | Halogen
------------|-----------------------------------|---------
White       | Soluble                           | Chlorine
Yellowish   | Sparingly soluble                 | Bromine
Yellow      | Insoluble                         | Iodine

!!! note "Note"

    If nitrogen or sulphur is also present, the extract is first **boiled with concentrated nitric acid** to decompose the NaCN or Na<sub>2</sub>S. Otherwise, CN<sup>-</sup> and S<sup>2-</sup> ions would react with silver nitrate and interfere with the test for halogens.

### (D) Test for phosphorus

* The compound is heated with an oxidising agent (**sodium peroxide**), which oxidises phosphorus to phosphate.
* The solution is boiled with nitric acid and treated with **ammonium molybdate**. A **yellow** colour or precipitate shows phosphorus.

!!! tip ""

    $$Na_3PO_4 + 3HNO_3 &rarr; H_3PO_4 + 3NaNO_3$$

    $$H_3PO_4 + 12(NH_4)_2MoO_4 + 21HNO_3 &rarr; \underset{Ammonium\ phosphomolybdate}{(NH_4)_3PO_4.12MoO_3} + 21NH_4NO_3 + 12H_2O$$

## Quantitative Analysis

In all the formulas below, m is the mass (in g) of the organic compound taken.

### 1. Carbon and hydrogen (Liebig's method)

* A known mass of the compound is burnt in excess oxygen and copper(II) oxide. Carbon and hydrogen are oxidised to CO<sub>2</sub> and H<sub>2</sub>O.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_xH_y + (x + {y \over 4})O_2 &rarr; xCO_2 + {y \over 2}H_2O$$

* The gases are passed through two weighed U-tubes connected in series: the first contains **anhydrous calcium chloride** (absorbs water) and the second contains **concentrated KOH solution** (absorbs CO<sub>2</sub>).
* The increase in mass of the U-tubes gives the mass of water (m<sub>1</sub>) and carbon dioxide (m<sub>2</sub>).

!!! tip ""

    $$Percentage\ of\ carbon = {12 \times m_2 \times 100 \over 44 \times m}$$

    $$Percentage\ of\ hydrogen = {2 \times m_1 \times 100 \over 18 \times m}$$

### 2. Nitrogen

#### (i) Dumas method

* The compound is heated with **copper oxide in an atmosphere of CO<sub>2</sub>**. It gives free nitrogen along with CO<sub>2</sub> and water.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_xH_yN_z + (2x + {y \over 2})CuO &rarr; xCO_2 + {y \over 2}H_2O + {z \over 2}N_2 + (2x + {y \over 2})Cu$$

* Traces of nitrogen oxides formed are reduced to N<sub>2</sub> by passing the gases over a **heated copper gauze**.
* The gases are collected over **aqueous KOH**, which absorbs CO<sub>2</sub>. Nitrogen collects in the upper part of a graduated tube (nitrometer).
* Let the volume of N<sub>2</sub> collected be V<sub>1</sub> mL at room temperature T<sub>1</sub> K and pressure p<sub>1</sub> mm. Here, p<sub>1</sub> is obtained by subtracting the aqueous tension from the atmospheric pressure, since nitrogen is collected over water.

!!! tip ""

    $$p_1 = Atmospheric\ pressure - Aqueous\ tension$$

    $$Volume\ of\ N_2\ at\ STP,\ V = {p_1V_1 \times 273 \over 760 \times T_1}\ mL$$

    $$22400\ mL\ of\ N_2\ at\ STP\ weighs\ 28\ g$$

    $$Percentage\ of\ nitrogen = {28 \times V \times 100 \over 22400 \times m}$$

#### (ii) Kjeldahl's method

* The compound is heated with **concentrated sulphuric acid**, which converts its nitrogen into ammonium sulphate.
* The mixture is then heated with excess **NaOH**. The liberated ammonia is absorbed in an **excess of standard H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>**.
* The unreacted acid is estimated by titrating with standard alkali. The difference between the acid taken and the acid left gives the acid that reacted with ammonia.

!!! tip ""

    $$Organic\ compound + H_2SO_4 &rarr; (NH_4)_2SO_4 \xrightarrow{2NaOH} Na_2SO_4 + 2NH_3 + 2H_2O$$

    $$2NH_3 + H_2SO_4 &rarr; (NH_4)_2SO_4$$

* Let V mL of H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> of molarity M be taken, and let the excess acid need V<sub>1</sub> mL of NaOH of molarity M.

!!! tip ""

    $$V_1\ mL\ of\ NaOH\ (M) = {V_1 \over 2}\ mL\ of\ H_2SO_4\ (M)$$

    $$H_2SO_4\ used\ by\ NH_3 = (V - {V_1 \over 2})\ mL$$

    $$(V - {V_1 \over 2})\ mL\ of\ H_2SO_4\ (M) = 2(V - {V_1 \over 2})\ mL\ of\ NH_3\ (M)$$

    $$1000\ mL\ of\ 1\ M\ NH_3\ contains\ 14\ g\ N$$

    $$Percentage\ of\ N = {14 \times M \times 2(V - V_1/2) \over 1000} \times {100 \over m} = {1.4 \times M \times 2(V - V_1/2) \over m}$$

!!! note "Note"

    Kjeldahl's method is **not applicable** to compounds containing nitrogen in **nitro and azo groups** and nitrogen present **in a ring (e.g. pyridine)**, because their nitrogen does not change into ammonium sulphate under these conditions.

### 3. Halogens (Carius method)

* A known mass of the compound is heated with **fuming nitric acid** in the presence of **silver nitrate** in a hard glass tube called a **Carius tube**, in a furnace.
* C and H are oxidised to CO<sub>2</sub> and H<sub>2</sub>O. The halogen forms silver halide (AgX), which is filtered, washed, dried and weighed. Let its mass be m<sub>1</sub> g.

!!! tip ""

    $$Percentage\ of\ halogen = {Atomic\ mass\ of\ X \times m_1 \times 100 \over Molecular\ mass\ of\ AgX \times m}$$

### 4. Sulphur

* A known mass of the compound is heated in a Carius tube with **sodium peroxide or fuming nitric acid**. Sulphur is oxidised to sulphuric acid.
* It is precipitated as **barium sulphate** by adding excess BaCl<sub>2</sub> solution. The precipitate (m<sub>1</sub> g) is filtered, washed, dried and weighed.

!!! tip ""

    $$1\ mol\ BaSO_4 = 233\ g\ BaSO_4 \equiv 32\ g\ S$$

    $$Percentage\ of\ sulphur = {32 \times m_1 \times 100 \over 233 \times m}$$

### 5. Phosphorus

* The compound is heated with **fuming nitric acid**, which oxidises phosphorus to phosphoric acid.
* It is precipitated as **ammonium phosphomolybdate**, (NH<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub>.12MoO<sub>3</sub>, by adding ammonia and ammonium molybdate. Alternatively, it is precipitated as MgNH<sub>4</sub>PO<sub>4</sub> using magnesia mixture, which on ignition gives Mg<sub>2</sub>P<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub>.

!!! tip ""

    $$Percentage\ of\ P\ (as\ ammonium\ phosphomolybdate,\ M = 1877\ g) = {31 \times m_1 \times 100 \over 1877 \times m}$$

    $$Percentage\ of\ P\ (as\ Mg_2P_2O_7,\ M = 222\ u) = {62 \times m_1 \times 100 \over 222 \times m}$$

    Where m<sub>1</sub> is the mass of the precipitate, and 62 is the mass of two P atoms in Mg<sub>2</sub>P<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub>.

### 6. Oxygen

* Usually, the percentage of oxygen is found **by difference**: 100 minus the sum of the percentages of all other elements.
* It can also be estimated directly. The compound is heated in a stream of nitrogen. The gaseous products containing oxygen are passed over **red-hot coke**, converting all oxygen into CO. The mixture is then passed through warm **iodine pentoxide (I<sub>2</sub>O<sub>5</sub>)**, which oxidises CO to CO<sub>2</sub>, liberating iodine.

!!! tip ""

    $$Compound \xrightarrow{heat} O_2 + other\ gaseous\ products$$

    $$2C + O_2 \xrightarrow{1373\ K} 2CO$$

    $$I_2O_5 + 5CO &rarr; I_2 + 5CO_2$$

    $$Percentage\ of\ oxygen = {32 \times m_1 \times 100 \over 44 \times m}$$

    Where m<sub>1</sub> is the mass of CO<sub>2</sub> produced.

* Today, C, H and N are estimated using an automatic **CHN elemental analyser**, which needs only 1-3 mg of the substance and displays the result quickly.

## Questions

!!! question "On complete combustion, 0.246 g of an organic compound gave 0.198 g of carbon dioxide and 0.1014 g of water. Determine the percentage composition of carbon and hydrogen in the compound."

    $$Percentage\ of\ carbon = {12 \times 0.198 \times 100 \over 44 \times 0.246} = 21.95\ \%$$

    $$Percentage\ of\ hydrogen = {2 \times 0.1014 \times 100 \over 18 \times 0.246} = 4.58\ \%$$

!!! question "In Dumas' method for estimation of nitrogen, 0.3 g of an organic compound gave 50 mL of nitrogen collected at 300 K temperature and 715 mm pressure. Calculate the percentage composition of nitrogen in the compound. (Aqueous tension at 300 K = 15 mm)"

    $$Actual\ pressure = 715 - 15 = 700\ mm$$

    $$Volume\ of\ N_2\ at\ STP = {273 \times 700 \times 50 \over 300 \times 760} = 41.9\ mL$$

    $$41.9\ mL\ of\ N_2\ weighs = {28 \times 41.9 \over 22400}\ g$$

    $$Percentage\ of\ nitrogen = {28 \times 41.9 \times 100 \over 22400 \times 0.3} = 17.46\ \%$$

!!! question "During estimation of nitrogen present in an organic compound by Kjeldahl's method, the ammonia evolved from 0.5 g of the compound neutralized 10 mL of 1 M H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>. Find out the percentage of nitrogen in the compound."

    $$10\ mL\ of\ 1\ M\ H_2SO_4 \equiv 20\ mL\ of\ 1\ M\ NH_3$$

    $$1000\ mL\ of\ 1\ M\ NH_3\ contains\ 14\ g\ N$$

    $$20\ mL\ of\ 1\ M\ NH_3\ contains\ {14 \times 20 \over 1000}\ g\ N$$

    $$Percentage\ of\ nitrogen = {14 \times 20 \times 100 \over 1000 \times 0.5} = 56.0\ \%$$

!!! question "A sample of 0.50 g of an organic compound was treated according to Kjeldahl's method. The ammonia evolved was absorbed in 50 mL of 0.5 M H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>. The residual acid required 60 mL of 0.5 M solution of NaOH for neutralisation. Find the percentage composition of nitrogen in the compound."

    $$Here,\ M = 0.5,\ V = 50\ mL,\ V_1 = 60\ mL,\ m = 0.50\ g$$

    $$H_2SO_4\ used\ by\ NH_3 = V - {V_1 \over 2} = 50 - 30 = 20\ mL$$

    $$Percentage\ of\ N = {1.4 \times M \times 2(V - V_1/2) \over m} = {1.4 \times 0.5 \times 2 \times 20 \over 0.50} = 56\ \%$$

!!! question "In Carius method of estimation of halogen, 0.15 g of an organic compound gave 0.12 g of AgBr. Find out the percentage of bromine in the compound."

    $$Molar\ mass\ of\ AgBr = 108 + 80 = 188\ g\ mol^{-1}$$

    $$188\ g\ AgBr\ contains\ 80\ g\ Br$$

    $$Percentage\ of\ bromine = {80 \times 0.12 \times 100 \over 188 \times 0.15} = 34.04\ \%$$

!!! question "0.3780 g of an organic chloro compound gave 0.5740 g of silver chloride in Carius estimation. Calculate the percentage of chlorine present in the compound."

    $$Molar\ mass\ of\ AgCl = 108 + 35.5 = 143.5\ g\ mol^{-1}$$

    $$Percentage\ of\ chlorine = {35.5 \times 0.5740 \times 100 \over 143.5 \times 0.3780} = 37.57\ \%$$

!!! question "In sulphur estimation, 0.157 g of an organic compound gave 0.4813 g of barium sulphate. What is the percentage of sulphur in the compound?"

    $$Molar\ mass\ of\ BaSO_4 = 137 + 32 + 64 = 233\ g$$

    $$Percentage\ of\ sulphur = {32 \times 0.4813 \times 100 \over 233 \times 0.157} = 42.10\ \%$$

!!! question "In the estimation of sulphur by Carius method, 0.468 g of an organic sulphur compound afforded 0.668 g of barium sulphate. Find out the percentage of sulphur in the given compound."

    $$Percentage\ of\ sulphur = {32 \times 0.668 \times 100 \over 233 \times 0.468} = 19.60\ \%$$

!!! question "An organic compound contains 69% carbon and 4.8% hydrogen, the remainder being oxygen. Calculate the masses of carbon dioxide and water produced when 0.20 g of this substance is subjected to complete combustion."

    $$Mass\ of\ C = {69 \times 0.20 \over 100} = 0.138\ g$$

    $$12\ g\ C\ gives\ 44\ g\ CO_2:\ Mass\ of\ CO_2 = {44 \times 0.138 \over 12} = 0.506\ g$$

    $$Mass\ of\ H = {4.8 \times 0.20 \over 100} = 0.0096\ g$$

    $$2\ g\ H\ gives\ 18\ g\ H_2O:\ Mass\ of\ H_2O = {18 \times 0.0096 \over 2} = 0.0864\ g$$
