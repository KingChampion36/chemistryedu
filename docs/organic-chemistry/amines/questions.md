# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Amines | Questions

In this article, we will solve some important questions on amines and diazonium salts, including distinction tests, basicity orders, conversions and reaction sequences.

## Distinction and Identification

!!! question "Give one chemical test to distinguish between the following pairs:<br>(i) Methylamine and dimethylamine (ii) Secondary and tertiary amines (iii) Ethylamine and aniline (iv) Aniline and benzylamine (v) Aniline and N-methylaniline"

    * (i) **Carbylamine test:** heat with CHCl<sub>3</sub> and ethanolic KOH. Methylamine (1°) gives the foul smell of methyl isocyanide; dimethylamine (2°) does not.

    $$CH_3NH_2 + CHCl_3 + 3KOH &rarr; CH_3NC + 3KCl + 3H_2O$$

    * (ii) **Hinsberg test:** with benzenesulphonyl chloride, a secondary amine gives N,N-dialkylbenzenesulphonamide, insoluble in alkali. A tertiary amine does not react.
    * (iii) **Azo dye test:** treat with NaNO<sub>2</sub> + HCl at 273-278 K, then add alkaline phenol. Aniline forms a diazonium salt that couples to give an orange dye (p-hydroxyazobenzene). Ethylamine gives brisk evolution of N<sub>2</sub> and no dye.
    * (iv) **Nitrous acid test:** benzylamine (NH<sub>2</sub> not on the ring) behaves like an aliphatic amine, giving N<sub>2</sub> gas and benzyl alcohol. Aniline gives a diazonium salt at 273-278 K which forms an orange dye with alkaline phenol.
    * (v) **Carbylamine test:** aniline (1°) gives a foul-smelling phenyl isocyanide; N-methylaniline (2°) does not.

!!! question "Describe a method for the identification of primary, secondary and tertiary amines. Write the equations involved."

    Use **Hinsberg's reagent** (benzenesulphonyl chloride, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>SO<sub>2</sub>Cl) followed by alkali.

    $$Primary:\ C_6H_5SO_2Cl + RNH_2 &rarr; C_6H_5SO_2NHR + HCl$$

    The N-alkylbenzenesulphonamide has an acidic H on N, so it **dissolves in KOH**.

    $$Secondary:\ C_6H_5SO_2Cl + R_2NH &rarr; C_6H_5SO_2NR_2 + HCl$$

    The N,N-dialkylbenzenesulphonamide has no H on N, so it is **insoluble in KOH**.

    $$Tertiary:\ R_3N + C_6H_5SO_2Cl &rarr; No\ reaction$$

    The primary amine can be confirmed further by the carbylamine test.

## Account for the Following

!!! question "(i) pK<sub>b</sub> of aniline is more than that of methylamine.<br>(ii) Ethylamine is soluble in water whereas aniline is not."

    (i) In aniline, the lone pair on N is delocalised into the benzene ring by resonance and is less available for a proton. In methylamine, the +I effect of CH<sub>3</sub> increases electron density on N. So methylamine is the stronger base (smaller pK<sub>b</sub>, 3.38) and aniline is weaker (larger pK<sub>b</sub>, 9.38).

    (ii) Ethylamine forms hydrogen bonds with water and its alkyl part is small. In aniline the large hydrophobic phenyl group outweighs the hydrogen bonding of -NH<sub>2</sub>, so it is only slightly soluble.

!!! question "Methylamine in water reacts with ferric chloride to precipitate hydrated ferric oxide."

    Methylamine is a stronger base than ammonia and produces OH<sup>-</sup> ions in water. These precipitate Fe<sup>3+</sup> as hydrated ferric oxide:

    $$CH_3NH_2 + H_2O &#8652; CH_3\overset{+}{N}H_3 + \overset{-}{O}H$$

    $$FeCl_3 + 3\overset{-}{O}H &rarr; Fe(OH)_3 &darr; + 3Cl^-$$

!!! question "Although the amino group is o- and p-directing, aniline on nitration gives a substantial amount of m-nitroaniline."

    Nitration is done in a strongly acidic medium. Here aniline is protonated to the anilinium ion, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>3</sub><sup>+</sup>. The -NH<sub>3</sub><sup>+</sup> group is electron withdrawing and **meta directing**. So along with o- and p-products (2% and 51%), about 47% of m-nitroaniline is formed.

!!! question "(i) Aniline does not undergo Friedel-Crafts reaction.<br>(ii) Diazonium salts of aromatic amines are more stable than those of aliphatic amines.<br>(iii) Gabriel phthalimide synthesis is preferred for synthesising primary amines."

    (i) Aniline (a Lewis base) forms a salt with AlCl<sub>3</sub> (a Lewis acid). Nitrogen then carries a positive charge and strongly deactivates the ring, so the reaction does not occur.

    (ii) In arenediazonium ions the positive charge is delocalised over the benzene ring by resonance. Alkyldiazonium ions have no such stabilisation and lose N<sub>2</sub> immediately.

    (iii) It gives only the primary amine, free from secondary and tertiary amines, because the nitrogen of N-alkylphthalimide cannot be alkylated further. Ammonolysis, by contrast, gives a mixture.

!!! question "Why are aliphatic amines stronger bases than aromatic amines?"

    In aliphatic amines, the +I effect of alkyl groups increases electron density on nitrogen and stabilises the ammonium ion. In aromatic amines, the lone pair is delocalised into the ring (aniline has five resonance structures but the anilinium ion only two), so it is less available for protonation. Hence aliphatic amines are stronger bases.

## Arranging in Order

!!! question "Arrange:<br>(i) In decreasing order of pK<sub>b</sub>: C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NHCH<sub>3</sub>, (C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>)<sub>2</sub>NH, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub><br>(ii) In increasing order of basic strength: C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>N(CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>, (C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>)<sub>2</sub>NH, CH<sub>3</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>"

    $$(i)\ C_6H_5NH_2\ (9.38) > C_6H_5NHCH_3\ (9.30) > C_2H_5NH_2\ (3.29) > (C_2H_5)_2NH\ (3.00)$$

    $$(ii)\ C_6H_5NH_2 < C_6H_5N(CH_3)_2 < CH_3NH_2 < (C_2H_5)_2NH$$

!!! question "Arrange in increasing order of basic strength:<br>(a) Aniline, p-nitroaniline and p-toluidine<br>(b) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NHCH<sub>3</sub>, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>"

    (a) -NO<sub>2</sub> withdraws electrons and lowers basicity; -CH<sub>3</sub> releases electrons and raises it.

    $$p-nitroaniline < aniline < p-toluidine$$

    (b) In benzylamine the lone pair is not delocalised, so it is the strongest.

    $$C_6H_5NH_2 < C_6H_5NHCH_3 < C_6H_5CH_2NH_2$$

!!! question "Arrange:<br>(i) In decreasing order of basic strength in gas phase: C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>, (C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>)<sub>2</sub>NH, (C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>)<sub>3</sub>N, NH<sub>3</sub><br>(ii) In increasing order of boiling point: C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH, (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>NH, C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub><br>(iii) In increasing order of solubility in water: C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>, (C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>)<sub>2</sub>NH, C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>"

    (i) In the gas phase only the +I effect matters:

    $$(C_2H_5)_3N > (C_2H_5)_2NH > C_2H_5NH_2 > NH_3$$

    (ii) O-H...O hydrogen bonds are strongest; a 1° amine has two N-H bonds, a 2° amine only one:

    $$(CH_3)_2NH < C_2H_5NH_2 < C_2H_5OH$$

    (iii) Solubility falls as the hydrophobic part grows and as H-bonding decreases:

    $$C_6H_5NH_2 < (C_2H_5)_2NH < C_2H_5NH_2$$

## Conversions

!!! question "How will you convert:<br>(i) Ethanoic acid into methanamine (ii) Hexanenitrile into 1-aminopentane (iii) Methanol to ethanoic acid (iv) Ethanamine into methanamine"

    $$(i)\ CH_3COOH \xrightarrow{NH_3,\ &Delta;} CH_3CONH_2 \xrightarrow{Br_2/NaOH} CH_3NH_2$$

    $$(ii)\ CH_3(CH_2)_4CN \xrightarrow{partial\ hydrolysis\ (OH^-)} CH_3(CH_2)_4CONH_2 \xrightarrow{Br_2/NaOH} CH_3(CH_2)_4NH_2$$

    $$(iii)\ CH_3OH \xrightarrow{PCl_5} CH_3Cl \xrightarrow{KCN} CH_3CN \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} CH_3COOH$$

    $$(iv)\ C_2H_5NH_2 \xrightarrow{HNO_2} C_2H_5OH \xrightarrow{KMnO_4/H^+} CH_3COOH \xrightarrow{NH_3,\ &Delta;} CH_3CONH_2 \xrightarrow{Br_2/NaOH} CH_3NH_2$$

!!! question "How will you convert:<br>(i) Ethanoic acid into propanoic acid (ii) Methanamine into ethanamine (iii) Nitromethane into dimethylamine (iv) Propanoic acid into ethanoic acid"

    $$(i)\ CH_3COOH \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} CH_3CH_2OH \xrightarrow{PCl_5} CH_3CH_2Cl \xrightarrow{KCN} CH_3CH_2CN \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} CH_3CH_2COOH$$

    $$(ii)\ CH_3NH_2 \xrightarrow{HNO_2} CH_3OH \xrightarrow{PCl_5} CH_3Cl \xrightarrow{KCN} CH_3CN \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} CH_3CH_2NH_2$$

    $$(iii)\ CH_3NO_2 \xrightarrow{Sn/HCl} CH_3NH_2 \xrightarrow{CHCl_3/KOH} CH_3NC \xrightarrow{H_2/Pt} CH_3NHCH_3$$

    In (iii), reduction of an isocyanide gives a secondary amine.

    $$(iv)\ CH_3CH_2COOH \xrightarrow{NH_3,\ &Delta;} CH_3CH_2CONH_2 \xrightarrow{Br_2/NaOH} CH_3CH_2NH_2 \xrightarrow{HNO_2} CH_3CH_2OH \xrightarrow{KMnO_4/H^+} CH_3COOH$$

!!! question "Accomplish the following conversions:<br>(i) Nitrobenzene to benzoic acid (ii) Benzene to m-bromophenol (iii) Benzoic acid to aniline (iv) Aniline to 2,4,6-tribromofluorobenzene"

    $$(i)\ C_6H_5NO_2 \xrightarrow{Sn/HCl} C_6H_5NH_2 \xrightarrow{NaNO_2/HCl,\ 273\ K} C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^- \xrightarrow{CuCN/KCN} C_6H_5CN \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} C_6H_5COOH$$

    $$(ii)\ C_6H_6 \xrightarrow{HNO_3/H_2SO_4} C_6H_5NO_2 \xrightarrow{Br_2/FeBr_3} m-BrC_6H_4NO_2 \xrightarrow{Sn/HCl} m-BrC_6H_4NH_2$$

    $$\xrightarrow{NaNO_2/HCl,\ 273\ K} m-BrC_6H_4N_2^+Cl^- \xrightarrow{H_2O,\ warm} m-BrC_6H_4OH$$

    (-NO<sub>2</sub> is meta directing, so Br enters the meta position.)

    $$(iii)\ C_6H_5COOH \xrightarrow{NH_3,\ &Delta;} C_6H_5CONH_2 \xrightarrow{Br_2/NaOH} C_6H_5NH_2$$

    $$(iv)\ C_6H_5NH_2 \xrightarrow{Br_2/H_2O} 2,4,6-Br_3C_6H_2NH_2 \xrightarrow{NaNO_2/HCl,\ 273\ K} 2,4,6-Br_3C_6H_2N_2^+Cl^-$$

    $$\xrightarrow{HBF_4} 2,4,6-Br_3C_6H_2N_2^+BF_4^- \xrightarrow{&Delta;} 2,4,6-Br_3C_6H_2F$$

!!! question "Accomplish the following conversions:<br>(i) Chlorobenzene to p-chloroaniline (ii) Aniline to p-bromoaniline (iii) Benzamide to toluene (iv) Aniline to benzyl alcohol"

    $$(i)\ C_6H_5Cl \xrightarrow{HNO_3/H_2SO_4} p-ClC_6H_4NO_2\ (major) \xrightarrow{Sn/HCl} p-ClC_6H_4NH_2$$

    $$(ii)\ C_6H_5NH_2 \xrightarrow{(CH_3CO)_2O} C_6H_5NHCOCH_3 \xrightarrow{Br_2/CH_3COOH} p-BrC_6H_4NHCOCH_3 \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} p-BrC_6H_4NH_2$$

    $$(iii)\ C_6H_5CONH_2 \xrightarrow{Br_2/NaOH} C_6H_5NH_2 \xrightarrow{NaNO_2/HCl,\ 273\ K} C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^- \xrightarrow{H_3PO_2} C_6H_6 \xrightarrow{CH_3Cl/AlCl_3} C_6H_5CH_3$$

    $$(iv)\ C_6H_5NH_2 \xrightarrow{NaNO_2/HCl,\ 273\ K} C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^- \xrightarrow{CuCN/KCN} C_6H_5CN \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} C_6H_5CH_2NH_2 \xrightarrow{HNO_2} C_6H_5CH_2OH$$

!!! question "Convert (i) 3-Methylaniline into 3-nitrotoluene (ii) Aniline into 1,3,5-tribromobenzene."

    $$(i)\ 3-CH_3C_6H_4NH_2 \xrightarrow{NaNO_2/HCl,\ 273\ K} 3-CH_3C_6H_4N_2^+Cl^- \xrightarrow{HBF_4} 3-CH_3C_6H_4N_2^+BF_4^- \xrightarrow{NaNO_2,\ Cu,\ &Delta;} 3-CH_3C_6H_4NO_2$$

    $$(ii)\ C_6H_5NH_2 \xrightarrow{Br_2/H_2O} 2,4,6-Br_3C_6H_2NH_2 \xrightarrow{NaNO_2/HCl,\ 273\ K} 2,4,6-Br_3C_6H_2N_2^+Cl^- \xrightarrow{H_3PO_2} 1,3,5-C_6H_3Br_3$$

    In (ii), -NH<sub>2</sub> places three Br atoms meta to each other and is then removed.

## Reaction Sequences

!!! question "Give the structures of A, B and C:<br>(i) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>I + NaCN &rarr; A; A + OH<sup>-</sup> (partial hydrolysis) &rarr; B; B + NaOH/Br<sub>2</sub> &rarr; C<br>(ii) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>N<sub>2</sub>Cl + CuCN &rarr; A; A + H<sub>2</sub>O/H<sup>+</sup> &rarr; B; B + NH<sub>3</sub>, &Delta; &rarr; C<br>(iii) CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Br + KCN &rarr; A; A + LiAlH<sub>4</sub> &rarr; B; B + HNO<sub>2</sub> (0°C) &rarr; C"

    * (i) A = CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CN, B = CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CONH<sub>2</sub>, C = CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>
    * (ii) A = C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CN, B = C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>COOH, C = C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CONH<sub>2</sub>
    * (iii) A = CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CN, B = CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>, C = CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>OH

!!! question "Give the structures of A, B and C:<br>(i) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NO<sub>2</sub> + Fe/HCl &rarr; A; A + NaNO<sub>2</sub>/HCl (273 K) &rarr; B; B + H<sub>2</sub>O/H<sup>+</sup>, &Delta; &rarr; C<br>(ii) CH<sub>3</sub>COOH + NH<sub>3</sub>, &Delta; &rarr; A; A + NaOBr &rarr; B; B + NaNO<sub>2</sub>/HCl &rarr; C<br>(iii) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NO<sub>2</sub> + Fe/HCl &rarr; A; A + HNO<sub>2</sub> (273 K) &rarr; B; B + C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH &rarr; C"

    * (i) A = C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>, B = C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>N<sub>2</sub><sup>+</sup>Cl<sup>-</sup>, C = C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH
    * (ii) A = CH<sub>3</sub>CONH<sub>2</sub>, B = CH<sub>3</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>, C = CH<sub>3</sub>OH
    * (iii) A = C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>, B = C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>N<sub>2</sub><sup>+</sup>Cl<sup>-</sup>, C = p-HO-C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>-N=N-C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub> (p-hydroxyazobenzene)

!!! question "An aromatic compound 'A' on treatment with aqueous ammonia and heating forms compound 'B', which on heating with Br<sub>2</sub> and KOH forms compound 'C' of molecular formula C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>7</sub>N. Write the structures and IUPAC names of A, B and C."

    * C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>7</sub>N formed by Hoffmann bromamide reaction is C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub>. So B is the amide with one more carbon, and A is the corresponding acid.
    * A = C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>COOH (benzoic acid or benzenecarboxylic acid)
    * B = C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>CONH<sub>2</sub> (benzamide or benzenecarboxamide)
    * C = C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub> (aniline or benzenamine)

    $$C_6H_5COOH \xrightarrow{NH_3,\ &Delta;} C_6H_5CONH_2 \xrightarrow{Br_2/KOH} C_6H_5NH_2$$

!!! question "Complete the following reactions:<br>(i) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub> + CHCl<sub>3</sub> + alc. KOH &rarr; (ii) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>N<sub>2</sub>Cl + H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O &rarr; (iii) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> (conc.) &rarr; (iv) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>N<sub>2</sub>Cl + C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH &rarr;<br>(v) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub> + Br<sub>2</sub>(aq) &rarr; (vi) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>NH<sub>2</sub> + (CH<sub>3</sub>CO)<sub>2</sub>O &rarr; (vii) C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>N<sub>2</sub>Cl + (i) HBF<sub>4</sub> (ii) NaNO<sub>2</sub>/Cu, &Delta; &rarr;"

    $$(i)\ C_6H_5NH_2 + CHCl_3 + 3KOH &rarr; C_6H_5NC + 3KCl + 3H_2O$$

    $$(ii)\ C_6H_5N_2Cl + H_3PO_2 + H_2O &rarr; C_6H_6 + N_2 + H_3PO_3 + HCl$$

    $$(iii)\ C_6H_5NH_2 + H_2SO_4 &rarr; C_6H_5NH_3^+HSO_4^-\ \xrightarrow{453-473\ K} p-H_2NC_6H_4SO_3H$$

    $$(iv)\ C_6H_5N_2Cl + C_2H_5OH &rarr; C_6H_6 + N_2 + CH_3CHO + HCl$$

    $$(v)\ C_6H_5NH_2 + 3Br_2 &rarr; 2,4,6-Br_3C_6H_2NH_2 &darr; + 3HBr$$

    $$(vi)\ C_6H_5NH_2 + (CH_3CO)_2O &rarr; C_6H_5NHCOCH_3 + CH_3COOH$$

    $$(vii)\ C_6H_5N_2Cl \xrightarrow{HBF_4} C_6H_5N_2^+BF_4^- \xrightarrow{NaNO_2/Cu,\ &Delta;} C_6H_5NO_2 + N_2 + NaBF_4$$

## Miscellaneous

!!! question "Write the reaction of aniline with benzoyl chloride and name the product."

    $$C_6H_5NH_2 + C_6H_5COCl &rarr; C_6H_5NHCOC_6H_5 + HCl$$

    The product is N-phenylbenzamide (benzanilide).

!!! question "Write structures of the isomers of C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>9</sub>N. Name the isomers which liberate nitrogen gas with nitrous acid."

    * CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>NH<sub>2</sub> - Propan-1-amine (1°)
    * CH<sub>3</sub>CH(NH<sub>2</sub>)CH<sub>3</sub> - Propan-2-amine (1°)
    * CH<sub>3</sub>NHCH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub> - N-Methylethanamine (2°)
    * (CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>3</sub>N - N,N-Dimethylmethanamine (3°)

    Only primary aliphatic amines liberate N<sub>2</sub> with nitrous acid, i.e. **propan-1-amine** and **propan-2-amine**.

    $$CH_3CH_2CH_2NH_2 + HNO_2 &rarr; CH_3CH_2CH_2OH + N_2 + H_2O$$

!!! question "Write the reactions of (i) aromatic and (ii) aliphatic primary amines with nitrous acid."

    $$(i)\ C_6H_5NH_2 + NaNO_2 + 2HCl \xrightarrow{273-278\ K} C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^- + NaCl + 2H_2O$$

    $$(ii)\ RNH_2 + HNO_2 \xrightarrow{NaNO_2 + HCl} [RN_2^+Cl^-] \xrightarrow{H_2O} ROH + N_2 + HCl$$

    Aromatic amines give a diazonium salt that is stable in the cold, while aliphatic amines give an unstable diazonium salt that decomposes to an alcohol with evolution of nitrogen.
