# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Chemistry in Everyday Life | Cleansing Agents

Two types of **detergents** are used as cleansing agents: **soaps** and **synthetic detergents**. Both improve the cleansing properties of water and help remove the fats which bind other materials (dirt) to the fabric or skin.

## Soaps

* Soaps are the detergents used since ancient times.
* Soaps used for cleaning are **sodium or potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids**, such as stearic, oleic and palmitic acids.

### Saponification

* Sodium soaps are made by heating a **fat** (a glyceryl ester of fatty acids) with aqueous **sodium hydroxide**. This reaction is called **saponification**.

!!! tip ""

    $$\underset{Glyceryl\ stearate\ (fat)}{(C_{17}H_{35}COO)_3C_3H_5} + 3NaOH &rarr; \underset{Sodium\ stearate\ (soap)}{3C_{17}H_{35}COONa} + \underset{Glycerol}{C_3H_5(OH)_3}$$

* In this reaction the esters of fatty acids are **hydrolysed**. The soap formed remains in **colloidal** form.
* The soap is precipitated by adding **sodium chloride** (salting out).
* The remaining solution contains **glycerol**, which is recovered by **fractional distillation**.
* Only **sodium and potassium** soaps are soluble in water and are used for cleaning.
* **Potassium soaps** are softer to the skin than sodium soaps. They are made using KOH solution instead of NaOH.

### Cleansing action of soap

* A soap anion, such as C<sub>17</sub>H<sub>35</sub>COO<sup>-</sup>, has two parts: a long **hydrophobic** (water-repelling, oil-loving) hydrocarbon tail and a **hydrophilic** (water-loving) ionic head, -COO<sup>-</sup>.
* In water, soap anions cluster into **micelles**: the tails point inwards and dissolve in the oily dirt, while the ionic heads face the water.
* The oil droplet is thus surrounded by negative charges and gets emulsified in water. The dirt is then washed away with the water.

### Types of soaps

All soaps are made by boiling fats or oils with a suitable soluble hydroxide. Different raw materials and additives give different types:

Type of soap                    | How it is made / what it contains
--------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Toilet soaps                    | Better grades of fats and oils; excess alkali removed; colour and perfume added
Floating soaps                  | Tiny air bubbles are beaten in before the soap hardens
Transparent soaps               | Soap is dissolved in ethanol and the excess solvent is evaporated
Medicated soaps                 | Substances of medicinal value added (some soaps also contain deodorants)
Shaving soaps                   | Contain glycerol to prevent rapid drying; rosin gum added, which forms sodium rosinate that lathers well
Laundry soaps                   | Contain fillers like sodium rosinate, sodium silicate, borax and sodium carbonate
Soap chips                      | A thin sheet of melted soap is run onto a cool cylinder and scraped off in small pieces
Soap granules                   | Dried miniature soap bubbles
Soap powders and scouring soaps | Some soap, an abrasive (powdered pumice or fine sand) and builders like sodium carbonate and trisodium phosphate

* **Builders** make soaps act more rapidly.

## Why Soaps Do Not Work in Hard Water

* Hard water contains **calcium and magnesium ions**. These react with sodium or potassium soaps to form **insoluble** calcium and magnesium soaps.

!!! tip ""

    $$\underset{Soap}{2C_{17}H_{35}COONa} + CaCl_2 &rarr; 2NaCl + \underset{Insoluble\ calcium\ stearate}{(C_{17}H_{35}COO)_2Ca} &darr;$$

* These insoluble soaps separate as **scum** and are useless as cleansing agents.
* They actually hinder washing: the precipitate sticks to the fibres of cloth as a **gummy mass**.
* Hair washed with hard water looks **dull** because of this sticky precipitate.
* Dyes do not absorb evenly on cloth washed with soap in hard water.

## Synthetic Detergents

* **Synthetic detergents** are cleansing agents which have all the properties of soaps but **do not contain any soap**.
* They can be used in both **soft and hard water**, as they foam even in hard water. Some give foam even in ice-cold water.
* They are classified into three categories: **anionic**, **cationic** and **non-ionic** detergents.

### 1. Anionic Detergents

* Anionic detergents are **sodium salts of sulphonated long-chain alcohols or hydrocarbons**.
* **Alkyl hydrogensulphates**, formed by treating long-chain alcohols with concentrated sulphuric acid, are neutralised with alkali to give anionic detergents.

!!! tip ""

    $$\underset{Lauryl\ alcohol}{CH_3(CH_2)_{10}CH_2OH} \xrightarrow{H_2SO_4} \underset{Lauryl\ hydrogensulphate}{CH_3(CH_2)_{10}CH_2OSO_3H} \xrightarrow{NaOH(aq)} \underset{Sodium\ laurylsulphate}{CH_3(CH_2)_{10}CH_2OSO_3^-Na^+}$$

* Similarly, **alkylbenzenesulphonates** are obtained by neutralising alkylbenzenesulphonic acids with alkali.

!!! tip ""

    $$\underset{Dodecylbenzene}{CH_3(CH_2)_{11}C_6H_5} \xrightarrow{H_2SO_4} \underset{Dodecylbenzenesulphonic\ acid}{CH_3(CH_2)_{11}C_6H_4SO_3H} \xrightarrow{NaOH(aq)} \underset{Sodium\ dodecylbenzenesulphonate}{CH_3(CH_2)_{11}C_6H_4SO_3^-Na^+}$$

    $$(The\ -SO_3H\ group\ enters\ para\ to\ the\ alkyl\ chain)$$

* The **anionic part** of the molecule is involved in the cleansing action.
* Sodium alkylbenzenesulphonates are an important class of anionic detergents.
* **Uses**: mostly for household work; also used in **toothpastes**.

### 2. Cationic Detergents

* Cationic detergents are **quaternary ammonium salts** of amines with acetates, chlorides or bromides as anions.
* The cationic part has a long hydrocarbon chain and a **positive charge on the nitrogen atom**, hence the name.
* Example: **cetyltrimethylammonium bromide**, a popular cationic detergent used in **hair conditioners**.

!!! tip ""

    $$[CH_3(CH_2)_{15}N^+(CH_3)_3]Br^-$$

    $$Cetyltrimethylammonium\ bromide$$

* Cationic detergents have **germicidal** properties, but they are **expensive**, so they are of limited use.

### 3. Non-ionic Detergents

* Non-ionic detergents do **not contain any ion** in their constitution.
* One such detergent is formed when **stearic acid** reacts with **polyethyleneglycol** (an esterification):

!!! tip ""

    $$\underset{Stearic\ acid}{CH_3(CH_2)_{16}COOH} + \underset{Polyethyleneglycol}{HO(CH_2CH_2O)_nCH_2CH_2OH} \xrightarrow{-H_2O} CH_3(CH_2)_{16}COO(CH_2CH_2O)_nCH_2CH_2OH$$

* **Liquid dishwashing detergents** are of the non-ionic type.
* Their cleansing mechanism is the same as that of soaps: they remove grease and oil by **micelle formation**.

### Comparison of Detergents

Type      | Nature of active part                         | Example                                    | Use
----------|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------
Anionic   | Large anion (sulphate / sulphonate)           | Sodium laurylsulphate, sodium dodecylbenzenesulphonate | Household cleaning, toothpastes
Cationic  | Large cation (quaternary ammonium)            | Cetyltrimethylammonium bromide             | Hair conditioners; germicidal
Non-ionic | No ions; polar ester/ether chain              | Ester of stearic acid and polyethyleneglycol | Liquid dishwashing detergents

## Biodegradability of Detergents

* The main problem with detergents arises when their **hydrocarbon chain is highly branched**: bacteria cannot degrade such chains easily.
* Such detergents are **non-biodegradable**. They accumulate, reach rivers and ponds through effluents, persist even after sewage treatment, cause **foaming** and pollute the water.
* Detergents with **unbranched (straight) hydrocarbon chains** are degraded easily by bacteria and are called **biodegradable** detergents.
* Today, branching of the hydrocarbon chain is controlled and kept to a minimum to prevent pollution.

!!! example "Example"

    * Biodegradable: sodium laurylsulphate, CH<sub>3</sub>(CH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>10</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>OSO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>Na<sup>+</sup> (straight chain).
    * Non-biodegradable: sodium 4-(1,3,5,7-tetramethyloctyl)benzenesulphonate (highly branched chain).

!!! tip "Conclusion"

    Synthetic detergents are preferred over soaps because they work even in hard water. Among detergents, straight-chain ones are preferred over branched-chain ones because the latter are non-biodegradable and cause pollution.
