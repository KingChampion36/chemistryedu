# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Hydrogen | Dihydrogen - Preparation, Properties and Uses

Dihydrogen, H<sub>2</sub>, is a colourless, odourless, tasteless and combustible gas. In this article, we will discuss how dihydrogen is prepared in the laboratory and on a commercial scale, its physical and chemical properties and its uses.

## Laboratory Preparation of Dihydrogen

### 1. From zinc and dilute acid

* Dihydrogen is usually prepared in the laboratory by the reaction of granulated zinc with dilute hydrochloric acid.

!!! tip ""

    $$Zn + 2H^+ &rarr; Zn^{2+} + H_2$$

### 2. From zinc and aqueous alkali

* It can also be prepared by the reaction of zinc with aqueous alkali. Sodium zincate is formed.

!!! tip ""

    $$Zn + 2NaOH &rarr; Na_2ZnO_2 + H_2$$

## Commercial Production of Dihydrogen

### 1. Electrolysis of acidified water

* Electrolysis of water containing traces of acid or base, using platinum electrodes, gives dihydrogen.

!!! tip ""

    $$2H_2O_{(l)} \xrightarrow[Traces\ of\ acid/base]{Electrolysis} 2H_{2(g)} + O_{2(g)}$$

* The acid or base is added because pure water is a very poor conductor. The electrolyte makes water conducting so that electrolysis can occur.

### 2. Electrolysis of barium hydroxide solution

* High purity dihydrogen (> 99.95%) is obtained by electrolysing warm aqueous barium hydroxide solution between nickel electrodes.

### 3. By-product of the chlor-alkali process

* Dihydrogen is obtained as a by-product in the manufacture of sodium hydroxide and chlorine by electrolysis of brine (aqueous NaCl).

!!! tip ""

    $$At\ anode:\ 2Cl^-_{(aq)} &rarr; Cl_{2(g)} + 2e^-$$

    $$At\ cathode:\ 2H_2O_{(l)} + 2e^- &rarr; H_{2(g)} + 2OH^-_{(aq)}$$

    $$Overall:\ 2Na^+_{(aq)} + 2Cl^-_{(aq)} + 2H_2O_{(l)} &rarr; Cl_{2(g)} + H_{2(g)} + 2Na^+_{(aq)} + 2OH^-_{(aq)}$$

### 4. From hydrocarbons or coke (syngas)

* Reaction of steam with hydrocarbons or coke at high temperature in the presence of a catalyst gives dihydrogen.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_nH_{2n+2} + nH_2O \xrightarrow[Ni]{1270\ K} nCO + (3n+1)H_2$$

    $$CH_{4(g)} + H_2O_{(g)} \xrightarrow[Ni]{1270\ K} CO_{(g)} + 3H_{2(g)}$$

* The mixture of CO and H<sub>2</sub> is called **water gas**.
* Since this mixture is used for the synthesis of methanol and many hydrocarbons, it is also called **synthesis gas** or **syngas**.
* Nowadays, syngas is also produced from sewage, saw-dust, scrap wood, newspapers, etc.
* The process of producing syngas from coal is called **coal gasification**.

!!! tip ""

    $$C_{(s)} + H_2O_{(g)} \xrightarrow{1270\ K} CO_{(g)} + H_{2(g)}$$

### 5. Water-gas shift reaction

* The yield of dihydrogen can be increased by reacting the carbon monoxide of syngas with steam in the presence of iron chromate as catalyst. This is called the **water-gas shift reaction**.

!!! tip ""

    $$CO_{(g)} + H_2O_{(g)} \xrightarrow[catalyst]{673\ K} CO_{2(g)} + H_{2(g)}$$

* The CO<sub>2</sub> formed is removed by scrubbing with sodium arsenite solution.

!!! note "Note"

    At present, about 77% of industrial dihydrogen is produced from petrochemicals, 18% from coal, 4% from electrolysis of aqueous solutions and 1% from other sources.

## Physical Properties of Dihydrogen

* It is a colourless, odourless, tasteless and combustible gas.
* It is lighter than air and insoluble in water.
* Its melting point is 13.96 K, boiling point is 20.39 K and density is 0.09 g L<sup>-1</sup>.

## Chemical Properties of Dihydrogen

* The H-H bond dissociation enthalpy (435.88 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>) is the highest for a single bond between two atoms of any element.
* Because of this, dissociation of H<sub>2</sub> into atoms is only about 0.081% at around 2000 K, rising to 95.5% at 5000 K. For the same reason, dihydrogen is relatively inert at room temperature.
* Atomic hydrogen is produced at high temperature in an electric arc or under ultraviolet radiation.
* Since its 1s orbital is incomplete, hydrogen combines with almost all elements. It does so by:
    * losing its electron to give H<sup>+</sup>,
    * gaining an electron to form H<sup>-</sup>, or
    * sharing electrons to form a single covalent bond.

### 1. Reaction with halogens

* Dihydrogen reacts with halogens to give hydrogen halides. The reaction with fluorine occurs even in the dark, while that with iodine needs a catalyst.

!!! tip ""

    $$H_{2(g)} + X_{2(g)} &rarr; 2HX_{(g)}\ \ (X = F, Cl, Br, I)$$

### 2. Reaction with dioxygen

* It reacts with dioxygen to form water. The reaction is highly exothermic.

!!! tip ""

    $$2H_{2(g)} + O_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{catalyst\ or\ heating} 2H_2O_{(l)};\ \ &Delta;H^0 = -285.9\ kJ\ mol^{-1}$$

### 3. Reaction with dinitrogen

* With dinitrogen it forms ammonia. This is the basis of the **Haber process** for the manufacture of ammonia.

!!! tip ""

    $$3H_{2(g)} + N_{2(g)} \xrightarrow[Fe]{673\ K,\ 200\ atm} 2NH_{3(g)};\ \ &Delta;H^0 = -92.6\ kJ\ mol^{-1}$$

### 4. Reaction with metals

* With many metals it combines at high temperature to give the corresponding hydrides.

!!! tip ""

    $$H_{2(g)} + 2M_{(g)} &rarr; 2MH_{(s)}\ \ (M = alkali\ metal)$$

### 5. Reaction with metal ions and metal oxides

* It reduces some metal ions in aqueous solution, and oxides of metals less active than iron, into the corresponding metals.

!!! tip ""

    $$H_{2(g)} + Pd^{2+}_{(aq)} &rarr; Pd_{(s)} + 2H^+_{(aq)}$$

    $$yH_{2(g)} + M_xO_{y(s)} &rarr; xM_{(s)} + yH_2O_{(l)}$$

### 6. Reaction with organic compounds

* In the presence of catalysts, it reacts with many organic compounds to give useful hydrogenated products.
* **Hydrogenation of vegetable oils**, using nickel as catalyst, gives edible fats (margarine and vanaspati ghee).
* **Hydroformylation of olefins** gives aldehydes, which are further reduced to alcohols.

!!! tip ""

    $$H_2 + CO + RCH=CH_2 &rarr; RCH_2CH_2CHO$$

    $$H_2 + RCH_2CH_2CHO &rarr; RCH_2CH_2CH_2OH$$

## Uses of Dihydrogen

* The largest single use is in the synthesis of ammonia, which is used to make nitric acid and nitrogenous fertilizers.
* It is used to manufacture vanaspati fat by hydrogenation of polyunsaturated vegetable oils like soyabean and cotton seed oils.
* It is used to manufacture bulk organic chemicals, particularly methanol.

!!! tip ""

    $$CO_{(g)} + 2H_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{cobalt\ catalyst} CH_3OH_{(l)}$$

* It is widely used to manufacture metal hydrides.
* It is used to prepare hydrogen chloride, a very useful chemical.
* In metallurgy, it is used to reduce heavy metal oxides to metals.
* **Atomic hydrogen and oxy-hydrogen torches** are used for cutting and welding. Dihydrogen is dissociated into atoms in an electric arc, and the atoms are allowed to recombine on the surface to be welded. The energy released on recombination produces a temperature of about 4000 K.
* It is used as a rocket fuel in space research.
* It is used in fuel cells to generate electrical energy. It produces no pollution and releases more energy per unit mass than gasoline and other fuels.

## Questions

!!! question "Comment on the reactions of dihydrogen with (i) chlorine, (ii) sodium, and (iii) copper(II) oxide."

    (i) Dihydrogen reduces chlorine to chloride (Cl<sup>-</sup>) ion and is itself oxidised to H<sup>+</sup>, forming hydrogen chloride. An electron pair is shared between H and Cl, so a covalent molecule is formed.

    $$H_2 + Cl_2 &rarr; 2HCl$$

    (ii) Dihydrogen is reduced by sodium to form NaH. An electron is transferred from Na to H, giving an ionic compound, Na<sup>+</sup>H<sup>-</sup>.

    $$2Na + H_2 &rarr; 2NaH$$

    (iii) Dihydrogen reduces copper(II) oxide to copper in the zero oxidation state and is itself oxidised to H<sub>2</sub>O, a covalent molecule.

    $$CuO + H_2 &rarr; Cu + H_2O$$

!!! question "How can the production of dihydrogen, obtained from 'coal gasification', be increased?"

    The CO present in the syngas obtained from coal gasification is reacted with steam at 673 K in the presence of iron chromate catalyst (water-gas shift reaction). This converts CO into CO<sub>2</sub> and produces more H<sub>2</sub>. The CO<sub>2</sub> is then removed by scrubbing with sodium arsenite solution.

    $$CO_{(g)} + H_2O_{(g)} \xrightarrow[catalyst]{673\ K} CO_{2(g)} + H_{2(g)}$$

!!! question "Describe the bulk preparation of dihydrogen by electrolytic method. What is the role of an electrolyte in this process?"

    Dihydrogen is prepared in bulk by electrolysis of water containing traces of acid or alkali, using platinum electrodes. H<sub>2</sub> is liberated at the cathode and O<sub>2</sub> at the anode.

    $$2H_2O_{(l)} \xrightarrow[Traces\ of\ acid/base]{Electrolysis} 2H_{2(g)} + O_{2(g)}$$

    Pure water is a very poor conductor of electricity. The added acid or alkali (electrolyte) provides ions that make water conducting, so that electrolysis can take place.

!!! question "Complete the following reactions:<br>(i) H<sub>2(g)</sub> + M<sub>m</sub>O<sub>o(s)</sub> &rarr; (heat)<br>(ii) CO<sub>(g)</sub> + H<sub>2(g)</sub> &rarr; (heat, catalyst)<br>(iii) C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>8(g)</sub> + 3H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>(g)</sub> &rarr; (heat, catalyst)<br>(iv) Zn<sub>(s)</sub> + NaOH<sub>(aq)</sub> &rarr; (heat)"

    $$(i)\ oH_{2(g)} + M_mO_{o(s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} mM_{(s)} + oH_2O_{(l)}$$

    $$(ii)\ CO_{(g)} + 2H_{2(g)} \xrightarrow[cobalt\ catalyst]{\Delta} CH_3OH_{(l)}$$

    $$(iii)\ C_3H_{8(g)} + 3H_2O_{(g)} \xrightarrow[Ni]{\Delta} 3CO_{(g)} + 7H_{2(g)}$$

    $$(iv)\ Zn_{(s)} + 2NaOH_{(aq)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} Na_2ZnO_{2(aq)} + H_{2(g)}$$

!!! question "Discuss the consequences of high enthalpy of H-H bond in terms of chemical reactivity of dihydrogen."

    The H-H bond dissociation enthalpy (435.88 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>) is the highest for a single bond between two atoms of any element. As a result:

    * Dihydrogen dissociates into atoms only to a very small extent (about 0.081% at 2000 K).
    * It is relatively inert at room temperature, and most of its reactions need high temperature, catalysts or electric discharge.

!!! question "How does the atomic hydrogen or oxy-hydrogen torch function for cutting and welding purposes? Explain."

    In an atomic hydrogen torch, dihydrogen is dissociated into hydrogen atoms with the help of an electric arc. This absorbs a large amount of energy because the H-H bond is very strong. The hydrogen atoms are then allowed to recombine on the metal surface to be welded. Recombination releases the same large amount of energy, producing a temperature of about 4000 K, which is enough to cut and weld metals.
