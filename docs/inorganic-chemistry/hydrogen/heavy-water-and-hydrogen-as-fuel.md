# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Hydrogen | Heavy Water and Hydrogen as a Fuel

In this article, we will discuss heavy water (D<sub>2</sub>O) and the use of dihydrogen as a fuel, including the idea of a hydrogen economy.

## Heavy Water, D<sub>2</sub>O

Heavy water, D<sub>2</sub>O, is water in which both protium atoms are replaced by deuterium.

### Preparation

* It is prepared by **exhaustive electrolysis** of ordinary water. Since H<sub>2</sub> is liberated faster than D<sub>2</sub>, the remaining water gets gradually enriched in D<sub>2</sub>O.
* It is also obtained as a by-product in some fertilizer industries.

### Properties

* Heavy water has a higher molecular mass, melting point, boiling point, density and viscosity than ordinary water.

Property                        | H<sub>2</sub>O | D<sub>2</sub>O |
--------------------------------|----------------|----------------|
Molecular mass (g mol<sup>-1</sup>) | 18.0151    | 20.0276        |
Melting point (K)               | 273.0          | 276.8          |
Boiling point (K)               | 373.0          | 374.4          |
Density at 298 K (g cm<sup>-3</sup>) | 1.0000    | 1.1059         |
Temperature of maximum density (K) | 276.98      | 284.2          |
Viscosity (centipoise)          | 0.8903         | 1.107          |

### Uses

* It is extensively used as a **moderator in nuclear reactors**.
* It is used in exchange reactions to study reaction mechanisms.
* It is used to prepare other deuterium compounds, for example:

!!! tip ""

    $$CaC_2 + 2D_2O &rarr; C_2D_2 + Ca(OD)_2$$

    $$SO_3 + D_2O &rarr; D_2SO_4$$

    $$Al_4C_3 + 12D_2O &rarr; 3CD_4 + 4Al(OD)_3$$

## Dihydrogen as a Fuel

Dihydrogen releases a large amount of heat on combustion. The energy released by burning different fuels is compared below.

Energy released on combustion (kJ) | Dihydrogen (gas) | Dihydrogen (liquid) | LPG   | CH<sub>4</sub> gas | Octane (liquid) |
-----------------------------------|------------------|---------------------|-------|--------------------|-----------------|
per mole                           | 286              | 285                 | 2220  | 880                | 5511            |
per gram                           | 143              | 142                 | 50    | 53                 | 47              |
per litre                          | 12               | 9968                | 25590 | 35                 | 34005           |

### Advantages

* On a mass basis, dihydrogen releases about **three times** more energy than petrol.
* It burns to give only water, so pollution is much lower than with petrol. The only pollutants are oxides of nitrogen, formed because dinitrogen is present as an impurity.
* Even this can be minimised by injecting a small amount of water into the cylinder. This lowers the temperature so that dinitrogen and dioxygen do not react.

### Limitations

* A cylinder of compressed dihydrogen weighs about **30 times** as much as a tank of petrol containing the same amount of energy.
* To liquefy dihydrogen it must be cooled to 20 K, which requires expensive insulated tanks.
* Tanks of metal alloys like NaNi<sub>5</sub>, Ti-TiH<sub>2</sub> and Mg-MgH<sub>2</sub> are used to store dihydrogen, but only in small quantities.

These limitations have led researchers to search for better ways of storing and using dihydrogen.

## Hydrogen Economy

* The basic principle of the **hydrogen economy** is the transportation and storage of energy in the form of liquid or gaseous dihydrogen.
* Its advantage is that energy is transmitted as dihydrogen and not as electric power.
* In India, a pilot project using dihydrogen as a fuel for automobiles was launched in October 2005. Initially, 5% dihydrogen was mixed with CNG for four-wheeler vehicles, and the percentage is to be raised gradually to an optimum level.
* Dihydrogen is also used in **fuel cells** to generate electric power.
* It is expected that economically viable and safe sources of dihydrogen will be found in the coming years, making it a common source of energy.

## Questions

!!! question "Knowing the properties of H<sub>2</sub>O and D<sub>2</sub>O, do you think that D<sub>2</sub>O can be used for drinking purposes?"

    No. D<sub>2</sub>O has different physical properties (higher density, viscosity, melting and boiling points) from H<sub>2</sub>O, and reactions involving D are slower than those involving H because the bond with D is stronger. Heavy water therefore slows down the biochemical reactions in living cells, so it is harmful and not suitable for drinking.

!!! question "What do you understand by the terms:<br>(i) hydrogen economy (ii) hydrogenation (iii) 'syngas' (iv) water-gas shift reaction (v) fuel-cell?"

    (i) **Hydrogen economy:** A system in which energy is stored and transported in the form of liquid or gaseous dihydrogen instead of electric power or fossil fuels.

    (ii) **Hydrogenation:** Addition of dihydrogen to a compound, usually an unsaturated one, in the presence of a catalyst. Example: hydrogenation of vegetable oils using nickel to make vanaspati ghee.

    (iii) **Syngas:** The mixture of CO and H<sub>2</sub>, obtained by reacting steam with hydrocarbons or coke. It is used to synthesise methanol and many hydrocarbons.

    $$CH_{4(g)} + H_2O_{(g)} \xrightarrow[Ni]{1270\ K} CO_{(g)} + 3H_{2(g)}$$

    (iv) **Water-gas shift reaction:** Reaction of the CO in syngas with steam over iron chromate catalyst to produce more dihydrogen.

    $$CO_{(g)} + H_2O_{(g)} \xrightarrow[catalyst]{673\ K} CO_{2(g)} + H_{2(g)}$$

    (v) **Fuel cell:** A device that converts the chemical energy of a fuel such as dihydrogen directly into electrical energy. In a H<sub>2</sub>-O<sub>2</sub> fuel cell, dihydrogen and dioxygen combine to form water, producing electricity without pollution.
