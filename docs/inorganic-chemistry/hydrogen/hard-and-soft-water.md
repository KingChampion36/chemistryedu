# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Hydrogen | Hard and Soft Water

Rain water is almost pure, though it may contain some dissolved gases from the atmosphere. Being a good solvent, water dissolves many salts as it flows over the surface of the earth.

* Water containing calcium and magnesium salts in the form of hydrogencarbonate, chloride and sulphate is called **hard water**. Hard water does not give lather with soap.
* Water free from soluble salts of calcium and magnesium is called **soft water**. It gives lather with soap easily.

## Why is Hard Water Undesirable?

* Hard water forms scum (a precipitate) with soap. Soap, which contains sodium stearate (C<sub>17</sub>H<sub>35</sub>COONa), reacts with hard water to precipitate calcium or magnesium stearate.

!!! tip ""

    $$2C_{17}H_{35}COONa_{(aq)} + M^{2+}_{(aq)} &rarr; (C_{17}H_{35}COO)_2M &darr; + 2Na^+_{(aq)}$$

    $$[M = Ca\ or\ Mg]$$

* So, hard water is unsuitable for laundry, as soap is wasted.
* It is also harmful for boilers, because salts get deposited as **scale**. This reduces the efficiency of the boiler.

Hardness of water is of two types:

* Temporary hardness
* Permanent hardness

## Temporary Hardness

Temporary hardness is due to the presence of **hydrogencarbonates** of magnesium and calcium. It can be removed by the following methods.

### 1. Boiling

* On boiling, soluble Mg(HCO<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub> is converted into insoluble Mg(OH)<sub>2</sub>, and Ca(HCO<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub> into insoluble CaCO<sub>3</sub>.
* Mg(OH)<sub>2</sub> (and not MgCO<sub>3</sub>) is precipitated because the solubility product of Mg(OH)<sub>2</sub> is lower than that of MgCO<sub>3</sub>.
* The precipitates are removed by filtration. The filtrate is soft water.

!!! tip ""

    $$Mg(HCO_3)_2 \xrightarrow{Heating} Mg(OH)_2 &darr; + 2CO_2 &uarr;$$

    $$Ca(HCO_3)_2 \xrightarrow{Heating} CaCO_3 &darr; + H_2O + CO_2 &uarr;$$

### 2. Clark's method

* A calculated amount of lime (Ca(OH)<sub>2</sub>) is added to hard water. It precipitates calcium carbonate and magnesium hydroxide, which are filtered off.

!!! tip ""

    $$Ca(HCO_3)_2 + Ca(OH)_2 &rarr; 2CaCO_3 &darr; + 2H_2O$$

    $$Mg(HCO_3)_2 + 2Ca(OH)_2 &rarr; 2CaCO_3 &darr; + Mg(OH)_2 &darr; + 2H_2O$$

## Permanent Hardness

Permanent hardness is due to the presence of soluble salts of magnesium and calcium in the form of **chlorides and sulphates**. It is not removed by boiling. It can be removed by the following methods.

### 1. Treatment with washing soda (sodium carbonate)

* Washing soda reacts with soluble calcium and magnesium chlorides and sulphates to form insoluble carbonates.

!!! tip ""

    $$MCl_2 + Na_2CO_3 &rarr; MCO_3 &darr; + 2NaCl$$

    $$MSO_4 + Na_2CO_3 &rarr; MCO_3 &darr; + Na_2SO_4$$

    $$[M = Mg,\ Ca]$$

### 2. Calgon's method

* Sodium hexametaphosphate (Na<sub>6</sub>P<sub>6</sub>O<sub>18</sub>), commercially called **calgon**, is added to hard water.

!!! tip ""

    $$Na_6P_6O_{18} &rarr; 2Na^+ + Na_4P_6O_{18}^{2-}$$

    $$M^{2+} + Na_4P_6O_{18}^{2-} &rarr; [Na_2MP_6O_{18}]^{2-} + 2Na^+$$

    $$[M = Mg,\ Ca]$$

* The complex anion keeps the Mg<sup>2+</sup> and Ca<sup>2+</sup> ions in solution, so they cannot react with soap.

### 3. Ion-exchange method (zeolite/permutit process)

* Hydrated sodium aluminium silicate is called zeolite or permutit. For simplicity, sodium aluminium silicate (NaAlSiO<sub>4</sub>) is written as NaZ.
* When it is added to hard water, Na<sup>+</sup> ions are exchanged for Ca<sup>2+</sup> and Mg<sup>2+</sup> ions.

!!! tip ""

    $$2NaZ_{(s)} + M^{2+}_{(aq)} &rarr; MZ_{2(s)} + 2Na^+_{(aq)}$$

    $$[M = Mg,\ Ca]$$

* Permutit/zeolite is said to be **exhausted** when all its sodium is used up. It is regenerated by treating it with aqueous sodium chloride solution.

!!! tip ""

    $$MZ_{2(s)} + 2NaCl_{(aq)} &rarr; 2NaZ_{(s)} + MCl_{2(aq)}$$

### 4. Synthetic resins method

* Nowadays, hard water is softened using synthetic cation exchangers. This method is more efficient than the zeolite process.
* Cation exchange resins are large, water-insoluble organic molecules containing the -SO<sub>3</sub>H group.
* The resin RSO<sub>3</sub>H is first changed to RNa by treating it with NaCl. Here R is the resin anion. The resin then exchanges its Na<sup>+</sup> ions with Ca<sup>2+</sup> and Mg<sup>2+</sup> ions of hard water.

!!! tip ""

    $$2RNa_{(s)} + M^{2+}_{(aq)} &rarr; R_2M_{(s)} + 2Na^+_{(aq)}$$

* The resin can be regenerated by adding aqueous NaCl solution.

## Demineralised (De-ionised) Water

Pure demineralised water, free from all soluble mineral salts, is obtained by passing water successively through a cation exchange resin (in the H<sup>+</sup> form) and an anion exchange resin (in the OH<sup>-</sup> form).

* **Cation exchange:** H<sup>+</sup> of the resin is exchanged for Na<sup>+</sup>, Ca<sup>2+</sup>, Mg<sup>2+</sup> and other cations present in water. This releases protons and makes the water acidic.

!!! tip ""

    $$2RH_{(s)} + M^{2+}_{(aq)} &#8652; MR_{2(s)} + 2H^+_{(aq)}$$

* **Anion exchange:** OH<sup>-</sup> of the resin is exchanged for anions like Cl<sup>-</sup>, HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>, SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup>, etc. present in water.

!!! tip ""

    $$RNH_{2(s)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; RNH_3^+.OH^-_{(s)}$$

    $$RNH_3^+.OH^-_{(s)} + X^-_{(aq)} &#8652; RNH_3^+.X^-_{(s)} + OH^-_{(aq)}$$

* The OH<sup>-</sup> ions released neutralise the H<sup>+</sup> ions set free in the cation exchange.

!!! tip ""

    $$H^+_{(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)} &rarr; H_2O_{(l)}$$

* The exhausted cation and anion exchange resin beds are regenerated by treatment with dilute acid and dilute alkali solutions respectively.

## Questions

!!! question "What causes the temporary and permanent hardness of water?"

    * **Temporary hardness** is caused by the presence of soluble hydrogencarbonates of calcium and magnesium, Ca(HCO<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub> and Mg(HCO<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>. It is removed by boiling.
    * **Permanent hardness** is caused by the presence of soluble chlorides and sulphates of calcium and magnesium (CaCl<sub>2</sub>, MgCl<sub>2</sub>, CaSO<sub>4</sub>, MgSO<sub>4</sub>). It is not removed by boiling.

!!! question "Discuss the principle and method of softening of hard water by synthetic ion-exchange resins."

    **Principle:** Ion-exchange resins are insoluble, giant organic molecules that can exchange their own ions for the ions present in water.

    **Method:** Water is passed through a cation exchange resin (RSO<sub>3</sub>H, or its sodium form RNa). The Ca<sup>2+</sup> and Mg<sup>2+</sup> ions are retained by the resin and Na<sup>+</sup> (or H<sup>+</sup>) ions are released into water.

    $$2RNa_{(s)} + M^{2+}_{(aq)} &rarr; R_2M_{(s)} + 2Na^+_{(aq)}$$

    For complete demineralisation, the water is then passed through an anion exchange resin (RNH<sub>3</sub><sup>+</sup>OH<sup>-</sup>), which removes anions and releases OH<sup>-</sup>. The OH<sup>-</sup> neutralises the H<sup>+</sup> released by the cation exchanger to form water. The exhausted resins are regenerated with NaCl solution (or dilute acid) and dilute alkali respectively.

!!! question "What is meant by 'demineralised' water and how can it be obtained?"

    Demineralised water is water free from all soluble mineral salts (both cations and anions). It is obtained by passing water successively through a cation exchange resin in the H<sup>+</sup> form and an anion exchange resin in the OH<sup>-</sup> form. Cations are replaced by H<sup>+</sup>, anions by OH<sup>-</sup>, and H<sup>+</sup> and OH<sup>-</sup> combine to form water.

!!! question "Is demineralised or distilled water useful for drinking purposes? If not, how can it be made useful?"

    No. Demineralised and distilled water lack the essential minerals (such as calcium, magnesium and potassium salts) that the body needs, and it also tastes flat. It can be made useful for drinking by adding the required minerals in proper amounts.
