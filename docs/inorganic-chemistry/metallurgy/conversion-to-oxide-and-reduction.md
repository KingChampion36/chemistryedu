# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Metallurgy | Conversion to Oxide and Reduction

The concentrated ore must be converted into a form which is suitable for reduction. Usually it is first converted into its oxide by calcination or roasting, and the oxide is then reduced to the metal.

* Isolation of a crude metal from the concentrated ore involves two major steps:
    * Conversion of the ore to oxide
    * Reduction of the oxide to metal
* Sulphide and carbonate ores are converted to oxides before reduction because oxides are easier to reduce.

!!! note "Why are oxides easier to reduce?"

    The standard Gibbs energy of formation of most metal sulphides is more negative than that of CS<sub>2</sub> and H<sub>2</sub>S (CS<sub>2</sub> is in fact an endothermic compound). So the reduction of a sulphide by carbon or hydrogen has a positive &Delta;G<sup>0</sup> and is not feasible. For oxides, the formation of CO or CO<sub>2</sub> can make the overall &Delta;G<sup>0</sup> negative. Hence, sulphide ores are roasted to oxides before reduction.

## Conversion to Oxide

### 1. Calcination

* Calcination is heating the ore (usually in the absence or limited supply of air) so that the volatile matter escapes, leaving behind the metal oxide.
* It is used for hydrated oxide ores and carbonate ores. Water and CO<sub>2</sub> are removed.

!!! tip ""

    $$Fe_2O_3.xH_2O_{(s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} Fe_2O_{3(s)} + xH_2O_{(g)}$$

    $$ZnCO_{3(s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} ZnO_{(s)} + CO_{2(g)}$$

    $$CaCO_3.MgCO_{3(s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} CaO_{(s)} + MgO_{(s)} + 2CO_{2(g)}$$

### 2. Roasting

* In roasting, the ore is heated in a regular supply of air in a furnace at a temperature below the melting point of the metal.
* It is mainly used for sulphide ores, which are oxidised to oxides.

!!! tip ""

    $$2ZnS + 3O_2 &rarr; 2ZnO + 2SO_2$$

    $$2PbS + 3O_2 &rarr; 2PbO + 2SO_2$$

    $$2Cu_2S + 3O_2 &rarr; 2Cu_2O + 2SO_2$$

* The SO<sub>2</sub> produced is utilised for manufacturing H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>.

### Roasting of copper ores

* The sulphide ores of copper are heated in a **reverberatory furnace**.
* If the ore contains iron, it is mixed with silica (SiO<sub>2</sub>) before heating.
* Silica acts as a flux: iron oxide forms a slag of iron silicate, and copper is produced in the form of **copper matte**, which contains Cu<sub>2</sub>S and FeS.

!!! tip ""

    $$FeO + SiO_2 &rarr; FeSiO_3\ (slag)$$

### Difference between Calcination and Roasting

Calcination                                              | Roasting
---------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------
Ore is heated in absence or limited supply of air.       | Ore is heated in a regular supply of air.
Used for carbonate and hydrated oxide ores.              | Used for sulphide ores.
Moisture, CO<sub>2</sub> and other volatile matter are removed. | The sulphide is oxidised; SO<sub>2</sub> is given off.
Example: ZnCO<sub>3</sub> &rarr; ZnO + CO<sub>2</sub>    | Example: 2ZnS + 3O<sub>2</sub> &rarr; 2ZnO + 2SO<sub>2</sub>

## Reduction of Oxide to the Metal

* Reduction of the metal oxide usually involves heating it with some other substance which acts as a reducing agent, like C, CO or even another metal.
* The reducing agent (e.g., carbon) combines with the oxygen of the metal oxide.

!!! tip ""

    $$M_xO_y + yC &rarr; xM + yCO$$

* Some metal oxides get reduced easily while others are very difficult to reduce. In every case, heating is required.
* Extraction of metals by thermal reduction at high temperature is called **pyrometallurgy**.
* To understand the temperature needed for a reduction, and to predict which element will be a suitable reducing agent for a given metal oxide (M<sub>x</sub>O<sub>y</sub>), we use the thermodynamic concept of Gibbs energy. This is discussed in the next part.
