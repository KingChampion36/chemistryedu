# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Metallurgy | Introduction

Metallurgy is the entire scientific and technological process used for the isolation of a metal from its ores.

* Only a few elements like carbon, sulphur, gold and the noble gases occur in the free (native) state in nature.
* Most other elements occur in combined form, i.e., as compounds such as oxides, sulphides, carbonates and silicates.
* Extracting an element from its combined form needs a process that is both chemically feasible and commercially viable.
* Every metal has its own extraction route, but a few general principles are common to all of them.

## Some Important Terms

### 1. Mineral

Minerals are naturally occurring chemical substances in the earth's crust which can be obtained by mining.

### 2. Ore

* A metal may be found in many minerals, but only a few of them are viable sources of that metal.
* A mineral from which a metal can be extracted conveniently and profitably is called an ore.
* Hence, all ores are minerals but all minerals are not ores.

!!! example "Example"

    Aluminium occurs in many minerals like mica and clays, but it is extracted only from bauxite. So bauxite is an ore of aluminium, while clay is just a mineral of aluminium.

### 3. Gangue

An ore rarely contains only the desired compound. It is usually contaminated with earthy or undesired materials like sand, clay and rocks, which are known as gangue (or matrix).

### 4. Flux and Slag

* A flux is a substance added during metallurgy which combines with the gangue to form a fusible material called slag.
* Slag separates more easily from the ore than the gangue does, so the removal of gangue becomes easier.

!!! tip ""

    $$Flux + Gangue &rarr; Slag$$

    $$FeO + SiO_2 &rarr; FeSiO_3\ (slag)$$

## Major Steps in Metallurgy

The extraction and isolation of a metal from its ore involves three major steps:

* **Concentration of the ore:** removal of gangue from the ore.
* **Isolation of the metal from the concentrated ore:** conversion of the ore into oxide followed by reduction of the oxide to metal.
* **Purification (refining) of the metal:** removal of the remaining minor impurities.

!!! note "Note"

    Before concentration, ores are graded and crushed to a reasonable size.

## Occurrence of Metals

* Elements vary greatly in their abundance in the earth's crust.
* **Aluminium** is the most abundant metal and the third most abundant element in the earth's crust (about 8.3% by weight).
* Aluminium is a major component of many igneous minerals including mica and clays.
* Many gemstones are impure forms of Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>. The impurities range from Cr (in ruby) to Co (in sapphire).
* **Iron** is the second most abundant metal in the earth's crust. It forms a variety of compounds and is also one of the essential elements in biological systems.

## Choice of Ore for Extraction

* **Aluminium:** bauxite is chosen.
* **Iron:** usually the oxide ores (haematite, magnetite) are used. They are abundant and do not produce polluting gases like SO<sub>2</sub>, which is produced when iron pyrites is used.
* **Copper and Zinc:** any of the listed ores may be used, depending upon availability and other relevant factors.
