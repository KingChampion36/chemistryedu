# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Chemical Bonding | Hydrogen Bonding

Hydrogen bond can be defined as the attractive force which binds the hydrogen atom of one molecule with the electronegative atom (F, O or N) of another molecule.

* Nitrogen, oxygen and fluorine are highly electronegative elements.
* When one of them is covalently bonded to hydrogen, the shared electrons shift towards the more electronegative atom. The H atom gets a partial positive charge.
* This partially positive H atom then forms a bond with the electronegative atom of another molecule. This bond is called a **hydrogen bond**, and it is weaker than a covalent bond.
* A hydrogen bond is shown by a dotted line (- - -), while a covalent bond is shown by a solid line.

!!! example "Hydrogen bonding in HF"

    $$- - - \overset{\delta+}{H} - \overset{\delta-}{F} - - - \overset{\delta+}{H} - \overset{\delta-}{F} - - - \overset{\delta+}{H} - \overset{\delta-}{F} - - -$$

    Here, each H atom acts as a bridge: it is held to one F atom by a covalent bond and to another F atom by a hydrogen bond.

## Cause of Formation of Hydrogen Bond

* When hydrogen is bonded to a strongly electronegative element X, the shared electron pair moves far away from the hydrogen atom.
* The H atom becomes highly electropositive with respect to X. H acquires a fractional positive charge (&delta;+) and X acquires a fractional negative charge (&delta;-).
* This gives a polar molecule with an electrostatic force of attraction to neighbouring molecules:

!!! tip ""

    $$\overset{\delta+}{H} - \overset{\delta-}{X} - - - \overset{\delta+}{H} - \overset{\delta-}{X}$$

* The magnitude of hydrogen bonding depends on the physical state of the compound. It is **maximum in the solid state** and **minimum in the gaseous state**.
* Hydrogen bonds have a strong influence on the structure and properties of compounds.

## Types of Hydrogen Bonds

### 1. Intermolecular Hydrogen Bond

* It is formed between two different molecules of the same or different compounds.
* Examples: hydrogen bonding in HF, in water and in alcohols.

!!! tip ""

    $$- - - \overset{\delta+}{H} - \overset{\delta-}{O}(H) - - - \overset{\delta+}{H} - \overset{\delta-}{O}(H) - - -$$

### 2. Intramolecular Hydrogen Bond

* It is formed when a hydrogen atom lies between two highly electronegative atoms (F, O, N) present **within the same molecule**.
* Example: In o-nitrophenol, the H of the -OH group is hydrogen bonded to an O atom of the neighbouring -NO<sub>2</sub> group, forming a ring within the molecule.

Intermolecular hydrogen bond | Intramolecular hydrogen bond |
-----------------------------|------------------------------|
Formed between two or more molecules | Formed within the same molecule |
Molecules get associated, so boiling point increases | No association between molecules, so boiling point is lower |
Examples: HF, H<sub>2</sub>O, alcohols, p-nitrophenol | Example: o-nitrophenol |

## Questions

!!! question "Define hydrogen bond. Is it weaker or stronger than the van der Waals forces?"

    * A hydrogen bond is the attractive force which binds the hydrogen atom of one molecule (or part of a molecule) with a highly electronegative atom (F, O or N) of another molecule (or another part of the same molecule).
    * A hydrogen bond is **stronger** than van der Waals forces, because it involves attraction between a strongly positive H and a strongly negative atom, while van der Waals forces are weak attractions between temporary or permanent dipoles. However, it is much weaker than a covalent bond.
