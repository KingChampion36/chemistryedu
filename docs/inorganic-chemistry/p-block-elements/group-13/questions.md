## Questions

!!! question "Discuss the pattern of variation in the oxidation states from B to Tl."

    * All group 13 elements have the valence configuration ns<sup>2</sup>np<sup>1</sup>, so the group oxidation state is +3.
    * Boron and aluminium show only the +3 oxidation state.
    * Ga, In and Tl show both +1 and +3 oxidation states. Due to the inert pair effect, the stability of the +1 state increases down the group:

    $$Al\ <\ Ga\ <\ In\ <\ Tl$$

    * In thallium, +1 is the predominant oxidation state and Tl(III) compounds are strong oxidising agents.

!!! question "How can you explain higher stability of BCl<sub>3</sub> as compared to TlCl<sub>3</sub>?"

    Boron has no d or f electrons in its inner core, so both of its 2s electrons take part in bonding and the +3 state is stable. In thallium, poor shielding by the 4f and 5d electrons holds the 6s<sup>2</sup> pair tightly (inert pair effect). So Tl prefers the +1 state, and TlCl<sub>3</sub> tends to change into the more stable TlCl. Hence BCl<sub>3</sub> is more stable than TlCl<sub>3</sub>.

!!! question "Consider the compounds, BCl<sub>3</sub> and CCl<sub>4</sub>. How will they behave with water? Justify."

    * BCl<sub>3</sub> is electron deficient. Boron accepts a lone pair from the oxygen of water, and the compound is hydrolysed to boric acid:

    $$BCl_3 + 3H_2O &rarr; B(OH)_3 + 3HCl$$

    * In CCl<sub>4</sub>, carbon already has a complete octet and has no vacant d orbitals to accept a lone pair from water. So CCl<sub>4</sub> is not hydrolysed by water.

!!! question "Is boric acid a protic acid? Explain."

    No. Boric acid does not donate H<sup>+</sup> ions by itself. It acts as a Lewis acid by accepting an OH<sup>-</sup> ion from water, which releases H<sub>3</sub>O<sup>+</sup>:

    $$B(OH)_3 + 2HOH &rarr; [B(OH)_4]^- + H_3O^+$$

!!! question "Explain what happens when boric acid is heated."

    On heating above 370 K, orthoboric acid first forms metaboric acid, which on stronger heating forms boric oxide:

    $$H_3BO_3 \xrightarrow{370\ K} HBO_2 + H_2O$$

    $$2HBO_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} B_2O_3 + H_2O$$

!!! question "Describe the shapes of BF<sub>3</sub> and BH<sub>4</sub><sup>-</sup>. Assign the hybridisation of boron in these species."

    * **BF<sub>3</sub>:** Boron forms three bond pairs and has no lone pair. It is sp<sup>2</sup> hybridised and the molecule is **trigonal planar** (bond angle 120°).
    * **BH<sub>4</sub><sup>-</sup>:** Boron forms four bond pairs (one of them by accepting H<sup>-</sup>). It is sp<sup>3</sup> hybridised and the ion is **tetrahedral** (bond angle 109.5°).

!!! question "Write reactions to justify amphoteric nature of aluminium."

    Aluminium dissolves both in acids and in alkalies, liberating dihydrogen:

    $$2Al_{(s)} + 6HCl_{(aq)} &rarr; 2Al^{3+}_{(aq)} + 6Cl^-_{(aq)} + 3H_{2(g)}$$

    $$2Al_{(s)} + 2NaOH_{(aq)} + 6H_2O_{(l)} &rarr; 2Na^+[Al(OH)_4]^-_{(aq)} + 3H_{2(g)}$$

!!! question "What are electron deficient compounds? Are BCl<sub>3</sub> and SiCl<sub>4</sub> electron deficient species? Explain."

    * Compounds in which the central atom has less than eight electrons in its valence shell are called electron deficient compounds.
    * **BCl<sub>3</sub>** is electron deficient: boron has only six electrons around it and acts as a Lewis acid.
    * **SiCl<sub>4</sub>** is not electron deficient: silicon has a complete octet (it is an electron precise molecule). However, it can still accept lone pairs because silicon has vacant 3d orbitals.

!!! question "Suggest reasons why the B-F bond lengths in BF<sub>3</sub> (130 pm) and BF<sub>4</sub><sup>-</sup> (143 pm) differ."

    * In BF<sub>3</sub>, boron is sp<sup>2</sup> hybridised and has a vacant 2p orbital. A filled 2p orbital of fluorine donates electron density into it (pπ-pπ back bonding). This gives the B-F bond partial double bond character, so it is shorter (130 pm).
    * In BF<sub>4</sub><sup>-</sup>, boron is sp<sup>3</sup> hybridised and has no vacant p orbital, so no back bonding is possible. The B-F bonds are pure single bonds and hence longer (143 pm).

!!! question "If B-Cl bond has a dipole moment, explain why BCl<sub>3</sub> molecule has zero dipole moment."

    BCl<sub>3</sub> is trigonal planar with three identical B-Cl bonds at 120° to each other. The three bond dipoles are equal in magnitude and point symmetrically, so they cancel each other. The resultant dipole moment is zero.

!!! question "Aluminium trifluoride is insoluble in anhydrous HF but dissolves on addition of NaF. Aluminium trifluoride precipitates out of the resulting solution when gaseous BF<sub>3</sub> is bubbled through. Give reasons."

    * Anhydrous HF is a covalent, hydrogen bonded liquid and provides almost no free F<sup>-</sup> ions, so AlF<sub>3</sub> does not dissolve in it.
    * NaF supplies F<sup>-</sup> ions, which form the soluble complex Na<sub>3</sub>[AlF<sub>6</sub>]:

    $$AlF_3 + 3NaF &rarr; Na_3[AlF_6]$$

    * Boron, being smaller and more electron deficient, has a stronger tendency to form BF<sub>4</sub><sup>-</sup>. BF<sub>3</sub> removes F<sup>-</sup> from the complex and AlF<sub>3</sub> precipitates:

    $$Na_3[AlF_6] + 3BF_3 &rarr; 3NaBF_4 + AlF_3&darr;$$

!!! question "What happens when <br>(a) Borax is heated strongly, <br>(b) Boric acid is added to water, <br>(c) Aluminium is treated with dilute NaOH, <br>(d) BF<sub>3</sub> is reacted with ammonia?"

    (a) Borax loses water, swells up and finally forms a glassy bead of sodium metaborate and boric anhydride:

    $$Na_2B_4O_7.10H_2O \xrightarrow{\Delta} Na_2B_4O_7 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2NaBO_2 + B_2O_3$$

    (b) Boric acid accepts OH<sup>-</sup> from water and acts as a weak monobasic acid:

    $$B(OH)_3 + 2HOH &rarr; [B(OH)_4]^- + H_3O^+$$

    (c) Dihydrogen is liberated and sodium tetrahydroxoaluminate(III) is formed:

    $$2Al + 2NaOH + 6H_2O &rarr; 2Na^+[Al(OH)_4]^- + 3H_2$$

    (d) BF<sub>3</sub> (Lewis acid) accepts a lone pair from NH<sub>3</sub> (Lewis base) to form an adduct:

    $$F_3B + :NH_3 &rarr; F_3B \leftarrow NH_3$$

!!! question "What happens when hydrated alumina is treated with aqueous NaOH solution?"

    Alumina is amphoteric, so it dissolves in NaOH to form soluble sodium tetrahydroxoaluminate(III):

    $$Al_2O_{3(s)} + 2NaOH_{(aq)} + 3H_2O_{(l)} &rarr; 2Na[Al(OH)_4]_{(aq)}$$

!!! question "Give reasons: <br>(i) Conc. HNO<sub>3</sub> can be transported in aluminium container. <br>(ii) A mixture of dilute NaOH and aluminium pieces is used to open drain. <br>(iii) Aluminium alloys are used to make aircraft body. <br>(iv) Aluminium utensils should not be kept in water overnight. <br>(v) Aluminium wire is used to make transmission cables."

    (i) Concentrated HNO<sub>3</sub> makes aluminium passive by forming a thin protective layer of Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> on its surface, which stops further reaction.

    (ii) Aluminium reacts with NaOH to liberate H<sub>2</sub> gas with evolution of heat. The pressure of the gas and the heat help loosen and clear the clogging matter.

    (iii) Aluminium alloys are light, strong and resistant to corrosion, which are ideal properties for an aircraft body.

    (iv) Aluminium slowly reacts with water and dissolved oxygen (and salts in water) on long contact. The oxide/hydroxide formed dissolves partly, which corrodes the utensil and introduces aluminium, a toxic metal, into the water.

    (v) On a weight-to-weight basis, aluminium conducts electricity about twice as well as copper. It is also light, cheap and corrosion resistant.

!!! question "In some of the reactions thallium resembles aluminium, whereas in others it resembles group 1 metals. Support this statement by giving some evidences."

    * **Like aluminium:** Thallium shows the +3 oxidation state and forms compounds such as TlCl<sub>3</sub> and Tl<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>, and complexes like [TlF<sub>6</sub>]<sup>3-</sup>, similar to AlCl<sub>3</sub>, Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> and [AlF<sub>6</sub>]<sup>3-</sup>.
    * **Like group 1 metals:** Due to the inert pair effect, its most stable state is +1, like alkali metals. Tl<sub>2</sub>O and TlOH are strongly basic, and TlOH is soluble in water, just like NaOH and KOH. Tl<sup>+</sup> compounds such as TlCl are largely ionic.

!!! question "When metal X is treated with sodium hydroxide, a white precipitate (A) is obtained, which is soluble in excess of NaOH to give soluble complex (B). Compound (A) is soluble in dilute HCl to form compound (C). The compound (A) when heated strongly gives (D), which is used to extract metal. Identify (X), (A), (B), (C) and (D). Write suitable equations to support their identities."

    * X = Al, A = Al(OH)<sub>3</sub>, B = Na[Al(OH)<sub>4</sub>], C = AlCl<sub>3</sub>, D = Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>

    $$Al^{3+} + 3OH^- &rarr; Al(OH)_3&darr;\ (white)$$

    $$Al(OH)_3 + NaOH &rarr; Na[Al(OH)_4]$$

    $$Al(OH)_3 + 3HCl &rarr; AlCl_3 + 3H_2O$$

    $$2Al(OH)_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} Al_2O_3 + 3H_2O$$

    * Alumina (D) is electrolysed to extract aluminium metal.

!!! question "What do you understand by inert pair effect?"

    In the heavier p-block elements, poor shielding by the inner d and f electrons increases the effective nuclear charge on the ns<sup>2</sup> electrons. These electrons are held tightly and do not participate in bonding. This reluctance of the ns<sup>2</sup> pair to take part in bonding is called the inert pair effect. It makes the oxidation state two units less than the group oxidation state more stable down the group (e.g., Tl<sup>+</sup> over Tl<sup>3+</sup>).

!!! question "A certain salt X gives the following results. <br>(i) Its aqueous solution is alkaline to litmus. <br>(ii) It swells up to a glassy material Y on strong heating. <br>(iii) When conc. H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> is added to a hot solution of X, white crystal of an acid Z separates out. <br>Write equations for all the above reactions and identify X, Y and Z."

    * X = Borax (Na<sub>2</sub>B<sub>4</sub>O<sub>7</sub>.10H<sub>2</sub>O), Y = glassy mass of NaBO<sub>2</sub> + B<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>, Z = Orthoboric acid (H<sub>3</sub>BO<sub>3</sub>)

    $$(i)\ Na_2B_4O_7 + 7H_2O &rarr; 2NaOH + 4H_3BO_3$$

    $$(ii)\ Na_2B_4O_7.10H_2O \xrightarrow{\Delta} Na_2B_4O_7 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2NaBO_2 + B_2O_3$$

    $$(iii)\ Na_2B_4O_7 + H_2SO_4 + 5H_2O &rarr; Na_2SO_4 + 4H_3BO_3$$

!!! question "Write balanced equations for: <br>(i) BF<sub>3</sub> + LiH &rarr; <br>(ii) B<sub>2</sub>H<sub>6</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O &rarr; <br>(iii) NaH + B<sub>2</sub>H<sub>6</sub> &rarr; <br>(iv) H<sub>3</sub>BO<sub>3</sub> on heating <br>(v) Al + NaOH &rarr; <br>(vi) B<sub>2</sub>H<sub>6</sub> + NH<sub>3</sub> &rarr;"

    $$(i)\ 8BF_3 + 6LiH &rarr; B_2H_6 + 6LiBF_4$$

    $$(ii)\ B_2H_6 + 6H_2O &rarr; 2B(OH)_3 + 6H_2$$

    $$(iii)\ 2NaH + B_2H_6 &rarr; 2Na^+[BH_4]^-$$

    $$(iv)\ H_3BO_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} HBO_2 + H_2O;\ \ 2HBO_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} B_2O_3 + H_2O$$

    $$(v)\ 2Al + 2NaOH + 6H_2O &rarr; 2Na^+[Al(OH)_4]^- + 3H_2$$

    $$(vi)\ 3B_2H_6 + 6NH_3 &rarr; 3[BH_2(NH_3)_2]^+[BH_4]^- \xrightarrow{Heat} 2B_3N_3H_6 + 12H_2$$

## Multiple Choice Questions

!!! question "An aqueous solution of borax is <br>(a) neutral (b) amphoteric (c) basic (d) acidic"

    **(c) basic.** Borax hydrolyses to give the strong base NaOH and the weak acid H<sub>3</sub>BO<sub>3</sub>, so the solution is alkaline.

!!! question "Boric acid is polymeric due to <br>(a) its acidic nature (b) the presence of hydrogen bonds (c) its monobasic nature (d) its geometry"

    **(b) the presence of hydrogen bonds.** Planar BO<sub>3</sub> units are linked into layers by hydrogen bonds.

!!! question "The type of hybridisation of boron in diborane is <br>(a) sp (b) sp<sup>2</sup> (c) sp<sup>3</sup> (d) dsp<sup>2</sup>"

    **(c) sp<sup>3</sup>.** Each boron atom uses four sp<sup>3</sup> hybrid orbitals, one of which is empty.
