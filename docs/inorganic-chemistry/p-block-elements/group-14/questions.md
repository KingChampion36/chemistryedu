## Questions

!!! question "Write the resonance structures of CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2-</sup> and HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>."

    * **CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2-</sup>:** Carbon is bonded to three oxygen atoms. In each structure, one oxygen is double bonded and the other two carry a negative charge. The double bond can be placed on any of the three oxygens, giving three equivalent structures:

    $$O=C(-O^-)_2\ \leftrightarrow\ ^-O-C(=O)-O^-\ \leftrightarrow\ (^-O)_2C=O$$

    * **HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>:** One oxygen is attached to hydrogen and cannot take part in resonance. The double bond and the negative charge are shared by the other two oxygens, giving two structures:

    $$HO-C(=O)-O^-\ \leftrightarrow\ HO-C(-O^-)=O$$

!!! question "What is the state of hybridisation of carbon in <br>(a) CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2-</sup> (b) diamond (c) graphite?"

    (a) CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2-</sup>: sp<sup>2</sup> (trigonal planar, three σ bonds)

    (b) Diamond: sp<sup>3</sup> (each carbon bonded tetrahedrally to four others)

    (c) Graphite: sp<sup>2</sup> (each carbon bonded to three others in a hexagonal layer)

!!! question "How is excessive content of CO<sub>2</sub> responsible for global warming?"

    CO<sub>2</sub> allows sunlight to pass through but absorbs the infrared (heat) radiation given off by the earth's surface and re-emits part of it back to the earth. This traps heat in the atmosphere (the greenhouse effect). Burning of fossil fuels and decomposition of limestone for cement have increased atmospheric CO<sub>2</sub>, which increases the greenhouse effect and raises the temperature of the atmosphere, causing global warming.

!!! question "Explain the following reactions: <br>(a) Silicon is heated with methyl chloride at high temperature in the presence of copper; <br>(b) Silicon dioxide is treated with hydrogen fluoride; <br>(c) CO is heated with ZnO."

    (a) A mixture of methyl substituted chlorosilanes (MeSiCl<sub>3</sub>, Me<sub>2</sub>SiCl<sub>2</sub>, Me<sub>3</sub>SiCl and a little Me<sub>4</sub>Si) is formed. These are the starting materials for silicones:

    $$2CH_3Cl + Si \xrightarrow[573\ K]{Cu\ powder} (CH_3)_2SiCl_2$$

    (b) SiO<sub>2</sub> is attacked by HF to form silicon tetrafluoride; with excess HF, fluorosilicic acid is formed:

    $$SiO_2 + 4HF &rarr; SiF_4 + 2H_2O$$

    $$SiF_4 + 2HF &rarr; H_2SiF_6$$

    (c) CO reduces zinc oxide to zinc metal:

    $$ZnO_{(s)} + CO_{(g)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} Zn_{(s)} + CO_{2(g)}$$

!!! question "Give reasons: <br>(i) Graphite is used as lubricant. <br>(ii) Diamond is used as an abrasive."

    (i) Graphite has a layered structure in which the layers are held by weak van der Waals forces. The layers slide easily over one another, making graphite soft and slippery. It is also stable at high temperatures, so it is used as a dry lubricant where oil cannot be used.

    (ii) Diamond has a rigid three-dimensional network of strong C-C covalent bonds and is the hardest substance on earth. So it can grind and sharpen other hard materials and is used as an abrasive.

!!! question "What are allotropes? Sketch the structure of two allotropes of carbon namely diamond and graphite. What is the impact of structure on physical properties of two allotropes?"

    * Different forms of the same element having different physical properties but similar chemical properties are called **allotropes**.
    * **Diamond:** Each sp<sup>3</sup> carbon is tetrahedrally bonded to four other carbons (C-C = 154 pm), forming a rigid 3D network. Hence it is very hard, has a very high melting point and does not conduct electricity.
    * **Graphite:** Each sp<sup>2</sup> carbon is bonded to three others in planar hexagonal layers (C-C = 141.5 pm), with layers 340 pm apart held by van der Waals forces. Hence it is soft, slippery, and conducts electricity due to delocalised π electrons.
    * The structures of diamond and graphite are shown on the [Allotropes of Carbon](allotropes-of-carbon.md) page.

!!! question "(a) Classify following oxides as neutral, acidic, basic or amphoteric: <br>CO, B<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>, SiO<sub>2</sub>, CO<sub>2</sub>, Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>, PbO<sub>2</sub>, Tl<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> <br>(b) Write suitable chemical equations to show their nature."

    (a) Neutral: CO; Acidic: B<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>, SiO<sub>2</sub>, CO<sub>2</sub>; Amphoteric: Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>, PbO<sub>2</sub>; Basic: Tl<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>

    (b) Acidic oxides react with bases:

    $$B_2O_3 + 2NaOH &rarr; 2NaBO_2 + H_2O$$

    $$SiO_2 + 2NaOH &rarr; Na_2SiO_3 + H_2O$$

    $$CO_2 + 2NaOH &rarr; Na_2CO_3 + H_2O$$

    Amphoteric oxides react with both acids and bases:

    $$Al_2O_3 + 6HCl &rarr; 2AlCl_3 + 3H_2O$$

    $$Al_2O_3 + 2NaOH + 3H_2O &rarr; 2Na[Al(OH)_4]$$

    $$PbO_2 + 4HCl &rarr; PbCl_2 + Cl_2 + 2H_2O$$

    $$PbO_2 + 2NaOH &rarr; Na_2PbO_3 + H_2O$$

    Basic oxides react with acids:

    $$Tl_2O_3 + 6HCl &rarr; 2TlCl_3 + 3H_2O$$

    CO, being neutral, reacts with neither acids nor bases.

!!! question "Give one method for industrial and one for laboratory preparation of CO and CO<sub>2</sub> each."

    * **CO (industrial):** Passing steam over hot coke gives water gas:

    $$C_{(s)} + H_2O_{(g)} \xrightarrow{473-1273\ K} CO_{(g)} + H_{2(g)}$$

    * **CO (laboratory):** Dehydration of formic acid with conc. H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>:

    $$HCOOH \xrightarrow[conc.\ H_2SO_4]{373\ K} H_2O + CO$$

    * **CO<sub>2</sub> (industrial):** Heating limestone:

    $$CaCO_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} CaO + CO_2$$

    * **CO<sub>2</sub> (laboratory):** Action of dilute HCl on calcium carbonate:

    $$CaCO_{3(s)} + 2HCl_{(aq)} &rarr; CaCl_{2(aq)} + CO_{2(g)} + H_2O_{(l)}$$

!!! question "If the starting material for the manufacture of silicones is RSiCl<sub>3</sub>, write the structure of the product formed."

    * Hydrolysis of RSiCl<sub>3</sub> gives RSi(OH)<sub>3</sub>, which has three OH groups:

    $$RSiCl_3 + 3H_2O &rarr; RSi(OH)_3 + 3HCl$$

    * Condensation can occur through all three OH groups, so the chains get linked to one another. The product is a **cross-linked (three-dimensional) silicone**, in which each silicon carries one R group and is bonded to three bridging oxygen atoms:

    $$n\,RSi(OH)_3 \xrightarrow{-H_2O} [RSiO_{3/2}]_n\ \ (cross\ linked\ network)$$

!!! question "What do you understand by allotropy?"

    The existence of an element in two or more forms which have different physical properties but similar chemical properties is called allotropy. For example, carbon exists as diamond, graphite and fullerenes.
