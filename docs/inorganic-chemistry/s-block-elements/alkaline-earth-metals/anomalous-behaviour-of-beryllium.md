## Anomalous behaviour of beryllium

Beryllium, the first member of group 2, behaves differently from magnesium and the rest of the group. Its anomalous behaviour is due to:

* exceptionally small size of its atom and ion (ionic radius of Be<sup>2+</sup> = 31 pm).
* its high ionization enthalpy.
* absence of d-orbitals in its valence shell.

## Differences between beryllium and other alkaline earth metals

| Beryllium                                                                                                                  | Other Alkaline Earth Metals                                                                                  |
|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Due to high ionization enthalpy and small size, Be forms compounds which are largely covalent and easily hydrolysed.       | Other members form mainly ionic compounds.                                                                   |
| Be cannot have a coordination number of more than four, as its valence shell has only four orbitals (one 2s and three 2p). | Other members can have a coordination number of six by making use of d-orbitals.                             |
| The oxide and hydroxide of beryllium are amphoteric.                                                                       | The oxides and hydroxides of other members are basic.                                                        |
| Be does not combine directly with hydrogen. BeH<sub>2</sub> is made from BeCl<sub>2</sub> and LiAlH<sub>4</sub>.            | Other members combine with hydrogen on heating to form MH<sub>2</sub>.                                       |
| Beryllium halides are covalent and soluble in organic solvents.                                                            | Halides of other members are ionic.                                                                          |
| Be and its salts do not impart colour to the flame.                                                                        | Ca, Sr and Ba give characteristic flame colours.                                                             |
| Beryllium carbonate is unstable and can be kept only in an atmosphere of CO<sub>2</sub>.                                   | Other carbonates are stable at room temperature and decompose only on heating.                               |

## Diagonal relationship between beryllium and aluminium

* The ionic radius of Be<sup>2+</sup> is estimated to be 31 pm. The **charge/radius ratio** of Be<sup>2+</sup> is nearly the same as that of the Al<sup>3+</sup> ion.
* Hence, beryllium resembles aluminium in some ways. This similarity between the first element of a group and the second element of the next group is called a **diagonal relationship**.

## Similarities between beryllium and aluminium

* Like aluminium, beryllium is not readily attacked by acids because of the presence of an oxide film on the surface of the metal.
* Beryllium hydroxide dissolves in excess of alkali to give a beryllate ion, [Be(OH)<sub>4</sub>]<sup>2-</sup>, just as aluminium hydroxide gives the aluminate ion, [Al(OH)<sub>4</sub>]<sup>-</sup>.

!!! tip ""

    $$Be(OH)_2 + 2OH^- &rarr; [Be(OH)_4]^{2-}$$

    $$Al(OH)_3 + OH^- &rarr; [Al(OH)_4]^-$$

* The chlorides of both beryllium and aluminium have Cl<sup>-</sup> bridged chloride structures in the vapour phase.
* Both the chlorides are soluble in organic solvents and are strong **Lewis acids**. They are used as Friedel-Crafts catalysts.
* Beryllium and aluminium ions have a strong tendency to form complexes.

!!! example "Example"

    BeF<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup> and AlF<sub>6</sub><sup>3-</sup>

!!! note "Note"

    Lithium (group 1) shows a similar diagonal relationship with magnesium (group 2). In both cases the relationship arises from similar ionic sizes and/or charge/radius ratios.

## Questions

!!! question "Beryllium shows a maximum coordination number of four whereas magnesium can show a coordination number of six. Explain."

    Beryllium has only four orbitals (one 2s and three 2p) in its valence shell, so it can accept at most four electron pairs. Magnesium and the heavier members can use their d-orbitals as well, which allows a coordination number of six.

!!! question "Beryllium belongs to group 2 but resembles aluminium of group 13 in many properties. Why?"

    The charge/radius ratio of Be<sup>2+</sup> is nearly the same as that of Al<sup>3+</sup>. Properties such as polarizing power, covalent character of compounds and complex formation depend on this ratio. Hence Be shows a diagonal relationship with Al, for example amphoteric hydroxides, covalent bridged chlorides which act as Lewis acids, and protective oxide films.
