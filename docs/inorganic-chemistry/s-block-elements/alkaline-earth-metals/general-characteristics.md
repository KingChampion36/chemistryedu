# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../../index.md)  Alkaline Earth Metals | General Characteristics

Group 2 elements are known as alkaline earth metals. These include Beryllium(Be), Magnesium(Mg), Calcium(Ca), Strontium(Sr), Barium(Ba) and Radium(Ra).

## Group 2 Elements

* Group 2 elements follow the alkali metals in the periodic table.
* With the exception of beryllium, these elements are called **alkaline earth metals** because their oxides and hydroxides are alkaline in nature and these metal oxides are found in the earth's crust.
* Calcium and magnesium are the fifth and sixth most abundant elements in the earth's crust. Strontium and barium are much less abundant.
* Beryllium is rare and radium is the rarest of all, making up only about 10<sup>-10</sup> per cent of igneous rocks.
* The first element, beryllium, differs from the rest of the group and shows a **diagonal relationship** with aluminium.

## Electronic configuration of Alkaline Earth Metals

!!! tip "General electronic configuration is [noble gas]ns<sup>2</sup>"

    $$Be &rarr; 1s^2\ 2s^2\ or\ [He]2s^2$$

    $$Mg &rarr; 1s^2\ 2s^2\ 2p^6\ 3s^2\ or\ [Ne]3s^2$$

    $$Ca &rarr; 1s^2\ 2s^2\ 2p^6\ 3s^2\ 3p^6\ 4s^2\ or\ [Ar]4s^2$$

    $$Sr &rarr; [Kr]5s^2$$

    $$Ba &rarr; [Xe]6s^2$$

    $$Ra &rarr; [Rn]7s^2$$

* These elements have two electrons in the s-orbital of the valence shell. They lose both of them to form dipositive M<sup>2+</sup> ions.
* Like alkali metals, the compounds of alkaline earth metals are also predominantly ionic.

## Atomic and Ionic radii of Alkaline Earth Metals

* The atomic and ionic radii of alkaline earth metals are **smaller** than those of the corresponding alkali metals in the same period. This is due to the increased nuclear charge in these elements, which pulls the electrons closer.
* Within the group, atomic and ionic radii increase with increase in atomic number because a new shell is added at each step down the group.

| Element   | Metallic radius / pm  | Ionic radius M<sup>2+</sup> / pm  |
|-----------|-----------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Be        | 112                   | 31                                |
| Mg        | 160                   | 72                                |
| Ca        | 197                   | 100                               |
| Sr        | 215                   | 118                               |
| Ba        | 222                   | 135                               |
| Ra        | -                     | 148                               |

## Ionization Enthalpy of Alkaline Earth Metals

* The alkaline earth metals have low ionization enthalpies due to the fairly large size of their atoms.
* Ionization enthalpy **decreases down the group** because atomic size increases down the group.
* The **first** ionization enthalpies of alkaline earth metals are **higher** than those of the corresponding group 1 metals. This is due to their smaller size; the electron is held more tightly by the nucleus. Also, removing an electron from a filled ns<sup>2</sup> subshell requires more energy.
* Interestingly, the **second** ionization enthalpies of alkaline earth metals are **smaller** than those of the corresponding alkali metals. For an alkali metal, the second electron has to be removed from a stable noble gas core, while for an alkaline earth metal the second electron still comes from the valence s-orbital.

| Element   | &Delta;<sub>i</sub>H<sub>1</sub> / kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>  | &Delta;<sub>i</sub>H<sub>2</sub> / kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>  |
|-----------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Be        | 899                           | 1757                          |
| Mg        | 737                           | 1450                          |
| Ca        | 590                           | 1145                          |
| Sr        | 549                           | 1064                          |
| Ba        | 503                           | 965                           |
| Ra        | 509                           | 979                           |

## Hydration Enthalpy of Alkaline Earth Metal Cations

* Like alkali metal ions, the hydration enthalpies of alkaline earth metal ions **decrease** with increase in ionic size down the group.

!!! tip ""

    $$Be^{2+} > Mg^{2+} > Ca^{2+} > Sr^{2+} > Ba^{2+}$$

* The hydration enthalpies of alkaline earth metal ions are **larger** than those of alkali metal ions because of their smaller size and higher (+2) charge.
* Therefore, compounds of alkaline earth metals are more extensively hydrated than those of alkali metals.

!!! example "Example"

    MgCl<sub>2</sub> and CaCl<sub>2</sub> exist as MgCl<sub>2</sub>.6H<sub>2</sub>O and CaCl<sub>2</sub>.6H<sub>2</sub>O, while NaCl and KCl do not form such hydrates.

|-----------|-------------------------------------------|
| Be        | -2494                                     |
| Mg        | -1921                                     |
| Ca        | -1577                                     |
| Sr        | -1443                                     |
| Ba        | -1305                                     |
