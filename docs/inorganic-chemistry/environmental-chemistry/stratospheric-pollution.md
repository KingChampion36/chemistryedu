# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Environmental Chemistry | Stratospheric Pollution

Stratospheric pollution mainly refers to the depletion of the protective ozone layer of the upper atmosphere by chemicals such as chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).

* The upper stratosphere contains a considerable amount of ozone (O<sub>3</sub>).
* It protects us from harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation (&lambda; 255 nm) coming from the sun. This radiation causes skin cancer (melanoma) in humans, so it is important to maintain the ozone shield.

## Formation and Breakdown of Ozone

* Ozone in the stratosphere is formed by the action of UV radiation on dioxygen (O<sub>2</sub>) molecules.
* UV radiation splits molecular oxygen into free oxygen atoms, which then combine with molecular oxygen to form ozone:

!!! tip ""

    $$O_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{UV} O_{(g)} + O_{(g)}$$

    $$O_{(g)} + O_{2(g)} \overset{UV}{&#8652;} O_{3(g)}$$

* Ozone is thermodynamically unstable and decomposes back to molecular oxygen.
* So a **dynamic equilibrium** exists between the production and decomposition of ozone molecules. Any chemical that speeds up the decomposition reduces the ozone level.

## Depletion of Ozone by CFCs

* The main cause of ozone layer depletion is believed to be the release of **chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)**, also called **freons**.
* CFCs are non-reactive, non-flammable, non-toxic organic molecules. So they are used in refrigerators, air conditioners, production of plastic foam and by the electronic industry for cleaning computer parts.
* Once released, CFCs mix with the normal atmospheric gases and eventually reach the stratosphere.
* In the stratosphere, powerful UV radiation breaks them down, releasing a chlorine free radical:

!!! tip ""

    $$CF_2Cl_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{UV} \dot{Cl}_{(g)} + \dot{C}F_2Cl_{(g)}\ \ \ ...(i)$$

* The chlorine radical reacts with stratospheric ozone to form a chlorine monoxide radical and molecular oxygen:

!!! tip ""

    $$\dot{Cl}_{(g)} + O_{3(g)} &rarr; Cl\dot{O}_{(g)} + O_{2(g)}\ \ \ ...(ii)$$

* The chlorine monoxide radical reacts with atomic oxygen and regenerates the chlorine radical:

!!! tip ""

    $$Cl\dot{O}_{(g)} + O_{(g)} &rarr; \dot{Cl}_{(g)} + O_{2(g)}\ \ \ ...(iii)$$

!!! tip "Conclusion"

    * Chlorine radicals are continuously regenerated in reactions (ii) and (iii), so a single chlorine radical can destroy a very large number of ozone molecules (a chain reaction).
    * Thus CFCs act as transporting agents that continuously generate chlorine radicals in the stratosphere and damage the ozone layer.

## The Ozone Hole

* In the 1980s, atmospheric scientists working in Antarctica reported depletion of the ozone layer over the South Pole, commonly known as the **ozone hole**.
* A unique set of conditions is responsible for it.

### Summer: formation of chlorine sinks

* Chlorine monoxide reacts with nitrogen dioxide, and chlorine atoms react with methane. These form **chlorine sinks** (chlorine nitrate and HCl), which prevent much ozone depletion:

!!! tip ""

    $$Cl\dot{O}_{(g)} + NO_{2(g)} &rarr; ClONO_{2(g)}\ \ \ ...(iv)$$

    $$\dot{Cl}_{(g)} + CH_{4(g)} &rarr; \dot{C}H_{3(g)} + HCl_{(g)}\ \ \ ...(v)$$

### Winter: polar stratospheric clouds

* In winter, a special type of cloud called **polar stratospheric clouds** forms over Antarctica.
* These clouds provide a surface on which chlorine nitrate is hydrolysed to hypochlorous acid. Chlorine nitrate also reacts with HCl to give molecular chlorine:

!!! tip ""

    $$ClONO_{2(g)} + H_2O_{(g)} &rarr; HOCl_{(g)} + HNO_{3(g)}\ \ \ ...(vi)$$

    $$ClONO_{2(g)} + HCl_{(g)} &rarr; Cl_{2(g)} + HNO_{3(g)}\ \ \ ...(vii)$$

### Spring: release of chlorine radicals

* When sunlight returns to Antarctica in spring, the sun's warmth breaks up the clouds, and HOCl and Cl<sub>2</sub> are photolysed:

!!! tip ""

    $$HOCl_{(g)} \xrightarrow{h\nu} \dot{O}H_{(g)} + \dot{Cl}_{(g)}\ \ \ ...(viii)$$

    $$Cl_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{h\nu} 2\dot{Cl}_{(g)}\ \ \ ...(ix)$$

* The chlorine radicals so formed start the chain reaction for ozone depletion described above. This is why the ozone hole appears over Antarctica in spring.

## Effects of Depletion of the Ozone Layer

With a thinner ozone layer, more UV radiation filters into the troposphere. This leads to:

* Ageing of skin, cataract, sunburn and skin cancer.
* Killing of many phytoplanktons and damage to fish productivity.
* Harmful mutation of cells, since plant proteins are easily affected by UV radiation.
* More evaporation of surface water through the stomata of leaves, decreasing the moisture content of the soil.
* Damage to paints and fibres, causing them to fade faster.
