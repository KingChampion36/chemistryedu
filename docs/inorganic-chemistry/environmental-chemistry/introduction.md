# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Environmental Chemistry | Introduction

Environmental chemistry deals with the study of the origin, transport, reactions, effects and fates of chemical species in the environment.

* Environmental studies cover all the social, economical, biological, physical and chemical interrelations with our surroundings.
* Environmental chemistry focuses only on the chemical side of this picture: where a chemical comes from, how it moves, what it reacts with and what finally happens to it.

## Environmental Pollution

Environmental pollution is the effect of undesirable changes in our surroundings that have harmful effects on plants, animals and human beings.

* A substance which causes pollution is called a **pollutant**.
* Pollutants can be solid, liquid or gaseous. They are substances present in a concentration greater than their natural abundance, produced either by human activities or by natural happenings.
* An average human being needs nearly 12-15 times more air than food. So even small amounts of pollutants in the air become significant compared to similar levels in food.

### Types of Pollutants

#### 1. Degradable Pollutants

* These are broken down rapidly by natural processes.
* Example: Discarded vegetables.

#### 2. Slowly Degradable / Non-Degradable Pollutants

* These remain in the environment in an unchanged form for many decades and are difficult to remove once released.
* They cannot be degraded by natural processes and are harmful to living organisms.
* Examples: Dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane (DDT), plastic materials, heavy metals, many chemicals and nuclear wastes.

!!! note "Note"

    In the process of environmental pollution, pollutants originate from a source and are then transported by air or water, or are dumped into the soil by human beings.

## Regions of the Atmosphere

The atmosphere around the earth does not have the same thickness at all heights. It consists of concentric layers of air, each with a different density.

### 1. Troposphere

* It is the lowest region of the atmosphere in which human beings and other organisms live.
* It extends up to a height of about 10 km from sea level.
* It is a turbulent, dusty zone containing air, much water vapour and clouds. Strong air movement and cloud formation take place here.

### 2. Stratosphere

* It lies between 10 km and 50 km above sea level.
* It contains dinitrogen, dioxygen, ozone and little water vapour.
* The ozone present in the stratosphere prevents about 99.5 per cent of the sun's harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation from reaching the earth's surface, thereby protecting humans and other animals.

| Region | Height above sea level | Main constituents |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Troposphere | 0 to ~10 km | Air, much water vapour, clouds, dust |
| Stratosphere | 10 to 50 km | N<sub>2</sub>, O<sub>2</sub>, O<sub>3</sub>, little water vapour |

## Atmospheric Pollution

Atmospheric pollution is generally studied under two heads:

* **Tropospheric pollution:** caused by undesirable solid or gaseous particles in the lower atmosphere (oxides of sulphur, nitrogen and carbon, hydrocarbons, particulates, smog).
* **Stratospheric pollution:** mainly the depletion of the protective ozone layer by chemicals such as chlorofluorocarbons.
