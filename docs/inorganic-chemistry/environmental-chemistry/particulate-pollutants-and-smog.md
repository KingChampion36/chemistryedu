# [![ChemistryEdu Logo](../../images/favicon.svg){ align=left, width=3.8% }](../../index.md)  Environmental Chemistry | Particulate Pollutants and Smog

Particulate pollutants are the minute solid particles or liquid droplets in air. They are present in vehicle emissions, smoke particles from fires, dust particles and ash from industries.

## Particulate Pollutants

Particulates in the atmosphere may be **viable** or **non-viable**.

### 1. Viable Particulates

* These are minute living organisms dispersed in the atmosphere, e.g., bacteria, fungi, moulds, algae etc.
* Human beings are allergic to some of the fungi found in air. These particulates can also cause plant diseases.

### 2. Non-Viable Particulates

Non-viable particulates are classified according to their nature and size:

#### (a) Smoke

* Solid particles, or a mixture of solid and liquid particles, formed during combustion of organic matter.
* Examples: Cigarette smoke, smoke from burning of fossil fuel, garbage and dry leaves, oil smoke.

#### (b) Dust

* Fine solid particles (over 1 &mu;m in diameter) produced during crushing, grinding and attrition of solid materials.
* Examples: Sand from sand blasting, saw dust from wood works, pulverized coal, cement and fly ash from factories, dust storms.

#### (c) Mists

* Produced by particles of spray liquids and by condensation of vapours in air.
* Examples: Sulphuric acid mist, and herbicides and insecticides that miss their targets and travel through air to form mists.

#### (d) Fumes

* Obtained by condensation of vapours during sublimation, distillation, boiling and several other chemical reactions.
* Generally, organic solvents, metals and metallic oxides form fume particles.

### Effects of Particulate Pollutants

* The effect depends largely on particle size. Air-borne particles such as dust, fumes and mist are dangerous for human health.
* Particles bigger than 5 microns are likely to lodge in the nasal passage, whereas particles of about 1.0 micron enter the lungs easily.
* **Lead** used to be a major air pollutant from vehicles running on leaded petrol. It interferes with the development and maturation of red blood cells. This problem has largely been overcome in Indian cities by using unleaded petrol.

## Smog

The word smog is derived from **smoke** and **fog**. It is the most common example of air pollution in many cities throughout the world. There are two types of smog:

| Property | Classical smog | Photochemical smog |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Climate | Cool, humid | Warm, dry and sunny |
| Composition | Smoke, fog and SO<sub>2</sub> | O<sub>3</sub>, NO, acrolein, formaldehyde, PAN |
| Origin | Burning of coal and fuels in humid air | Action of sunlight on unsaturated hydrocarbons and nitrogen oxides from automobiles and factories |
| Chemical nature | Reducing mixture | High concentration of oxidising agents |
| Also called | Reducing smog | Oxidising smog |

## Formation of Photochemical Smog

* Burning of fossil fuels releases hydrocarbons (unburnt fuels) and nitric oxide (NO) into the troposphere.
* When these build up to sufficiently high levels, a chain reaction occurs due to their interaction with sunlight, in which NO is converted into NO<sub>2</sub>.
* NO<sub>2</sub> absorbs energy from sunlight and breaks up into nitric oxide and a free oxygen atom:

!!! tip ""

    $$NO_{2(g)} \xrightarrow{h\nu} NO_{(g)} + O_{(g)}\ \ \ ...(i)$$

* Oxygen atoms are very reactive and combine with O<sub>2</sub> in air to produce ozone:

!!! tip ""

    $$O_{(g)} + O_{2(g)} &#8652; O_{3(g)}\ \ \ ...(ii)$$

* The ozone formed reacts rapidly with the NO formed in reaction (i) to regenerate NO<sub>2</sub>. NO<sub>2</sub> is a brown gas and at high enough levels it contributes to haze:

!!! tip ""

    $$NO_{(g)} + O_{3(g)} &rarr; NO_{2(g)} + O_{2(g)}\ \ \ ...(iii)$$

* Ozone is a toxic gas. Both NO<sub>2</sub> and O<sub>3</sub> are strong oxidising agents and react with unburnt hydrocarbons in the polluted air to produce chemicals such as formaldehyde, acrolein and peroxyacetyl nitrate (PAN):

!!! tip ""

    $$3CH_4 + 2O_3 &rarr; 3CH_2{=}O + 3H_2O$$

    $$Formaldehyde:\ HCHO\ \ \ \ Acrolein:\ CH_2{=}CH{-}CHO\ \ \ \ PAN:\ CH_3COOONO_2$$

!!! note "Note"

    PAN is peroxyacetyl nitrate, CH<sub>3</sub>C(=O)OONO<sub>2</sub>. It contains an acetyl group joined through a peroxide (-O-O-) link to an NO<sub>2</sub> group.

## Effects of Photochemical Smog

* The common components are ozone, nitric oxide, acrolein, formaldehyde and PAN.
* Ozone and PAN are powerful eye irritants.
* Ozone and nitric oxide irritate the nose and throat. Their high concentration causes headache, chest pain, dryness of the throat, cough and difficulty in breathing.
* Photochemical smog causes cracking of rubber and extensive damage to plant life.
* It also corrodes metals, stones, building materials, rubber and painted surfaces.

## Control of Photochemical Smog

* If the **primary precursors** (NO<sub>2</sub> and hydrocarbons) are controlled, the **secondary precursors** (ozone and PAN) are automatically reduced.
* Catalytic converters in automobiles prevent the release of nitrogen oxide and hydrocarbons into the atmosphere.
* Certain plants, e.g., Pinus, Juniparus, Quercus, Pyrus and Vitis, can metabolise nitrogen oxide. Planting them can help.
