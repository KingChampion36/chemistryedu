    Group 15 Elements | Physical and Atomic Properties#

    Introduction#

    • The elements of group 15 are Nitrogen(N), Phosphorus(P), Arsenic(As), Antimony(Sb) and Bismuth(Bi).
    • Group 15 is also known as the nitrogen family.
    • On moving down the group, the character of the elements changes from non-metallic to metallic through metalloidic.
    • Nitrogen and phosphorus are non-metals, arsenic and antimony are metalloids and bismuth is a typical metal.

    Occurrence#

    • Nitrogen: Molecular nitrogen makes up about 78% of the atmosphere by volume. In the earth's crust, it occurs as sodium nitrate, NaNO3 (Chile saltpetre) and potassium nitrate, KNO3 (Indian saltpetre). It is also present in proteins of plants and animals.
    • Phosphorus: It occurs in minerals of the apatite family, Ca9(PO4)6.CaX2 (X = F, Cl or OH), for example fluorapatite Ca9(PO4)6.CaF2. These are the main components of phosphate rocks.
    • Phosphorus is an essential constituent of animal and plant matter. It is present in bones and in living cells. Phosphoproteins are present in milk and eggs.
    • Arsenic, antimony and bismuth are found mainly as sulphide minerals.

    Atomic Properties of Group 15 Elements#

    1. Electronic Configuration#

    • The general valence shell electronic configuration of group 15 elements is ns2np3.
    • The s orbital is completely filled and the three p orbitals are exactly half-filled. This makes their electronic configuration extra stable.

    2. Atomic and Ionic Radii#

    • Covalent and ionic (in a particular state) radii increase down the group because a new shell is added at each step.
    • There is a large increase in covalent radius from N to P.
    • From As to Bi, however, the increase in covalent radius is only small. This is due to the presence of completely filled d and/or f orbitals in the heavier members, which shield the nuclear charge poorly.

    3. Ionisation Enthalpy#

    • Ionisation enthalpy decreases down the group due to the gradual increase in atomic size.
    • Because of the extra stable half-filled p orbitals and smaller size, the ionisation enthalpy of group 15 elements is much greater than that of group 14 elements in the corresponding periods.

    4. Electronegativity#

    • Electronegativity generally decreases down the group as the atomic size increases.
    • Amongst the heavier elements (As, Sb, Bi), the difference in electronegativity is not very pronounced.

    Physical Properties of Group 15 Elements#

    1. Physical State#

    • All the elements of this group are polyatomic.
    • Dinitrogen is a diatomic gas while all the other elements are solids.

    2. Metallic Character#

    • Metallic character increases down the group. Nitrogen and phosphorus are non-metals, arsenic and antimony are metalloids and bismuth is a metal.
    • This is due to the decrease in ionisation enthalpy and increase in atomic size down the group.

    3. Melting and Boiling Points#

    • Boiling points, in general, increase from top to bottom in the group.
    • Melting point increases up to arsenic and then decreases up to bismuth.

    4. Allotropy#

    • All the elements except nitrogen show allotropy. For example, phosphorus exists as white, red and black phosphorus.

    Questions#

    Why is the ionisation enthalpy of group 15 elements much higher than that of group 14 elements in the corresponding periods?

    Group 15 elements have the configuration ns2np3, in which the p orbitals are exactly half-filled. This half-filled configuration is extra stable. Also, group 15 atoms are smaller than the corresponding group 14 atoms. Hence, more energy is needed to remove an electron, and their ionisation enthalpy is much higher.

    Why is there only a small increase in covalent radius from As to Bi, whereas the increase from N to P is large?

    Arsenic, antimony and bismuth contain completely filled d orbitals (and bismuth also has filled 4f orbitals). These inner d and f electrons shield the nucleus poorly, so the effective nuclear charge felt by the valence electrons remains high. As a result, the size increases only slightly from As to Bi.

    Why does metallic character increase down group 15?

    Down the group, atomic size increases and ionisation enthalpy decreases. So the heavier atoms lose electrons more easily, which is a characteristic of metals. Thus nitrogen and phosphorus are non-metals, arsenic and antimony are metalloids, and bismuth is a metal.
