    Group 14 Elements | Physical and Atomic Properties#

    Introduction#

    • The elements of group 14 are Carbon(C), Silicon(Si), Germanium(Ge), Tin(Sn) and Lead(Pb). This group is known as the carbon family.

    Occurrence#

    • Carbon is the seventeenth most abundant element by mass in the earth's crust. It occurs in the free state as coal, graphite and diamond, and in the combined state as metal carbonates, hydrocarbons and carbon dioxide (0.03% in air).
    • Carbon is the most versatile element. Combined with hydrogen, oxygen, chlorine and sulphur, it forms an enormous range of materials, from living tissues to drugs and plastics. Organic chemistry is devoted to carbon compounds.
    • Naturally occurring carbon contains two stable isotopes, 12C and 13C. A third, radioactive isotope 14C (half-life 5770 years) is used for radiocarbon dating.
    • Silicon is the second most abundant element (27.7% by mass) in the earth's crust. It occurs as silica and silicates and is an important component of ceramics, glass and cement.
    • Germanium exists only in traces. Tin occurs mainly as cassiterite (SnO2) and lead as galena (PbS).
    • Ultrapure germanium and silicon are used to make transistors and semiconductor devices.

    Atomic Properties of Group 14 Elements#

    1. Electronic Configuration#

    • General valence shell electronic configuration: ns2np2
    • As in group 13, the inner core of the electronic configuration differs from element to element.

    2. Covalent Radius#

    • There is a considerable increase in covalent radius from C to Si.
    • From Si to Pb, only a small increase in radius is observed. This is due to the presence of completely filled d and f orbitals in the heavier members, which shield the outer electrons poorly.

    3. Ionization Enthalpy#

    • The first ionization enthalpy of group 14 elements is higher than that of the corresponding group 13 elements.
    • In general, ionization enthalpy decreases down the group.
    • The small decrease in ΔiH from Si to Ge to Sn, and the slight increase from Sn to Pb, are due to the poor shielding effect of the intervening d and f orbitals together with the increase in atomic size.

    4. Electronegativity#

    • Due to their smaller size, group 14 elements are slightly more electronegative than group 13 elements.
    • The electronegativity values of the elements from Si to Pb are almost the same.

    Physical Properties of Group 14 Elements#

    • All group 14 elements are solids.
    • Carbon and silicon are non-metals, germanium is a metalloid, whereas tin and lead are soft metals with low melting points.
    • The melting and boiling points of group 14 elements are much higher than those of the corresponding group 13 elements.

    Questions#

    Explain why is there a phenomenal decrease in ionization enthalpy from carbon to silicon?

    Carbon is very small (covalent radius 77 pm), so its valence electrons are held very strongly by the nucleus. In silicon, a new shell is added and the covalent radius jumps to 118 pm. The valence electrons are much farther from the nucleus and are also shielded by more inner electrons. Hence the ionization enthalpy drops sharply from 1086 kJ mol-1 (C) to 786 kJ mol-1 (Si).

    Why are group 14 elements slightly more electronegative than group 13 elements?

    In a period, the nuclear charge increases by one unit from group 13 to group 14 while the electrons enter the same shell. The atoms of group 14 are therefore smaller and attract shared electrons more strongly, making them slightly more electronegative.
