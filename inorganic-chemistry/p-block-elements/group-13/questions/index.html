    Group 13 Elements | Questions#

    Questions#

    Discuss the pattern of variation in the oxidation states from B to Tl.

    • All group 13 elements have the valence configuration ns2np1, so the group oxidation state is +3.
    • Boron and aluminium show only the +3 oxidation state.
    • Ga, In and Tl show both +1 and +3 oxidation states. Due to the inert pair effect, the stability of the +1 state increases down the group:
    \[Al\ <\ Ga\ <\ In\ <\ Tl\]
    • In thallium, +1 is the predominant oxidation state and Tl(III) compounds are strong oxidising agents.

    How can you explain higher stability of BCl3 as compared to TlCl3?

    Boron has no d or f electrons in its inner core, so both of its 2s electrons take part in bonding and the +3 state is stable. In thallium, poor shielding by the 4f and 5d electrons holds the 6s2 pair tightly (inert pair effect). So Tl prefers the +1 state, and TlCl3 tends to change into the more stable TlCl. Hence BCl3 is more stable than TlCl3.

    Consider the compounds, BCl3 and CCl4. How will they behave with water? Justify.

    • BCl3 is electron deficient. Boron accepts a lone pair from the oxygen of water, and the compound is hydrolysed to boric acid:
    \[BCl_3 + 3H_2O &rarr; B(OH)_3 + 3HCl\]
    • In CCl4, carbon already has a complete octet and has no vacant d orbitals to accept a lone pair from water. So CCl4 is not hydrolysed by water.

    Is boric acid a protic acid? Explain.

    No. Boric acid does not donate H+ ions by itself. It acts as a Lewis acid by accepting an OH- ion from water, which releases H3O+:

    \[B(OH)_3 + 2HOH &rarr; [B(OH)_4]^- + H_3O^+\]

    Explain what happens when boric acid is heated.

    On heating above 370 K, orthoboric acid first forms metaboric acid, which on stronger heating forms boric oxide:

    \[H_3BO_3 \xrightarrow{370\ K} HBO_2 + H_2O\]
    \[2HBO_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} B_2O_3 + H_2O\]

    Describe the shapes of BF3 and BH4-. Assign the hybridisation of boron in these species.

    • BF3: Boron forms three bond pairs and has no lone pair. It is sp2 hybridised and the molecule is trigonal planar (bond angle 120°).
    • BH4-: Boron forms four bond pairs (one of them by accepting H-). It is sp3 hybridised and the ion is tetrahedral (bond angle 109.5°).

    Write reactions to justify amphoteric nature of aluminium.

    Aluminium dissolves both in acids and in alkalies, liberating dihydrogen:

    \[2Al_{(s)} + 6HCl_{(aq)} &rarr; 2Al^{3+}_{(aq)} + 6Cl^-_{(aq)} + 3H_{2(g)}\]
    \[2Al_{(s)} + 2NaOH_{(aq)} + 6H_2O_{(l)} &rarr; 2Na^+[Al(OH)_4]^-_{(aq)} + 3H_{2(g)}\]

    What are electron deficient compounds? Are BCl3 and SiCl4 electron deficient species? Explain.

    • Compounds in which the central atom has less than eight electrons in its valence shell are called electron deficient compounds.
    • BCl3 is electron deficient: boron has only six electrons around it and acts as a Lewis acid.
    • SiCl4 is not electron deficient: silicon has a complete octet (it is an electron precise molecule). However, it can still accept lone pairs because silicon has vacant 3d orbitals.

    Suggest reasons why the B-F bond lengths in BF3 (130 pm) and BF4- (143 pm) differ.

    • In BF3, boron is sp2 hybridised and has a vacant 2p orbital. A filled 2p orbital of fluorine donates electron density into it (pπ-pπ back bonding). This gives the B-F bond partial double bond character, so it is shorter (130 pm).
    • In BF4-, boron is sp3 hybridised and has no vacant p orbital, so no back bonding is possible. The B-F bonds are pure single bonds and hence longer (143 pm).

    If B-Cl bond has a dipole moment, explain why BCl3 molecule has zero dipole moment.

    BCl3 is trigonal planar with three identical B-Cl bonds at 120° to each other. The three bond dipoles are equal in magnitude and point symmetrically, so they cancel each other. The resultant dipole moment is zero.

    Aluminium trifluoride is insoluble in anhydrous HF but dissolves on addition of NaF. Aluminium trifluoride precipitates out of the resulting solution when gaseous BF3 is bubbled through. Give reasons.

    • Anhydrous HF is a covalent, hydrogen bonded liquid and provides almost no free F- ions, so AlF3 does not dissolve in it.
    • NaF supplies F- ions, which form the soluble complex Na3[AlF6]:
    \[AlF_3 + 3NaF &rarr; Na_3[AlF_6]\]
    • Boron, being smaller and more electron deficient, has a stronger tendency to form BF4-. BF3 removes F- from the complex and AlF3 precipitates:
    \[Na_3[AlF_6] + 3BF_3 &rarr; 3NaBF_4 + AlF_3&darr;\]

    What happens when
    (a) Borax is heated strongly,
    (b) Boric acid is added to water,
    (c) Aluminium is treated with dilute NaOH,
    (d) BF3 is reacted with ammonia?

    (a) Borax loses water, swells up and finally forms a glassy bead of sodium metaborate and boric anhydride:

    \[Na_2B_4O_7.10H_2O \xrightarrow{\Delta} Na_2B_4O_7 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2NaBO_2 + B_2O_3\]

    (b) Boric acid accepts OH- from water and acts as a weak monobasic acid:

    \[B(OH)_3 + 2HOH &rarr; [B(OH)_4]^- + H_3O^+\]

    (c) Dihydrogen is liberated and sodium tetrahydroxoaluminate(III) is formed:

    \[2Al + 2NaOH + 6H_2O &rarr; 2Na^+[Al(OH)_4]^- + 3H_2\]

    (d) BF3 (Lewis acid) accepts a lone pair from NH3 (Lewis base) to form an adduct:

    \[F_3B + :NH_3 &rarr; F_3B \leftarrow NH_3\]

    What happens when hydrated alumina is treated with aqueous NaOH solution?

    Alumina is amphoteric, so it dissolves in NaOH to form soluble sodium tetrahydroxoaluminate(III):

    \[Al_2O_{3(s)} + 2NaOH_{(aq)} + 3H_2O_{(l)} &rarr; 2Na[Al(OH)_4]_{(aq)}\]

    Give reasons:
    (i) Conc. HNO3 can be transported in aluminium container.
    (ii) A mixture of dilute NaOH and aluminium pieces is used to open drain.
    (iii) Aluminium alloys are used to make aircraft body.
    (iv) Aluminium utensils should not be kept in water overnight.
    (v) Aluminium wire is used to make transmission cables.

    (i) Concentrated HNO3 makes aluminium passive by forming a thin protective layer of Al2O3 on its surface, which stops further reaction.

    (ii) Aluminium reacts with NaOH to liberate H2 gas with evolution of heat. The pressure of the gas and the heat help loosen and clear the clogging matter.

    (iii) Aluminium alloys are light, strong and resistant to corrosion, which are ideal properties for an aircraft body.

    (iv) Aluminium slowly reacts with water and dissolved oxygen (and salts in water) on long contact. The oxide/hydroxide formed dissolves partly, which corrodes the utensil and introduces aluminium, a toxic metal, into the water.

    (v) On a weight-to-weight basis, aluminium conducts electricity about twice as well as copper. It is also light, cheap and corrosion resistant.

    In some of the reactions thallium resembles aluminium, whereas in others it resembles group 1 metals. Support this statement by giving some evidences.

    • Like aluminium: Thallium shows the +3 oxidation state and forms compounds such as TlCl3 and Tl2O3, and complexes like [TlF6]3-, similar to AlCl3, Al2O3 and [AlF6]3-.
    • Like group 1 metals: Due to the inert pair effect, its most stable state is +1, like alkali metals. Tl2O and TlOH are strongly basic, and TlOH is soluble in water, just like NaOH and KOH. Tl+ compounds such as TlCl are largely ionic.

    When metal X is treated with sodium hydroxide, a white precipitate (A) is obtained, which is soluble in excess of NaOH to give soluble complex (B). Compound (A) is soluble in dilute HCl to form compound (C). The compound (A) when heated strongly gives (D), which is used to extract metal. Identify (X), (A), (B), (C) and (D). Write suitable equations to support their identities.

    • X = Al, A = Al(OH)3, B = Na[Al(OH)4], C = AlCl3, D = Al2O3
    \[Al^{3+} + 3OH^- &rarr; Al(OH)_3&darr;\ (white)\]
    \[Al(OH)_3 + NaOH &rarr; Na[Al(OH)_4]\]
    \[Al(OH)_3 + 3HCl &rarr; AlCl_3 + 3H_2O\]
    \[2Al(OH)_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} Al_2O_3 + 3H_2O\]
    • Alumina (D) is electrolysed to extract aluminium metal.

    What do you understand by inert pair effect?

    In the heavier p-block elements, poor shielding by the inner d and f electrons increases the effective nuclear charge on the ns2 electrons. These electrons are held tightly and do not participate in bonding. This reluctance of the ns2 pair to take part in bonding is called the inert pair effect. It makes the oxidation state two units less than the group oxidation state more stable down the group (e.g., Tl+ over Tl3+).

    A certain salt X gives the following results.
    (i) Its aqueous solution is alkaline to litmus.
    (ii) It swells up to a glassy material Y on strong heating.
    (iii) When conc. H2SO4 is added to a hot solution of X, white crystal of an acid Z separates out.
    Write equations for all the above reactions and identify X, Y and Z.

    • X = Borax (Na2B4O7.10H2O), Y = glassy mass of NaBO2 + B2O3, Z = Orthoboric acid (H3BO3)
    \[(i)\ Na_2B_4O_7 + 7H_2O &rarr; 2NaOH + 4H_3BO_3\]
    \[(ii)\ Na_2B_4O_7.10H_2O \xrightarrow{\Delta} Na_2B_4O_7 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2NaBO_2 + B_2O_3\]
    \[(iii)\ Na_2B_4O_7 + H_2SO_4 + 5H_2O &rarr; Na_2SO_4 + 4H_3BO_3\]

    Multiple Choice Questions#

    An aqueous solution of borax is
    (a) neutral (b) amphoteric (c) basic (d) acidic

    (c) basic. Borax hydrolyses to give the strong base NaOH and the weak acid H3BO3, so the solution is alkaline.

    Boric acid is polymeric due to
    (a) its acidic nature (b) the presence of hydrogen bonds (c) its monobasic nature (d) its geometry

    (b) the presence of hydrogen bonds. Planar BO3 units are linked into layers by hydrogen bonds.

    The type of hybridisation of boron in diborane is
    (a) sp (b) sp2 (c) sp3 (d) dsp2

    (c) sp3. Each boron atom uses four sp3 hybrid orbitals, one of which is empty.
