    Alkaline Earth Metals | Chemical Characteristics#

    In this article, we will discuss the chemical properties of alkaline earth metals like reactivity towards air and water, halogens, hydrogen and acids, reducing nature, solutions in liquid ammonia and their uses.

    Note

    Alkaline earth metals are less reactive than the alkali metals because of their smaller size and higher ionization enthalpy. Their reactivity increases on going down the group.

    Reactivity towards air and water#

    • Beryllium and magnesium are kinetically inert towards oxygen and water because a protective oxide film forms on their surface.
    • However, powdered beryllium burns brilliantly on ignition in air to give BeO and Be3N2.
    • Magnesium is more electropositive and burns with dazzling brilliance in air to give MgO and Mg3N2.
    \[2Mg + O_2 &rarr; 2MgO\]
    \[3Mg + N_2 &rarr; Mg_3N_2\]
    • Calcium, strontium and barium are readily attacked by air to form the oxide and nitride.
    • Calcium, strontium and barium also react with water with increasing vigour, even in cold, to form hydroxides.
    \[M + 2H_2O &rarr; M(OH)_2 + H_2 &uarr;\]

    Here, M = Ca, Sr, Ba

    Reactivity towards halogens#

    • All alkaline earth metals combine with halogens at elevated temperatures to form their halides.
    \[M + X_2 &rarr; MX_2\]

    Here, X = F, Cl, Br, I

    • Thermal decomposition of (NH4)2BeF4 is the best route for the preparation of BeF2.
    • BeCl2 is conveniently made from the oxide by heating it with carbon and chlorine.
    \[BeO + C + Cl_2 &#8652; BeCl_2 + CO\]
    \[[600 - 800\ K]\]

    Reactivity towards hydrogen#

    • All the alkaline earth metals except beryllium combine with hydrogen on heating to form their hydrides, MH2.
    \[M + H_2 &rarr; MH_2\]
    • BeH2 cannot be made directly. It is prepared by the reaction of BeCl2 with LiAlH4.
    \[2BeCl_2 + LiAlH_4 &rarr; 2BeH_2 + LiCl + AlCl_3\]

    Reactivity towards acids#

    • The alkaline earth metals readily react with acids, liberating dihydrogen.
    \[M + 2HCl &rarr; MCl_2 + H_2 &uarr;\]

    Reducing Nature#

    • Like alkali metals, the alkaline earth metals are strong reducing agents. This is indicated by the large negative values of their standard reduction potentials.
    • However, their reducing power is less than that of the corresponding alkali metals.
    Element E0 / V for (M2+/M)
    Be -1.97
    Mg -2.36
    Ca -2.84
    Sr -2.89
    Ba -2.92
    Ra -2.92

    Exception

    Beryllium has a less negative E0 value compared to other alkaline earth metals. Even so, it is a reducing agent because of the large hydration energy of the small Be2+ ion, which is partly offset by the relatively large atomization enthalpy of the metal.

    Solutions in Liquid Ammonia#

    • Like alkali metals, the alkaline earth metals dissolve in liquid ammonia to give deep blue-black solutions containing ammoniated ions.
    \[M + (x + y)NH_3 &rarr; [M(NH_3)_x]^{2+} + 2[e(NH_3)_y]^-\]
    • From these solutions, the ammoniates, [M(NH3)6]2+, can be recovered.

    Uses of Alkaline Earth Metals#

    1. Beryllium#

    • Beryllium is used in the manufacture of alloys. Copper-beryllium alloys are used to prepare high strength springs.
    • Metallic beryllium is used for making windows of X-ray tubes.

    2. Magnesium#

    • Magnesium forms alloys with aluminium, zinc, manganese and tin. Magnesium-aluminium alloys, being light in mass, are used in aircraft construction.
    • Magnesium powder and ribbon are used in flash powders and bulbs, incendiary bombs and signals.
    • A suspension of magnesium hydroxide in water, called milk of magnesia, is used as an antacid in medicine.
    • Magnesium carbonate is an ingredient of toothpaste.

    3. Calcium and Barium#

    • Calcium is used in the extraction of metals from oxides which are difficult to reduce with carbon.
    • Calcium and barium, owing to their reactivity with oxygen and nitrogen at elevated temperatures, are used to remove air from vacuum tubes.

    4. Radium#

    • Radium salts are used in radiotherapy, for example, in the treatment of cancer.
