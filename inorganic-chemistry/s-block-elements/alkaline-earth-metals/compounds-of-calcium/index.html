    Alkaline Earth Metals | Some Important Compounds of Calcium#

    In this article, we will discuss some important compounds of calcium like quick lime, slaked lime, calcium carbonate, plaster of paris and cement, and the biological importance of magnesium and calcium.

    Calcium Oxide or Quick Lime (CaO)#

    Preparation of Calcium Oxide#

    • Calcium oxide is prepared on a commercial scale by heating limestone (CaCO3) in a rotary kiln at 1070-1270 K.
    \[CaCO_3 + Heat &#8652; CaO + CO_2\]
    • The reaction is reversible, so carbon dioxide is removed as soon as it is produced. This allows the reaction to proceed to completion (Le Chatelier's principle).

    Properties of Calcium Oxide#

    • Calcium oxide is a white amorphous solid with a melting point of 2870 K.
    • On exposure to the atmosphere, it absorbs moisture and carbon dioxide.
    \[CaO + H_2O &rarr; Ca(OH)_2\]
    \[CaO + CO_2 &rarr; CaCO_3\]
    • Adding a limited amount of water breaks up the lump of lime. This process is called slaking of lime.
    • Quick lime slaked with soda gives solid soda lime.
    • Being a basic oxide, CaO combines with acidic oxides at high temperature.
    \[CaO + SiO_2 &rarr; CaSiO_3\]
    \[6CaO + P_4O_{10} &rarr; 2Ca_3(PO_4)_2\]

    Uses of Calcium Oxide#

    • It is an important primary material for manufacturing cement and is the cheapest form of alkali.
    • It is used in the manufacture of sodium carbonate from caustic soda.
    • It is used in the purification of sugar and in the manufacture of dye stuffs.

    Calcium Hydroxide or Slaked Lime (Ca(OH)2)#

    • Calcium hydroxide is prepared by adding water to quick lime, CaO.
    \[CaO + H_2O &rarr; Ca(OH)_2\]

    Properties of Calcium Hydroxide#

    • It is a white amorphous powder and is sparingly soluble in water.
    • Its aqueous solution is known as lime water and a suspension of slaked lime in water is known as milk of lime.
    • When carbon dioxide is passed through lime water, it turns milky due to the formation of insoluble calcium carbonate.
    \[Ca(OH)_2 + CO_2 &rarr; CaCO_3 &darr; + H_2O\]
    • On passing excess of carbon dioxide, the precipitate dissolves to form soluble calcium hydrogencarbonate and the milkiness disappears.
    \[CaCO_3 + CO_2 + H_2O &rarr; Ca(HCO_3)_2\]
    • Milk of lime reacts with chlorine to form hypochlorite, a constituent of bleaching powder.
    \[2Ca(OH)_2 + 2Cl_2 &rarr; CaCl_2 + Ca(OCl)_2 + 2H_2O\]

    Uses of Calcium Hydroxide#

    • It is used in the preparation of mortar, a building material.
    • It is used in white wash due to its disinfectant nature.
    • It is used in glass making, in the tanning industry, for the preparation of bleaching powder and for the purification of sugar.

    Calcium Carbonate (CaCO3)#

    • Calcium carbonate occurs in nature in several forms like limestone, chalk and marble.

    Preparation of Calcium Carbonate#

    • It can be prepared by passing carbon dioxide through slaked lime or by adding sodium carbonate to calcium chloride.
    \[Ca(OH)_2 + CO_2 &rarr; CaCO_3 + H_2O\]
    \[CaCl_2 + Na_2CO_3 &rarr; CaCO_3 + 2NaCl\]
    • Excess of carbon dioxide should be avoided, since it leads to the formation of water soluble calcium hydrogencarbonate.

    Properties of Calcium Carbonate#

    • Calcium carbonate is a white fluffy powder which is almost insoluble in water.
    • When heated to 1200 K, it decomposes to evolve carbon dioxide.
    \[CaCO_3 + Heat\ (1200\ K) &rarr; CaO + CO_2\]
    • It reacts with dilute acids to liberate carbon dioxide.
    \[CaCO_3 + 2HCl &rarr; CaCl_2 + H_2O + CO_2\]
    \[CaCO_3 + H_2SO_4 &rarr; CaSO_4 + H_2O + CO_2\]

    Uses of Calcium Carbonate#

    • It is used as a building material in the form of marble and in the manufacture of quick lime.
    • Calcium carbonate along with magnesium carbonate is used as a flux in the extraction of metals such as iron.
    • Specially precipitated CaCO3 is used extensively in the manufacture of high quality paper.
    • It is also used as an antacid, a mild abrasive in tooth paste, a constituent of chewing gum and a filler in cosmetics.

    Calcium Sulphate or Plaster of Paris (CaSO4.½H2O)#

    • Plaster of Paris is a hemihydrate of calcium sulphate: one molecule of water is shared between two units of CaSO4.

    Preparation of Plaster of Paris#

    • It is obtained when gypsum, CaSO4.2H2O, is heated to 393 K.
    \[2(CaSO_4.2H_2O) + Heat\ (393\ K) &rarr; 2(CaSO_4).H_2O + 3H_2O\]
    • Above 393 K, no water of crystallization is left and anhydrous calcium sulphate, CaSO4, is formed. This is known as dead burnt plaster.

    Properties of Plaster of Paris#

    • It has a remarkable property of setting with water. On mixing with an adequate quantity of water, it forms a plastic mass that sets into a hard solid in 5 to 15 minutes.

    Uses of Plaster of Paris#

    • Its largest use is in the building industry as well as in plasters.
    • It is used for immobilizing the affected part of the body where there is a bone fracture or sprain.
    • It is also employed in dentistry, in ornamental work and for making casts of statues and busts.

    Cement#

    • Cement is an important building material. It was first introduced in England in 1824 by Joseph Aspdin.
    • It is also called Portland cement because it resembles the natural limestone quarried in the Isle of Portland, England.
    • Cement is obtained by combining a material rich in lime, CaO, with other material such as clay which contains silica, SiO2, along with the oxides of aluminium, iron and magnesium.

    Composition of Portland Cement#

    Constituent Percentage
    CaO 50-60%
    SiO2 20-25%
    Al2O3 5-10%
    MgO 2-3%
    Fe2O3 1-2%
    SO3 1-2%
    • For a good quality cement, the following ratios should be maintained:
    \[{SiO_2 \over Al_2O_3} = 2.5\ to\ 4\]
    \[{CaO \over SiO_2 + Al_2O_3 + Fe_2O_3} &asymp; 2\]

    Manufacture of Cement#

    • The raw materials for the manufacture of cement are limestone and clay.
    • When clay and lime are strongly heated together, they fuse and react to form cement clinker.
    • This clinker is mixed with 2-3% by weight of gypsum (CaSO4.2H2O) to form cement.
    • The important ingredients present in Portland cement are dicalcium silicate (Ca2SiO4) 26%, tricalcium silicate (Ca3SiO5) 51% and tricalcium aluminate (Ca3Al2O6) 11%.

    Setting of Cement#

    • When mixed with water, cement sets to give a hard mass. This is due to the hydration of the molecules of the constituents and their rearrangement.
    • Gypsum is added only to slow down the process of setting of cement so that it gets sufficiently hardened.

    Uses of Cement#

    • Cement is a commodity of national necessity for any country, next to iron and steel.
    • It is used in concrete and reinforced concrete, in plastering and in the construction of bridges, dams and buildings.

    Biological Importance of Magnesium and Calcium#

    • An adult body contains about 25 g of Mg and 1200 g of Ca, compared with only 5 g of iron and 0.06 g of copper. The daily requirement in the human body is estimated to be 200-300 mg.

    Magnesium#

    • All enzymes that utilise ATP in phosphate transfer require magnesium as the cofactor.
    • Chlorophyll, the main pigment for the absorption of light in plants, contains magnesium.

    Calcium#

    • About 99% of body calcium is present in bones and teeth.
    • It plays important roles in neuromuscular function, interneuronal transmission, cell membrane integrity and blood coagulation.
    • The calcium concentration in plasma is regulated at about 100 mg L-1. It is maintained by two hormones: calcitonin and parathyroid hormone.
    • Bone is not an inert and unchanging substance. It is continuously being solubilised and redeposited to the extent of about 400 mg per day in man, and all this calcium passes through the plasma.
