    Environmental Chemistry | Pollution Control and Green Chemistry#

    Controlling environmental pollution needs two things: proper management of the wastes we already produce, and new ways of doing chemistry that produce fewer pollutants in the first place.

    Strategies to Control Environmental Pollution#

    • Waste management, i.e., reduction of waste and its proper disposal, and recycling of materials and energy.
    • Adopting methods in day-to-day life which reduce environmental pollution.
    • Green chemistry, which uses existing knowledge and practices to reduce the production of pollutants.

    Waste Management#

    • Besides household solid waste, there are medical, agricultural, industrial and mining wastes.
    • Improper disposal of wastes is one of the major causes of environmental degradation, so waste management is very important.

    Collection and Disposal#

    • Domestic wastes are collected in small bins and transferred to community bins by private or municipal workers. From there they are carried to the disposal site.
    • At the site, garbage is sorted into biodegradable and non-biodegradable materials:
      • Non-biodegradable materials such as plastic, glass and metal scraps are sent for recycling.
      • Biodegradable wastes are deposited in land fills and converted into compost.
    • Waste not collected in bins finds its way into sewers. Some of it is eaten by cattle. Polythene bags and metal scraps choke sewers, and polythene swallowed by cattle can kill them.
    • Poor waste management causes health problems and epidemics due to contamination of ground water. It is especially hazardous for rag pickers and waste workers who handle waste without gloves, waterproof boots or gas masks.

    Waste Recycling#

    • Fuel obtained from plastic waste has a high octane rating and contains no lead. It is known as "green fuel".
    • Clothes can be made from recycled plastic waste.
    • Electricity can be produced from garbage: after removing ferrous metals, plastic, glass and paper, the garbage is mixed with water and cultured with bacteria that produce methane (biogas). The biogas is used to generate electricity and the remaining product is used as manure.

    Green Chemistry#

    Introduction#

    • India achieved self-sufficiency in food by the late 20th century using fertilizers, pesticides and improved farming methods. But over-exploitation of soil and excessive use of fertilizers and pesticides have degraded soil, water and air.
    • The solution is not to stop development but to find methods that reduce damage to the environment.

    Definition

    Green chemistry is a way of thinking that uses the existing knowledge and principles of chemistry and other sciences to reduce the adverse impact on the environment. It is a production process that brings about minimum pollution or deterioration of the environment.

    • By-products of a process, if not used gainfully, add to environmental pollution. Such processes are environmentally unfriendly and also cost-ineffective, since both waste generation and its disposal are economically unsound.

    Principles of Green Chemistry#

    • A chemical reaction involves reactants, attacking reagents and the medium. Its extent depends on temperature, pressure and catalyst.
    • If reactants are fully converted into useful, environment-friendly products using an environment-friendly medium, no chemical pollutants enter the environment.
    • Choose starting materials that can be converted into end products with a yield of about 100 per cent, by arriving at optimum conditions of synthesis.
    • Prefer water as the reaction medium wherever possible. Water has high specific heat and low volatility, and is cost effective, non-inflammable and free of carcinogenic effects.
    • Organic solvents such as benzene, toluene and carbon tetrachloride are highly toxic and should be used with care.

    Note

    Yves Chauvin, Robert H. Grubbs and Richard R. Schrock won the 2005 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for developing the metathesis method in organic synthesis, a way to rearrange groups of atoms within molecules. It reduces hazardous waste through smarter production and is used in the pharmaceutical, biotechnology and food industries and in making environment-friendlier polymers.

    Green Chemistry in Day-to-Day Life#

    1. Dry Cleaning of Clothes#

    • Tetrachloroethene (Cl2C=CCl2) was earlier used as a dry cleaning solvent. It contaminates ground water and is a suspected carcinogen.
    • It is being replaced by liquefied carbon dioxide with a suitable detergent. Replacing halogenated solvents with liquid CO2 causes less harm to ground water.
    • Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) is now used for bleaching clothes in laundry. It gives better results and uses less water.

    2. Bleaching of Paper#

    • Chlorine gas was earlier used for bleaching paper.
    • Now hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) is used with a suitable catalyst that promotes its bleaching action.

    3. Synthesis of Chemicals#

    • Ethanal (CH3CHO) is now prepared commercially by one-step oxidation of ethene using an ionic catalyst in aqueous medium, with a yield of 90%:
    \[CH_2{=}CH_2 + O_2 \xrightarrow[Pd(II)/Cu(II)\ (in\ water)]{Catalyst} CH_3CHO\ (90\%)\]

    Conclusion

    Green chemistry, in a nutshell, is a cost-effective approach which involves reduction in material, energy consumption and waste generation.

    Our responsibility

    • Set up a compost tin at home and use it to make manure, reducing the use of fertilizers.
    • Use a cloth bag and avoid plastic carry bags.
    • Make sure newspapers, glass, aluminium and other items are recycled.
