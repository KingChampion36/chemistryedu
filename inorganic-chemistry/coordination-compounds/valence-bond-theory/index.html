    Coordination Compounds | Valence Bond Theory#

    Bonding in Coordination Compounds#

    Werner was the first to describe bonding in coordination compounds, but his theory could not answer some basic questions:

    • Why do only certain elements form coordination compounds?
    • Why do the bonds in coordination compounds have directional properties?
    • Why do coordination compounds have characteristic magnetic and optical properties?

    Several theories explain bonding in coordination compounds - Valence Bond Theory (VBT), Crystal Field Theory (CFT), Ligand Field Theory (LFT) and Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT). Here we will study VBT; CFT is discussed in the next article.

    Valence Bond Theory#

    • According to VBT, the metal atom or ion, under the influence of ligands, uses its (n-1)d, ns, np or ns, np, nd orbitals for hybridisation.
    • This gives a set of equivalent hybrid orbitals of definite geometry such as octahedral, tetrahedral or square planar.
    • These hybrid orbitals overlap with ligand orbitals that donate electron pairs, forming coordinate bonds.
    Coordination number Type of hybridisation Distribution of hybrid orbitals in space
    4 sp3 Tetrahedral
    4 dsp2 Square planar
    5 sp3d Trigonal bipyramidal
    6 sp3d2 Octahedral
    6 d2sp3 Octahedral
    • It is usually possible to predict the geometry of a complex if its magnetic behaviour is known.
    • In the orbital diagrams below, each column is one orbital, ↑↓ are the metal's own electrons, and xx is an electron pair donated by a ligand.

    Octahedral Complexes#

    1. [Co(NH3)6]3+ - inner orbital complex#

    • Cobalt is in the +3 oxidation state. Co3+ has the configuration 3d6.
    • NH3 causes the 3d electrons to pair up, leaving two 3d orbitals empty. These two 3d orbitals, one 4s and three 4p orbitals hybridise to give six d2sp3 hybrid orbitals.
    Species 3d 3d 3d 3d 3d 4s 4p 4p 4p
    Co3+ ion ↑↓ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑
    d2sp3 hybridised Co3+ ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑↓
    [Co(NH3)6]3+ ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑↓ xx xx xx xx xx xx
    • Six pairs of electrons, one from each NH3, occupy the six hybrid orbitals. The complex is octahedral and diamagnetic (no unpaired electron).
    • Since the inner d orbitals (3d) are used in hybridisation, [Co(NH3)6]3+ is called an inner orbital or low spin or spin paired complex.

    2. [CoF6]3- - outer orbital complex#

    • Co3+ is again 3d6, but F- does not cause pairing of the 3d electrons.
    • The outer 4d orbitals are used: one 4s, three 4p and two 4d orbitals give six sp3d2 hybrid orbitals.
    Species 3d 3d 3d 3d 3d 4s 4p 4p 4p 4d 4d
    Co3+ ion ↑↓ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑
    [CoF6]3- ↑↓ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ xx xx xx xx xx xx
    • The complex is octahedral and paramagnetic with four unpaired electrons.
    • Since the outer d orbitals (4d) are used, [CoF6]3- is called an outer orbital or high spin or spin free complex.

    Inner vs Outer Orbital Complexes

    • Inner orbital (low spin): d2sp3 hybridisation using (n-1)d orbitals; electrons paired as far as possible. Example: [Co(NH3)6]3+.

    • Outer orbital (high spin): sp3d2 hybridisation using nd orbitals; maximum number of unpaired electrons. Example: [CoF6]3-.

    Tetrahedral Complexes#

    [NiCl4]2-#

    • In tetrahedral complexes, one s and three p orbitals hybridise to give four sp3 orbitals oriented tetrahedrally.
    • Nickel is in the +2 oxidation state with configuration 3d8.
    Species 3d 3d 3d 3d 3d 4s 4p 4p 4p
    Ni2+ ion ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑ ↑
    [NiCl4]2- ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑ ↑ xx xx xx xx
    • Each Cl- donates one electron pair. The complex is tetrahedral and paramagnetic with two unpaired electrons (high spin).
    • Similarly, [Ni(CO)4] is tetrahedral, but it is diamagnetic because nickel is in the zero oxidation state (3d10 after rearrangement of the 4s electrons) and has no unpaired electron.

    Square Planar Complexes#

    [Ni(CN)4]2-#

    • In square planar complexes, the hybridisation is dsp2 (one 3d, one 4s and two 4p orbitals).
    • Nickel is in the +2 oxidation state (3d8). CN- forces the two unpaired 3d electrons to pair up, making one 3d orbital empty.
    Species 3d 3d 3d 3d 3d 4s 4p 4p 4p
    Ni2+ ion ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑ ↑
    dsp2 hybridised Ni2+ ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑↓
    [Ni(CN)4]2- ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑↓ xx xx xx xx
    • Each hybrid orbital receives an electron pair from a cyanide ion. The complex is diamagnetic (low spin).

    Note

    Hybrid orbitals do not actually exist. Hybridisation is only a mathematical manipulation of the wave equation for the atomic orbitals involved.

    Magnetic Properties of Coordination Compounds#

    • The magnetic moment of a coordination compound can be measured by magnetic susceptibility experiments. The results tell us about the structure of the complex.
    • The spin-only magnetic moment is related to the number of unpaired electrons (n):
    \[&mu; = \sqrt{n(n+2)}\ BM\]
    Unpaired electrons (n) 1 2 3 4 5
    μ (BM) 1.73 2.83 3.87 4.90 5.92

    Magnetic behaviour and the number of d electrons#

    • For metal ions with up to three 3d electrons, like Ti3+ (d1), V3+ (d2) and Cr3+ (d3), two 3d orbitals are already vacant for d2sp3 hybridisation. Their free ions and complexes have similar magnetic behaviour.
    • When more than three 3d electrons are present, a vacant pair of 3d orbitals is obtained only by pairing electrons against Hund's rule. For d4 (Cr2+, Mn3+), d5 (Mn2+, Fe3+) and d6 (Fe2+, Co3+), this pairing leaves two, one and zero unpaired electrons respectively.
    • The magnetic data agree with maximum spin pairing in many cases, especially for d6 ions. But for d4 and d5 ions, there are complications, which VBT explains using inner and outer orbital complexes:
    Complex Metal ion Hybridisation Type Unpaired electrons
    [Mn(CN)6]3- Mn3+ (d4) d2sp3 Inner orbital 2
    [MnCl6]3- Mn3+ (d4) sp3d2 Outer orbital 4
    [Fe(CN)6]3- Fe3+ (d5) d2sp3 Inner orbital 1
    [FeF6]3- Fe3+ (d5) sp3d2 Outer orbital 5
    [Co(C2O4)3]3- Co3+ (d6) d2sp3 Inner orbital 0 (diamagnetic)
    [CoF6]3- Co3+ (d6) sp3d2 Outer orbital 4

    Limitations of Valence Bond Theory#

    VBT explains the formation, structures and magnetic behaviour of coordination compounds to a large extent, but it has the following shortcomings:

    1. It involves a number of assumptions.
    2. It does not give a quantitative interpretation of magnetic data.
    3. It does not explain the colour exhibited by coordination compounds.
    4. It does not give a quantitative interpretation of the thermodynamic or kinetic stabilities of coordination compounds.
    5. It does not make exact predictions about tetrahedral and square planar structures of 4-coordinate complexes.
    6. It does not distinguish between weak and strong ligands.

    Questions#

    The spin only magnetic moment of [MnBr4]2- is 5.9 BM. Predict the geometry of the complex ion.

    • The coordination number of Mn2+ is 4, so the complex is either tetrahedral (sp3) or square planar (dsp2).
    \[5.9 = \sqrt{n(n+2)}\ &rArr;\ n = 5\]
    • Mn2+ is 3d5, and all five electrons remain unpaired. No 3d orbital is vacant for dsp2 hybridisation, so sp3 hybridisation occurs.

    Hence, [MnBr4]2- is tetrahedral.

    Explain on the basis of valence bond theory that [Ni(CN)4]2- ion with square planar structure is diamagnetic and the [NiCl4]2- ion with tetrahedral geometry is paramagnetic.

    In both complexes, Ni is in the +2 state with 3d8 configuration (two unpaired electrons).

    • CN- is a strong ligand. It pairs up the two unpaired 3d electrons, leaving one 3d orbital empty. dsp2 hybridisation gives a square planar complex with no unpaired electrons - diamagnetic.

    • Cl- is a weak ligand and cannot pair up the 3d electrons. sp3 hybridisation (4s + 4p) gives a tetrahedral complex with two unpaired electrons - paramagnetic.

    [NiCl4]2- is paramagnetic while [Ni(CO)4] is diamagnetic though both are tetrahedral. Why?

    • In [Ni(CO)4], Ni is in the zero oxidation state (3d84s2). In the presence of CO, the 4s electrons move into 3d and all d electrons pair up (3d10). sp3 hybridisation gives a tetrahedral complex with no unpaired electron - diamagnetic.

    • In [NiCl4]2-, Ni is in the +2 state (3d8). Cl- is a weak ligand and cannot pair up the two unpaired electrons - paramagnetic.

    [Fe(H2O)6]3+ is strongly paramagnetic whereas [Fe(CN)6]3- is weakly paramagnetic. Explain.

    In both, Fe is in the +3 state (3d5).

    • CN- is a strong ligand. The 3d electrons pair up, leaving only one unpaired electron. d2sp3 hybridisation gives an inner orbital complex - weakly paramagnetic.

    • H2O is a weak ligand. The 3d electrons do not pair up. sp3d2 hybridisation gives an outer orbital complex with five unpaired electrons - strongly paramagnetic.

    Explain why [Co(NH3)6]3+ is an inner orbital complex whereas [Ni(NH3)6]2+ is an outer orbital complex.

    • In [Co(NH3)6]3+, Co3+ is 3d6. In the presence of NH3, the 3d electrons pair up, leaving two 3d orbitals empty for d2sp3 hybridisation. So it is an inner orbital complex.

    • In [Ni(NH3)6]2+, Ni2+ is 3d8. Even after pairing, only one 3d orbital can be emptied, so two vacant 3d orbitals are not available. The outer 4d orbitals are used, giving sp3d2 hybridisation. So it is an outer orbital complex.

    Predict the number of unpaired electrons in the square planar [Pt(CN)4]2- ion.

    Pt2+ has the configuration 5d8. For a square planar shape, the hybridisation is dsp2. The two unpaired electrons in 5d pair up to make one 5d orbital empty for dsp2 hybridisation.

    Hence, there is no unpaired electron in [Pt(CN)4]2-.

    Discuss the nature of bonding in the following coordination entities on the basis of valence bond theory:
    (i) [Fe(CN)6]4- (ii) [FeF6]3- (iii) [Co(C2O4)3]3- (iv) [CoF6]3-

    Complex Metal ion Ligand Hybridisation Geometry Unpaired e- Nature
    [Fe(CN)6]4- Fe2+, 3d6 Strong d2sp3 Octahedral 0 Inner orbital, diamagnetic
    [FeF6]3- Fe3+, 3d5 Weak sp3d2 Octahedral 5 Outer orbital, paramagnetic
    [Co(C2O4)3]3- Co3+, 3d6 Pairs electrons d2sp3 Octahedral 0 Inner orbital, diamagnetic
    [CoF6]3- Co3+, 3d6 Weak sp3d2 Octahedral 4 Outer orbital, paramagnetic

    [Cr(NH3)6]3+ is paramagnetic while [Ni(CN)4]2- is diamagnetic. Explain why.

    • Cr3+ is 3d3. Two 3d orbitals are already empty, so d2sp3 hybridisation takes place without any pairing. The three unpaired electrons remain, so [Cr(NH3)6]3+ is paramagnetic.
    \[&mu; = \sqrt{3(3+2)} = 3.87\ BM\]
    • Ni2+ is 3d8. CN- pairs up the two unpaired electrons, and dsp2 hybridisation gives a square planar complex with no unpaired electrons - diamagnetic.
