    Coordination Compounds | Questions#

    In this article, we will solve some questions on coordination compounds covering nomenclature, isomerism, valence bond theory, crystal field theory and stability.

    Questions#

    Explain the bonding in coordination compounds in terms of Werner's postulates.

    • Metals show two types of valences - primary and secondary.

    • Primary valences are ionisable and are satisfied by negative ions. They correspond to the oxidation state of the metal.

    • Secondary valences are non-ionisable and are satisfied by neutral molecules or negative ions. The secondary valence equals the coordination number and is fixed for a metal.

    • The groups bound by secondary valences have a characteristic spatial arrangement (coordination polyhedron).

    Example: In [Co(NH3)6]Cl3, the primary valence of Co is 3 (satisfied by three Cl-) and the secondary valence is 6 (satisfied by six NH3), with an octahedral arrangement.

    Aqueous copper sulphate solution (blue in colour) gives:
    (i) a green precipitate with aqueous potassium fluoride and
    (ii) a bright green solution with aqueous potassium chloride.
    Explain these experimental results.

    Aqueous CuSO4 contains the blue complex [Cu(H2O)4]2+. The water ligands are weak and are easily replaced by F- and Cl-:

    \[[Cu(H_2O)_4]^{2+} + 4F^- &rarr; [CuF_4]^{2-}\ (green\ ppt.) + 4H_2O\]
    \[[Cu(H_2O)_4]^{2+} + 4Cl^- &rarr; [CuCl_4]^{2-}\ (bright\ green\ solution) + 4H_2O\]

    The change of ligand changes the crystal field splitting and hence the colour.

    What is the coordination entity formed when excess of aqueous KCN is added to an aqueous solution of copper sulphate? Why is it that no precipitate of copper sulphide is obtained when H2S(g) is passed through this solution?

    With excess KCN, Cu2+ is reduced and forms the very stable complex [Cu(CN)4]3-:

    \[2CuSO_4 + 10KCN &rarr; 2K_3[Cu(CN)_4] + 2K_2SO_4 + (CN)_2\]

    This complex has a very high stability constant and does not dissociate to give free copper ions. Hence, when H2S is passed, no precipitate of copper sulphide is formed.

    Write down the IUPAC name for each of the following complexes and indicate the oxidation state, electronic configuration and coordination number. Also give the stereochemistry and magnetic moment of the complex:
    (i) K[Cr(H2O)2(C2O4)2].3H2O (ii) [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2 (iii) CrCl3(py)3 (iv) Cs[FeCl4] (v) K4[Mn(CN)6]

    (i) Potassium diaquadioxalatochromate(III) trihydrate

    • Oxidation state = +3, configuration 3d3 (t2g3), CN = 6, octahedral

    • Shows geometrical isomerism (cis, trans); the cis form is optically active

    \[&mu; = \sqrt{3(3+2)} = 3.87\ BM\]

    (ii) Pentaamminechloridocobalt(III) chloride

    • Oxidation state = +3, configuration 3d6 (t2g6, low spin), CN = 6, octahedral

    • No stereoisomers; diamagnetic, μ = 0

    (iii) Trichloridotripyridinechromium(III)

    • Oxidation state = +3, configuration 3d3 (t2g3), CN = 6, octahedral

    • Type [MA3B3], shows fac and mer isomers

    \[&mu; = \sqrt{3(3+2)} = 3.87\ BM\]

    (iv) Caesium tetrachloridoferrate(III)

    • Oxidation state = +3, configuration 3d5 (e2t23), CN = 4, tetrahedral (sp3)

    • No stereoisomers

    \[&mu; = \sqrt{5(5+2)} = 5.92\ BM\]

    (v) Potassium hexacyanomanganate(II)

    • Oxidation state = +2, configuration 3d5 (t2g5, low spin since CN- is a strong ligand), CN = 6, octahedral (d2sp3)

    • No stereoisomers

    \[&mu; = \sqrt{1(1+2)} = 1.73\ BM\]

    Draw all the isomers (geometrical and optical) of:
    (i) [Co(NH3)Cl(en)2]2+ (ii) [Co(NH3)2Cl2(en)]+

    (i) [Co(NH3)Cl(en)2]2+

    • NH3 and Cl can be trans (opposite) or cis (adjacent) to each other - two geometrical isomers.

    • The trans isomer has a plane of symmetry and is optically inactive. The cis isomer is chiral and exists as d and l forms.

    • Total isomers = 3 (trans, d-cis, l-cis).

    (ii) [Co(NH3)2Cl2(en)]+

    • Two Cl trans to each other (both NH3 cis) - optically inactive.

    • Two NH3 trans to each other (both Cl cis) - optically inactive.

    • Both Cl cis and both NH3 cis - chiral, exists as d and l forms.

    • Total = 3 geometrical isomers, 4 isomers in all.

    What is meant by stability of a coordination compound in solution? State the factors which govern stability of complexes.

    The stability of a complex in solution is the degree of association between the metal ion and the ligands at equilibrium. It is expressed by the stability (formation) constant; a larger value means a more stable complex.

    \[M + nL &#8652; ML_n,\ \ &beta;_n = {[ML_n] \over [M][L]^n}\]

    Factors governing stability:

    • Charge on the central metal ion: Higher charge gives a more stable complex.

    • Size of the metal ion: Smaller size (higher charge density) gives a more stable complex.

    • Nature of the ligand: More basic ligands (better electron pair donors) form more stable complexes.

    • Chelation: Chelating ligands form more stable complexes than unidentate ligands (chelate effect).

    Discuss briefly, giving an example in each case, the role of coordination compounds in:
    (i) biological systems (ii) medicinal chemistry (iii) analytical chemistry (iv) extraction/metallurgy of metals

    • (i) Biological systems: Chlorophyll (Mg complex) is responsible for photosynthesis; haemoglobin (Fe complex) carries oxygen in blood; vitamin B12 is a cobalt complex.

    • (ii) Medicinal chemistry: EDTA is used to treat lead poisoning; cis-platin is used as an anti-tumour drug; D-penicillamine removes excess copper.

    • (iii) Analytical chemistry: Hardness of water is estimated by titration with Na2EDTA; DMG is used to detect Ni2+.

    • (iv) Metallurgy: Gold and silver are extracted as cyanide complexes ([Au(CN)2]-); nickel is purified via [Ni(CO)4].

    How many ions are produced from the complex Co(NH3)6Cl2 in solution?
    (i) 6 (ii) 4 (iii) 3 (iv) 2

    \[[Co(NH_3)_6]Cl_2 &rarr; [Co(NH_3)_6]^{2+} + 2Cl^-\]

    Three ions are produced. Answer: (iii) 3

    Amongst the following ions, which one has the highest magnetic moment value?
    (i) [Cr(H2O)6]3+ (ii) [Fe(H2O)6]2+ (iii) [Zn(H2O)6]2+

    • Cr3+: 3d3, 3 unpaired electrons

    • Fe2+: 3d6, H2O is weak field (t2g4eg2), 4 unpaired electrons

    • Zn2+: 3d10, 0 unpaired electrons

    Maximum unpaired electrons give the highest magnetic moment. Answer: (ii) [Fe(H2O)6]2+

    \[&mu; = \sqrt{4(4+2)} = 4.90\ BM\]

    The oxidation number of cobalt in K[Co(CO)4] is:
    (i) +1 (ii) +3 (iii) -1 (iv) -3

    CO is a neutral ligand.

    \[(+1) + x + 4(0) = 0\]
    \[x = -1\]

    Answer: (iii) -1
