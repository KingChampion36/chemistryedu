    Metallurgy | Questions#

    In this article, we will solve some important questions based on the general principles and processes of isolation of elements.

    Concentration of Ores#

    Which of the ores of aluminium, iron, copper and zinc can be concentrated by magnetic separation method?

    Ores in which one of the components (either the impurity or the actual ore) is magnetic can be concentrated by this method. For example, the iron ores: haematite (Fe2O3), magnetite (Fe3O4), siderite (FeCO3) and iron pyrites (FeS2).

    What is the significance of leaching in the extraction of aluminium?

    Leaching of bauxite with concentrated NaOH dissolves Al2O3 as sodium aluminate. This removes impurities like SiO2, Fe2O3 and TiO2 from the bauxite ore, giving pure alumina for electrolysis.

    What is the role of depressant in froth floatation process?

    A depressant selectively prevents one sulphide ore from coming into the froth, so that two sulphide ores present together can be separated. For example, in an ore containing ZnS and PbS, NaCN acts as a depressant. It prevents ZnS from forming froth but allows PbS to come with the froth.

    How can you separate alumina from silica in a bauxite ore associated with silica? Give equations, if any.

    The powdered ore is digested with concentrated NaOH at 473 – 523 K and 35 – 36 bar. Al2O3 dissolves as sodium aluminate and SiO2 as sodium silicate:

    \[Al_2O_3 + 2NaOH + 3H_2O &rarr; 2Na[Al(OH)_4]\]
    \[SiO_2 + 2NaOH &rarr; Na_2SiO_3 + H_2O\]

    CO2 is passed through the solution (seeded with fresh hydrated alumina). Only hydrated alumina precipitates, while sodium silicate stays in solution:

    \[2Na[Al(OH)_4] + CO_2 &rarr; Al_2O_3.xH_2O &darr; + 2NaHCO_3\]

    The precipitate is filtered and heated to get pure alumina:

    \[Al_2O_3.xH_2O \xrightarrow{1470\ K} Al_2O_3 + xH_2O\]

    How is leaching carried out in case of low grade copper ores?

    Low grade copper ores are leached with acid (or bacteria) in the presence of air. Copper goes into solution as Cu2+ ions:

    \[Cu_{(s)} + 2H^+_{(aq)} + {1 \over 2}O_{2(g)} &rarr; Cu^{2+}_{(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)}\]

    The solution is then treated with scrap iron or H2 to recover copper:

    \[Cu^{2+}_{(aq)} + Fe_{(s)} &rarr; Cu_{(s)} + Fe^{2+}_{(aq)}\]
    \[Cu^{2+}_{(aq)} + H_{2(g)} &rarr; Cu_{(s)} + 2H^+_{(aq)}\]

    Differentiate between minerals and ores.

    Minerals are naturally occurring chemical substances in the earth's crust obtainable by mining. Ores are those minerals from which a metal can be extracted conveniently and profitably. Thus, all ores are minerals but all minerals are not ores. Example: aluminium occurs in clay and bauxite, but only bauxite is an ore of aluminium.

    Giving examples, differentiate between roasting and calcination.

    • Calcination: the ore is heated in absence or limited supply of air to remove volatile matter like water and CO2. Used for carbonate and hydrated ores.
    \[ZnCO_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} ZnO + CO_2\]
    \[Fe_2O_3.xH_2O \xrightarrow{\Delta} Fe_2O_3 + xH_2O\]
    • Roasting: the ore is heated in a regular supply of air below the melting point of the metal. Used for sulphide ores.
    \[2ZnS + 3O_2 &rarr; 2ZnO + 2SO_2\]
    \[2Cu_2S + 3O_2 &rarr; 2Cu_2O + 2SO_2\]

    Thermodynamic Principles#

    The reaction Cr2O3 + 2Al → Al2O3 + 2Cr (ΔG0 = -421 kJ) is thermodynamically feasible as is apparent from the Gibbs energy value. Why does it not take place at room temperature?

    A negative ΔG0 only shows that the reaction is possible. It says nothing about its rate. All the reactants are solids, and a certain amount of activation energy is needed even for a thermodynamically feasible reaction. Therefore heating is required to start the reaction.

    Is it true that under certain conditions, Mg can reduce Al2O3 and Al can reduce MgO? What are those conditions?

    Yes. The MgO and Al2O3 lines in the Ellingham diagram cross at about 1350 °C (point A).

    • Below 1350 °C, the MgO line lies lower, so Mg can reduce Al2O3.
    • Above 1350 °C, the Al2O3 line lies lower, so Al can reduce MgO.

    Predict conditions under which Al might be expected to reduce MgO.

    Above the point of intersection of the Al2O3 and MgO curves in the Ellingham diagram (about 1350 °C), ΔfG0 of Al2O3 is more negative than that of MgO. So above this temperature, the reaction below has a negative ΔG0:

    \[3MgO + 2Al &rarr; Al_2O_3 + 3Mg\]

    The value of ΔfG0 for formation of Cr2O3 is -540 kJ mol-1 and that of Al2O3 is -827 kJ mol-1. Is the reduction of Cr2O3 possible with Al?

    \[Cr_2O_3 + 2Al &rarr; Al_2O_3 + 2Cr\]
    \[\Delta_rG^0 = \Delta_fG^0(Al_2O_3) - \Delta_fG^0(Cr_2O_3)\]
    \[\Delta_rG^0 = -827 - (-540) = -287\ kJ\ mol^{-1}\]

    Since ΔrG0 is negative, the reduction of Cr2O3 by Al is possible.

    Out of C and CO, which is a better reducing agent at 673 K?

    At 673 K, the line for CO → CO2 lies below the line for C → CO in the Ellingham diagram, i.e., the oxidation of CO to CO2 has the more negative ΔG0. Hence CO is a better reducing agent than C at 673 K. (Above about 983 K, carbon becomes the better reducing agent.)

    Out of C and CO, which is a better reducing agent for ZnO?

    The ZnO line lies below the CO → CO2 line at all temperatures, so CO cannot reduce ZnO. The C → CO line slopes downwards and crosses below the ZnO line at high temperature (above about 1270 K). Hence carbon is the better reducing agent for ZnO.

    \[ZnO + C &rarr; Zn + CO\]

    Why is zinc not extracted from zinc oxide through reduction using CO?

    For reduction by CO, the sum of ΔG0 for ZnO → Zn and CO → CO2 must be negative. In the Ellingham diagram, the line for 2CO + O2 → 2CO2 lies above the line for 2Zn + O2 → 2ZnO, so the overall ΔG0 stays positive. Hence CO cannot reduce ZnO, and coke is used instead at a high temperature.

    The choice of a reducing agent in a particular case depends on thermodynamic factor. How far do you agree with this statement? Support your opinion with two examples.

    The statement is largely true. A reducing agent works only if the overall ΔG0 of the coupled reaction is negative, i.e., the line for its oxide lies below that of the metal oxide in the Ellingham diagram at the chosen temperature.

    • Cu2O lies near the top of the diagram, so it is easily reduced by coke even at low temperatures.
    • ZnO is reduced by coke only at high temperatures where the C → CO line falls below the ZnO line; CO cannot reduce it.
    • Cr2O3 is reduced by Al, since ΔfG0 of Al2O3 is much more negative.

    However, kinetic and economic factors (rate, cost, temperature required) also matter. For example, Mg is not used to reduce alumina even though it is feasible.

    Extraction of Metals#

    Copper can be extracted by hydrometallurgy but not zinc. Explain.

    In hydrometallurgy, the metal ion in solution is displaced by a more reactive metal.

    \[E^0_{Cu^{2+}/Cu} = +0.34\ V,\ \ E^0_{Zn^{2+}/Zn} = -0.76\ V\]

    Cu2+ is easily displaced by a cheap metal like iron (E0 = -0.44 V). Zinc is itself a highly reactive metal, so displacing Zn2+ would need still more reactive metals like Al, Mg, Ca or K. These are costlier, and many of them also react with water. So zinc cannot be extracted by hydrometallurgy economically.

    Why is the extraction of copper from pyrites more difficult than that from its oxide ore through reduction?

    The ΔfG0 of Cu2S is not compensated by the oxidation of carbon or hydrogen, because CS2 and H2S have less negative (CS2 even positive) Gibbs energies of formation. So copper sulphide cannot be reduced directly by C or H2. Pyrites must first be roasted to the oxide, and its iron must be removed as slag. On the other hand, Cu2O lies near the top of the Ellingham diagram and is easily reduced by coke.

    Write down the reactions taking place in different zones in the blast furnace during the extraction of iron.

    • Lower temperature zone (500 – 800 K):
    \[3Fe_2O_3 + CO &rarr; 2Fe_3O_4 + CO_2\]
    \[Fe_3O_4 + 4CO &rarr; 3Fe + 4CO_2\]
    \[Fe_2O_3 + CO &rarr; 2FeO + CO_2\]
    • Higher temperature zone (900 – 1500 K):
    \[C + CO_2 &rarr; 2CO\]
    \[FeO + CO &rarr; Fe + CO_2\]
    • Slag formation:
    \[CaCO_3 &rarr; CaO + CO_2\]
    \[CaO + SiO_2 &rarr; CaSiO_3\]
    • Bottom (combustion) zone, about 2170 K:
    \[C + O_2 &rarr; CO_2\]
    \[FeO + C &rarr; Fe + CO\]

    Write chemical reactions taking place in the extraction of zinc from zinc blende.

    • Concentration by froth floatation, followed by roasting:
    \[2ZnS + 3O_2 &rarr; 2ZnO + 2SO_2\]
    • Reduction with coke at high temperature:
    \[ZnO + C &rarr; Zn + CO\]
    • The zinc vapour is distilled off and collected by rapid chilling. It may be purified by fractional distillation.

    State the role of silica in the metallurgy of copper.

    Silica acts as a flux. It removes the iron oxide present in the copper ore as a fusible slag of iron silicate, both in the reverberatory furnace and in the converter:

    \[FeO + SiO_2 &rarr; FeSiO_3\ (slag)\]

    Why is copper matte put in silica lined converter?

    Copper matte contains Cu2S and FeS. In the silica lined converter, a hot air blast converts FeS to FeO, which combines with silica to form FeSiO3 slag. Cu2S is partly oxidised to Cu2O, which then reacts with the remaining Cu2S to give copper:

    \[2FeS + 3O_2 &rarr; 2FeO + 2SO_2\]
    \[FeO + SiO_2 &rarr; FeSiO_3\]
    \[2Cu_2S + 3O_2 &rarr; 2Cu_2O + 2SO_2\]
    \[2Cu_2O + Cu_2S &rarr; 6Cu + SO_2\]

    How is cast iron different from pig iron?

    Pig iron, obtained from the blast furnace, contains about 4% carbon and many impurities (S, P, Si, Mn). Cast iron is made by melting pig iron with scrap iron and coke using a hot air blast. It has a slightly lower carbon content (about 3%) and is extremely hard and brittle.

    What is the role of cryolite in the metallurgy of aluminium?

    Cryolite (Na3AlF6) is mixed with purified alumina before electrolysis. It lowers the melting point of the mixture and increases its electrical conductivity, so the electrolysis can be carried out at a lower temperature with less energy.

    What is the role of graphite rod in the electrometallurgy of aluminium?

    The graphite rod acts as the anode. The oxygen liberated at the anode reacts with the carbon to form CO and CO2, so the graphite helps in the reduction of alumina to aluminium. Without it, the liberated oxygen could re-oxidise some of the aluminium. In the process the anode is burnt away (about 0.5 kg per kg of Al) and must be replaced periodically.

    \[C + O^{2-} &rarr; CO + 2e^-\]
    \[C + 2O^{2-} &rarr; CO_2 + 4e^-\]

    Name the processes from which chlorine is obtained as a by-product. What will happen if an aqueous solution of NaCl is subjected to electrolysis?

    Chlorine is obtained as a by-product in the electrolysis of molten NaCl (manufacture of sodium metal). It is also a product of the electrolysis of brine.

    On electrolysis of aqueous NaCl, Cl2 is liberated at the anode, H2 at the cathode, and NaOH remains in the solution:

    \[2Cl^-_{(aq)} + 2H_2O_{(l)} &rarr; 2OH^-_{(aq)} + H_{2(g)} + Cl_{2(g)}\]

    Refining#

    Name the common elements present in the anode mud in electrolytic refining of copper. Why are they so present?

    Anode mud contains antimony, selenium, tellurium, silver, gold and platinum. These elements are less reactive (less electropositive) than copper, so they are not oxidised at the anode and do not dissolve in the electrolyte. They simply fall below the anode as the anode dissolves.

    Describe a method for refining nickel.

    Nickel is refined by the Mond process (vapour phase refining). Impure nickel is heated in a stream of CO to form volatile nickel tetracarbonyl. The vapour is taken to another chamber and heated to a higher temperature, where it decomposes to give pure nickel:

    \[Ni + 4CO \xrightarrow{330 - 350\ K} Ni(CO)_4\]
    \[Ni(CO)_4 \xrightarrow{450 - 470\ K} Ni + 4CO\]

    Explain: (i) Zone refining (ii) Column chromatography.

    • Zone refining: Impurities are more soluble in the melt than in the solid metal. A mobile heater moves along a rod of impure metal; the pure metal crystallises behind the molten zone while impurities move with it. After several passes, impurities collect at one end, which is cut off. Used for Ge, Si, B, Ga and In.
    • Column chromatography: A column of adsorbent like Al2O3 is packed in a glass tube. The mixture is passed through it in a liquid medium. Components are adsorbed to different extents and form separate bands, which are eluted one by one with suitable solvents. It is used for elements present in minute amounts whose impurities have similar chemical properties.

    Outline the principles of refining of metals by the following methods: (i) Zone refining (ii) Electrolytic refining (iii) Vapour phase refining

    • Zone refining: impurities are more soluble in the molten state than in the solid state of the metal.
    • Electrolytic refining: impure metal is the anode and pure metal the cathode in a solution of its salt. The metal dissolves at the anode and deposits in pure form at the cathode; more reactive impurities stay in solution and less reactive ones form anode mud.
    \[Anode:\ M &rarr; M^{n+} + ne^-\]
    \[Cathode:\ M^{n+} + ne^- &rarr; M\]
    • Vapour phase refining: the metal is converted into a volatile compound, which is collected and then decomposed to give pure metal. Examples: Mond process for Ni and van Arkel method for Zr and Ti.
    \[Zr + 2I_2 &rarr; ZrI_4 \xrightarrow{1800\ K} Zr + 2I_2\]

    What is meant by the term chromatography?

    Chromatography is a technique for separating the components of a mixture, based on the fact that different components are adsorbed (or distributed) to different extents between a stationary phase and a mobile phase. As the mobile phase moves through the stationary phase, the components travel at different rates and get separated.

    What criterion is followed for the selection of the stationary phase in chromatography?

    The stationary phase is chosen such that the components of the mixture have different solubilities (or different extents of adsorption) in it. A component more strongly held by the stationary phase moves slowly, while a component less strongly held moves faster with the mobile phase, so the components get separated. The stationary phase must also be immobile and immiscible with the mobile phase.
