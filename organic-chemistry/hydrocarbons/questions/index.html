    Hydrocarbons | Questions#

    In this article, we will solve some questions based on alkanes, alkenes, alkynes and aromatic hydrocarbons.

    Questions on Alkanes#

    Write structures of different chain isomers of alkanes corresponding to the molecular formula C6H14. Also write their IUPAC names.

    \[(i)\ CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3\ \ \ (n\text{-}Hexane)\]
    \[(ii)\ CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3\ \ \ (2\text{-}Methylpentane)\]
    \[(iii)\ CH_3-CH_2-CH(CH_3)-CH_2-CH_3\ \ \ (3\text{-}Methylpentane)\]
    \[(iv)\ CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CH(CH_3)-CH_3\ \ \ (2,3\text{-}Dimethylbutane)\]
    \[(v)\ CH_3-C(CH_3)_2-CH_2-CH_3\ \ \ (2,2\text{-}Dimethylbutane)\]

    Write IUPAC names of the following compounds:
    (i) (CH3)3CCH2C(CH3)3
    (ii) (CH3)2C(C2H5)2
    (iii) tetra-tert-butylmethane

    • (i) 2,2,4,4-Tetramethylpentane
    • (ii) 3,3-Dimethylpentane
    • (iii) 3,3-Di-tert-butyl-2,2,4,4-tetramethylpentane

    Write structures for the following compounds. Why are the given names incorrect? Write correct IUPAC names.
    (i) 2-Ethylpentane
    (ii) 5-Ethyl-3-methylheptane

    • (i) The structure is:
    \[CH_3-CH(C_2H_5)-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3\]

    The longest chain has six carbon atoms, not five. Hence, the correct name is 3-methylhexane.

    • (ii) The structure is:
    \[CH_3-CH_2-CH(CH_3)-CH_2-CH(C_2H_5)-CH_2-CH_3\]

    Numbering must start from the end that gives the lower number to the ethyl group (alphabetical priority, since both sets of locants are 3,5). Hence, the correct name is 3-ethyl-5-methylheptane.

    Sodium salt of which acid will be needed for the preparation of propane? Write the chemical equation for the reaction.

    Decarboxylation removes one carbon atom, so a four carbon acid is needed: butanoic acid.

    \[CH_3CH_2CH_2COO^-Na^+ + NaOH \xrightarrow{CaO} CH_3CH_2CH_3 + Na_2CO_3\]

    How do you account for the formation of ethane during chlorination of methane?

    Chlorination of methane is a free radical chain reaction. During propagation, methyl free radicals (\(\dot{C}H_3\)) are produced:

    \[CH_4 + \dot{C}l &rarr; \dot{C}H_3 + HCl\]

    In the termination step, two methyl radicals can combine with each other:

    \[\dot{C}H_3 + \dot{C}H_3 &rarr; CH_3-CH_3\]

    Hence, ethane is obtained as a by-product.

    Why is Wurtz reaction not preferred for the preparation of alkanes containing an odd number of carbon atoms? Illustrate your answer by taking one example.

    To get an odd-carbon alkane, two different alkyl halides must be used. For propane, CH3Br and C2H5Br would be needed. But each halide can also couple with itself, so three alkanes form:

    \[CH_3Br + C_2H_5Br + 2Na \xrightarrow{dry\ ether} CH_3-C_2H_5\ (propane) + 2NaBr\]
    \[CH_3Br + CH_3Br + 2Na &rarr; CH_3-CH_3\ (ethane) + 2NaBr\]
    \[C_2H_5Br + C_2H_5Br + 2Na &rarr; C_2H_5-C_2H_5\ (butane) + 2NaBr\]

    The mixture of ethane, propane and butane is difficult to separate. Hence, Wurtz reaction is not preferred for odd-carbon alkanes.

    Questions on Alkenes#

    Write structures and IUPAC names of different structural isomers of alkenes corresponding to C5H10.

    \[(a)\ CH_2=CH-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3\ \ \ (Pent\text{-}1\text{-}ene)\]
    \[(b)\ CH_3-CH=CH-CH_2-CH_3\ \ \ (Pent\text{-}2\text{-}ene)\]
    \[(c)\ CH_3-C(CH_3)=CH-CH_3\ \ \ (2\text{-}Methylbut\text{-}2\text{-}ene)\]
    \[(d)\ CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CH=CH_2\ \ \ (3\text{-}Methylbut\text{-}1\text{-}ene)\]
    \[(e)\ CH_2=C(CH_3)-CH_2-CH_3\ \ \ (2\text{-}Methylbut\text{-}1\text{-}ene)\]

    Which of the following compounds will show cis-trans isomerism?
    (i) (CH3)2C=CH–C2H5
    (ii) CH2=CBr2
    (iii) C6H5CH=CH–CH3
    (iv) CH3CH=CClCH3

    Compounds (iii) and (iv) show cis-trans isomerism. In (i) and (ii), one of the doubly bonded carbons carries two identical groups (two CH3 in (i), two H and two Br in (ii)), so cis and trans forms are not possible.

    Draw the cis and trans structures of hex-2-ene. Which isomer will have higher b.p. and why?

    • cis-Hex-2-ene: the CH3 and C3H7 groups are on the same side of the double bond.
    • trans-Hex-2-ene: the CH3 and C3H7 groups are on opposite sides of the double bond.

    The cis isomer has the higher boiling point. In the cis form, the bond dipoles do not cancel, so it has a net dipole moment and stronger dipole-dipole attractions. In the trans form, the dipoles nearly cancel, so it is almost non-polar.

    Write IUPAC names of the products obtained by addition reactions of HBr to hex-1-ene (i) in the absence of peroxide and (ii) in the presence of peroxide.

    • (i) Without peroxide, addition follows the Markovnikov rule:
    \[CH_2=CH-(CH_2)_3-CH_3 + HBr &rarr; CH_3-CHBr-(CH_2)_3-CH_3\ \ \ (2\text{-}Bromohexane)\]
    • (ii) With peroxide, anti-Markovnikov addition takes place:
    \[CH_2=CH-(CH_2)_3-CH_3 + HBr \xrightarrow{Peroxide} BrCH_2-CH_2-(CH_2)_3-CH_3\ \ \ (1\text{-}Bromohexane)\]

    An alkene 'A' on ozonolysis gives a mixture of ethanal and pentan-3-one. Write structure and IUPAC name of 'A'.

    Join the two carbonyl carbons by a double bond (removing the oxygens):

    \[CH_3CHO + O=C(C_2H_5)_2 &rArr; CH_3-CH=C(C_2H_5)-CH_2-CH_3\]

    'A' is 3-ethylpent-2-ene.

    An alkene 'A' contains three C–C, eight C–H σ bonds and one C–C π bond. 'A' on ozonolysis gives two moles of an aldehyde of molar mass 44 u. Write IUPAC name of 'A'.

    • An aldehyde of molar mass 44 u is ethanal, CH3CHO (24 + 4 + 16 = 44).
    • Two moles of ethanal come from a symmetrical alkene: joining two CH3CH= units gives CH3–CH=CH–CH3.
    • Check: it has three C–C σ bonds (including the σ part of C=C), eight C–H σ bonds and one π bond.

    'A' is but-2-ene.

    Propanal and pentan-3-one are the ozonolysis products of an alkene. What is the structural formula of the alkene?

    \[CH_3CH_2CHO + O=C(C_2H_5)_2 &rArr; CH_3CH_2-CH=C(CH_2CH_3)-CH_2CH_3\]

    The alkene is 3-ethylhex-3-ene.

    Questions on Alkynes#

    How will you convert ethanoic acid into benzene?

    \[CH_3COOH \xrightarrow{NaOH(aq)} CH_3COONa \xrightarrow[&Delta;]{Soda\ lime} CH_4\]
    \[CH_4 \xrightarrow[h\nu]{Cl_2} CH_3Cl \xrightarrow[Wurtz\ reaction]{Na/dry\ ether} C_2H_6 \xrightarrow[h\nu]{Cl_2} C_2H_5Cl\]
    \[C_2H_5Cl \xrightarrow{alc.\ KOH} CH_2=CH_2 \xrightarrow{Br_2} BrCH_2-CH_2Br\]
    \[BrCH_2-CH_2Br \xrightarrow{alc.\ KOH} CH_2=CHBr \xrightarrow{NaNH_2} CH \equiv CH\]
    \[3CH \equiv CH \xrightarrow[873\ K]{Red\ hot\ iron\ tube} C_6H_6\]

    Arrange benzene, n-hexane and ethyne in decreasing order of acidic behaviour. Also give reason for this behaviour.

    \[Ethyne > Benzene > n\text{-}Hexane\]

    The C–H carbon is sp hybridised in ethyne (50% s character), sp2 in benzene (33% s character) and sp3 in n-hexane (25% s character). Greater s character means greater electronegativity of carbon, so the C–H electron pair is held more tightly by carbon and the H is released as a proton more easily.

    Questions on Aromatic Hydrocarbons#

    Why is benzene extraordinarily stable though it contains three double bonds?

    Benzene is a resonance hybrid of two Kekulé structures. Its six π electrons are delocalised over all six carbon atoms, forming electron clouds above and below the ring. The delocalised cloud is held more strongly by the nuclei than localised π bonds, which lowers the energy of the molecule. All C–C bonds are equal (139 pm), so there are no true double bonds, and benzene resists addition reactions.

    Explain why the following systems are not aromatic:
    (i) cyclohexadiene with an exocyclic =CH2 group (methylenecyclohexadiene)
    (ii) cyclopentadiene
    (iii) cyclooctatetraene

    • (i) One ring carbon is sp3 hybridised, so the π electrons are not completely delocalised around the ring.
    • (ii) The CH2 carbon of the ring is sp3 hybridised, so there is no complete delocalisation. Also, it has only 4 π electrons, which does not fit (4n + 2).
    • (iii) It has 8 π electrons, which is not a (4n + 2) number. It is also non-planar (tub shaped), so complete delocalisation is not possible.

    How will you convert benzene into
    (i) p-nitrobromobenzene
    (ii) m-nitrochlorobenzene
    (iii) p-nitrotoluene
    (iv) acetophenone?

    • (i) Brominate first, because –Br is o, p directing. Then nitrate and separate the para isomer:
    \[C_6H_6 \xrightarrow{Br_2/FeBr_3} C_6H_5Br \xrightarrow{Conc.\ HNO_3/H_2SO_4} p\text{-}BrC_6H_4NO_2\ (+\ ortho\ isomer)\]
    • (ii) Nitrate first, because –NO2 is meta directing. Then chlorinate:
    \[C_6H_6 \xrightarrow{Conc.\ HNO_3/H_2SO_4} C_6H_5NO_2 \xrightarrow{Cl_2/anhyd.\ AlCl_3} m\text{-}ClC_6H_4NO_2\]
    • (iii) Methylate first by Friedel-Crafts alkylation (–CH3 is o, p directing), then nitrate and separate the para isomer:
    \[C_6H_6 \xrightarrow{CH_3Cl/anhyd.\ AlCl_3} C_6H_5CH_3 \xrightarrow{Conc.\ HNO_3/H_2SO_4} p\text{-}CH_3C_6H_4NO_2\ (+\ ortho\ isomer)\]
    • (iv) Friedel-Crafts acylation:
    \[C_6H_6 + CH_3COCl \xrightarrow{anhyd.\ AlCl_3} C_6H_5COCH_3 + HCl\]

    Write down the products of ozonolysis of 1,2-dimethylbenzene (o-xylene). How does the result support Kekulé structure for benzene?

    o-Xylene can be written as two Kekulé structures. Ozonolysis of each gives a different set of products:

    \[Structure\ I &rarr; 2\ CH_3COCHO\ (methylglyoxal) + OHC-CHO\ (glyoxal)\]
    \[Structure\ II &rarr; CH_3COCOCH_3\ (dimethylglyoxal) + 2\ OHC-CHO\ (glyoxal)\]

    In practice, all three products (glyoxal, methylglyoxal and dimethylglyoxal) are obtained. This can only happen if o-xylene (and hence benzene) is a resonance hybrid of both Kekulé structures.

    Arrange the following in decreasing order of reactivity with an electrophile, E+:
    (a) Chlorobenzene, 2,4-dinitrochlorobenzene, p-nitrochlorobenzene
    (b) Toluene, p-H3C–C6H4–NO2, p-O2N–C6H4–NO2

    Electron withdrawing –NO2 groups deactivate the ring, while the electron donating –CH3 group activates it.

    \[(a)\ Chlorobenzene > p\text{-}Nitrochlorobenzene > 2,4\text{-}Dinitrochlorobenzene\]
    \[(b)\ Toluene > p\text{-}CH_3C_6H_4NO_2 > p\text{-}O_2NC_6H_4NO_2\]

    Out of benzene, m-dinitrobenzene and toluene, which will undergo nitration most easily and why?

    Toluene undergoes nitration most easily. The –CH3 group is electron donating and activates the ring, increasing its electron density. Two –NO2 groups in m-dinitrobenzene strongly deactivate the ring. So the order is: toluene > benzene > m-dinitrobenzene.

    Suggest the name of a Lewis acid other than anhydrous aluminium chloride which can be used during ethylation of benzene.

    Anhydrous ferric chloride, FeCl3.

    Why does benzene undergo electrophilic substitution reactions easily and nucleophilic substitutions with difficulty?

    Benzene has a cloud of six delocalised π electrons above and below the ring, which makes it electron rich. It therefore attracts electrophiles (electron seeking species) easily. Nucleophiles are electron rich themselves and are repelled by the π electron cloud, so nucleophilic substitution is difficult.
