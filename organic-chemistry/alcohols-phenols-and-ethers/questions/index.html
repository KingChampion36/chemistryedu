    Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers | Questions#

    In this article, we will solve some questions based on nomenclature, preparation, properties and reactions of alcohols, phenols and ethers.

    Classification and Nomenclature#

    Classify the following as primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols, and identify the allylic alcohols:
    (i) (CH3)3C–CH2OH
    (ii) H2C=CH–CH2OH
    (iii) CH3–CH2–CH2–OH
    (iv) C6H5–CH(OH)–CH3
    (v) C6H5–CH2–CH(OH)–CH3
    (vi) C6H5–CH=CH–C(CH3)2–OH

    • Primary alcohols: (i), (ii), (iii) — the carbinol carbon is bonded to only one carbon.

    • Secondary alcohols: (iv) and (v) — the carbinol carbon is bonded to two carbons.

    • Tertiary alcohol: (vi) — the carbinol carbon is bonded to three carbons.

    • Allylic alcohols: (ii) and (vi), since in both the –OH is on an sp3 carbon next to a C=C double bond.

    Give IUPAC names of the following compounds:
    (i) CH3–CH(Cl)–CH(CH3)–CH(CH3)–CH2OH
    (ii) CH3–CH(CH3)–O–CH2CH3
    (iii) Phenol with –CH3 groups at both ortho positions
    (iv) Cyclohexane with –OC2H5 and –NO2 on adjacent carbons

    • (i) The chain is numbered from the –CH2OH end: 4-Chloro-2,3-dimethylpentan-1-ol

    • (ii) The larger group (propane) is the parent; ethoxy is the substituent: 2-Ethoxypropane

    • (iii) 2,6-Dimethylphenol

    • (iv) Substituents are numbered in alphabetical order (ethoxy before nitro): 1-Ethoxy-2-nitrocyclohexane

    Name the following according to the IUPAC system:
    (i) CH3–CH2–CH(CH2Cl)–CH(CH2OH)–CH(CH3)–CH3
    (ii) (CH3)2CH–CH2–CH(OH)–CH(CH3)–CH2OH
    (iii) Cyclohexanol with Br at C-3
    (iv) H2C=CH–CH(OH)–CH2–CH2–CH3
    (v) CH3–C(CH3)=C(Br)–CH2OH

    • (i) The longest chain containing the –CH2OH carbon is numbered from that carbon: 3-Chloromethyl-2-isopropylpentan-1-ol

    • (ii) Both –OH groups lie on the main chain: 2,5-Dimethylhexane-1,3-diol

    • (iii) 3-Bromocyclohexanol

    • (iv) Hex-1-en-3-ol

    • (v) 2-Bromo-3-methylbut-2-en-1-ol

    Give the structures and IUPAC names of monohydric phenols of molecular formula C7H8O.

    C7H8O as a monohydric phenol means C6H4(CH3)OH. The –CH3 can be ortho, meta or para to –OH:

    • o-CH3C6H4OH: 2-Methylphenol (o-cresol)

    • m-CH3C6H4OH: 3-Methylphenol (m-cresol)

    • p-CH3C6H4OH: 4-Methylphenol (p-cresol)

    Preparation#

    Give the structures and IUPAC names of the products expected from:
    (a) Catalytic reduction of butanal.
    (b) Hydration of propene in the presence of dilute sulphuric acid.
    (c) Reaction of propanone with methylmagnesium bromide followed by hydrolysis.

    \[(a)\ CH_3CH_2CH_2CHO + H_2 \xrightarrow{Pd} CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-OH\ \ (Butan-1-ol)\]
    \[(b)\ CH_3CH=CH_2 + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+} CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_3\ \ (Propan-2-ol)\]

    Markovnikov addition places –OH on the middle carbon.

    \[(c)\ CH_3COCH_3 + CH_3MgBr &rarr; (CH_3)_3C-OMgBr \xrightarrow{H_2O} (CH_3)_3C-OH\]

    The product is 2-methylpropan-2-ol (a ketone gives a tertiary alcohol).

    Show how the following alcohols are prepared by the reaction of a suitable Grignard reagent on methanal:
    (i) (CH3)2CH–CH2OH
    (ii) Cyclohexylmethanol, C6H11CH2OH

    Methanal adds one –CH2OH carbon to the R group of RMgX, so R must be the rest of the molecule.

    \[(i)\ (CH_3)_2CH-MgBr + HCHO &rarr; (CH_3)_2CH-CH_2-OMgBr \xrightarrow{H_2O} (CH_3)_2CH-CH_2OH + Mg(OH)Br\]
    \[(ii)\ C_6H_{11}MgBr + HCHO &rarr; C_6H_{11}CH_2OMgBr \xrightarrow{H_2O} C_6H_{11}CH_2OH + Mg(OH)Br\]

    Write the products of the following reactions:
    (i) CH3–CH=CH2 with H2O/H+
    (ii) Methyl 2-(2-oxocyclohexyl)ethanoate (cyclohexanone carrying a –CH2COOCH3 group on C-2) with NaBH4
    (iii) CH3–CH2–CH(CH3)–CHO with NaBH4

    • (i) CH3–CH(OH)–CH3 (Markovnikov addition)

    • (ii) NaBH4 reduces the ketone C=O to –CH(OH)– but does not reduce the ester group. The product is the cyclohexanol carrying the unchanged –CH2COOCH3 group on the adjacent carbon.

    • (iii) The aldehyde is reduced to a primary alcohol: CH3–CH2–CH(CH3)–CH2OH

    How are the following conversions carried out?
    (i) Propene to propan-2-ol
    (ii) Benzyl chloride to benzyl alcohol
    (iii) Ethylmagnesium chloride to propan-1-ol
    (iv) Methylmagnesium bromide to 2-methylpropan-2-ol

    \[(i)\ CH_3CH=CH_2 + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+} CH_3CH(OH)CH_3\]
    \[(ii)\ C_6H_5CH_2Cl + aq\ NaOH &rarr; C_6H_5CH_2OH + NaCl\]
    \[(iii)\ C_2H_5MgCl + HCHO &rarr; CH_3CH_2CH_2OMgCl \xrightarrow{H_2O} CH_3CH_2CH_2OH\]
    \[(iv)\ CH_3MgBr + CH_3COCH_3 &rarr; (CH_3)_3COMgBr \xrightarrow{H_2O} (CH_3)_3COH\]

    Physical Properties and Acidity#

    Arrange the following in increasing order of boiling points:
    (a) Pentan-1-ol, butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, ethanol, propan-1-ol, methanol.
    (b) Pentan-1-ol, n-butane, pentanal, ethoxyethane.

    • (a) Boiling point increases with molar mass; among isomers, the branched one (butan-2-ol) boils lower:
    \[Methanol < Ethanol < Propan-1-ol < Butan-2-ol < Butan-1-ol < Pentan-1-ol\]
    • (b) n-Butane is non-polar; ethoxyethane is weakly polar; pentanal has a stronger C=O dipole; pentan-1-ol has hydrogen bonding:
    \[n-Butane < Ethoxyethane < Pentanal < Pentan-1-ol\]

    Why does ethanol have a higher boiling point than methoxymethane, although both have molecular mass 46?

    Ethanol has an O–H group, so its molecules are associated through intermolecular hydrogen bonding. Methoxymethane has no hydrogen attached to oxygen and cannot form hydrogen bonds between its own molecules. More energy is needed to separate ethanol molecules, so ethanol boils at 351 K while methoxymethane boils at 248 K.

    Arrange the following in increasing order of acid strength:
    Propan-1-ol, 2,4,6-trinitrophenol, 3-nitrophenol, 3,5-dinitrophenol, phenol, 4-methylphenol.

    • An alcohol is the weakest acid. Electron releasing –CH3 makes 4-methylphenol weaker than phenol.

    • Electron withdrawing –NO2 groups increase acidity; the more nitro groups, and the more of them at ortho/para positions, the stronger the acid.

    \[Propan-1-ol < 4-Methylphenol < Phenol < 3-Nitrophenol < 3,5-Dinitrophenol < 2,4,6-Trinitrophenol\]

    Explain why ortho-nitrophenol is more acidic than ortho-methoxyphenol.

    • –NO2 is an electron withdrawing group. It pulls electron density away from oxygen and delocalises the negative charge of the phenoxide ion onto itself, stabilising the ion. So o-nitrophenol ionises more easily.

    • –OCH3 is an electron releasing group (by resonance). It increases the electron density on the ring and on the phenoxide oxygen, destabilising the phenoxide ion. So o-methoxyphenol is less acidic.

    While separating a mixture of ortho and para nitrophenols by steam distillation, which isomer will be steam volatile? Give reason.

    o-Nitrophenol is steam volatile. It has intramolecular hydrogen bonding between the –OH and the neighbouring –NO2 group, so its molecules do not associate with each other. p-Nitrophenol has intermolecular hydrogen bonding, which associates its molecules and lowers its volatility.

    Reactions of Alcohols and Phenols#

    Give the products when (i) butan-1-ol and (ii) 2-methylbutan-2-ol react with (a) HCl–ZnCl2 (b) HBr and (c) SOCl2.

    In each case, –OH is replaced by a halogen.

    Reagent Butan-1-ol 2-Methylbutan-2-ol
    HCl–ZnCl2 CH3CH2CH2CH2Cl (slow; no turbidity at room temperature) CH3CH2C(CH3)2Cl (immediate turbidity)
    HBr CH3CH2CH2CH2Br CH3CH2C(CH3)2Br
    SOCl2 CH3CH2CH2CH2Cl CH3CH2C(CH3)2Cl

    Predict the major product of acid catalysed dehydration of (i) 1-methylcyclohexanol and (ii) butan-1-ol.

    • (i) The tertiary carbocation loses a proton from the ring carbon to give the more substituted alkene: 1-Methylcyclohexene.

    • (ii) Loss of water and a proton from C-2 gives but-1-ene.

    \[CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2OH \xrightarrow{H^+} CH_3CH_2CH=CH_2 + H_2O\]

    Write the mechanism of acid dehydration of ethanol to yield ethene.

    \[Step\ 1\ (fast):\ CH_3CH_2OH + H^+ &#8652; CH_3CH_2\overset{+}{O}H_2\]
    \[Step\ 2\ (slow):\ CH_3CH_2\overset{+}{O}H_2 &#8652; CH_3\overset{+}{C}H_2 + H_2O\]
    \[Step\ 3:\ CH_3\overset{+}{C}H_2 &#8652; CH_2=CH_2 + H^+\]

    Step 2 is the rate determining step. Ethene is removed as it forms to drive the equilibrium forward.

    Name the reagents used for:
    (i) Oxidation of a primary alcohol to carboxylic acid
    (ii) Oxidation of a primary alcohol to aldehyde
    (iii) Bromination of phenol to 2,4,6-tribromophenol
    (iv) Benzyl alcohol to benzoic acid
    (v) Dehydration of propan-2-ol to propene
    (vi) Butan-2-one to butan-2-ol

    • (i) Acidified potassium permanganate (KMnO4/H+)

    • (ii) Pyridinium chlorochromate (PCC), or CrO3 in anhydrous medium

    • (iii) Bromine water

    • (iv) Acidified KMnO4

    • (v) 85% H3PO4 at 440 K (or conc. H2SO4)

    • (vi) NaBH4 or LiAlH4 (or H2 with Pt/Pd/Ni)

    Write the structures of the major products expected from:
    (a) Mononitration of 3-methylphenol
    (b) Dinitration of 3-methylphenol

    Both –OH and –CH3 are ortho, para directing; –OH is the stronger director. The nitro group enters positions that are ortho/para to –OH and not too crowded.

    • (a) A mixture of 3-methyl-4-nitrophenol (–NO2 para to –OH) and 5-methyl-2-nitrophenol (–NO2 ortho to –OH, on the side away from –CH3).

    • (b) 5-Methyl-2,4-dinitrophenol (nitro groups at one ortho and the para position of –OH).

    Ortho and para nitrophenols are more acidic than phenol. Explain using the resonance structures of the corresponding phenoxide ions.

    • In o- and p-nitrophenoxide ions, the negative charge on oxygen is delocalised into the ring and onto the ortho and para carbons, just as in phenoxide ion.

    • Since the –NO2 group sits on one of these carbons, the charge can move further onto the nitro group, giving an extra resonance structure in which the negative charge is on the oxygen of –NO2 (–N+(O-)O-).

    • This extra delocalisation stabilises the o- and p-nitrophenoxide ions more than phenoxide ion, so o- and p-nitrophenols ionise more readily. In m-nitrophenoxide, the charge never reaches the carbon bearing –NO2, so only the weaker inductive effect operates, which is why m-nitrophenol (pKa 8.3) is less acidic than the o- and p-isomers.

    When 3-methylbutan-2-ol is treated with HBr, 2-bromo-2-methylbutane is formed. Give a mechanism for this reaction.

    \[Step\ 1:\ (CH_3)_2CH-CH(OH)-CH_3 + H^+ &rarr; (CH_3)_2CH-CH(\overset{+}{O}H_2)-CH_3\]
    \[Step\ 2:\ (CH_3)_2CH-CH(\overset{+}{O}H_2)-CH_3 &rarr; (CH_3)_2CH-\overset{+}{C}H-CH_3 + H_2O\]

    A secondary carbocation is formed. A hydride ion shifts from the neighbouring (3rd) carbon to give a more stable tertiary carbocation:

    \[Step\ 3:\ (CH_3)_2CH-\overset{+}{C}H-CH_3 &rarr; (CH_3)_2\overset{+}{C}-CH_2-CH_3\]
    \[Step\ 4:\ (CH_3)_2\overset{+}{C}-CH_2-CH_3 + Br^- &rarr; (CH_3)_2C(Br)-CH_2-CH_3\]

    Ethers#

    The reaction C2H5ONa + (CH3)3C–Cl is not appropriate for preparing t-butyl ethyl ether.
    (i) What would be the major product?
    (ii) Write a suitable reaction for the preparation of t-butyl ethyl ether.

    • (i) The major product is 2-methylprop-1-ene, (CH3)2C=CH2. Sodium ethoxide is a strong base as well as a nucleophile, and with a tertiary halide elimination predominates over substitution.

    • (ii) Take the tertiary group from the alkoxide and use a primary halide:

    \[(CH_3)_3C-O^-Na^+ + CH_3CH_2Cl &rarr; (CH_3)_3C-OC_2H_5 + NaCl\]

    Write the reactions of Williamson synthesis of 2-ethoxy-3-methylpentane starting from ethanol and 3-methylpentan-2-ol.

    The secondary group comes from the alkoxide; ethanol is converted into the primary halide.

    \[CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)CH(OH)CH_3 + Na &rarr; CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)CH(ONa)CH_3 + {1 \over 2}H_2\]
    \[C_2H_5OH \xrightarrow{HBr} C_2H_5Br\]
    \[CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)CH(ONa)CH_3 + C_2H_5Br &rarr; CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)CH(OC_2H_5)CH_3 + NaBr\]

    Which of the following is an appropriate set of reactants for the preparation of 1-methoxy-4-nitrobenzene, and why?
    (i) p-BrC6H4NO2 + CH3ONa
    (ii) p-NaOC6H4NO2 + CH3Br

    Set (ii) is appropriate. Williamson synthesis needs SN2 attack on an alkyl halide. In (ii), the phenoxide ion attacks CH3Br easily. In (i), the halogen is on an aryl (sp2) carbon, and aryl halides do not undergo SN2 substitution under these conditions because the C–Br bond has partial double bond character.

    \[p-O_2NC_6H_4ONa + CH_3Br &rarr; p-O_2NC_6H_4OCH_3 + NaBr\]

    Why is acid dehydration of secondary or tertiary alcohols not a suitable method for preparing ethers?

    With secondary and tertiary alcohols, the reaction goes through a carbocation (SN1). Such carbocations readily lose a proton, so elimination competes strongly with substitution and alkenes are formed instead of ethers.

    Give the major products formed by heating each of the following ethers with HI:
    (i) CH3–CH2–CH(CH3)–CH2–O–CH2–CH3
    (ii) CH3–CH2–CH2–O–C(CH3)2–CH2CH3
    (iii) C6H5–CH2–O–C6H5

    • (i) Both groups are primary; I- attacks the less hindered ethyl group (SN2):
    \[CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)CH_2OH + CH_3CH_2I\]
    • (ii) One group is tertiary, so the reaction is SN1 and the tertiary iodide forms:
    \[CH_3CH_2CH_2OH + CH_3CH_2C(CH_3)_2I\]
    • (iii) The aryl–O bond is not broken; the benzyl–O bond breaks:
    \[C_6H_5CH_2I + C_6H_5OH\]

    Predict the products of the following reactions:
    (i) CH3–CH2–CH2–O–CH3 + HBr
    (ii) C6H5OC2H5 + HBr
    (iii) C6H5OC2H5 with conc. H2SO4 / conc. HNO3
    (iv) (CH3)3C–OC2H5 + HI

    \[(i)\ CH_3CH_2CH_2OH + CH_3Br\]
    \[(ii)\ C_6H_5OH + C_2H_5Br\]
    \[(iii)\ o-O_2NC_6H_4OC_2H_5 + p-O_2NC_6H_4OC_2H_5\]
    \[(iv)\ (CH_3)_3C-I + C_2H_5OH\]

    In (i), Br- attacks the smaller methyl group (SN2); in (iv), the tertiary carbocation forms (SN1).
