    Drugs and Drug-Target Interaction#

    Why do we need to classify drugs in different ways?

    Different classifications serve different users:

    • Classification by pharmacological effect helps doctors, as it gives the whole range of drugs available for a particular problem.
    • Classification by drug action groups drugs that act on the same biochemical process.
    • Classification by chemical structure groups drugs with common structural features, which often have similar activity.
    • Classification by molecular targets is most useful to medicinal chemists for designing drugs.

    Explain the term target molecules or drug targets as used in medicinal chemistry. Name the macromolecules that are chosen as drug targets.

    Drugs interact with biological macromolecules in the body to produce their effect. These macromolecules are called target molecules or drug targets.

    The macromolecules chosen as drug targets are carbohydrates, lipids, proteins (such as enzymes and receptors) and nucleic acids.

    Define the term chemotherapy.

    The use of chemicals for therapeutic effect, i.e., for the diagnosis, prevention and treatment of diseases, is called chemotherapy.

    Why should medicines not be taken without consulting doctors? (Also: why are sleeping pills not to be taken without a doctor's advice?)

    Most drugs act as poisons if taken in doses higher than recommended, and a drug may have side effects or act on unintended targets. Only a doctor can decide the correct drug and safe dose. Sleeping pills, for example, are tranquilizers; an overdose can be harmful or even fatal. Hence a doctor should always be consulted before taking medicine.

    Which forces are involved in holding the drugs to the active site of enzymes?

    Drugs (and substrates) are held at the active site of enzymes by ionic bonding, hydrogen bonding, van der Waals interactions and dipole-dipole interactions.

    With reference to which classification has the statement, 'ranitidine is an antacid' been given?

    It refers to classification based on pharmacological effect, because any drug used to counteract the effect of excess acid in the stomach is called an antacid.

    Therapeutic Action of Drugs#

    While antacids and antiallergic drugs interfere with the function of histamines, why do these not interfere with the function of each other?

    Antacids (like cimetidine and ranitidine) and antiallergic drugs (antihistamines like brompheniramine) work on different receptors. Since each receptor is selective for its binding partner, a drug that blocks one type of histamine receptor does not affect the other.

    Low level of noradrenaline is the cause of depression. What type of drugs are needed to cure this problem? Name two drugs.

    Antidepressant drugs are needed. They inhibit the enzymes that degrade noradrenaline, so noradrenaline is metabolised slowly and activates its receptor for longer.

    Examples: iproniazid and phenelzine.

    Why are cimetidine and ranitidine better antacids than sodium hydrogencarbonate or magnesium or aluminium hydroxide?

    • NaHCO3 and metal hydroxides only neutralise the acid already present; they control the symptoms, not the cause. Excess NaHCO3 can even make the stomach alkaline and trigger more acid production.
    • Cimetidine and ranitidine prevent the interaction of histamine with the receptors in the stomach wall. Since histamine stimulates acid secretion, less acid is released. They therefore act on the cause of hyperacidity.

    What is meant by the term 'broad spectrum antibiotics'? Explain.

    Antibiotics which kill or inhibit a wide range of Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria are called broad spectrum antibiotics.

    Examples: ampicillin, amoxycillin, chloramphenicol, vancomycin and ofloxacin. In contrast, penicillin G is a narrow spectrum antibiotic.

    How do antiseptics differ from disinfectants? Give one example of each.

    • Antiseptics are applied to living tissues such as wounds, cuts, ulcers and diseased skin. Example: dettol (or tincture of iodine).
    • Disinfectants are applied to inanimate objects such as floors, drains and instruments. Example: 1% phenol solution (or chlorine at 0.2 - 0.4 ppm).

    Name a substance which can be used as an antiseptic as well as disinfectant.

    Phenol: a 0.2% solution is an antiseptic, while a 1% solution is a disinfectant.

    What are the main constituents of dettol?

    Dettol is a mixture of chloroxylenol and terpineol.

    What is tincture of iodine? What is its use?

    A 2-3% solution of iodine in an alcohol-water mixture is called tincture of iodine. It is a powerful antiseptic and is applied on wounds.

    Chemicals in Food#

    What are food preservatives?

    Food preservatives are chemicals that prevent the spoilage of food due to microbial growth. Examples: table salt, sugar, vegetable oils, sodium benzoate (C6H5COONa), and salts of sorbic acid and propanoic acid.

    Why do we require artificial sweetening agents?

    Natural sweeteners like sucrose add to the calorie intake and are harmful for diabetic patients. Artificial sweeteners give a sweet taste while adding little or no calories. They are useful for diabetic persons and for people who need to control calorie intake.

    What are artificial sweetening agents? Give two examples.

    Artificial sweetening agents are chemical substances that taste sweet but add little or no calories to the body. Examples: saccharin and aspartame (others: sucrolose, alitame).

    Why is use of aspartame limited to cold foods and drinks?

    Aspartame is unstable at cooking temperature; it decomposes on heating. Hence it is used only in cold foods and soft drinks.

    Name the sweetening agent used in the preparation of sweets for a diabetic patient.

    Sucrolose is suitable, as it is stable at cooking temperature and provides no calories. (Saccharin and alitame can also be used.)

    What problem arises in using alitame as artificial sweetener?

    Alitame is a high-potency sweetener (about 2000 times as sweet as cane sugar), so it is difficult to control the sweetness of food while using it.

    Cleansing Agents#

    Write the chemical equation for preparing sodium soap from glyceryl oleate and glyceryl palmitate.
    (i) (C15H31COO)3C3H5 - Glyceryl palmitate
    (ii) (C17H32COO)3C3H5 - Glyceryl oleate

    Heating each fat with aqueous NaOH (saponification) gives the sodium soap and glycerol:

    \[\underset{Glyceryl\ palmitate}{(C_{15}H_{31}COO)_3C_3H_5} + 3NaOH &rarr; \underset{Sodium\ palmitate}{3C_{15}H_{31}COONa} + \underset{Glycerol}{C_3H_5(OH)_3}\]
    \[\underset{Glyceryl\ oleate}{(C_{17}H_{32}COO)_3C_3H_5} + 3NaOH &rarr; \underset{Sodium\ oleate}{3C_{17}H_{32}COONa} + \underset{Glycerol}{C_3H_5(OH)_3}\]

    Why do soaps not work in hard water?

    Hard water contains Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions. These form insoluble calcium and magnesium soaps with sodium or potassium soaps:

    \[2C_{17}H_{35}COONa + CaCl_2 &rarr; 2NaCl + (C_{17}H_{35}COO)_2Ca &darr;\]

    The insoluble soap separates as scum, which is useless for cleaning and sticks to cloth as a gummy mass. So soap is wasted and does not lather.

    How are synthetic detergents better than soaps?

    • Synthetic detergents can be used in both soft and hard water, because their calcium and magnesium salts are soluble. Soaps form scum in hard water.
    • Some detergents give foam even in ice-cold water.
    • They do not leave a gummy precipitate on clothes.

    Explain the following terms with suitable examples: (i) cationic detergents (ii) anionic detergents and (iii) non-ionic detergents.

    (i) Cationic detergents: Quaternary ammonium salts of amines with acetate, chloride or bromide anions. The cationic part has a long hydrocarbon chain and a positive charge on nitrogen. Example: cetyltrimethylammonium bromide, [CH3(CH2)15N(CH3)3]+Br-, used in hair conditioners.

    (ii) Anionic detergents: Sodium salts of sulphonated long-chain alcohols or hydrocarbons; the anionic part does the cleansing. Examples: sodium laurylsulphate, CH3(CH2)10CH2OSO3-Na+, and sodium dodecylbenzenesulphonate.

    (iii) Non-ionic detergents: Contain no ions. Example: the ester formed from stearic acid and polyethyleneglycol, CH3(CH2)16COO(CH2CH2O)nCH2CH2OH, used in liquid dishwashing detergents.

    What are biodegradable and non-biodegradable detergents? Give one example of each.

    • Biodegradable detergents have straight (unbranched) hydrocarbon chains and are easily degraded by bacteria. Example: sodium laurylsulphate.
    • Non-biodegradable detergents have highly branched hydrocarbon chains which bacteria cannot degrade easily, so they accumulate and pollute water bodies. Example: sodium 4-(1,3,5,7-tetramethyloctyl)benzenesulphonate.

    Can you use soaps and synthetic detergents to check the hardness of water?

    Soaps can be used: in hard water they form a curdy precipitate (scum) and lather poorly, while in soft water they lather easily.

    Synthetic detergents cannot be used, because they lather well in both hard and soft water.

    If water contains dissolved calcium hydrogencarbonate, out of soaps and synthetic detergents which one will you use for cleaning clothes?

    Water containing Ca(HCO3)2 is hard. Soap would form insoluble calcium soap (scum) with Ca2+ ions. Hence synthetic detergents should be used, as they work well even in hard water.

    Explain the cleansing action of soaps.

    • A soap anion (e.g., C17H35COO-) has a long non-polar hydrophobic tail and a polar hydrophilic head (-COO-).
    • In water, the anions form micelles: the hydrophobic tails dissolve in the oily dirt, while the ionic heads stay in water, pointing outwards.
    • The oil drop is thus surrounded by negatively charged heads. These charged droplets repel each other and stay emulsified in water instead of coalescing.
    • On rinsing, the emulsified oil along with the dirt is washed away.

    The non-ionic detergent C9H19-C6H4-O(CH2CH2O)xCH2CH2OH (x = 5 to 10, para-substituted benzene ring) is present in liquid detergents, emulsifying agents and wetting agents. Label the hydrophilic and hydrophobic parts in the molecule. Identify the functional group(s) present.

    • Hydrophobic (non-polar) part: the C9H19-C6H4- portion (alkyl chain plus benzene ring).
    • Hydrophilic (polar) part: the -O(CH2CH2O)xCH2CH2OH chain.
    • Functional groups: ether linkages (-O-) and a primary alcohol group (-OH).
