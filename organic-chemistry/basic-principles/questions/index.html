    Basic Principles of Organic Chemistry | Questions#

    In this article, we will solve some questions related to hybridisation, structural formulas, IUPAC nomenclature, reaction intermediates, electronic effects and purification of organic compounds.

    Hybridisation and Bonding#

    How many σ and π bonds are present in each of the following molecules?
    (a) HC≡CCH=CHCH3
    (b) CH2=C=CHCH3

    • (a) σC-C: 4; σC-H: 6; πC=C: 1; πC≡C: 2
    • (b) σC-C: 3; σC-H: 6; πC=C: 2

    What is the type of hybridisation of each carbon in the following compounds?
    (a) CH3Cl (b) (CH3)2CO (c) CH3CN (d) HCONH2 (e) CH3CH=CHCN

    • (a) sp3
    • (b) sp3 (CH3), sp2 (C=O)
    • (c) sp3 (CH3), sp (C≡N)
    • (d) sp2
    • (e) sp3, sp2, sp2, sp

    Write the state of hybridisation of carbon in the following compounds and shapes of each of the molecules.
    (a) H2C=O (b) CH3F (c) HC≡N

    • (a) sp2 hybridised carbon, trigonal planar
    • (b) sp3 hybridised carbon, tetrahedral
    • (c) sp hybridised carbon, linear

    What are the hybridisation states of each carbon atom in the following compounds?
    CH2=C=O, CH3CH=CH2, (CH3)2CO, CH2=CHCN, C6H6

    • CH2=C=O: sp2, sp
    • CH3CH=CH2: sp3, sp2, sp2
    • (CH3)2CO: sp3 (both CH3), sp2 (C=O)
    • CH2=CHCN: sp2, sp2, sp
    • C6H6: all six carbons are sp2

    Indicate the σ and π bonds in the following molecules:
    C6H6, C6H12, CH2Cl2, CH2=C=CH2, CH3NO2, HCONHCH3

    Molecule σ bonds π bonds
    C6H6 12 3
    C6H12 (cyclohexane) 18 0
    CH2Cl2 4 0
    CH2=C=CH2 6 2
    CH3NO2 6 1
    HCONHCH3 8 1

    In the organic compound CH2=CH-CH2-CH2-C≡CH, the pair of hybridised orbitals involved in the formation of the C2-C3 bond is:
    (a) sp-sp2 (b) sp-sp3 (c) sp2-sp3 (d) sp3-sp3

    • C2 is a doubly bonded carbon (sp2) and C3 is a CH2 carbon (sp3).
    • Answer: (c) sp2-sp3

    Structural Formulas and Nomenclature#

    Write a condensed formula for:
    (a) HOCH2CH2CH2CH(CH3)CH(CH3)CH3
    (b) N≡C-CH(OH)-C≡N

    • (a) HO(CH2)3CH(CH3)CH(CH3)2
    • (b) HOCH(CN)2

    Explain why the following names are incorrect:
    (a) CH3CH(CH3)CH2CH2CH(CH3)CH(CH3)CH2CH3: 2,5,6-Trimethyloctane [and not 3,4,7-Trimethyloctane]
    (b) CH3CH2CH(C2H5)CH2CH(CH3)CH2CH3: 3-Ethyl-5-methylheptane [and not 5-Ethyl-3-methylheptane]

    • (a) Lowest locant rule: the set 2,5,6 is lower than 3,4,7.
    • (b) The substituents are in equivalent positions, so the lower number is given to the one that comes first in alphabetical order (ethyl).

    Write the IUPAC names of the following compounds:
    (i) CH3CH2CH(OH)CH2CH2CH(CH3)CH2CH3
    (ii) CH3CH2COCH2COCH3
    (iii) CH3COCH2CH2CH2COOH
    (iv) CH≡C-CH=CH-CH=CH2

    • (i) The functional group is -OH (suffix '-ol'). The longest chain containing it has 8 carbons (octane). -OH is on C-3 and a methyl group on C-6. Name: 6-Methyloctan-3-ol.
    • (ii) Two keto groups (suffix '-dione') on C-2 and C-4 of a six-carbon chain. Name: Hexane-2,4-dione.
    • (iii) Carboxylic acid is the principal group (suffix '-oic acid'); numbering starts from -COOH. The keto group at C-5 is shown as 'oxo'. Name: 5-Oxohexanoic acid.
    • (iv) Two C=C at carbons 1 and 3 and one C≡C at carbon 5 of a six-carbon chain. Name: Hexa-1,3-dien-5-yne.

    Derive the structures of:
    (i) 2-Chlorohexane (ii) Pent-4-en-2-ol (iii) 3-Nitrocyclohexene (iv) Cyclohex-2-en-1-ol (v) 6-Hydroxyheptanal

    • (i) CH3CH2CH2CH2CH(Cl)CH3
    • (ii) CH2=CHCH2CH(OH)CH3
    • (iii) A cyclohexene ring numbered so that the double bond is between C-1 and C-2; -NO2 is on C-3. The double bond is a suffixed group, so it gets preference over the prefixed -NO2.
    • (iv) -OH on C-1 of the ring and the double bond between C-2 and C-3. -OH (suffix) gets preference over C=C.
    • (v) CH3CH(OH)CH2CH2CH2CH2CHO (the -CHO carbon is C-1).

    Give the IUPAC names of:
    (a) C6H5CH2CH2CH3 (b) CH3CH2CH(CH3)CH2CN (c) CH3CH(CH3)CH2CH2CH(CH3)CH2CH3
    (d) CH3CH2C(Cl)(Br)CH2CH2CH2CH3 (e) ClCH2CH2CHO (f) Cl2CHCH2OH

    • (a) Propylbenzene
    • (b) 3-Methylpentanenitrile
    • (c) 2,5-Dimethylheptane
    • (d) 3-Bromo-3-chloroheptane
    • (e) 3-Chloropropanal
    • (f) 2,2-Dichloroethanol

    Which of the following represents the correct IUPAC name?
    (a) 2,2-Dimethylpentane or 2-Dimethylpentane
    (b) 2,4,7-Trimethyloctane or 2,5,7-Trimethyloctane
    (c) 2-Chloro-4-methylpentane or 4-Chloro-2-methylpentane
    (d) But-3-yn-1-ol or But-4-ol-1-yne

    • (a) 2,2-Dimethylpentane: each methyl group needs its own locant.
    • (b) 2,4,7-Trimethyloctane: the locant set 2,4,7 is lower than 2,5,7.
    • (c) 2-Chloro-4-methylpentane: both sets are 2,4; the lower number goes to chloro, which comes first alphabetically.
    • (d) But-3-yn-1-ol: -OH is the principal group and gets the lowest number.

    Give condensed formulas and identify the functional groups present in:
    (a) 2,2,4-Trimethylpentane (b) 2-Hydroxy-1,2,3-propanetricarboxylic acid (c) Hexanedial

    • (a) (CH3)3CCH2CH(CH3)2: no functional group (alkane).
    • (b) HOOCCH2C(OH)(COOH)CH2COOH: hydroxyl (-OH) and carboxylic acid (-COOH) groups.
    • (c) OHC(CH2)4CHO: two aldehyde (-CHO) groups.

    Reaction Intermediates and Reagents#

    Show the formation of reactive intermediates when the following covalent bonds undergo heterolytic cleavage:
    (a) CH3-SCH3 (b) CH3-CN (c) CH3-Cu

    \[(a)\ CH_3-SCH_3 &rarr; \overset{+}{C}H_3 + \overset{-}{S}CH_3\]
    \[(b)\ CH_3-CN &rarr; \overset{+}{C}H_3 + \overset{-}{C}N\]
    \[(c)\ CH_3-Cu &rarr; \overset{-}{C}H_3 + \overset{+}{C}u\]

    In (a) and (b) the more electronegative group takes the electron pair, giving a carbocation. In (c), carbon is more electronegative than Cu, so it keeps the pair and a carbanion forms.

    Categorise the following as nucleophile or electrophile:
    HS-, BF3, C2H5O-, (CH3)3N:, Cl+, CH3-C+=O, H2N:-, NO2+

    • Nucleophiles: HS-, C2H5O-, (CH3)3N:, H2N:-. They have unshared pairs of electrons which can be donated to an electrophile.
    • Electrophiles: BF3, Cl+, CH3-C+=O, NO2+. Their reactive sites have only six valence electrons and can accept an electron pair.

    Identify the electrophilic centre in: CH3CH=O, CH3CN, CH3I.

    • The carbonyl carbon in CH3HC=O, the cyano carbon in H3C-C≡N and the CH3 carbon in H3C-I are electrophilic centres, as they carry a partial positive charge due to bond polarity.

    Identify the reagents in bold as nucleophiles or electrophiles:
    (a) CH3COOH + HO- → CH3COO- + H2O
    (b) CH3COCH3 + -CN → (CH3)2C(CN)(OH)
    (c) C6H6 + CH3C+O → C6H5COCH3

    • (a) HO-: nucleophile (b) -CN: nucleophile (c) CH3C+O: electrophile

    Classify the following reactions:
    (a) CH3CH2Br + HS- → CH3CH2SH
    (b) (CH3)2C=CH2 + HCl → (CH3)2ClC-CH3
    (c) CH3CH2Br + HO- → CH2=CH2 + H2O
    (d) (CH3)3C-CH2OH + HBr → (CH3)2CBrCH2CH3

    • (a) Substitution (nucleophilic) (b) Addition (electrophilic) (c) Elimination (d) Rearrangement (along with substitution)

    Classify each bond cleavage as homolysis or heterolysis and identify the intermediate:
    (a) CH3O-OCH3 → 2CH3O·
    (b) Removal of an α-H+ from a ketone by -OH
    (c) (CH3)3C-Br → (CH3)3C+ + Br-
    (d) Benzene + E+ → arenium ion

    • (a) Homolysis; free radicals (CH3O·).
    • (b) Heterolysis; carbanion.
    • (c) Heterolysis; carbocation.
    • (d) Heterolysis; carbocation (the positive charge is on the ring).

    Which of the following carbocations is most stable?
    (a) (CH3)3C-C+H2 (b) (CH3)3C+ (c) CH3CH2C+H2 (d) CH3C+HCH2CH3

    • Answer: (b) (CH3)3C+. It is tertiary and is stabilised by hyperconjugation with nine C-H bonds and by the +I effect of three methyl groups.

    Electronic Effects#

    Which bond is more polar in the following pairs?
    (a) H3C-H, H3C-Br (b) H3C-NH2, H3C-OH (c) H3C-OH, H3C-SH

    • (a) C-Br, since Br is more electronegative than H.
    • (b) C-O (c) C-O

    In which C-C bond of CH3CH2CH2Br is the inductive effect expected to be the least?

    • The magnitude of the inductive effect decreases as the number of intervening bonds increases. Hence, it is least in the bond between carbon-3 and hydrogen (the bonds farthest from Br).

    Write resonance structures of CH3COO-.

    \[CH_3-C(=O)-O^- \longleftrightarrow CH_3-C(-O^-)=O\]
    • A lone pair on the negative oxygen moves to form a C=O bond, while the π electrons of the original C=O move onto the other oxygen. The two structures are equivalent.

    Explain why the following two structures, I and II, cannot be the major contributors to the real structure of CH3COOCH3:
    I: CH3-C+(O-)-OCH3
    II: CH3-C(O-)=O+-CH3

    • Both structures involve separation of charge, so they are less important contributors.
    • Additionally, in structure I, the carbon atom has an incomplete octet.

    Explain why (CH3)3C+ is more stable than CH3CH2+, and CH3+ is the least stable cation.

    • Hyperconjugation in (CH3)3C+ is greater than in CH3CH2+, as (CH3)3C+ has nine C-H bonds (CH3CH2+ has only three).
    • In CH3+, the vacant p orbital is perpendicular to the plane in which the C-H bonds lie, so they cannot overlap with it. Thus, CH3+ has no hyperconjugative stability.

    Which of the two, O2NCH2CH2O- or CH3CH2O-, is expected to be more stable and why?

    • O2NCH2CH2O- is more stable. The -NO2 group is electron withdrawing (-I effect) and disperses the negative charge on oxygen.
    • In CH3CH2O-, the ethyl group is electron donating (+I effect) and intensifies the negative charge, making it less stable.

    Explain why alkyl groups act as electron donors when attached to a π system.

    • Due to hyperconjugation. The σ electrons of the C-H bonds of the alkyl group are delocalised into the adjacent π system, increasing its electron density. More C-H bonds give a greater effect.

    Which electron displacement effect explains the following orders of acidity?
    (a) Cl3CCOOH > Cl2CHCOOH > ClCH2COOH
    (b) CH3CH2COOH > (CH3)2CHCOOH > (CH3)3CCOOH

    • (a) -I effect of chlorine. More Cl atoms withdraw more electron density, stabilising the carboxylate ion and increasing acidity.
    • (b) +I effect of alkyl groups. More alkyl groups push more electron density towards -COO-, destabilising it and decreasing acidity.

    Purification and Analysis#

    Name a suitable technique to separate the components of a mixture of calcium sulphate and camphor.

    • Sublimation. Camphor sublimes on heating, while calcium sulphate does not.

    Explain why an organic liquid vaporises at a temperature below its boiling point in steam distillation.

    • The liquid boils when the sum of its vapour pressure (p1) and that of water (p2) equals atmospheric pressure, i.e. p = p1 + p2. Since p1 needs to be only a part of p, the organic liquid vaporises at a temperature lower than its boiling point.

    Why is nitric acid added to sodium extract before adding silver nitrate for testing halogens?

    • To decompose NaCN and Na2S (if N or S is present). Otherwise, CN- and S2- would react with Ag+ and interfere with the test.

    Explain the reason for the fusion of an organic compound with metallic sodium for testing nitrogen, sulphur and halogens.

    • In organic compounds these elements are covalently bonded and cannot be detected directly. Fusion with sodium converts them into ionic compounds (NaCN, Na2S, NaX), which dissolve in water and give the usual ionic tests.

    Will CCl4 give a white precipitate of AgCl on heating it with silver nitrate? Give reason.

    • No. CCl4 is a covalent compound and does not ionise to give Cl- ions, so no AgCl is formed.

    Why is a solution of potassium hydroxide used to absorb carbon dioxide evolved during the estimation of carbon?

    • CO2 is acidic and is completely absorbed by KOH. The increase in mass of the KOH tube gives the mass of CO2.
    \[2KOH + CO_2 &rarr; K_2CO_3 + H_2O\]

    Why is acetic acid and not sulphuric acid used for acidification of sodium extract for testing sulphur by the lead acetate test?

    • Sulphuric acid would react with lead acetate to form a white precipitate of lead sulphate, which interferes with the detection of black PbS. Acetic acid does not cause this.

    In Lassaigne's test for nitrogen, the Prussian blue colour is due to the formation of:
    (a) Na4[Fe(CN)6] (b) Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3 (c) Fe2[Fe(CN)6] (d) Fe3[Fe(CN)6]4

    • Answer: (b) Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3, iron(III) hexacyanoferrate(II).

    The reaction CH3CH2I + KOH(aq) → CH3CH2OH + KI is classified as:
    (a) electrophilic substitution (b) nucleophilic substitution (c) elimination (d) addition

    • Answer: (b) nucleophilic substitution. The nucleophile OH- replaces I-.
