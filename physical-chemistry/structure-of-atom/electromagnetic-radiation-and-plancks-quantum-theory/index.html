    Structure of Atom | Electromagnetic Radiation and Planck's Quantum Theory#

    Niels Bohr improved Rutherford's model using two developments: (i) the dual character of electromagnetic radiation, i.e. radiation has both wave like and particle like properties, and (ii) atomic spectra, which can be explained only by assuming quantized electronic energy levels.

    Wave Nature of Electromagnetic Radiation#

    • James Maxwell (1870) suggested that when an electrically charged particle moves under acceleration, alternating electric and magnetic fields are produced and transmitted. These are transmitted as waves called electromagnetic waves or electromagnetic radiation.
    • Newton thought light was made of particles (corpuscles). The wave nature of light was established only in the 19th century.

    Properties of Electromagnetic Waves#

    1. The oscillating electric and magnetic fields are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation of the wave. Both have the same wavelength, frequency, speed and amplitude.
    2. Unlike sound or water waves, electromagnetic waves do not need a medium and can travel in vacuum.
    3. There are many types of electromagnetic radiation which differ in wavelength (or frequency). Together they form the electromagnetic spectrum.
    4. Different units are used to represent electromagnetic radiation.

    Regions of the Electromagnetic Spectrum#

    Region Approximate frequency Use / Example
    Radio frequency ~106 Hz Broadcasting
    Microwave ~1010 Hz Radar
    Infrared ~1013 Hz Heating
    Visible ~1015 Hz The only part our eyes can detect
    Ultraviolet ~1016 Hz Component of sun's radiation
    • In increasing order of frequency: radio waves < microwaves < infrared < visible < ultraviolet < X-rays < γ-rays.
    • The visible spectrum extends from violet (about 400 nm) to red (about 750 nm).

    Characteristics of Electromagnetic Radiation#

    1. Frequency (ν)#

    • Frequency is the number of waves that pass a given point in one second.
    • SI unit is hertz (Hz, s-1).

    2. Wavelength (λ)#

    • Wavelength is the distance between two successive crests (or troughs) of a wave. SI unit is metre (m), but smaller units like nm and Å are commonly used.

    3. Speed of Light (c)#

    • In vacuum, all types of electromagnetic radiation travel at the same speed, c = 3.0 × 108 m s-1 (2.997925 × 108 m s-1 to be precise).
    \[c = &nu; &lambda;\]

    4. Wavenumber (\(\bar{\nu}\))#

    • Wavenumber is the number of wavelengths per unit length. Its SI unit is m-1, but cm-1 is commonly used.
    \[\bar{\nu} = {1 \over &lambda;}\]

    Particle Nature of Electromagnetic Radiation: Planck's Quantum Theory#

    Some phenomena could not be explained by the wave nature of light (classical physics):

    1. Emission of radiation from hot bodies (black body radiation).
    2. Ejection of electrons from a metal surface when radiation strikes it (photoelectric effect).
    3. Variation of heat capacity of solids with temperature.
    4. Line spectra of atoms, especially hydrogen.

    Black Body Radiation#

    • When an iron rod is heated, it first turns dull red, then more and more red, then white and finally blue at very high temperature. So the emitted radiation goes from lower to higher frequency as temperature rises.
    • An ideal body which emits and absorbs radiation of all frequencies is called a black body, and the radiation emitted by it is called black body radiation.
    • The intensity versus frequency curve of a black body depends only on its temperature. At a given temperature, intensity increases with decrease of wavelength, reaches a maximum and then decreases.

    Planck's Quantum Theory#

    • Max Planck (1900) suggested that atoms and molecules can emit or absorb energy only in discrete quantities and not continuously.
    • The smallest quantity of energy that can be emitted or absorbed as electromagnetic radiation is called a quantum.
    • The energy of a quantum is proportional to the frequency of radiation:
    \[E = h&nu; = {hc \over &lambda;}\]

    Where, h = Planck's constant = 6.626 × 10-34 J s

    • Using this theory, Planck could explain the intensity distribution of black body radiation at different temperatures.

    Photoelectric Effect#

    • H. Hertz (1887) observed that electrons are ejected when certain metals (like potassium, rubidium, caesium) are exposed to a beam of light. This is called the photoelectric effect.

    Observations#

    1. Electrons are ejected as soon as light strikes the surface. There is no time lag.
    2. The number of electrons ejected is proportional to the intensity (brightness) of light.
    3. For each metal, there is a minimum frequency ν0, called the threshold frequency, below which no photoelectric effect is seen. For ν > ν0, electrons come out with some kinetic energy.
    4. The kinetic energy of ejected electrons increases with the frequency of light, but does not depend on its intensity.

    Example

    Red light (ν = 4.3 to 4.6 × 1014 Hz) of any brightness may shine on potassium for hours and no electrons are ejected. But even a very weak yellow light (ν = 5.1 to 5.2 × 1014 Hz) ejects electrons, because the threshold frequency of potassium is 5.0 × 1014 Hz.

    • According to classical physics, the energy of light depends on its brightness, so both the number and the kinetic energy of electrons should depend on brightness. This was not observed.

    Einstein's Explanation#

    • Einstein (1905) explained the effect using Planck's theory. A beam of light is a stream of particles called photons, each with energy hν.
    • When a photon of enough energy strikes an electron, it transfers its energy instantly and the electron is ejected without delay.
    • The minimum energy needed to eject an electron is hν0, called the work function (W0). The remaining energy appears as kinetic energy of the photoelectron:
    \[h&nu; = h&nu;_0 + {1 \over 2}m_ev^2\]
    \[K.E. = {1 \over 2}m_ev^2 = h(&nu; - &nu;_0)\]
    • A more intense beam has more photons, so more electrons are ejected.
    Metal Li Na K Mg Cu Ag
    W0 / eV 2.42 2.3 2.25 3.7 4.8 4.3

    Dual Behaviour of Electromagnetic Radiation#

    • The particle nature of light explains black body radiation and the photoelectric effect, but the wave nature is needed to explain interference and diffraction.
    • So light has dual behaviour: it behaves as a wave or as a stream of particles depending on the experiment.
    • When radiation interacts with matter it shows particle like properties, and when it propagates it shows wave like properties.
    • Microscopic particles like electrons also show this wave-particle duality.

    Questions#

    The Vividh Bharati station of All India Radio, Delhi, broadcasts on a frequency of 1,368 kHz. Calculate the wavelength of the electromagnetic radiation emitted. Which part of the spectrum does it belong to?

    \[&lambda; = {c \over &nu;} = {3.00 \times 10^8\ m\ s^{-1} \over 1368 \times 10^3\ s^{-1}}\]
    \[&lambda; = 219.3\ m\]

    This is a characteristic radiowave wavelength.

    The visible spectrum extends from violet (400 nm) to red (750 nm). Express these wavelengths in frequencies (Hz).

    \[&nu;_{violet} = {c \over &lambda;} = {3.00 \times 10^8 \over 400 \times 10^{-9}} = 7.50 \times 10^{14}\ Hz\]
    \[&nu;_{red} = {3.00 \times 10^8 \over 750 \times 10^{-9}} = 4.00 \times 10^{14}\ Hz\]

    So the visible spectrum extends from 4.0 × 1014 Hz to 7.5 × 1014 Hz.

    Calculate (a) wavenumber and (b) frequency of yellow radiation having wavelength 5800 Å.

    \[&lambda; = 5800\ Å = 5800 \times 10^{-10}\ m\]
    \[\bar{\nu} = {1 \over &lambda;} = {1 \over 5800 \times 10^{-10}\ m} = 1.724 \times 10^6\ m^{-1}\]
    \[&nu; = {c \over &lambda;} = {3 \times 10^8 \over 5800 \times 10^{-10}} = 5.17 \times 10^{14}\ s^{-1}\]

    Calculate energy of one mole of photons of radiation whose frequency is 5 × 1014 Hz.

    \[E = h&nu; = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 5 \times 10^{14} = 3.313 \times 10^{-19}\ J\]
    \[Energy\ of\ 1\ mole\ photons = 3.313 \times 10^{-19} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23}\]
    \[= 199.51\ kJ\ mol^{-1}\]

    A 100 watt bulb emits monochromatic light of wavelength 400 nm. Calculate the number of photons emitted per second by the bulb.

    \[Power = 100\ W = 100\ J\ s^{-1}\]
    \[E = {hc \over &lambda;} = {6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^8 \over 400 \times 10^{-9}} = 4.969 \times 10^{-19}\ J\]
    \[Number\ of\ photons = {100\ J\ s^{-1} \over 4.969 \times 10^{-19}\ J} = 2.012 \times 10^{20}\ s^{-1}\]

    When electromagnetic radiation of wavelength 300 nm falls on the surface of sodium, electrons are emitted with a kinetic energy of 1.68 × 105 J mol-1. What is the minimum energy needed to remove an electron from sodium? What is the maximum wavelength that will cause a photoelectron to be emitted?

    \[E = {hc \over &lambda;} = {6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^8 \over 300 \times 10^{-9}} = 6.626 \times 10^{-19}\ J\]
    \[Energy\ of\ 1\ mole\ photons = 6.626 \times 10^{-19} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = 3.99 \times 10^5\ J\ mol^{-1}\]
    \[Minimum\ energy\ per\ mole = (3.99 - 1.68) \times 10^5 = 2.31 \times 10^5\ J\ mol^{-1}\]
    \[Minimum\ energy\ per\ electron = {2.31 \times 10^5 \over 6.022 \times 10^{23}} = 3.84 \times 10^{-19}\ J\]
    \[&lambda;_{max} = {hc \over E} = {6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^8 \over 3.84 \times 10^{-19}} = 517\ nm\]

    This corresponds to green light.

    The threshold frequency ν0 for a metal is 7.0 × 1014 s-1. Calculate the kinetic energy of an electron emitted when radiation of frequency ν = 1.0 × 1015 s-1 hits the metal.

    \[K.E. = h(&nu; - &nu;_0)\]
    \[K.E. = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times (10.0 \times 10^{14} - 7.0 \times 10^{14})\]
    \[K.E. = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.0 \times 10^{14} = 1.988 \times 10^{-19}\ J\]

    A photon of wavelength 4 × 10-7 m strikes a metal surface, the work function of the metal being 2.13 eV. Calculate (i) the energy of the photon (eV), (ii) the kinetic energy of the emission and (iii) the velocity of the photoelectron (1 eV = 1.6020 × 10-19 J).

    \[E = {hc \over &lambda;} = {6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^8 \over 4 \times 10^{-7}} = 4.97 \times 10^{-19}\ J\]
    \[E = {4.97 \times 10^{-19} \over 1.602 \times 10^{-19}} = 3.10\ eV\]
    \[K.E. = 3.10 - 2.13 = 0.97\ eV = 1.55 \times 10^{-19}\ J\]
    \[v = \sqrt{2 \times K.E. \over m_e} = \sqrt{2 \times 1.55 \times 10^{-19} \over 9.11 \times 10^{-31}}\]
    \[v = 5.84 \times 10^5\ m\ s^{-1}\]
