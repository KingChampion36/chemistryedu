    Solid State | Questions on Solid State#

    In this article, we will solve conceptual and numerical questions on solid state, including classification of solids, voids, packing efficiency, density of unit cells, defects and semiconductors.

    Conceptual Questions#

    Why are solids rigid and why do they have a definite volume?

    • The constituent particles of solids are held at fixed positions by strong intermolecular forces and can only oscillate about their mean positions. So they cannot move or flow, which makes solids rigid.
    • Since the particles are closely packed and the intermolecular distances are short and fixed, solids are incompressible and have a definite volume.

    Classify the following as amorphous or crystalline solids: Polyurethane, naphthalene, benzoic acid, teflon, potassium nitrate, cellophane, polyvinyl chloride, fibre glass, copper.

    • Amorphous: Polyurethane, teflon, cellophane, polyvinyl chloride, fibre glass.
    • Crystalline: Naphthalene, benzoic acid, potassium nitrate, copper.

    Why is glass considered a super cooled liquid?

    Glass is an amorphous solid with only short range order, similar to a liquid. Like liquids, it has a tendency to flow, though very slowly. For example, glass panes in old buildings become slightly thicker at the bottom. Hence glass is called a super cooled liquid or pseudo solid.

    Refractive index of a solid is observed to have the same value along all directions. Comment on the nature of this solid. Would it show cleavage property?

    A solid having the same value of a physical property in all directions is isotropic. So it is an amorphous solid. When cut with a sharp edged tool, it would split into pieces with irregular surfaces, so it will not show a clean cleavage property.

    Classify the following solids based on the nature of intermolecular forces: Potassium sulphate, tin, benzene, urea, ammonia, water, zinc sulphide, graphite, rubidium, argon, silicon carbide.

    Type of solid Substances
    Ionic Potassium sulphate, zinc sulphide
    Metallic Tin, rubidium
    Molecular (non-polar) Benzene, argon
    Molecular (hydrogen bonded) Urea, ammonia, water
    Covalent or network Graphite, silicon carbide

    P4O10, (NH4)3PO4, SiC, I2, P4, plastic, graphite, brass, Rb, LiBr, Si.

    Solid A is a very hard electrical insulator in solid as well as in molten state and melts at extremely high temperature. What type of solid is it?

    It is a covalent or network solid (like diamond or SiC). Its atoms are held by strong covalent bonds throughout the crystal and there are no free electrons or ions to carry current.

    Ionic solids conduct electricity in molten state but not in solid state. Explain.

    In the solid state, the ions are held at fixed positions by strong electrostatic forces and cannot move, so ionic solids are insulators. In the molten state, the ions become free to move and carry current.

    How many lattice points are there in one unit cell of (i) face-centred cubic (ii) face-centred tetragonal (iii) body-centred lattice?

    • (i) Face-centred cubic: 8 corners x 1/8 + 6 face-centres x 1/2 = 4
    • (ii) Face-centred tetragonal: 8 corners x 1/8 + 6 face-centres x 1/2 = 4
    • (iii) Body-centred: 8 corners x 1/8 + 1 body-centre = 2

    Explain how much portion of an atom located at (i) corner and (ii) body-centre of a cubic unit cell is part of its neighbouring unit cell.

    • (i) A corner atom is shared by 8 unit cells, so only 1/8 of it belongs to one unit cell and 7/8 belongs to the neighbouring unit cells.
    • (ii) A body-centre atom belongs wholly to its own unit cell and is not shared with any neighbouring unit cell.

    What type of defect can arise when a solid is heated? Which physical property is affected by it and in what way?

    When a solid is heated, a vacancy defect can arise because some lattice sites become vacant. It decreases the density of the substance.

    What type of stoichiometric defect is shown by (i) ZnS (ii) AgBr?

    • (i) ZnS shows Frenkel defect, due to the large difference in size between Zn2+ and S2-.
    • (ii) AgBr shows both Frenkel and Schottky defects.

    Explain how vacancies are introduced in an ionic solid when a cation of higher valence is added as an impurity in it.

    When NaCl is crystallised with a little SrCl2, each Sr2+ ion replaces two Na+ ions to keep the crystal neutral. It occupies the site of one Na+ and the other site remains vacant. So one cation vacancy is created for every Sr2+ ion added.

    Ionic solids which have anionic vacancies due to metal excess defect develop colour. Explain with a suitable example.

    When NaCl crystals are heated in sodium vapour, Na atoms deposit on the surface and lose electrons to form Na+. Cl- ions diffuse to the surface and form NaCl with them. The released electrons occupy the anionic vacancies, forming F-centres. These electrons absorb energy from visible light and get excited, giving the crystal a yellow colour.

    A group 14 element is to be converted into an n-type semiconductor by doping it with a suitable impurity. To which group should this impurity belong?

    The impurity should belong to group 15 (like P or As). It has five valence electrons; four form bonds and the fifth becomes a free, delocalised electron.

    What type of substances would make better permanent magnets, ferromagnetic or ferrimagnetic? Justify your answer.

    Ferromagnetic substances make better permanent magnets. In a magnetic field, all their domains align in the same direction and this alignment persists after the field is removed. In ferrimagnetic substances, domains are aligned in parallel and anti-parallel directions in unequal numbers, so their net magnetism is weaker.

    Classify each of the following as being either a p-type or an n-type semiconductor: (i) Ge doped with In (ii) B doped with Si.

    • (i) In belongs to group 13 and has one electron less than Ge (group 14). It creates holes, so it is a p-type semiconductor.
    • (ii) Si belongs to group 14 and has one electron more than B (group 13). It provides extra electrons, so it is an n-type semiconductor.

    Non-stoichiometric cuprous oxide, Cu2O, has a copper to oxygen ratio slightly less than 2:1. Account for the fact that this substance is a p-type semiconductor.

    Since there is less copper than the ideal ratio, some Cu+ ions are missing (metal deficiency defect). To keep the crystal neutral, some Cu+ ions change to Cu2+. Each Cu2+ ion can accept an electron from a neighbour, which behaves like a positive hole. Conduction takes place through these positive holes, so it is a p-type semiconductor.

    Questions on Voids and Formula#

    What is the two dimensional coordination number of a molecule in a square close-packed layer?

    In square close packing, each sphere touches 4 neighbours. So the two dimensional coordination number is 4.

    A compound forms hexagonal close-packed structure. What is the total number of voids in 0.5 mol of it? How many of these are tetrahedral voids?

    \[Number\ of\ particles,\ N = 0.5 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = 3.011 \times 10^{23}\]
    \[Octahedral\ voids = N = 3.011 \times 10^{23}\]
    \[Tetrahedral\ voids = 2N = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\]
    \[Total\ voids = 3N = 9.033 \times 10^{23}\]

    A compound is formed by two elements M and N. The element N forms ccp and atoms of M occupy 1/3rd of tetrahedral voids. What is the formula of the compound?

    \[Let\ number\ of\ N\ atoms = x\]
    \[Tetrahedral\ voids = 2x\]
    \[Number\ of\ M\ atoms = {1 \over 3} \times 2x = {2x \over 3}\]
    \[M : N = {2x \over 3} : x = 2 : 3\]

    Hence, the formula of the compound is M2N3.

    Ferric oxide crystallises in a hexagonal close-packed array of oxide ions with two out of every three octahedral holes occupied by ferric ions. Derive the formula of the ferric oxide.

    \[Let\ number\ of\ O^{2-}\ ions = x\]
    \[Octahedral\ voids = x\]
    \[Number\ of\ Fe^{3+}\ ions = {2 \over 3}x\]
    \[Fe : O = {2 \over 3}x : x = 2 : 3\]

    Hence, the formula is Fe2O3.

    A cubic solid is made of two elements P and Q. Atoms of Q are at the corners of the cube and P at the body-centre. What is the formula of the compound? What are the coordination numbers of P and Q?

    \[Q\ atoms\ per\ unit\ cell = 8 \times {1 \over 8} = 1\]
    \[P\ atoms\ per\ unit\ cell = 1\]
    • So the formula is PQ.
    • The P atom at the body-centre is surrounded by 8 Q atoms at the corners, and each Q atom is surrounded by 8 P atoms of the neighbouring unit cells. So the coordination number of both P and Q is 8.

    If the radius of the octahedral void is r and radius of the atoms in close-packing is R, derive the relation between r and R.

    • An octahedral void is surrounded by six spheres. Consider the four spheres lying in one plane around the void; their centres form a square of side 2R.
    • The void sphere touches two spheres placed diagonally across this square, so the diagonal of the square = 2R + 2r.
    \[Diagonal\ of\ square = \sqrt{2} \times 2R\]
    \[2R + 2r = 2\sqrt{2}R\]
    \[r = (\sqrt{2} - 1)R\]
    \[r = 0.414\ R\]

    Which of the following lattices has the highest packing efficiency: (i) simple cubic (ii) body-centred cubic (iii) hexagonal close-packed lattice?

    Packing efficiencies are: simple cubic = 52.4%, bcc = 68%, hcp = 74%. Hence, hexagonal close-packed lattice has the highest packing efficiency.

    Numerical Questions on Unit Cell#

    Silver crystallises in fcc lattice. If edge length of the cell is 4.07 x 10-8 cm and density is 10.5 g cm-3, calculate the atomic mass of silver.

    • For fcc, z = 4.
    \[M = {d a^3 N_A \over z}\]
    \[M = {10.5 \times (4.07 \times 10^{-8})^3 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} \over 4}\]
    \[M = {10.5 \times 6.742 \times 10^{-23} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} \over 4}\]
    \[M = 106.6\ g\ mol^{-1}\]

    Hence, the atomic mass of silver is about 106.6 u.

    Niobium crystallises in body-centred cubic structure. If density is 8.55 g cm-3, calculate the atomic radius of niobium using its atomic mass 93 u.

    • For bcc, z = 2.
    \[a^3 = {zM \over d N_A} = {2 \times 93 \over 8.55 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23}}\]
    \[a^3 = 3.612 \times 10^{-23}\ cm^3 = 36.12 \times 10^{-24}\ cm^3\]
    \[a = 3.306 \times 10^{-8}\ cm = 330.6\ pm\]
    • For bcc:
    \[r = {\sqrt{3} \over 4}a = 0.433 \times 330.6\]
    \[r = 143.1\ pm\]

    Copper crystallises into an fcc lattice with edge length 3.61 x 10-8 cm. Show that the calculated density is in agreement with its measured value of 8.92 g cm-3. (Atomic mass of Cu = 63.5 u)

    • For fcc, z = 4.
    \[d = {zM \over a^3 N_A} = {4 \times 63.5 \over (3.61 \times 10^{-8})^3 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23}}\]
    \[d = {254 \over 4.705 \times 10^{-23} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23}} = {254 \over 28.33}\]
    \[d = 8.97\ g\ cm^{-3}\]

    The calculated value (8.97 g cm-3) is in close agreement with the measured value (8.92 g cm-3).

    Gold (atomic radius = 0.144 nm) crystallises in a face-centred unit cell. What is the length of a side of the cell?

    • For fcc:
    \[a = 2\sqrt{2}r = 2 \times 1.414 \times 0.144\]
    \[a = 0.407\ nm = 407\ pm\]

    Aluminium crystallises in a cubic close-packed structure. Its metallic radius is 125 pm.
    (i) What is the length of the side of the unit cell?
    (ii) How many unit cells are there in 1.00 cm3 of aluminium?

    • (i) For ccp (fcc):
    \[a = 2\sqrt{2}r = 2 \times 1.414 \times 125 = 353.6\ pm\]
    • (ii) Volume of one unit cell:
    \[a^3 = (353.6 \times 10^{-10}\ cm)^3 = 4.42 \times 10^{-23}\ cm^3\]
    \[Number\ of\ unit\ cells = {1.00 \over 4.42 \times 10^{-23}} = 2.26 \times 10^{22}\]

    Numerical Questions on Defects#

    Analysis shows that nickel oxide has the formula Ni0.98O1.00. What fractions of nickel exist as Ni2+ and Ni3+ ions?

    • Let the number of Ni2+ ions = x. Then the number of Ni3+ ions = 0.98 - x.
    • Total positive charge must balance the charge of one O2- ion:
    \[2x + 3(0.98 - x) = 2\]
    \[2.94 - x = 2\]
    \[x = 0.94\]
    \[Fraction\ of\ Ni^{2+} = {0.94 \over 0.98} \times 100 = 96\ \%\]
    \[Fraction\ of\ Ni^{3+} = {0.04 \over 0.98} \times 100 = 4\ \%\]

    If NaCl is doped with 10-3 mol % of SrCl2, what is the concentration of cation vacancies?

    • 10-3 mol % means 100 mol of NaCl is doped with 10-3 mol of SrCl2.
    \[SrCl_2\ per\ mol\ of\ NaCl = {10^{-3} \over 100} = 10^{-5}\ mol\]
    • Each Sr2+ ion creates one cation vacancy.
    \[Cation\ vacancies = 10^{-5} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23}\]
    \[Cation\ vacancies = 6.022 \times 10^{18}\ per\ mol\ of\ NaCl\]
