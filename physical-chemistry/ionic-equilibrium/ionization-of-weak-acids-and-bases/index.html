    Ionic Equilibrium | Ionization of Weak Acids and Bases#

    The equilibrium constant for the ionization of a weak acid is called its ionization constant, Ka, and for a weak base is called the base ionization constant, Kb. These constants tell us how strong a weak acid or base is.

    Ionization Constant of Weak Acids (Ka)#

    • Consider a weak acid HX that is partially ionized in water. Let c be its initial concentration and α the degree of ionization (the fraction of HX ionized).
    \[HX_{(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; H_3O^+_{(aq)} + X^-_{(aq)}\]
    \[Initial\ concentration\ (M):\ \ c\ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ \ \ 0\]
    \[Change\ (M):\ \ -c&alpha;\ \ \ \ \ \ +c&alpha;\ \ \ \ \ \ +c&alpha;\]
    \[Equilibrium\ concentration\ (M):\ \ c - c&alpha;\ \ \ \ \ \ c&alpha;\ \ \ \ \ \ c&alpha;\]
    • The equilibrium constant for this acid dissociation is:
    \[K_a = {[H^+][X^-] \over [HX]}\]
    \[K_a = {c^2&alpha;^2 \over c(1-&alpha;)} = {c&alpha;^2 \over 1-&alpha;}\]
    • Ka is called the dissociation or ionization constant of the acid HX.
    • At a given temperature, Ka is a measure of the strength of the acid. The larger the value of Ka, the stronger the acid.
    • Ka is a dimensionless quantity, since the standard state concentration of all species is taken as 1 M.
    • The pH idea is extended to Ka as well:
    \[pK_a = -log\ K_a\]
    • A larger Ka means a smaller pKa. So, the smaller the pKa, the stronger the acid.

    Ostwald's dilution law (approximation)#

    • For weak acids, α is usually very small, so 1 - α ≈ 1. Then:
    \[K_a = c&alpha;^2\ \ \ \ or\ \ \ \ &alpha; = \sqrt{K_a \over c}\]
    \[[H^+] = c&alpha; = \sqrt{K_a\ c}\]
    \[pH = {1 \over 2}(pK_a - log\ c)\]
    • So the degree of ionization of a weak electrolyte increases on dilution (as c decreases).
    • This approximation works well when α is less than about 5%. If α comes out larger, solve the full quadratic equation.

    Ionization constants of some weak acids (298 K)#

    Acid Ka
    Hydrofluoric acid (HF) 3.5 × 10-4
    Nitrous acid (HNO2) 4.5 × 10-4
    Formic acid (HCOOH) 1.8 × 10-4
    Niacin (C5H4NCOOH) 1.5 × 10-5
    Acetic acid (CH3COOH) 1.74 × 10-5
    Benzoic acid (C6H5COOH) 6.5 × 10-5
    Hypochlorous acid (HClO) 3.0 × 10-8
    Hydrocyanic acid (HCN) 4.9 × 10-10
    Phenol (C6H5OH) 1.3 × 10-10

    Step-wise approach to find the pH of a weak electrolyte#

    Knowing Ka and the initial concentration c, we can find the concentration of all species, the degree of ionization and the pH:

    • Step 1: Identify the species present before dissociation as Brönsted-Lowry acids/bases.
    • Step 2: Write balanced equations for all possible reactions, including species acting both as acid and base (such as water).
    • Step 3: The reaction with the higher Ka is the primary reaction; the other is a subsidiary reaction.
    • Step 4: For the primary reaction, tabulate (a) initial concentration, (b) change on reaching equilibrium in terms of α, and (c) equilibrium concentration.
    • Step 5: Substitute the equilibrium concentrations in the Ka expression and solve for α.
    • Step 6: Calculate the concentration of each species.
    • Step 7: Calculate pH = -log[H3O+].

    Percent dissociation#

    • Another useful measure of the strength of a weak acid is percent dissociation:
    \[Percent\ dissociation = {[HA]_{dissociated} \over [HA]_{initial}} \times 100\%\]

    Ionization Constant of Weak Bases (Kb)#

    • A base MOH ionizes as:
    \[MOH_{(aq)} &#8652; M^+_{(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)}\]
    \[K_b = {[M^+][OH^-] \over [MOH]}\]
    • A weak base ionizes only partially, just like a weak acid. Kb is called the base ionization constant.
    • If c is the initial concentration and α the degree of ionization:
    \[K_b = {(c&alpha;)^2 \over c(1-&alpha;)} = {c&alpha;^2 \over 1-&alpha;}\]
    \[If\ &alpha; \ll 1:\ \ &alpha; = \sqrt{K_b \over c},\ \ [OH^-] = \sqrt{K_b\ c}\]
    \[pK_b = -log\ K_b\]
    • Many organic compounds like amines (derivatives of ammonia in which H atoms are replaced by other groups) are weak bases. Methylamine, codeine, quinine and nicotine are very weak bases due to their very small Kb.
    \[NH_{3(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; NH^+_{4(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)}\]

    Ionization constants of some weak bases (298 K)#

    Base Kb
    Dimethylamine, (CH3)2NH 5.4 × 10-4
    Triethylamine, (C2H5)3N 6.45 × 10-5
    Ammonia, NH3 or NH4OH 1.77 × 10-5
    Quinine (a plant product) 1.10 × 10-6
    Pyridine, C5H5N 1.77 × 10-9
    Aniline, C6H5NH2 4.27 × 10-10
    Urea, CO(NH2)2 1.3 × 10-14

    Relation between Ka and Kb#

    • For a conjugate acid-base pair, Ka and Kb are related, so if one is known the other can be found. Consider NH4+ and NH3:
    \[NH^+_{4(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; H_3O^+_{(aq)} + NH_{3(aq)},\ \ K_a = {[H_3O^+][NH_3] \over [NH_4^+]} = 5.6 \times 10^{-10}\]
    \[NH_{3(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; NH^+_{4(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)},\ \ K_b = {[NH_4^+][OH^-] \over [NH_3]} = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}\]
    \[Net:\ 2H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; H_3O^+_{(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)},\ \ K_w = [H_3O^+][OH^-] = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}\]
    • The net reaction is the sum of the two reactions, so its equilibrium constant is the product of the two constants:
    \[K_a \times K_b = {[H_3O^+][NH_3] \over [NH_4^+]} \times {[NH_4^+][OH^-] \over [NH_3]} = [H_3O^+][OH^-] = K_w\]
    \[(5.6 \times 10^{-10}) \times (1.8 \times 10^{-5}) = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}\]

    Conclusion

    • The equilibrium constant for a net reaction obtained by adding two (or more) reactions equals the product of the equilibrium constants of the individual reactions: KNET = K1 × K2 × ...

    • For a conjugate acid-base pair:

    \[K_a \times K_b = K_w\]
    \[pK_a + pK_b = pK_w = 14\ (at\ 298\ K)\]
    • A strong acid has a weak conjugate base and vice-versa.

    Alternative derivation#

    • Consider a general base B:
    \[B_{(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; BH^+_{(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)},\ \ K_b = {[BH^+][OH^-] \over [B]}\]

    Multiplying and dividing by [H+]:

    \[K_b = {[BH^+][OH^-][H^+] \over [B][H^+]} = [OH^-][H^+] \times {[BH^+] \over [B][H^+]} = {K_w \over K_a}\]
    \[&Therefore;\ K_a \times K_b = K_w\]

    Questions#

    The ionization constant of HF is 3.2 × 10-4. Calculate the degree of dissociation of HF in its 0.02 M solution. Calculate the concentration of all species present (H3O+, F- and HF) in the solution and its pH.

    Possible proton transfer reactions:

    \[(1)\ HF + H_2O &#8652; H_3O^+ + F^-,\ \ K_a = 3.2 \times 10^{-4}\]
    \[(2)\ H_2O + H_2O &#8652; H_3O^+ + OH^-,\ \ K_w = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}\]

    As Ka >> Kw, reaction (1) is the principal reaction.

    \[Initial\ (M):\ HF = 0.02,\ H_3O^+ = 0,\ F^- = 0\]
    \[Equilibrium\ (M):\ HF = 0.02 - 0.02&alpha;,\ H_3O^+ = 0.02&alpha;,\ F^- = 0.02&alpha;\]
    \[K_a = {(0.02&alpha;)^2 \over 0.02(1-&alpha;)} = {0.02&alpha;^2 \over 1-&alpha;} = 3.2 \times 10^{-4}\]
    \[&alpha;^2 + 1.6 \times 10^{-2}&alpha; - 1.6 \times 10^{-2} = 0\]

    Solving, α = +0.12 or -0.12. The negative root is not acceptable, so α = 0.12.

    \[[H_3O^+] = [F^-] = c&alpha; = 0.02 \times 0.12 = 2.4 \times 10^{-3}\ M\]
    \[[HF] = c(1-&alpha;) = 0.02(1 - 0.12) = 17.6 \times 10^{-3}\ M\]
    \[pH = -log(2.4 \times 10^{-3}) = 2.62\]

    The pH of 0.1 M monobasic acid is 4.50. Calculate the concentration of species H+, A- and HA at equilibrium. Also, determine the value of Ka and pKa of the monobasic acid.

    \[[H^+] = 10^{-pH} = 10^{-4.50} = 3.16 \times 10^{-5}\ M\]
    \[[H^+] = [A^-] = 3.16 \times 10^{-5}\ M\]
    \[[HA]_{eqlbm} = 0.1 - (3.16 \times 10^{-5}) &asymp; 0.1\ M\]
    \[K_a = {[H^+][A^-] \over [HA]} = {(3.16 \times 10^{-5})^2 \over 0.1} = 1.0 \times 10^{-8}\]
    \[pK_a = -log(10^{-8}) = 8\]

    Calculate the pH of 0.08 M solution of hypochlorous acid, HOCl. The ionization constant of the acid is 2.5 × 10-5. Determine the percent dissociation of HOCl.

    \[HOCl_{(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; H_3O^+_{(aq)} + ClO^-_{(aq)}\]
    \[Equilibrium\ (M):\ HOCl = 0.08 - x,\ H_3O^+ = x,\ ClO^- = x\]
    \[K_a = {x^2 \over 0.08 - x} = 2.5 \times 10^{-5}\]

    As x << 0.08, 0.08 - x ≈ 0.08:

    \[x^2 = 0.08 \times 2.5 \times 10^{-5} = 2.0 \times 10^{-6}\]
    \[x = [H^+] = 1.41 \times 10^{-3}\ M\]
    \[Percent\ dissociation = {1.41 \times 10^{-3} \over 0.08} \times 100 = 1.76\%\]
    \[pH = -log(1.41 \times 10^{-3}) = 2.85\]

    The pH of 0.004 M hydrazine solution is 9.7. Calculate its ionization constant Kb and pKb.

    \[NH_2NH_2 + H_2O &#8652; NH_2NH_3^+ + OH^-\]

    From pH, find [H+], then [OH-] using Kw:

    \[[H^+] = 10^{-9.7} = 2.0 \times 10^{-10}\ M\]
    \[[OH^-] = {K_w \over [H^+]} = {1 \times 10^{-14} \over 2.0 \times 10^{-10}} = 5.0 \times 10^{-5}\ M\]

    [NH2NH3+] = [OH-]. Both are very small, so the concentration of undissociated base ≈ 0.004 M.

    \[K_b = {[NH_2NH_3^+][OH^-] \over [NH_2NH_2]} = {(5.0 \times 10^{-5})^2 \over 0.004} = 6.3 \times 10^{-7}\]
    \[pK_b = -log(6.3 \times 10^{-7}) = 6.20\]

    Determine the degree of ionization and pH of a 0.05 M ammonia solution (Kb = 1.77 × 10-5). Also, calculate the ionization constant of the conjugate acid of ammonia.

    \[NH_3 + H_2O &#8652; NH_4^+ + OH^-\]
    \[K_b = {0.05&alpha;^2 \over 1-&alpha;}\]

    α is small, so 1 - α ≈ 1:

    \[&alpha; = \sqrt{1.77 \times 10^{-5} \over 0.05} = 0.0188\]
    \[[OH^-] = c&alpha; = 0.05 \times 0.0188 = 9.4 \times 10^{-4}\ M\]
    \[[H^+] = {10^{-14} \over 9.4 \times 10^{-4}} = 1.06 \times 10^{-11}\ M\]
    \[pH = -log(1.06 \times 10^{-11}) = 10.97\]

    For the conjugate acid NH4+:

    \[K_a = {K_w \over K_b} = {10^{-14} \over 1.77 \times 10^{-5}} = 5.64 \times 10^{-10}\]

    The ionization constant of HF, HCOOH and HCN at 298 K are 6.8 × 10-4, 1.8 × 10-4 and 4.8 × 10-9 respectively. Calculate the ionization constants of the corresponding conjugate base.

    Using Kb = Kw/Ka:

    \[F^-:\ K_b = {10^{-14} \over 6.8 \times 10^{-4}} = 1.47 \times 10^{-11}\]
    \[HCOO^-:\ K_b = {10^{-14} \over 1.8 \times 10^{-4}} = 5.56 \times 10^{-11}\]
    \[CN^-:\ K_b = {10^{-14} \over 4.8 \times 10^{-9}} = 2.08 \times 10^{-6}\]

    The ionization constant of acetic acid is 1.74 × 10-5. Calculate the degree of dissociation of acetic acid in its 0.05 M solution. Calculate the concentration of acetate ion in the solution and its pH.

    \[&alpha; = \sqrt{K_a \over c} = \sqrt{1.74 \times 10^{-5} \over 0.05} = 1.87 \times 10^{-2}\]
    \[[CH_3COO^-] = [H^+] = c&alpha; = 0.05 \times 1.87 \times 10^{-2} = 9.33 \times 10^{-4}\ M\]
    \[pH = -log(9.33 \times 10^{-4}) = 3.03\]

    It has been found that the pH of a 0.01 M solution of an organic acid is 4.15. Calculate the concentration of the anion, the ionization constant of the acid and its pKa.

    \[[H^+] = [A^-] = 10^{-4.15} = 7.08 \times 10^{-5}\ M\]
    \[K_a = {[H^+][A^-] \over [HA]} = {(7.08 \times 10^{-5})^2 \over 0.01 - 7.08 \times 10^{-5}} = 5.05 \times 10^{-7}\]
    \[pK_a = -log(5.05 \times 10^{-7}) = 6.30\]

    The degree of ionization of a 0.1 M bromoacetic acid solution is 0.132. Calculate the pH of the solution and the pKa of bromoacetic acid.

    \[[H^+] = c&alpha; = 0.1 \times 0.132 = 0.0132\ M\]
    \[pH = -log(0.0132) = 1.88\]

    Here α is not small, so use the full expression:

    \[K_a = {c&alpha;^2 \over 1-&alpha;} = {0.1 \times (0.132)^2 \over 1 - 0.132} = 2.0 \times 10^{-3}\]
    \[pK_a = -log(2.0 \times 10^{-3}) = 2.70\]

    The pH of 0.005 M codeine (C18H21NO3) solution is 9.95. Calculate its ionization constant and pKb.

    \[pOH = 14 - 9.95 = 4.05\]
    \[[OH^-] = 10^{-4.05} = 8.91 \times 10^{-5}\ M\]
    \[K_b = {[OH^-]^2 \over c} = {(8.91 \times 10^{-5})^2 \over 0.005} = 1.59 \times 10^{-6}\]
    \[pK_b = -log(1.59 \times 10^{-6}) = 5.80\]

    What is the pH of 0.001 M aniline solution? The ionization constant of aniline is 4.27 × 10-10. Calculate the degree of ionization of aniline in the solution. Also calculate the ionization constant of the conjugate acid of aniline.

    \[[OH^-] = \sqrt{K_b\ c} = \sqrt{4.27 \times 10^{-10} \times 0.001} = 6.53 \times 10^{-7}\ M\]
    \[&alpha; = {[OH^-] \over c} = {6.53 \times 10^{-7} \over 0.001} = 6.53 \times 10^{-4}\]
    \[pOH = 6.19,\ \ pH = 14 - 6.19 = 7.81\]

    For the conjugate acid (anilinium ion, C6H5NH3+):

    \[K_a = {K_w \over K_b} = {10^{-14} \over 4.27 \times 10^{-10}} = 2.34 \times 10^{-5}\]

    The pH of 0.1 M solution of cyanic acid (HCNO) is 2.34. Calculate the ionization constant of the acid and its degree of ionization in the solution.

    \[[H^+] = 10^{-2.34} = 4.57 \times 10^{-3}\ M\]
    \[K_a = {[H^+]^2 \over c} = {(4.57 \times 10^{-3})^2 \over 0.1} = 2.09 \times 10^{-4}\]
    \[&alpha; = {[H^+] \over c} = {4.57 \times 10^{-3} \over 0.1} = 0.0457\]
