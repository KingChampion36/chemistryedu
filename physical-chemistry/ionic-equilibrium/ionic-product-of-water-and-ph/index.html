    Ionic Equilibrium | Ionic Product of Water and pH#

    Strong and Weak Acids and Bases#

    • Acids like perchloric acid (HClO4), hydrochloric acid (HCl), hydrobromic acid (HBr), hydroiodic acid (HI), nitric acid (HNO3) and sulphuric acid (H2SO4) are strong acids, as they are almost completely dissociated in water and act as good proton donors.
    • Bases like LiOH, NaOH, KOH, CsOH and Ba(OH)2 are strong bases, as they are almost completely dissociated in water giving OH- ions.
    • In the Brönsted-Lowry sense, a strong acid is a good proton donor and a strong base is a good proton acceptor.
    \[HA_{(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; H_3O^+_{(aq)} + A^-_{(aq)}\]
    \[(Acid)\ \ \ \ (Base)\ \ \ \ (Conjugate\ acid)\ \ \ \ (Conjugate\ base)\]
    • In the above equilibrium, HA and H3O+ compete to donate a proton. If HA is a stronger acid than H3O+, HA donates its proton and the solution mainly contains A- and H3O+.
    • The equilibrium always moves in the direction of formation of the weaker acid and weaker base, because the stronger acid donates a proton to the stronger base.

    Conclusion

    • Strong acids have very weak conjugate bases. Example: ClO4-, Cl-, Br-, I-, NO3- and HSO4- are much weaker bases than H2O.

    • Weak acids have very strong conjugate bases. Example: NH2-, O2- and H- are very good proton acceptors and much stronger bases than H2O.

    • Weak acids like nitrous acid (HNO2), hydrofluoric acid (HF) and acetic acid (CH3COOH) are only partially dissociated. Their solutions mainly contain undissociated HA molecules.

    Acid-base indicators#

    • Some water-soluble organic compounds like phenolphthalein and bromothymol blue behave as weak acids. They show different colours in their acid form (HIn) and conjugate base form (In-).
    \[HIn_{(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; H_3O^+_{(aq)} + In^-_{(aq)}\]
    \[(Colour\ A)\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ (Colour\ B)\]
    • Such compounds are used as indicators in acid-base titrations and for finding H+ ion concentration.

    Ionic Product of Water (Kw)#

    • Water can act both as an acid and a base. In pure water, one H2O molecule donates a proton (acid) and another accepts it (base). This is called self-ionization or auto-protolysis of water.
    \[H_2O_{(l)} + H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; H_3O^+_{(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)}\]
    \[K = {[H_3O^+][OH^-] \over [H_2O]}\]
    • Water is a pure liquid, so its concentration remains constant and is included in the equilibrium constant. This gives a new constant Kw, called the ionic product of water.
    \[K_w = [H^+][OH^-]\]
    • At 298 K, [H+] in pure water is found to be 1.0 × 10-7 M. Since water gives equal numbers of H+ and OH- ions, [OH-] = [H+] = 1.0 × 10-7 M.
    \[K_w = (1.0 \times 10^{-7})^2 = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}\ M^2\ (at\ 298\ K)\]
    • Kw is an equilibrium constant, so it depends on temperature. Since ionization of water is endothermic, Kw increases with temperature.

    How much water is ionized?#

    \[[H_2O] = {1000\ g/L \over 18.0\ g/mol} = 55.55\ M\]
    \[Ratio\ of\ dissociated\ to\ undissociated\ water = {10^{-7} \over 55.55} = 1.8 \times 10^{-9}\]
    • So only about 2 in 109 water molecules are ionized, i.e., the equilibrium lies mainly towards undissociated water.

    Acidic, neutral and basic solutions#

    Solution Condition pH at 298 K
    Acidic [H3O+] > [OH-] pH < 7
    Neutral [H3O+] = [OH-] pH = 7
    Basic [H3O+] < [OH-] pH > 7

    The pH Scale#

    • The pH of a solution is defined as the negative logarithm (to base 10) of the activity of hydrogen ions, aH+.
    • In dilute solutions (< 0.01 M), activity of H+ is equal in magnitude to its molarity [H+]. Activity has no units.
    \[a_{H^+} = {[H^+] \over mol\ L^{-1}}\]
    \[pH = -log\ a_{H^+} = -log[H^+]\]
    \[[H^+] = 10^{-pH}\]

    Examples

    • An HCl solution with [H+] = 10-2 M has pH = 2.

    • A NaOH solution with [OH-] = 10-4 M has [H+] = 10-10 M, so pH = 10.

    • Pure water at 25°C has [H+] = 10-7 M, so pH = -log(10-7) = 7.

    Relation between pH and pOH#

    • Similarly, pOH = -log[OH-] and pKw = -log Kw. Taking negative log of Kw at 298 K:
    \[K_w = [H_3O^+][OH^-] = 10^{-14}\]
    \[-log\ K_w = -log[H_3O^+] - log[OH^-] = -log\ 10^{-14}\]
    \[pK_w = pH + pOH = 14\]
    • Although Kw changes with temperature, the change in pH is small and is often ignored.
    • The pH scale is logarithmic. A change of 1 unit in pH means [H+] changes by a factor of 10. A change of 2 units means a factor of 100.

    Measurement of pH#

    • pH can be found roughly using pH paper, which shows different colours at different pH. pH paper with four strips can measure pH in the range 1–14 with an accuracy of about 0.5.
    • For greater accuracy, a pH meter is used. It measures the pH-dependent electrical potential of the solution to within 0.001.

    pH of Strong Acids and Bases#

    • A strong acid or base is completely dissociated, so [H+] or [OH-] follows directly from its concentration and the number of H+/OH- it releases.
    \[For\ strong\ acid:\ [H^+] = n \times C\]
    \[For\ strong\ base:\ [OH^-] = n \times C,\ \ pH = 14 - pOH\]

    Where, n = number of H+ or OH- per formula unit and C = molar concentration.

    • For very dilute solutions (around 10-6 M or less), the H+ from ionization of water cannot be ignored (see the question on 10-8 M HCl below).

    Questions#

    The concentration of hydrogen ion in a sample of soft drink is 3.8 × 10-3 M. What is its pH?

    \[pH = -log(3.8 \times 10^{-3})\]
    \[pH = -\{log(3.8) + log(10^{-3})\} = -\{0.58 - 3.0\} = 2.42\]

    Therefore, the pH of the soft drink is 2.42 and it is acidic.

    Calculate pH of a 1.0 × 10-8 M solution of HCl.

    • Taking pH = -log(10-8) = 8 would be wrong, because an acid solution cannot be basic. In such a dilute solution, H3O+ from water must also be considered.
    \[2H_2O_{(l)} &#8652; H_3O^+_{(aq)} + OH^-_{(aq)},\ \ K_w = 10^{-14}\]

    Let x = [OH-] = [H3O+] from water. H3O+ comes from both HCl and water:

    \[[H_3O^+] = 10^{-8} + x\]
    \[K_w = (10^{-8} + x)(x) = 10^{-14}\]
    \[x^2 + 10^{-8}x - 10^{-14} = 0\]
    \[[OH^-] = x = 9.5 \times 10^{-8}\ M\]
    \[pOH = 7.02,\ \ pH = 14 - 7.02 = 6.98\]

    The pH of a sample of vinegar is 3.76. Calculate the concentration of hydrogen ion in it.

    \[[H^+] = 10^{-pH} = 10^{-3.76}\]
    \[[H^+] = 1.74 \times 10^{-4}\ M\]

    Assuming complete dissociation, calculate the pH of the following solutions:
    (a) 0.003 M HCl (b) 0.005 M NaOH (c) 0.002 M HBr (d) 0.002 M KOH

    \[(a)\ [H^+] = 0.003\ M,\ pH = -log(3 \times 10^{-3}) = 2.52\]
    \[(b)\ [OH^-] = 0.005\ M,\ pOH = 2.30,\ pH = 14 - 2.30 = 11.70\]
    \[(c)\ [H^+] = 0.002\ M,\ pH = -log(2 \times 10^{-3}) = 2.70\]
    \[(d)\ [OH^-] = 0.002\ M,\ pOH = 2.70,\ pH = 14 - 2.70 = 11.30\]

    Calculate the pH of the following solutions:
    (a) 2 g of TlOH dissolved in water to give 2 litre of solution.
    (b) 0.3 g of Ca(OH)2 dissolved in water to give 500 mL of solution.
    (c) 0.3 g of NaOH dissolved in water to give 200 mL of solution.
    (d) 1 mL of 13.6 M HCl is diluted with water to give 1 litre of solution.

    (a) Molar mass of TlOH = 204.4 + 17 = 221.4 g/mol

    \[[TlOH] = {2 \over 221.4 \times 2} = 4.52 \times 10^{-3}\ M = [OH^-]\]
    \[pOH = 2.35,\ \ pH = 14 - 2.35 = 11.65\]

    (b) Molar mass of Ca(OH)2 = 74 g/mol. Each Ca(OH)2 gives 2 OH-.

    \[[Ca(OH)_2] = {0.3 \over 74 \times 0.5} = 8.11 \times 10^{-3}\ M\]
    \[[OH^-] = 2 \times 8.11 \times 10^{-3} = 1.62 \times 10^{-2}\ M\]
    \[pOH = 1.79,\ \ pH = 12.21\]

    (c) Molar mass of NaOH = 40 g/mol

    \[[OH^-] = {0.3 \over 40 \times 0.2} = 0.0375\ M\]
    \[pOH = 1.43,\ \ pH = 12.57\]

    (d) On dilution, M1V1 = M2V2

    \[13.6 \times 1 = M_2 \times 1000,\ \ M_2 = 0.0136\ M = [H^+]\]
    \[pH = -log(0.0136) = 1.87\]

    Calculate the hydrogen ion concentration in the following biological fluids whose pH are given below:
    (a) Human muscle-fluid, 6.83 (b) Human stomach fluid, 1.2 (c) Human blood, 7.38 (d) Human saliva, 6.4

    Using [H+] = 10-pH:

    \[(a)\ [H^+] = 10^{-6.83} = 1.48 \times 10^{-7}\ M\]
    \[(b)\ [H^+] = 10^{-1.2} = 6.3 \times 10^{-2}\ M\]
    \[(c)\ [H^+] = 10^{-7.38} = 4.17 \times 10^{-8}\ M\]
    \[(d)\ [H^+] = 10^{-6.4} = 3.98 \times 10^{-7}\ M\]

    The pH of milk, black coffee, tomato juice, lemon juice and egg white are 6.8, 5.0, 4.2, 2.2 and 7.8 respectively. Calculate the corresponding hydrogen ion concentration in each.

    \[Milk:\ [H^+] = 10^{-6.8} = 1.58 \times 10^{-7}\ M\]
    \[Black\ coffee:\ [H^+] = 10^{-5.0} = 1.0 \times 10^{-5}\ M\]
    \[Tomato\ juice:\ [H^+] = 10^{-4.2} = 6.31 \times 10^{-5}\ M\]
    \[Lemon\ juice:\ [H^+] = 10^{-2.2} = 6.31 \times 10^{-3}\ M\]
    \[Egg\ white:\ [H^+] = 10^{-7.8} = 1.58 \times 10^{-8}\ M\]

    If 0.561 g of KOH is dissolved in water to give 200 mL of solution at 298 K, calculate the concentrations of potassium, hydrogen and hydroxyl ions. What is its pH?

    \[Moles\ of\ KOH = {0.561 \over 56} = 0.01\ mol\]
    \[[KOH] = {0.01 \over 0.2} = 0.05\ M\]
    \[[K^+] = [OH^-] = 0.05\ M\]
    \[[H^+] = {K_w \over [OH^-]} = {10^{-14} \over 0.05} = 2.0 \times 10^{-13}\ M\]
    \[pH = -log(2.0 \times 10^{-13}) = 12.70\]

    The solubility of Sr(OH)2 at 298 K is 19.23 g/L of solution. Calculate the concentrations of strontium and hydroxyl ions and the pH of the solution.

    Molar mass of Sr(OH)2 = 87.6 + 34 = 121.6 g/mol

    \[[Sr(OH)_2] = {19.23 \over 121.6} = 0.1581\ M\]
    \[Sr(OH)_2 &rarr; Sr^{2+} + 2OH^-\]
    \[[Sr^{2+}] = 0.1581\ M,\ \ [OH^-] = 2 \times 0.1581 = 0.3162\ M\]
    \[pOH = -log(0.3162) = 0.50,\ \ pH = 14 - 0.50 = 13.50\]

    Ionic product of water at 310 K is 2.7 × 10-14. What is the pH of neutral water at this temperature?

    For neutral water, [H+] = [OH-].

    \[[H^+]^2 = K_w = 2.7 \times 10^{-14}\]
    \[[H^+] = 1.64 \times 10^{-7}\ M\]
    \[pH = -log(1.64 \times 10^{-7}) = 6.78\]

    Note that the water is still neutral even though pH < 7, because [H+] = [OH-].

    Calculate the pH of the resultant mixtures:
    (a) 10 mL of 0.2 M Ca(OH)2 + 25 mL of 0.1 M HCl
    (b) 10 mL of 0.01 M H2SO4 + 10 mL of 0.01 M Ca(OH)2
    (c) 10 mL of 0.1 M H2SO4 + 10 mL of 0.1 M KOH

    Find the millimoles of H+ and OH- (millimoles = M × V in mL), neutralize, and divide the excess by total volume.

    (a) Ca(OH)2 gives 2 OH-:

    \[mmol\ OH^- = 2 \times 0.2 \times 10 = 4,\ \ mmol\ H^+ = 0.1 \times 25 = 2.5\]
    \[Excess\ OH^- = 1.5\ mmol\ in\ 35\ mL,\ \ [OH^-] = {1.5 \over 35} = 0.0429\ M\]
    \[pOH = 1.37,\ \ pH = 12.63\]

    (b) H2SO4 gives 2 H+:

    \[mmol\ H^+ = 2 \times 0.01 \times 10 = 0.2,\ \ mmol\ OH^- = 2 \times 0.01 \times 10 = 0.2\]

    Complete neutralization, so pH = 7.00.

    (c)

    \[mmol\ H^+ = 2 \times 0.1 \times 10 = 2,\ \ mmol\ OH^- = 0.1 \times 10 = 1\]
    \[Excess\ H^+ = 1\ mmol\ in\ 20\ mL,\ \ [H^+] = 0.05\ M\]
    \[pH = -log(0.05) = 1.30\]
