    Buffer Solutions#

    The solutions which resist change in pH on dilution or with the addition of small amounts of acid or alkali are called buffer solutions.

    • Many body fluids like blood and urine have a definite pH, and any deviation indicates malfunctioning of the body. Control of pH is also important in many chemical and biochemical processes, and in medical and cosmetic formulations.
    • A buffer of known pH can be prepared from the pKa of an acid (or pKb of a base) by controlling the ratio of salt and acid (or salt and base).

    Types of Buffer Solutions#

    1. Acidic buffer#

    • A mixture of a weak acid and its salt with a strong base.
    • Example: Acetic acid and sodium acetate (CH3COOH + CH3COONa) acts as a buffer around pH 4.75.

    2. Basic buffer#

    • A mixture of a weak base and its salt with a strong acid.
    • Example: Ammonium hydroxide and ammonium chloride (NH4OH + NH4Cl) acts as a buffer around pH 9.25.

    Buffer Action#

    • A buffer contains a large reserve of both an acid (to consume added OH-) and a base (to consume added H+). The common ion effect keeps the weak electrolyte almost unionized.

    Acidic buffer: CH3COOH + CH3COONa

    \[CH_3COONa &rarr; CH_3COO^- + Na^+\ (complete)\]
    \[CH_3COOH &#8652; CH_3COO^- + H^+\ (suppressed\ by\ common\ ion)\]
    • On adding a small amount of acid, the added H+ combine with the large reserve of acetate ions:
    \[CH_3COO^- + H^+ &rarr; CH_3COOH\]
    • On adding a small amount of base, the added OH- are neutralized by acetic acid:
    \[CH_3COOH + OH^- &rarr; CH_3COO^- + H_2O\]

    Basic buffer: NH4OH + NH4Cl

    • Added H+ are consumed by NH4OH:
    \[NH_4OH + H^+ &rarr; NH_4^+ + H_2O\]
    • Added OH- are consumed by NH4+:
    \[NH_4^+ + OH^- &rarr; NH_4OH\]
    • In each case, only the ratio [salt]/[acid] changes slightly, so the pH changes very little.

    Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation#

    1. For an acidic buffer#

    • For a weak acid HA in presence of its salt:
    \[HA &#8652; H^+ + A^-,\ \ K_a = {[H^+][A^-] \over [HA]}\]
    \[[H^+] = K_a {[HA] \over [A^-]}\]

    Taking negative log on both sides:

    \[-log[H^+] = -log\ K_a - log{[HA] \over [A^-]}\]
    \[pH = pK_a + log{[A^-] \over [HA]}\]
    • Since the acid is weakly ionized (and further suppressed by the common ion), [HA] ≈ initial concentration of acid, and [A-] ≈ concentration of salt.
    \[pH = pK_a + log{[Salt] \over [Acid]}\]

    2. For a basic buffer#

    \[BOH &#8652; B^+ + OH^-,\ \ K_b = {[B^+][OH^-] \over [BOH]}\]
    \[pOH = pK_b + log{[Salt] \over [Base]}\]
    \[pH = 14 - pOH = 14 - pK_b - log{[Salt] \over [Base]}\]

    Conclusion

    • The pH of a buffer depends on the ratio [salt]/[acid] (or [salt]/[base]), not on the actual amounts. Since dilution does not change this ratio, the pH of a buffer does not change on dilution.

    • When [salt] = [acid], pH = pKa. When [salt] = [base], pOH = pKb. The buffer resists change best (maximum buffer capacity) at this point.

    • A buffer works effectively in the range pH = pKa ± 1 (i.e., [salt]/[acid] between 1/10 and 10). So, to prepare a buffer of a desired pH, choose a weak acid whose pKa is close to that pH.

    • Since the Henderson equation uses concentrations in a ratio, moles (or millimoles) can be used directly when acid and salt are in the same solution.

    Questions#

    Calculate the pH of solution formed on mixing 0.2 M NH4Cl and 0.1 M NH3. The pOH of ammonia solution is 4.75.

    The pOH value given here is used as pKb of ammonia:

    \[K_b = antilog(-pK_b) = 10^{-4.75} = 1.77 \times 10^{-5}\]
    \[NH_3 + H_2O &#8652; NH_4^+ + OH^-\]
    \[Equilibrium\ (M):\ \ 0.10 - x\ \ \ \ \ \ 0.20 + x\ \ \ \ \ \ x\]
    \[K_b = {(0.20 + x)(x) \over 0.10 - x} = 1.77 \times 10^{-5}\]

    As Kb is small, x can be neglected compared to 0.1 M and 0.2 M:

    \[[OH^-] = x = 1.77 \times 10^{-5} \times {0.10 \over 0.20} = 0.88 \times 10^{-5}\ M\]
    \[[H^+] = {10^{-14} \over 0.88 \times 10^{-5}} = 1.12 \times 10^{-9}\ M\]
    \[pH = -log(1.12 \times 10^{-9}) = 8.95\]

    Using the Henderson equation directly:

    \[pOH = 4.75 + log{0.2 \over 0.1} = 4.75 + 0.30 = 5.05,\ \ pH = 8.95\]

    Calculate the pH of a buffer containing 0.1 M acetic acid and 0.2 M sodium acetate. (pKa of acetic acid = 4.74)

    \[pH = pK_a + log{[Salt] \over [Acid]}\]
    \[pH = 4.74 + log{0.2 \over 0.1} = 4.74 + 0.30 = 5.04\]

    In what ratio should sodium acetate and acetic acid be mixed to obtain a buffer of pH 5.74? (pKa of acetic acid = 4.74)

    \[5.74 = 4.74 + log{[Salt] \over [Acid]}\]
    \[log{[Salt] \over [Acid]} = 1\]
    \[{[Salt] \over [Acid]} = 10\]

    So sodium acetate and acetic acid must be mixed in a 10 : 1 molar ratio.

    1 litre of a buffer contains 0.1 mol acetic acid and 0.1 mol sodium acetate (pKa = 4.74). Calculate the change in pH on adding (a) 0.01 mol HCl (b) 0.01 mol NaOH. Compare with the change when 0.01 mol HCl is added to 1 litre of pure water.

    Initial pH:

    \[pH = 4.74 + log{0.1 \over 0.1} = 4.74\]

    (a) Added H+ converts acetate into acetic acid:

    \[CH_3COO^- + H^+ &rarr; CH_3COOH\]
    \[Acetate = 0.1 - 0.01 = 0.09\ mol,\ \ Acid = 0.1 + 0.01 = 0.11\ mol\]
    \[pH = 4.74 + log{0.09 \over 0.11} = 4.74 - 0.09 = 4.65\]

    (b) Added OH- converts acetic acid into acetate:

    \[Acetate = 0.11\ mol,\ \ Acid = 0.09\ mol\]
    \[pH = 4.74 + log{0.11 \over 0.09} = 4.74 + 0.09 = 4.83\]

    The pH changes by only about 0.09 unit. In pure water, 0.01 mol HCl in 1 litre gives [H+] = 0.01 M, so the pH falls from 7 to 2, a change of 5 units.

    50 mL of 0.2 M acetic acid is mixed with 50 mL of 0.1 M NaOH. Calculate the pH of the resulting solution. (pKa = 4.74)

    \[mmol\ CH_3COOH = 0.2 \times 50 = 10,\ \ mmol\ NaOH = 0.1 \times 50 = 5\]
    \[CH_3COOH + NaOH &rarr; CH_3COONa + H_2O\]
    \[After\ reaction:\ \ CH_3COOH = 5\ mmol,\ \ CH_3COONa = 5\ mmol\]

    The solution is a buffer with equal amounts of acid and salt:

    \[pH = 4.74 + log{5 \over 5} = 4.74\]

    This is why the pH at half-neutralization of a weak acid equals its pKa.

    How many grams of NH4Cl must be added to 500 mL of 0.1 M NH3 solution to obtain a buffer of pH 9.0? (pKb of NH3 = 4.75, assume no change in volume)

    \[pOH = 14 - 9.0 = 5.0\]
    \[5.0 = 4.75 + log{[NH_4Cl] \over [NH_3]}\]
    \[{[NH_4Cl] \over [NH_3]} = 10^{0.25} = 1.78\]
    \[[NH_4Cl] = 1.78 \times 0.1 = 0.178\ M\]
    \[Moles\ of\ NH_4Cl = 0.178 \times 0.5 = 0.089\ mol\]
    \[Mass\ of\ NH_4Cl = 0.089 \times 53.5 = 4.76\ g\]
