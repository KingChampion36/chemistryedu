    Chemical Kinetics | Questions Based on Integrated Rate Equations#

    In this article, we will solve some questions related to integrated rate equations and half-life of zero and first order reactions.

    Formulas used

    \[First\ order:\ k = {2.303 \over t} log{[R]_0 \over [R]}\]
    \[First\ order:\ t_{1/2} = {0.693 \over k}\]
    \[Zero\ order:\ k = {[R]_0 - [R] \over t},\ \ t_{1/2} = {[R]_0 \over 2k}\]

    Questions#

    Calculate the half-life of a first order reaction from their rate constants given below:
    (i) 200 s-1 (ii) 2 min-1 (iii) 4 years-1

    \[(i)\ t_{1/2} = {0.693 \over 200\ s^{-1}} = 3.47 \times 10^{-3}\ s\]
    \[(ii)\ t_{1/2} = {0.693 \over 2\ min^{-1}} = 0.35\ min\]
    \[(iii)\ t_{1/2} = {0.693 \over 4\ years^{-1}} = 0.173\ years\]

    The half-life for radioactive decay of 14C is 5730 years. An archaeological artifact containing wood had only 80% of the 14C found in a living tree. Estimate the age of the sample.

    • Radioactive decay follows first order kinetics.
    \[k = {0.693 \over t_{1/2}} = {0.693 \over 5730}\ years^{-1}\]
    \[t = {2.303 \over k} log{[R]_0 \over [R]} = {2.303 \times 5730 \over 0.693} log{100 \over 80}\]
    \[t = {2.303 \times 5730 \over 0.693} \times 0.0969\]
    \[t = 1845\ years\]

    The experimental data for decomposition of N2O5 [2N2O5 → 4NO2 + O2] in gas phase at 318 K are given below:
    t/s: 0, 400, 800, 1200, 1600, 2000, 2400, 2800, 3200
    102 × [N2O5]/mol L-1: 1.63, 1.36, 1.14, 0.93, 0.78, 0.64, 0.53, 0.43, 0.35
    (i) Plot [N2O5] against t. (ii) Find the half-life period for the reaction. (iii) Draw a graph between log[N2O5] and t. (iv) What is the rate law? (v) Calculate the rate constant. (vi) Calculate the half-life period from k and compare it with (ii).

    • (i) The plot of [N2O5] against t is a curve that falls steeply at first and then more slowly (an exponential decay).

    • (ii) Initial concentration = 1.63 × 10-2 mol L-1. Half of it is 0.815 × 10-2 mol L-1. From the graph, this concentration is reached at about t1/2 ≈ 1440 s.

    • (iii) Values of log[N2O5]:

    t/s 0 400 800 1200 1600 2000 2400 2800 3200
    log[N2O5] -1.79 -1.87 -1.94 -2.03 -2.11 -2.19 -2.28 -2.37 -2.46

    The plot of log[N2O5] against t is a straight line with a negative slope.

    • (iv) Since log[N2O5] vs t is a straight line, the reaction is first order:
    \[Rate = k[N_2O_5]\]
    • (v) For a first order reaction, slope of log[R] vs t = -k/2.303:
    \[Slope = {-2.46 - (-1.79) \over 3200 - 0} = -2.09 \times 10^{-4}\ s^{-1}\]
    \[-{k \over 2.303} = -2.09 \times 10^{-4}\]
    \[k = 4.81 \times 10^{-4}\ s^{-1}\]
    • (vi) Half-life from k:
    \[t_{1/2} = {0.693 \over k} = {0.693 \over 4.81 \times 10^{-4}} = 1441\ s\]

    This agrees closely with the value of about 1440 s read from the graph in (ii).

    The rate constant for a first order reaction is 60 s-1. How much time will it take to reduce the initial concentration of the reactant to its 1/16th value?

    \[t = {2.303 \over k} log{[R]_0 \over [R]} = {2.303 \over 60} log\ 16\]
    \[t = {2.303 \over 60} \times 1.2041\]
    \[t = 4.62 \times 10^{-2}\ s\]
    • Alternatively, 1/16 = (1/2)4, so the time needed is 4 half-lives: 4 × (0.693/60) = 4.62 × 10-2 s.

    During nuclear explosion, one of the products is 90Sr with half-life of 28.1 years. If 1 μg of 90Sr was absorbed in the bones of a newly born baby instead of calcium, how much of it will remain after 10 years and 60 years if it is not lost metabolically?

    \[k = {0.693 \over 28.1} = 2.466 \times 10^{-2}\ years^{-1}\]
    • After 10 years:
    \[log{[R]_0 \over [R]} = {kt \over 2.303} = {2.466 \times 10^{-2} \times 10 \over 2.303} = 0.1071\]
    \[{[R]_0 \over [R]} = 1.280\]
    \[[R] = {1 \over 1.280} = 0.7814\ &mu;g\]
    • After 60 years:
    \[log{[R]_0 \over [R]} = {2.466 \times 10^{-2} \times 60 \over 2.303} = 0.6425\]
    \[{[R]_0 \over [R]} = 4.392\]
    \[[R] = {1 \over 4.392} = 0.2277\ &mu;g\]

    For a first order reaction, show that time required for 99% completion is twice the time required for the completion of 90% of reaction.

    • For 99% completion, [R] = 0.01[R]0:
    \[t_{99\%} = {2.303 \over k} log{[R]_0 \over 0.01[R]_0} = {2.303 \over k} log\ 100 = {2 \times 2.303 \over k}\]
    • For 90% completion, [R] = 0.1[R]0:
    \[t_{90\%} = {2.303 \over k} log{[R]_0 \over 0.1[R]_0} = {2.303 \over k} log\ 10 = {2.303 \over k}\]
    \[{t_{99\%} \over t_{90\%}} = 2\]
    \[&Therefore;\ t_{99\%} = 2 \times t_{90\%}\]

    A first order reaction takes 40 min for 30% decomposition. Calculate t1/2.

    • After 30% decomposition, 70% of the reactant is left.
    \[k = {2.303 \over 40} log{100 \over 70} = {2.303 \over 40} \times 0.1549\]
    \[k = 8.918 \times 10^{-3}\ min^{-1}\]
    \[t_{1/2} = {0.693 \over k} = {0.693 \over 8.918 \times 10^{-3}}\]
    \[t_{1/2} = 77.7\ min\]

    For the decomposition of azoisopropane to hexane and nitrogen at 543 K, the following data are obtained:
    t (sec): 0, 360, 720
    P (mm of Hg): 35.0, 54.0, 63.0
    Calculate the rate constant.

    • The reaction is:
    \[(CH_3)_2CHN=NCH(CH_3)_2 &rarr; C_6H_{14} + N_2\]
    • Let the pressure of azoisopropane decrease by x. Then hexane and nitrogen each increase by x.
    \[At\ t = 0:\ p_i,\ 0,\ 0\]
    \[At\ time\ t:\ p_i - x,\ x,\ x\]
    \[p_t = p_i + x,\ \ p_A = p_i - x = 2p_i - p_t\]
    • At t = 360 s:
    \[p_A = 2 \times 35.0 - 54.0 = 16.0\ mm\ Hg\]
    \[k = {2.303 \over 360} log{35.0 \over 16.0} = 2.175 \times 10^{-3}\ s^{-1}\]
    • At t = 720 s:
    \[p_A = 2 \times 35.0 - 63.0 = 7.0\ mm\ Hg\]
    \[k = {2.303 \over 720} log{35.0 \over 7.0} = 2.235 \times 10^{-3}\ s^{-1}\]
    \[Average\ k = {(2.175 + 2.235) \times 10^{-3} \over 2} = 2.20 \times 10^{-3}\ s^{-1}\]

    The following data were obtained during the first order thermal decomposition of SO2Cl2 at a constant volume: SO2Cl2(g) → SO2(g) + Cl2(g)
    At t = 0 s, total pressure = 0.5 atm; at t = 100 s, total pressure = 0.6 atm.
    Calculate the rate of the reaction when total pressure is 0.65 atm.

    • The reaction is of the type A → B + C, so:
    \[p_{SO_2Cl_2} = 2p_i - p_t\]
    • At t = 100 s:
    \[p_{SO_2Cl_2} = 2 \times 0.5 - 0.6 = 0.4\ atm\]
    \[k = {2.303 \over 100} log{0.5 \over 0.4} = {2.303 \over 100} \times 0.0969\]
    \[k = 2.23 \times 10^{-3}\ s^{-1}\]
    • When total pressure is 0.65 atm:
    \[p_{SO_2Cl_2} = 2 \times 0.5 - 0.65 = 0.35\ atm\]
    \[Rate = k \times p_{SO_2Cl_2} = 2.23 \times 10^{-3} \times 0.35\]
    \[Rate = 7.8 \times 10^{-4}\ atm\ s^{-1}\]

    Consider a certain reaction A → Products with k = 2.0 × 10-2 s-1. Calculate the concentration of A remaining after 100 s if the initial concentration of A is 1.0 mol L-1.

    • The unit of k is s-1, so this is a first order reaction.
    \[log{[A]_0 \over [A]} = {kt \over 2.303} = {2.0 \times 10^{-2} \times 100 \over 2.303} = 0.8684\]
    \[{[A]_0 \over [A]} = 7.389\]
    \[[A] = {1.0 \over 7.389} = 0.135\ mol\ L^{-1}\]

    Sucrose decomposes in acid solution into glucose and fructose according to the first order rate law, with t1/2 = 3.00 hours. What fraction of sample of sucrose remains after 8 hours?

    \[k = {0.693 \over t_{1/2}} = {0.693 \over 3.00} = 0.231\ h^{-1}\]
    \[log{[R]_0 \over [R]} = {kt \over 2.303} = {0.231 \times 8 \over 2.303} = 0.8024\]
    \[{[R]_0 \over [R]} = 6.345\]
    \[{[R] \over [R]_0} = {1 \over 6.345} = 0.158\]
    • So, about 0.158 (15.8%) of the sucrose remains after 8 hours.
