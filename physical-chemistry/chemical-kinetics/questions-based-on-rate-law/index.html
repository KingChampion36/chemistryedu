    Chemical Kinetics | Questions Based on Rate Law#

    In this article, we will solve some questions related to rate of reaction, rate law, order of reaction and units of rate constant.

    Questions#

    From the rate expression for the following reactions, determine their order of reaction and the dimensions of the rate constants.
    (i) 3NO(g) → N2O(g); Rate = k[NO]2
    (ii) H2O2(aq) + 3I-(aq) + 2H+ → 2H2O(l) + I3-; Rate = k[H2O2][I-]
    (iii) CH3CHO(g) → CH4(g) + CO(g); Rate = k[CH3CHO]3/2
    (iv) C2H5Cl(g) → C2H4(g) + HCl(g); Rate = k[C2H5Cl]

    • Units of k for order n:
    \[Units\ of\ k = (mol\ L^{-1})^{1-n}\ s^{-1}\]
    Reaction Order Units of k
    (i) 2 mol-1L s-1
    (ii) 1 + 1 = 2 mol-1L s-1
    (iii) 3/2 mol-1/2L1/2s-1
    (iv) 1 s-1

    For the reaction: 2A + B → A2B, the rate = k[A][B]2 with k = 2.0 × 10-6 mol-2L2s-1. Calculate the initial rate of the reaction when [A] = 0.1 mol L-1, [B] = 0.2 mol L-1. Calculate the rate of reaction after [A] is reduced to 0.06 mol L-1.

    • Initial rate:
    \[Rate = k[A][B]^2 = 2.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 0.1 \times (0.2)^2\]
    \[Rate = 8.0 \times 10^{-9}\ mol\ L^{-1}s^{-1}\]
    • When [A] falls to 0.06 mol L-1, A consumed = 0.1 - 0.06 = 0.04 mol L-1.
    • From the equation, 2 mol A reacts with 1 mol B. So B consumed = 0.04/2 = 0.02 mol L-1.
    \[[B] = 0.2 - 0.02 = 0.18\ mol\ L^{-1}\]
    \[Rate = 2.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 0.06 \times (0.18)^2\]
    \[Rate = 3.89 \times 10^{-9}\ mol\ L^{-1}s^{-1}\]

    The decomposition of NH3 on platinum surface is zero order reaction. What are the rates of production of N2 and H2 if k = 2.5 × 10-4 mol L-1s-1?

    \[2NH_3 &rarr; N_2 + 3H_2\]
    \[Rate = -{1 \over 2}{d[NH_3] \over dt} = {d[N_2] \over dt} = {1 \over 3}{d[H_2] \over dt}\]
    • For a zero order reaction, rate = k = 2.5 × 10-4 mol L-1s-1.
    \[Rate\ of\ production\ of\ N_2 = {d[N_2] \over dt} = 2.5 \times 10^{-4}\ mol\ L^{-1}s^{-1}\]
    \[Rate\ of\ production\ of\ H_2 = {d[H_2] \over dt} = 3 \times 2.5 \times 10^{-4} = 7.5 \times 10^{-4}\ mol\ L^{-1}s^{-1}\]

    The decomposition of dimethyl ether leads to the formation of CH4, H2 and CO and the reaction rate is given by Rate = k[CH3OCH3]3/2. The rate of reaction is followed by increase in pressure in a closed vessel, so the rate can also be expressed in terms of the partial pressure of dimethyl ether, i.e., Rate = k(pCH3OCH3)3/2. If the pressure is measured in bar and time in minutes, then what are the units of rate and rate constants?

    • Rate is the change in pressure per unit time:
    \[Units\ of\ rate = bar\ min^{-1}\]
    \[k = {Rate \over (p_{CH_3OCH_3})^{3/2}} = {bar\ min^{-1} \over bar^{3/2}}\]
    \[Units\ of\ k = bar^{-1/2}\ min^{-1}\]

    Mention the factors that affect the rate of a chemical reaction.

    • Concentration of reactants: Rate generally increases with increase in concentration (pressure for gases).
    • Temperature: Rate increases with temperature. A 10° rise nearly doubles the rate constant.
    • Catalyst: A catalyst lowers the activation energy and increases the rate.
    • Nature of reactants and surface area: Ionic reactions are faster than covalent ones, and finely divided solids react faster.
    • Radiation: Some reactions, such as photochemical reactions, speed up in the presence of light.

    A reaction is second order with respect to a reactant. How is the rate of reaction affected if the concentration of the reactant is (i) doubled (ii) reduced to half?

    \[Rate = k[A]^2\]
    • (i) When [A] is doubled:
    \[Rate' = k(2[A])^2 = 4k[A]^2 = 4 \times Rate\]

    The rate becomes 4 times.

    • (ii) When [A] is halved:
    \[Rate' = k\left({[A] \over 2}\right)^2 = {1 \over 4}k[A]^2\]

    The rate becomes one-fourth.

    What is the effect of temperature on the rate constant of a reaction? How can this temperature effect on rate constant be represented quantitatively?

    • The rate constant increases with temperature. For a 10° rise in temperature, the rate constant nearly doubles.
    • This effect is represented quantitatively by the Arrhenius equation:
    \[k = A\ e^{-E_a/RT}\]

    Where A is the Arrhenius (frequency) factor, Ea is the activation energy, R is the gas constant and T is the absolute temperature.

    In a pseudo first order hydrolysis of ester in water, the following results were obtained:
    t/s: 0, 30, 60, 90
    [Ester]/mol L-1: 0.55, 0.31, 0.17, 0.085
    (i) Calculate the average rate of reaction between the time interval 30 to 60 seconds.
    (ii) Calculate the pseudo first order rate constant for the hydrolysis of ester.

    • (i) Average rate between 30 s and 60 s:
    \[r_{av} = -{&Delta;[Ester] \over &Delta;t} = -{(0.17 - 0.31) \over (60 - 30)}\]
    \[r_{av} = 4.67 \times 10^{-3}\ mol\ L^{-1}s^{-1}\]
    • (ii) Using the first order equation:
    \[k = {2.303 \over t} log{[R]_0 \over [R]}\]
    \[At\ t = 30\ s:\ k = {2.303 \over 30} log{0.55 \over 0.31} = 1.911 \times 10^{-2}\ s^{-1}\]
    \[At\ t = 60\ s:\ k = {2.303 \over 60} log{0.55 \over 0.17} = 1.957 \times 10^{-2}\ s^{-1}\]
    \[At\ t = 90\ s:\ k = {2.303 \over 90} log{0.55 \over 0.085} = 2.075 \times 10^{-2}\ s^{-1}\]
    \[Average\ k = {(1.911 + 1.957 + 2.075) \times 10^{-2} \over 3} = 1.98 \times 10^{-2}\ s^{-1}\]

    A reaction is first order in A and second order in B.
    (i) Write the differential rate equation.
    (ii) How is the rate affected on increasing the concentration of B three times?
    (iii) How is the rate affected when the concentrations of both A and B are doubled?

    • (i) Differential rate equation:
    \[-{d[R] \over dt} = k[A][B]^2\]
    • (ii) When [B] is made three times:
    \[Rate' = k[A](3[B])^2 = 9k[A][B]^2\]

    The rate increases 9 times.

    • (iii) When both [A] and [B] are doubled:
    \[Rate' = k(2[A])(2[B])^2 = 8k[A][B]^2\]

    The rate increases 8 times.

    In a reaction between A and B, the initial rate of reaction (r0) was measured for different initial concentrations of A and B as given below:
    [A]/mol L-1: 0.20, 0.20, 0.40
    [B]/mol L-1: 0.30, 0.10, 0.05
    r0/mol L-1s-1: 5.07 × 10-5, 5.07 × 10-5, 1.43 × 10-4
    What is the order of the reaction with respect to A and B?

    • Let rate = k[A]x[B]y.
    \[(1):\ 5.07 \times 10^{-5} = k(0.20)^x(0.30)^y\]
    \[(2):\ 5.07 \times 10^{-5} = k(0.20)^x(0.10)^y\]
    \[(3):\ 1.43 \times 10^{-4} = k(0.40)^x(0.05)^y\]
    • Dividing (1) by (2):
    \[1 = \left({0.30 \over 0.10}\right)^y = 3^y\]
    \[y = 0\]
    • Dividing (3) by (2), with y = 0:
    \[{1.43 \times 10^{-4} \over 5.07 \times 10^{-5}} = \left({0.40 \over 0.20}\right)^x\]
    \[2.82 = 2^x\]
    \[x = {log\ 2.82 \over log\ 2} = {0.4503 \over 0.3010} = 1.5\]
    • Hence, the order with respect to A is 1.5 and with respect to B is zero.

    The following results have been obtained during the kinetic studies of the reaction: 2A + B → C + D
    Experiment I: [A] = 0.1, [B] = 0.1, initial rate of formation of D = 6.0 × 10-3 mol L-1min-1
    Experiment II: [A] = 0.3, [B] = 0.2, rate = 7.2 × 10-2
    Experiment III: [A] = 0.3, [B] = 0.4, rate = 2.88 × 10-1
    Experiment IV: [A] = 0.4, [B] = 0.1, rate = 2.40 × 10-2
    Determine the rate law and the rate constant for the reaction.

    • Let rate = k[A]x[B]y.
    • Experiments I and IV ([B] constant):
    \[{2.40 \times 10^{-2} \over 6.0 \times 10^{-3}} = \left({0.4 \over 0.1}\right)^x\]
    \[4 = 4^x,\ \ x = 1\]
    • Experiments II and III ([A] constant):
    \[{2.88 \times 10^{-1} \over 7.2 \times 10^{-2}} = \left({0.4 \over 0.2}\right)^y\]
    \[4 = 2^y,\ \ y = 2\]
    \[Rate = k[A][B]^2\]
    • Rate constant from Experiment I:
    \[k = {Rate \over [A][B]^2} = {6.0 \times 10^{-3} \over 0.1 \times (0.1)^2}\]
    \[k = 6.0\ mol^{-2}L^2min^{-1}\]

    The reaction between A and B is first order with respect to A and zero order with respect to B. Fill in the blanks in the following table:
    Experiment I: [A] = 0.1, [B] = 0.1, initial rate = 2.0 × 10-2 mol L-1min-1
    Experiment II: [A] = ?, [B] = 0.2, rate = 4.0 × 10-2
    Experiment III: [A] = 0.4, [B] = 0.4, rate = ?
    Experiment IV: [A] = ?, [B] = 0.2, rate = 2.0 × 10-2

    \[Rate = k[A]^1[B]^0 = k[A]\]
    • From Experiment I:
    \[k = {2.0 \times 10^{-2} \over 0.1} = 0.2\ min^{-1}\]
    \[Experiment\ II:\ [A] = {4.0 \times 10^{-2} \over 0.2} = 0.2\ mol\ L^{-1}\]
    \[Experiment\ III:\ Rate = 0.2 \times 0.4 = 8.0 \times 10^{-2}\ mol\ L^{-1}min^{-1}\]
    \[Experiment\ IV:\ [A] = {2.0 \times 10^{-2} \over 0.2} = 0.1\ mol\ L^{-1}\]
